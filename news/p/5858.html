拖行一英里的不是车，是选择What Dragged Him a Mile Was a Choice

其他 直接层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
他知道人在车下，还是踩了油门。
He knew the man was under the car. He drove anyway.

西蒙·理查森知道米凯尔·穆林斯在车底下。他自己说的——他的表亲、副驾驶加布里埃尔·德博特在法庭上作证，听到外面传来呻吟声，然后他说：“他在车下面。”

从那一刻起，之后发生的每一件事都不是事故。他把车开到一英里外的偏僻农场小路，两次试图把人拽出来，没成功，然后回到车上，故意左右猛打方向，压上路边的草地。穆林斯被甩出来，他又倒车碾过去，开走。一个摩托车骑手顺着帕多克巷的血迹找过去，发现穆林斯一个人躺在草地上，几乎没了呼吸。

检方说得很准：穆林斯是“他正在逃离的那件事的唯一、确凿的证据”。所以理查森要处理的不是一个人，是一件证据。

这里没有结构暴力可谈，也不必往文化层上扯。这是直接暴力，赤裸的、有明确施暴者、有可见事件的暴力。但直接暴力从来不是凭空发生的，它发生在一个允许它被“处理”的叙事里——在那个叙事里，一个骑车的人挡了路，是麻烦，是障碍，是可以被甩掉的东西。理查森在撞上穆林斯之前就已经冲过了让行标志。让行标志是给别人的，不是给他的。

这就是为什么陪审团花了不到三天就定了谋杀。不是因为证据复杂，是因为证据简单到只有一种读法：他想了半秒钟要不要下车，然后决定不下。

穆林斯的家人说，他是两个孩子的父亲，是斯特雷特福德文法学校的校长，心都放在家里。一个骑车回家的人，在傍晚六点，被一个闯了让行标志的司机撞上，然后被拖了一英里，然后被倒车碾过，然后被留在草地上等死。

这件事里唯一值得说的“好”，是那个摩托车骑手顺着血迹跟了过去，是陪审团做了他们该做的事，是法官把陪审团十年内免除再次陪审的义务——因为她知道他们看到的东西有多难受。这些都是真的。但它们缩小的差额，只是“一个人被谋杀之后，有没有人为此负责”这一小段。

穆林斯已经死了。理查森周一量刑。那个让行标志还在阿尔特林厄姆的路口立着，下一个闯过去的人，会想什么？

Simon Richardson knew Michael Mullins was under his car. He said so himself — his cousin Gabrielle Debotte, in the passenger seat, testified that they both heard moaning from outside, and then he said: “He’s under the car.”

From that moment on, nothing that followed was an accident. He drove to a secluded farm track 1.3 miles away, twice tried to pull Mullins out, failed, got back in, and veered sharply and deliberately from side to side before mounting a grass verge. Mullins came free, and Richardson reversed over him and drove off. A motorcyclist followed the trail of blood from Paddock Lane and found Mullins alone on the grass, barely breathing.

The prosecution put it exactly right: Mullins was “the single, solid piece of evidence of what he was driving away from.” Richardson wasn’t dealing with a person. He was dealing with evidence.

There is no structural violence to unpack here, and no need to reach for the cultural layer. This is direct violence — naked, with a named perpetrator and a visible event. But direct violence never comes from nowhere. It comes from a narrative in which a cyclist on the road is a nuisance, an obstacle, a thing to be shaken off. Richardson went straight through give-way markers before he ever hit Mullins. Give-way markers are for other people. Not for him.

That’s why the jury took less than three days. Not because the evidence was complicated, but because it only reads one way: he spent half a second deciding whether to get out, and decided not to.

Mullins was a father of two, the headteacher of Stretford Grammar School, devoted to his family. A man cycling home at six in the evening, struck by a driver who blew through a junction, dragged for a mile, reversed over, and left on the grass to die.

The only good in this story is that a motorcyclist followed the blood, that the jury did what juries are supposed to do, that the judge discharged them from jury service for ten years because she knew what they had seen. All of that is real. But the gap it closes is only the small stretch between “a man was murdered” and “someone was held responsible for it.”

Mullins is dead. Richardson is sentenced on Monday. The give-way markers are still standing at that junction in Altrincham. What will the next person who drives through them be thinking?
