十七个席位，一张不存在的选票Seventeen Seats, One Erased Ballot

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Democracy Now! ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
选票可以增加，人可以被宣布不存在。
You can add seats. You can still declare a people nonexistent.

以色列大选的结果里有一个数字最刺眼：Joint List 和 Ra'am 合计拿下 17 席，史上最多。然后 Netanyahu 和 Eisenkot 同时宣布，不会和阿拉伯政党联合组阁。17 席的意义是零。这不是政治分歧，这是把一个族群在议会里保留成装饰品——让你投票，让你计票，让你在新闻里出现，然后告诉你，你的席位不进入组阁的算术。

Khalidi 说得很准：问题不是两派有什么不同，而是两派在涉及阿拉伯人、巴勒斯坦人、加沙战争、对伊朗的敌意上，根本没有不同。他们争的是犹太公民内部的司法改革和威权化，巴勒斯坦人的处境不在桌面上。这就是共谋的经典结构：两个看起来对立的阵营，在一个不需要讨论的前提上完全一致。这个前提就是元暴力——谁算人，谁不算，谁的解释权不需要被质询。

东耶路撒冷、约旦河西岸、加沙的几百万巴勒斯坦人没有投票资格。以色列国内的巴勒斯坦公民有投票资格，但投出来的席位被两大阵营共同否决。两条路，一条是法律上的排除，一条是政治上的排除，通向同一个结论：巴勒斯坦人的表达不进入决策。这是 structural violence 和 cultural violence 咬合的地方——制度取消你的票，叙事取消你的存在。

Kanafani 那句话是整本书的骨头：占领者和被占领者之间的和平谈判，是剑和脖子之间的对话。1972 年他被以色列炸死在车里，同车的还有他 17 岁的侄女。Khalidi 说，他被杀是因为他的话太危险。这是最直白的证据：当一个人说的话让暴力无法自我辩护，暴力就转向消灭说话的人。武器化的终点不是让你说错，是让你不能再说。

美国的角色 Khalidi 讲得很直接：每一架战机、每一枚 2000 磅炸弹、安理会上的每一次否决、几十亿美元，都是美国的。所以这是美国-以色列的战争。而美国国内，公众舆论已经转向，政客和精英没有。Khalidi 说人们看到了无法当作没看到的东西。这句话的另一面是：看到了，不等于能改变。民意和政策的落差，就是这轮博弈里还没被缩小的差额。

英国那段更值得看。Palestine Action 是非暴力的，陪审团一次次判他们无罪，于是有人提议废掉陪审团——问题不是以色列在做什么，是陪审团在做什么。然后他们去碰了 Trump 在苏格兰的高尔夫球场，禁令才真正落地。Ali 说，如果政府聪明，就让最高法院判他们输，但他们不敢，因为那会是政策的巨大失败。Ed Miliband 在台上说“你们是对的”，台下前一天警察抓了五十多个人，包括 Massive Attack 的主唱，举着“我反对种族灭绝，我支持 Palestine Action”的牌子。Ali 的判断是：他可能真信，但底下有赢回穆斯林选票的算计，而且“毫无影响”。判断一个表达，看它改变了什么，不看它说得多好。

Ali 最后说，六十年代反战运动的触发点是越南，今天是巴勒斯坦，连从来没有声援运动历史的国家都冒出了巴勒斯坦旗，因为他们的政府在压制异议，人们就在 defiance 自己的政府。这是 cultural 层唯一还在动的地方：一个被垄断的叙事正在被反向使用。但旗子不是席位，席位不是组阁权。

17 席换不来一个部长职位，几十万人的街头换不来一个禁运。差额还在那里，而且是被精确地维护着的——不是没人看见，是看见的人手里没有那把剑。

One number from the Israeli election matters most: the Joint List and Ra'am took 17 seats, their strongest showing ever. Then Netanyahu and Eisenkot both ruled out governing with the Arab parties. The 17 seats mean nothing. This is not political disagreement. This is keeping a population inside parliament as decoration — let them vote, let them be counted, let them appear on the news, then tell them their seats do not enter the arithmetic of coalition-building.

Khalidi puts it precisely: the question is not how the two blocs differ, but that on anything touching Arabs, Palestinians, the war on Gaza, hostility to Iran, they do not differ at all. What they contest is judicial reform and authoritarianism among Jewish citizens. The Palestinian condition is not on the table. This is the classic structure of complicity: two camps that look opposed, in complete agreement on a premise that never needs discussing. That premise is meta-violence — who counts as a person, who does not, whose interpretation never has to be questioned.

Millions of Palestinians in East Jerusalem, the West Bank and Gaza have no ballot. Palestinians who are citizens of Israel have a ballot, and the seats it produces are jointly vetoed by both blocs. Two roads, one legal exclusion, one political exclusion, arriving at the same conclusion: Palestinian expression does not enter decision-making. This is where structural violence and cultural violence interlock — the institution cancels your vote, the narrative cancels your existence.

Kanafani's line is the spine of the book: peace talks between occupier and occupied are a conversation between the sword and the neck. In 1972 Israel blew him up in his car, together with his 17-year-old niece. Khalidi says he was murdered because his words were too dangerous. That is the plainest evidence available: when someone's words make the violence unable to defend itself, the violence turns to eliminating the speaker. The endpoint of weaponized expression is not making you wrong. It is making you unable to speak.

The American role, per Khalidi: every combat aircraft, every 2,000-pound bomb, every Security Council veto, tens of billions of dollars. So this was an American-Israeli war. And inside the US, public opinion has shifted; the politicians and elites have not. People have seen something that cannot be unseen, Khalidi says. The other side of that sentence: seeing is not the same as changing. That gap between opinion and policy is the差额 this round of the game has not closed.

The UK section is the one to study. Palestine Action is nonviolent; juries kept acquitting them; so the proposal became abolish the juries — the problem is not what Israel does, it is what juries do. Then they damaged a bit of Trump's golf course in Scotland, and only then did the ban become real. Ali's read: if the government were clever it would let the Supreme Court rule against them, but it cannot, because that would be a huge defeat for a central plank of policy. Ed Miliband says from the stage, you were right; the day before, police arrested more than fifty people outside the conference, including Massive Attack's frontman holding a banner reading I oppose genocide, I support Palestine Action. Ali's verdict: he probably means it, there is a cynical calculation underneath about winning back Muslim votes, and it has had nil impact. Judge an expression by what it changes, not by how well it is said.

Ali closes on this: in the '60s and '70s Vietnam triggered the mega-protests; today the anti-imperial issue is Palestine, and even in countries with no history of a solidarity movement the flag suddenly emerges — because their governments are trying to stop dissent, so people defy their governments. That is the one place where the cultural layer is still moving: a monopolized narrative being turned against itself. But a flag is not a seat, and a seat is not coalition power.

Seventeen seats buy no ministry. Hundreds of thousands in the street buy no arms embargo. The差额 is still there, and it is being maintained with precision — not because nobody sees, but because the people who see are not holding the sword.
