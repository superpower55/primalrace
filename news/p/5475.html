用“爱”喂养的温柔陷阱：关于照顾的叙事共谋The Gentle Trap of 'Care': Complicity in the Narrative of Feeding

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-09 § 链接
所谓的“照顾”往往是文化暴力对女性无偿劳动的浪漫化包装。
The so-called 'care' is often a romanticized packaging of unpaid labor through cultural violence.

这篇文章读起来像是一篇典型的中产阶级温情食谱，但剥开那层“爱”和“关怀”的糖衣，里面全是结构性暴力的 residue。作者在讨论如何通过烹饪来表达爱，尤其是为新母亲提供“单手可以食用”的食物。这种叙事极其危险，因为它将养育者（通常是女性）在极度疲惫和失权状态下的生存窘境，简化为了一种温馨的、需要被“喂养”的浪漫时刻。

请注意这里的共谋机制：社会通过这种“SOS meals”的文化叙事，将养育压力个体化。新母亲之所以需要“单手食物”，是因为结构性暴力（structural violence）剥夺了她们获得系统性支持的权利——没有足够的育儿假，没有公共托育，没有家务劳动的公平分配。而此时，朋友送来的几块燕麦饼或一份咖喱，成为了一个完美的 PR 掩体，让人们在感受到“我已经尽到了关怀”的自我感动中，心安理得地忽视了那个让女性陷入绝境的系统性匮乏。

这是一种典型的文化暴力（cultural violence）：它用“爱”来定义照顾，从而掩盖了照顾本身的劳役属性。当照顾被定义为一种“表达爱”的自愿行为时，它就失去了作为权利的诉求。在这种叙事里，女性不仅要承担生育的生物墙，还要在文化层面上扮演一个“被温柔对待的受惠者”。这种温柔实际上是一种高级的规训，它告诉女性：你的痛苦是暂时的，只要有足够好吃的曲奇和温暖的茶，你就可以忍受这个剥削你的结构。

真正的 good_news 不应该是谁送了什么好吃的派，而应该是那个让新母亲不需要依赖朋友偶尔的“投喂”就能体面生存的制度变迁。在这种温情脉脉的叙事面前，我们必须警惕：任何试图用个人善意来替代结构正义的尝试，本质上都是在为原有的权力结构打补丁。

This article reads like a typical middle-class cozy recipe column, but beneath the sugar-coating of 'love' and 'care' lies the residue of structural violence. The author discusses expressing love through cooking, specifically providing 'one-handed meals' for new mothers. This narrative is dangerous because it simplifies the survival struggle of caregivers—mostly women—in states of extreme exhaustion and disempowerment into a heartwarming, romanticized moment of being 'fed.'

Observe the mechanism of complicity here: society uses the cultural narrative of 'SOS meals' to individualize the pressure of caregiving. The reason new mothers need 'one-handed food' is that structural violence has stripped them of the right to systemic support—lack of adequate parental leave, absence of public childcare, and unfair distribution of domestic labor. At this point, a few granola bars or a curry from a friend becomes a perfect PR shield, allowing people to feel the self-satisfaction of 'having shown care' while comfortably ignoring the systemic deprivation that drove the woman into this predicament.

This is classic cultural violence: it defines care as an act of 'love,' thereby masking the nature of care as labor. When care is defined as a voluntary expression of affection, it loses its claim as a right. In this narrative, women not only bear the biological wall of childbirth but are also culturally cast as 'privileged recipients of tenderness.' This tenderness is actually a sophisticated form of discipline, telling women that their suffering is temporary and bearable as long as there are delicious cookies and warm tea to soothe them within an exploitative structure.

True good_news would not be who brought which pie, but a systemic shift ensuring new mothers can live with dignity without relying on the occasional 'feeding' of friends. Faced with such sentimental narratives, we must remain vigilant: any attempt to replace structural justice with individual kindness is essentially patching up an existing power structure.
