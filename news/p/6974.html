死者被做成一面旗They Made a Dead Man Into a Flag

其他 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要去年，Henry Nowak 被 Vickrum Digwa 刺死；他倒地濒死时，因凶手诬称他种族辱骂而被警察戴上手铐。改革党领袖 Nigel Farage 随后发表紧急讲话，呼吁公众以「纯粹的冷怒」回应，并要求终止他所称的「双标执法」。数周后，包括 Ukip、Britain First 领袖在内的抗议者在南安普顿示威。Nowak 的家人接受 ITV 纪录片采访时说，他们努力把政治排除在儿子的故事之外，并指有人利用他的死推进政治议程。
The newsHenry Nowak was fatally stabbed by Vickrum Digwa last year; as he lay dying, police handcuffed him after his attacker falsely accused him of racial abuse. Reform UK leader Nigel Farage then gave an emergency address urging the public to respond with "pure cold rage" and demanding an end to what he called "two-tier policing". Weeks later, protesters including the leaders of Ukip and Britain First demonstrated in Southampton. In an ITV documentary, Nowak's family said they had worked hard to keep politics out of his story and accused some people of using his death to advance political arguments.
他们用他的死打一场他生前没参加过的仗。
They used his death to fight a war he never joined.

Henry Nowak 的家人说，他「会讨厌」那些骚乱。这句话把整件事的重心挪回了它该在的地方：一个被刺死的人，和一群拿他当旗子的人，不是同一件事。

先看警察做了什么。他被 Vickrum Digwa 刺中，倒地濒死，凶手诬称他种族辱骂，警察于是给他戴上手铐。这个动作不需要恶意，只需要一个默认：先信那个还站着说话的人，再处理那个躺着流血的人。凶手的一句话，在那一刻比受害者的身体更有分量。这是直接暴力之后的第二次暴力，施暴者换成了程序。

然后是 Farage。他发表紧急讲话，要公众以「纯粹的冷怒」回应，要求终止「双标执法」。这套话术的入口很熟：把一桩具体的凶杀改写成一条抽象的战线，把死者的名字变成动员的口号。抗议者来了，Ukip 和 Britain First 的领袖来了，National Rebirth 党朴茨茅斯支部来了。他们不是来问 Henry 为什么躺在地上被铐着，他们是来用他的尸体给自己的立场加一票。

母亲 Lucy Ross 那句话问得比任何评论都准：「我想知道为什么 Henry 躺在地上，被那样对待，而那一家人被另样对待。这是什么？」她要的是答案，不是口号。她明确说，她不感兴趣他们的政治游戏。家属说 Henry「不是一个制造分裂的人」，而政治回应「不符合 Henry 的价值观，也不符合我们的价值观」。一个被谋杀的人，死后被安上了一个他生前拒绝的立场。

这里最值得指出的，是解释权在谁手里。家属在 ITV 的纪录片里说话，说的是「我们努力把政治挡在 Henry 的故事之外」；Farage 在紧急讲话里说话，说的是「纯粹的冷怒」。两套叙述争夺同一个死人，一套要他安静，一套要他燃烧。谁的声音更大，谁就定义了 Henry Nowak 是谁。这就是元暴力在日常里的样子：不是有人拿刀，是有人拿麦克风，决定一个死者的名字该被念成什么。

而制度这边，IOPC 四月写信给家属，说经过「仔细考虑现有证据」，没有警员会面临纪律或刑事调查，理由是没有任何关于警员行为和决定的担忧严重到足以启动程序。七月，IOPC 又宣布两名警员因潜在严重不当行为接受调查。内政大臣 Shabana Mahmood 委托前大法官 Alex Chalk 领导独立审查，查是否有「错失的干预机会」。IOPC 说对到场警员的调查预计明年初结束。这些动作是真的在查，还是在给一个已经烧起来的舆论场降温，要看最后有没有人真的为那副手铐负责。

家属要的从来不是骚乱，是一个解释：为什么他躺着，铐着，而另一边被另样对待。这个问题至今没有答案。Farage 们已经用完了他的死，家属还在等。

Henry Nowak's family said he "would have hated" the riots. That sentence moves the weight of this story back to where it belongs: a man who was stabbed to death and a crowd that turned him into a flag are not the same thing.

Start with what the police did. He was stabbed by Vickrum Digwa and lay dying when his attacker falsely accused him of racial abuse, so the police handcuffed him. That action needs no malice, only a default: believe the man still standing and talking, deal with the man lying down and bleeding later. One sentence from a killer weighed more, in that moment, than a victim's body. This is the second violence after the direct one, and the perpetrator is the procedure.

Then came Farage. He delivered an emergency address calling on the public to respond with "pure cold rage" and demanding an end to what he called "two-tier policing". The entry point is familiar: turn a specific killing into an abstract front line, turn the dead man's name into a rallying cry. The protesters came. The leaders of Ukip and Britain First came. The Portsmouth branch of the National Rebirth party came. They did not come to ask why Henry was on the floor in handcuffs. They came to add a vote to their own position using his corpse.

Lucy Ross's question is sharper than any commentary: "I want to know why Henry was on the floor, and he got treated the way he did, but that family got treated in a different way. What is that?" She wants answers, not slogans. She said plainly she is not interested in their political game. The family said Henry "was not a divisive person", and that the political response "didn't sit with Henry's values or our values". A murdered man has been assigned, after death, a position he refused in life.

What deserves pointing out here is who holds the power of interpretation. The family spoke in an ITV documentary about working hard to keep politics out of Henry's story. Farage spoke in an emergency address about "pure cold rage". Two narratives fight over the same dead man: one wants him quiet, one wants him burning. Whoever is louder defines who Henry Nowak was. This is meta-violence in its everyday form: not someone holding a knife, but someone holding a microphone, deciding how a dead man's name gets pronounced.

On the institutional side, the IOPC wrote to the family in April saying that after "careful consideration of the available evidence", no officers would be subject to conduct or criminal investigation, because no concerns about officer actions and decisions were serious enough to justify proceedings. In July the IOPC announced that two officers were under investigation for potential gross misconduct. The home secretary, Shabana Mahmood, commissioned an independent review led by the former lord chancellor Alex Chalk to examine whether there were "any missed opportunities to intervene". The IOPC said its investigation into the attending officers is expected to wrap up early next year. Whether these moves are a real inquiry or a way to cool a burning舆论场 will be settled only when someone is actually held accountable for those handcuffs.

The family never wanted riots. They wanted an explanation: why he was on the floor, in handcuffs, while the other side was treated differently. That question still has no answer. Farage and his people have finished using Henry's death. The family is still waiting.
