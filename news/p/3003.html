文明崩塌的叙事，不过是另一种中产阶级的生存焦虑Civilizational Collapse: Another Middle-Class Anxiety Narrative

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
将结构性暴力包装成“复杂系统崩溃”，是典型的认知逃避。
Framing structural violence as 'systemic collapse' is a classic cognitive escape.

Sarah Wilson 把文明的崩塌比作罗马或玛雅的重复，试图用一种“复杂系统论”来消解当下的痛苦。这是一种典型的 weaponized narrative：当你把资源匮乏、政治极化和气候危机统称为“系统性崩溃”时，你实际上在抹除施暴者。在她的逻辑里，没有具体的剥削者，只有被“复杂度”压垮的文明。这种叙事最危险的地方在于，它把 structural violence 变成了某种不可抗力的自然规律，让人们在“临界点”的恐惧中获得一种虚假的认知共识。

她提到的“后工业、殖民主义、榨取式系统”确实是元暴力的温床，但她的药方却是让个体“简化生活”或“建立有机社区”。这不过是中产阶级在意识到资产可能缩水后的生存策略。对于那些已经在苏丹或加沙经历“崩塌”的人来说，这种所谓的“liminal moment”不是什么“惊人的可能性”，而是 direct violence 的日常。把全球性的结构压迫简化为一次“自然校准”，是对原初种族和被殖民者苦难的再次消解。

最讽刺的是，她呼吁我们要“活得美丽”，在崩塌中寻找诗意。这又是另一种浪漫爱叙事的变体——将绝望美学化。当一个系统在榨取全球资源的同时，建议受害者通过“骑自行车”和“用指南针”来准备未来，这根本不是在对抗系统，而是在为系统的死亡写一份体面的悼词。真正的最优解不是在废墟上练习生活美学，而是拆穿那个定义“文明”的男本位权力结构。

Sarah Wilson likens the collapse of civilization to the fall of Rome or the Maya, attempting to dissolve current suffering into a 'complex systems theory.' This is a textbook weaponized narrative: by labeling resource scarcity, political polarization, and climate crisis as 'systemic collapse,' she effectively erases the perpetrators. In her logic, there are no specific exploiters, only a civilization crushed by 'complexity.' The danger here is that structural violence is rebranded as an inevitable natural law, allowing people to find a false consensus in the fear of a 'liminal moment.'

While she identifies the 'post-industrial, colonialist, extractivist system' as the root, her prescription is for individuals to 'simplify life' or 'build organic communities.' This is nothing more than a survival strategy for the middle class realizing their assets might shrink. For those already experiencing 'collapse' in Sudan or Gaza, this 'liminal moment' is not a 'stunning possibility' but the daily reality of direct violence. Reducing global structural oppression to a 'natural recalibration' is yet another erasure of the suffering of the Primal Race and the colonized.

Most ironic is her plea to 'live beautifully' amidst the ruins, a variant of the romantic narrative—aestheticizing despair. When a system continues to extract global resources while suggesting victims prepare for the future by 'riding bikes' and 'using compasses,' it is not fighting the system; it is writing an elegant obituary for it. The true optimal expression is not practicing the aesthetics of life atop ruins, but dismantling the masculine-centric power structure that defines 'civilization' in the first place.
