片酬谣言背后是定价权The Rumor Is the Pricing Mechanism

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《好汉两个半》演员梅兰妮·林斯基回应网络传言，否认自己每集片酬在20万至30万美元之间。她表示自己只出演了该剧262集中的63集，起薪每集2万美元、每季涨薪约5%，最高一集约4.3万美元，且这是扣除经纪人和税费前的数字。同剧演员霍兰·泰勒也在播客中说该剧从未给她像样的涨薪。
The newsMelanie Lynskey denied an online claim that she earned $200,000 to $300,000 per episode of Two and a Half Men. She said she appeared in only 63 of the show's 262 episodes, started at $20,000 per episode with roughly a 5% seasonal raise, and peaked at about $43,000 before agent cuts and taxes. Co-star Holland Taylor said the show never gave her a raise either.
她不是被低估，她是被定价。
She wasn't underpaid. She was priced.

这条新闻真正值得看的不是梅兰妮·林斯基赚了多少，而是那个谣言为什么能流传——一个只演了63集、角色是跟踪狂配角的女演员，被默认「应该」拿每集二三十万。这个默认本身就是定价机制的一部分：它假装这个行业对女性已经足够慷慨，慷慨到不需要再谈钱。

林斯基自己把账算得很清楚：起薪每集两万，每季涨约5%，最高一集四万三，还是税前、扣完经纪人和经理人之前，到手大约一半。霍兰·泰勒拿过四次艾美奖提名，她的说法是「他们从来没给我涨过薪」——「通常他们会，出于礼貌。这部剧不太讲礼貌。」同剧的乔恩·克莱尔说，他拿的是查理·辛的三分之一。这不是一个女演员的委屈，这是一套分账逻辑：谁被认定为「撑起这部剧的人」，谁就拿走大头，而撑起这部剧的人，默认是男的。

用《原初种族》的话说，这是元暴力在片场里的日常形态——不是谁当面说了句歧视的话，而是「谁值多少钱」这个判断本身的解释权，长期握在同一批人手里。泰勒的「礼貌性涨薪」说法最狠：涨薪不是对她表演的定价，是对她「在场」的礼节性补偿，礼节可以给，也可以不给。

林斯基说她很感激这份工作，它让她付得起小额房贷、付得起账单、能去拍独立电影。这句话是真的，也是这套结构最稳固的地方——它给的刚好够你感激，不够你议价。

谣言说她赚了几百万，她说「这太荒谬了」。荒谬的不是数字，是那个数字被造出来时，没有人觉得需要先问一句：她演了几集？

The interesting thing here isn't what Melanie Lynskey made. It's why the rumor — a supporting actress in 63 episodes, playing a stalker, supposedly pulling $200,000 to $300,000 a episode — sounded plausible enough to circulate. That plausibility is itself part of the pricing machine: it lets the industry pretend it is already generous to women, generous enough that money no longer needs discussing.

Lynskey did the math herself. $20,000 a episode to start, roughly a 5% raise each season, a peak of about $43,000 before agents, managers and taxes — take home around half. Holland Taylor, four Emmy nominations, says the show never gave her a raise. 「They usually do, just as a courtesy,」 she said. 「They were not full of courtesies.」 Jon Cryer says he made a third of Charlie Sheen's salary. This isn't one actress's grievance. It's a split-the-pot logic: whoever gets defined as carrying the show takes the lion's share, and the one carrying the show is, by default, the man.

In the vocabulary of the book, this is meta-violence in its most banal form. Nobody says anything discriminatory on set. The power to decide who is worth what simply sits with the same people it has always sat with. Taylor's line about courtesy is the sharpest: a raise isn't a price on her performance, it's a politeness extended to her presence. Politeness can be withheld.

Lynskey says she was grateful — the job paid a small mortgage, paid bills, let her do indie films. That's true, and it's exactly what makes the structure stable. It pays just enough to be grateful for, not enough to bargain with.

The rumor said millions. She said that's ridiculous. What's ridiculous isn't the number. It's that nobody who made it up thought to ask how many episodes she was in.
