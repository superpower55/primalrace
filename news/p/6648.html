叛逃不是意外，是共谋的账单Defection Isn't an Accident. It's a Bill Coming Due.

国际 元暴力 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
全党劝不住的倒戈，说明她从来不是失控，是被默许。
The defection nobody could stop was never out of control. It was permitted.

Haley Stevens 在周日 endorsement 了共和党人 Mike Rogers，前一天晚上她还在向同僚保证会支持 El-Sayed。Raskin 说自己被"blindsided"，"dumbfounded"。一个众议员在二十四小时内对党内同僚撒谎、对幕僚撒谎（"Omg why would you think I would do that?!!"），然后走进底特律的黑人教堂和共和党人站在一起——这件事被写成"党控制不住她"。

这就是共谋者理论最标准的伪装：把一次利益站队包装成一次情绪失控。报道里所有高层都在说同一句话——不是我们的错，是 Haley Stevens 一个人的错。"I certainly don't think it's somebody else's fault, other than Haley Stevens." 说得好像党内没有任何人从她输掉初选之后的冷处理里获利。Stevens 输给 El-Sayed 之后，密歇根的一批民主党人因为 El-Sayed 和 Hasan Piker 的关系、因为他在以色列问题上的立场，一直和他保持距离。这个距离不是 Stevens 一个人划出来的，是全党一起划的。距离划了几个月，现在人走了，大家开始惊讶。

真正该看的是谁在给这件事定价。Schumer 一开始就认为 Stevens 比 El-Sayed 更适合打大选，现在他说这是"despicable betrayal"。Jeffries 支持 El-Sayed 的声明里连 Stevens 的名字都不提——一个党团领袖对自己党团成员的处理方式是"不提"，这不是愤怒，这是止损。他们愤怒的不是背叛本身，是背叛被看见了。

Cenk Uygur 那句话说得比谁都诚实："it caused two weather fronts to collide"。他的反以色列广告本来是要打 El-Sayed 的对手，结果把 Stevens 推到了 Rogers 那边。他形容这是一场爆炸，语气里甚至有点得意。一个 PAC 投放的广告，把一个众议员推过党线，然后这个 PAC 的负责人出来说"我也没想到"——制造可能性的人从来不需要为可能性负责。

这里没有人在谈密歇根的选民要什么。报道说 El-Sayed 在犹太选民和黑人选民里本来就吃力，现在整个州又被放进了全国第一批初选州的位置。党的分裂被写成一场关于以色列和左翼的路线之争，但路线之争的底下是席位之争：谁能在 2028 年的初选里说"我早就说过"。如果 El-Sayed 输了，出钱支持他的建制派和说建制派不可信的左翼会互相追责；如果他赢了，反以色列的一翼就能拿这个结果去削弱 AIPAC 那句"good policy and good politics"。两种结果都有人已经准备好了台词。

Stevens 的幕僚辞职了，州党部把她开除出党，众议院党团有人在讨论惩罚她。惩罚会来，但惩罚的对象是一个已经不打算回来的人。真正没人追问的是：一个输掉初选的人，为什么能在几个月里一直拿着党的资源、党的信任、党的沉默，直到她走进那间教堂。

Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers on Sunday, less than a day after assuring colleagues she would back El-Sayed. Raskin says he was "blindsided," "dumbfounded." A congresswoman lies to her colleagues, lies to her own staff ("Omg why would you think I would do that?!!"), then walks into a Black church in Detroit with a Republican—and the story gets written as the party failing to control her.

This is the co-conspirator theory in its standard costume: a factional alignment dressed up as a personal meltdown. Every leader in the piece says the same thing—not our fault, it's Haley Stevens alone. "I certainly don't think it's somebody else's fault, other than Haley Stevens." As if nobody benefited from the cold shoulder she got after losing the primary. Michigan Democrats kept their distance from El-Sayed for months over Hasan Piker and over Israel. That distance wasn't Stevens's private project. The whole party drew it. They drew it for months, and now they're surprised the person walked.

What matters is who was pricing this. Schumer had already decided Stevens was the better general-election candidate. Now he calls it a "despicable betrayal." Jeffries's statement backing El-Sayed doesn't mention Stevens by name—a caucus leader's way of handling his own member is to not say her name. That isn't anger. That's damage control. They're not furious about the betrayal. They're furious it was seen.

Cenk Uygur is the most honest person in the article: "it caused two weather fronts to collide." His anti-Israel ad was meant to hit El-Sayed's opponents; instead it pushed Stevens toward Rogers. He calls it an explosion with something close to satisfaction. A PAC runs an ad, a congresswoman crosses the party line, and the man who made it says he never imagined the outcome. The people who manufacture possibilities never have to answer for them.

Nobody in this piece is talking about what Michigan voters want. El-Sayed was already struggling with Jewish and Black voters before this week, and the state now sits among the first-in-the-nation Democratic nominating states. The split gets written as a fight over Israel and the left, but under the fight is a fight over seats: who gets to say "I told you so" in the 2028 primary. If El-Sayed loses, the establishment that spent tens of millions on him and the left that says the establishment can't be trusted will blame each other. If he wins, the anti-Israel wing gets to weaken AIPAC's line that its agenda is "good policy and good politics." Both outcomes already have their scripts written.

Stevens's staff resigned. The state party expelled her. The caucus is discussing punishment. The punishment will come, but it lands on someone who isn't coming back. The question nobody asks: why did a candidate who lost her primary keep the party's resources, the party's trust, and the party's silence for months—right up until she walked into that church.
