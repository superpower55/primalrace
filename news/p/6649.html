围城里的医院只剩两成In Taiz, the Hospitals Are Down to a Fifth

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
战争叙事里没有平民，只有战线和士气
War coverage counts terrain, morale and supply lines. It does not count the people those lines run through.

塔伊兹的医院只剩两成产能，120 家卫生中心全部关闭，这是这场战争里唯一一个不需要解释的数字。

其余的数字都在讲别的事。政府军收复了 Huwaishan 山脉，指挥官说那是胡塞在东线的第一道防线；研究员说这给了政府军监视和切断补给线的能力；另一位准将强调推进要「持续但不快速」。82% 的省域面积、五个省的连接、与沙特的边界——全篇报道里，每一段都在算地形的价值，没有一段在算人的价值。

这就是战争叙事的标准工序：把地理翻译成战略，把战略翻译成士气，士气再翻译成「未来行动的进程」。人在这条翻译链上被一路稀释，最后剩下的只有「超过 30 万人逃离家园」这样一句放在文末的收尾。谁逃的、逃去哪、路上死了多少、剩下的人靠什么活，报道没写。这不是 Al Jazeera 一家的疏漏，这是战争报道这个文体的默认设置——它默认读者关心的是谁在赢。

塔伊兹的围城把这件事摊开得最清楚。狙击手在周围的山上，食物和燃料被严格限制，省长向国际社会呼吁「至少开放一条安全通道」。注意这个措辞：一条。不是两条，不是解除围困，是一条能让物资进来的路。围城作为一种战术之所以长期有效，正因为它不产生可见的战斗，只产生缓慢的消耗——医院从满负荷降到两成，卫生中心一家一家关掉，这些都不是「事件」，是「现状」。加尔通那个公式在这里算得明明白白：本可以不死的人死了，差额就是暴力，而施暴者不需要出现在现场。

也门这场仗打了十二年，Al-Hazm 在 2020 年被胡塞拿下，如今政府军要打回去。控制权来回换，地形反复易手，指挥官换了一个又一个——新上任的 Al-Akimi 少将带着部落影响力来「统一战线」。分析人士说得很实在：能不能把影响力转化成可持续的战果，取决于指挥统一、后勤保障和反腐。翻译一下：能不能打赢，取决于能不能把资源有效地送到前线。至于这些资源从哪来、平民为此付了什么，不在分析框架里。

十二年了。十二年间，一场又一场「黎明行动」，一次又一次「战略高地」，而塔伊兹的医院还在等那一条路。

报道最后提到，胡塞方面称他们挫败了政府军的推进。两边都在宣布自己的版本。而 120 家关掉的卫生中心不会宣布任何事——它们只是关着。

Taiz's hospitals are running at 20 percent capacity, and 120 health centres are entirely shut. That is the only number in this story that does not need explaining.

Every other number is about something else. Government forces took the Huwaishan mountain range, which a field commander calls the Houthis' first line of defence on the eastern axis. A researcher says the heights give them surveillance, movement and the ability to cut supply routes. Another officer stresses that advances must be "continuous but not rapid." Eighty-two percent of the governorate's area, borders with Saudi Arabia, links to five provinces — paragraph after paragraph prices the terrain. None of them prices the people standing on it.

This is the standard procedure of war narrative: geography becomes strategy, strategy becomes morale, morale becomes "the course of future operations." People get diluted at every step of that translation, until all that's left is one line near the bottom — more than 300,000 displaced from western Yemen. Who they are, where they went, how many died on the road, what the ones who stayed are living on: not in the story. This isn't Al Jazeera's failure alone. It's the default setting of the war-reporting genre, which assumes the reader wants to know who is winning.

The siege of Taiz lays the mechanism bare. Snipers on the surrounding hills, food and fuel severely restricted, and the governor appealing to the international community for "at least one safe route" between Taiz and Aden. Note the wording: one. Not two, not a lifting of the siege — one road so goods can get in. A siege works as a tactic precisely because it produces no visible battle, only slow attrition. Hospitals drop from full capacity to a fifth; health centres close one by one. None of this is an "event." It is "the situation." Run Galtung's formula on it and the arithmetic is plain: people who could have lived are dead, and the difference is violence — violence that requires no perpetrator on the scene.

This war is twelve years old. Al-Hazm fell to the Houthis in March 2020; now the government wants it back. Control changes hands, terrain changes hands, commanders change hands — the newly appointed Major General Al-Akimi arrives with tribal influence to "unify the front." An analyst puts it bluntly: turning that influence into sustainable gains depends on unified command, logistics, and anti-corruption efforts. Translated: winning depends on delivering resources to the front efficiently. Where those resources come from, and what civilians pay for them, is outside the analytical frame.

Twelve years. One "Dawn of Yemen" after another, one strategic height after another, and Taiz is still waiting for that one road.

The report notes that the Houthis say they have thwarted the government's advances. Both sides get to announce their version. The 120 closed health centres will announce nothing. They are simply closed.
