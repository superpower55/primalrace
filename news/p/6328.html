强制快乐是另一种规训Mandatory Fun Is Just Another Drill

文娱 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
当快乐被规定为义务，它就成了服从性测试。
When joy becomes an obligation, it is a loyalty test.

华盛顿的万圣节不是节日，是一次服从性测试。作者写得很清楚：请柬上写着装扮“必须”，不参加的人会被当成背叛者，仿佛那个清醒的旁观者正在记录、评判，甚至可能搅黄你二十年后的任命听证会。

这就是表达被武器化的日常版本，只是武器很小，小到只是一套服装。在一个“受控、紧张、不喝酒、时刻提防二十年后那场听证会”的城市里，一年中被允许失控一次，而失控的方式必须提前几周想好、必须符合主题、最好还要是政治梗（性感版约翰·博纳）。这不是放纵，这是把放纵也纳入管理。

作者说这是“反乐趣”（anti-fun），说得对，但还可以再进一步：强制快乐的功能不是让人快乐，是让人证明自己属于这里。装扮是表达，而在这里表达被规定了格式。你穿什么不重要，重要的是你穿了，并且穿得能被认出来是“我们的人”。不穿的人不是无趣，是不合群，而不合群在华盛顿是一种政治风险。

最讽刺的是作者自己的位置。他列了证据：31岁，扮过Nelly、默片明星、他想象中Ali G的女朋友，还保持着同龄人早已放弃的那种乐趣（证据留在莫斯科）。他必须证明自己“不是讨厌乐趣”，才能被允许说讨厌万圣节。这就是规训完成的样子：连拒绝都要先递交忠诚声明。

一个城市把一年的失控压缩进一个晚上，还要求这个晚上整齐划一，说明它平时有多不能失控。万圣节在这里不是出口，是阀门。阀门开一下，管道继续承压。

作者最后说，你们逼我写了这篇。他没说出口的是：这篇也是那套服装的一部分，一个“我讨厌万圣节”的人设，同样是在这个城市里被允许的一种表演。

Halloween in Washington isn't a holiday. It's a compliance test. The invitations say the costume is "mandatory." Skip it and you feel the quiet accusation of betrayal, as if you were the sober observer taking notes, judging, possibly scuttling someone's confirmation hearing two decades out.

This is the everyday, miniature version of expression weaponized. The weapon is just a costume. In a city described as controlled, tense, unboozy, forever braced for a hearing twenty years away, one night a year is licensed for losing control—provided the loss is planned weeks ahead, on theme, ideally a political joke (Sexy John Boehner). That isn't abandon. That's abandon with a management layer.

The author calls it anti-fun, and he's right, but push it further: mandatory fun doesn't exist to make anyone happy. It exists so people can prove they belong. Dress-up is expression, and here expression comes with a required format. What you wear matters less than the fact that you wore something recognizable as one of us. The one who doesn't dress isn't a bore; he's a defector, and defection in Washington is a political risk.

The sharpest part is the author's own position. He has to list his credentials—31 years old, past costumes including Nelly, a silent film star, his imagined version of Ali G's girlfriend, still having the kind of fun his peers gave up years ago (evidence stays in Moscow)—before he's allowed to say he hates it. That's what a finished discipline looks like: even refusal requires a loyalty statement first.

A city that compresses a year of losing control into one evening, then demands that evening be uniform, is telling you how little it can afford to lose control the rest of the time. Halloween here isn't an exit. It's a valve. The valve opens once, the pipe keeps its pressure.

He ends by saying Washington drove him to write this. What he doesn't say: the post is also part of the costume. "The guy who hates Halloween" is one of the personas this city permits—and performing it is still performing.
