社交媒体上的“登山表演”与国家机器的强制纠偏Social Media 'Hiking Performance' and the State's Forced Correction

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-03 § 链接
当表达被异化为社交货币，生存本能就成了表演的代价。
When expression is alienated into social currency, survival instinct becomes the price of performance.

这是一场典型的关于“最优解表达”的集体性误判。在 Hike-tok 这种认知入口的操纵下，登山不再是与自然的交互，而是一次关于“专业度”的身份表演。为了在社交媒体上获得那个被定义为“酷”的标签，登山者们通过削减装备、压缩行程来伪装成专业跑者。这种假.最优解表达（Fake Optimal Expression）的代价是极高的：它要求个体在生物墙和自然规律面前，用表演性的轻盈去置换生存的冗余。

当这种个体的存在性博弈演变为群体性的 trend，它就变成了对公共资源的结构性掠夺。每一次因为“追求内容”而导致的失踪，都在强迫国家机器投入巨大的 SAR（搜救）人力与财力。此时，政府的介入不再是单纯的行政管理，而是在 Potential（本可安全登山）与 Actual（陷入险境）的差额被无限拉大时，通过 Structural Violence（强制禁令、强制向导、健康筛查）来对冲 Direct Violence（死亡与伤残）的发生。

有趣的是，这套逻辑与商业世界的“审美定价权”如出一辙。当“轻量化”被定义为高级，而“冗余装备”被定义为业余时，人们为了晋升社交圈层，不惜在真实世界中扮演一个随时可能死掉的专业人士。这种自我规训让人们在追求“存在感”的过程中，反而加速了主体性的消失——你不再是一个在山林中行走的人，而是一个被算法驱动的、正在完成“登顶”指标的生物采样点。

禁令或许能减少失踪人数，但它无法解决认知入口的污染。只要“成就导向”的自然观依然是社交货币的最高兑换率，人们就会在禁令的边缘寻找新的、更危险的表演方式。

This is a classic case of collective misjudgment regarding 'Optimal Expression'. Under the cognitive entry of 'Hike-tok', hiking is no longer an interaction with nature, but a performance of identity centered on 'professionalism'. To acquire the label of 'cool' defined by social media, hikers strip away essential gear and compress schedules to masquerade as professional trail runners. This Fake Optimal Expression comes at a steep price: it demands that individuals replace survival redundancy with a performative lightness in the face of biological walls and natural laws.

When this existential game of the individual evolves into a collective trend, it becomes a structural predation of public resources. Every disappearance driven by the 'need for content' forces the state machinery to invest massive SAR manpower and finances. Here, government intervention is not mere administration, but an attempt to offset Direct Violence (death and injury) by implementing Structural Violence (mandatory guides, bans, and screenings) when the gap between Potential and Actual safety is widened to an extreme.

Interestingly, this logic mirrors the 'pricing power of aesthetics' in the commercial world. When 'minimalism' is defined as high-class and 'redundancy' as amateur, people risk their lives to fit into a social stratum. This self-discipline leads to the death of subjectivity—you are no longer a human walking in the woods, but a biological sampling point driven by algorithms to tick off an 'achievement' box.

Bans may reduce the number of missing persons, but they cannot cleanse the polluted cognitive entries. As long as an 'achievement-based' relationship with nature remains the highest exchange rate for social currency, people will continue to seek new, more dangerous ways to perform on the edge of the law.
