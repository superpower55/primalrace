票房七十三万，先骂影评人$725,000, and the Critics Are the Problem

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
解释权打不过票房，就先取消影评人的资格。
When you can't win the argument, disqualify the people making it.

维塔提的反击不是关于电影，是关于谁有资格说话。他把影评人叫作"可悲的迷途灵魂"——没当成艺术家，才去当评论家。这句话的功能很明确：把批评从审美判断降格为失败者的怨气，于是批评本身失效了。这是最古老的认知入口操作，先定义对方是谁，再让对方说的话不算数。

他给出的证据是烂番茄上78%的观众分对28%的影评人分。这个对比本身值得看：观众分是买票进场的人打的，影评人分是职业看片的人打的，两个样本从一开始就不是同一群人。把它当成"人民对精英"的公投，等于用投票率不同的两个池子证明自己赢了。真正能说明问题的是另一个数字——上映首周末约72.5万美元，880家北美影院，没进前十，全球约170万美元。一部由石黑一雄小说改编、詹娜·奥尔特加和艾米·亚当斯主演的电影，这个体量说明的不是"被误解"，是没什么人去看。

"我十五年没读过任何针对我或我同行的评论"——这句话和后面那句"但我听人民的"放在一起，是完整的操作：先宣布自己不看批评，再宣布自己只听观众，于是批评这个职业被移出了他的世界，而观众被留下来当他的证人。他不读评论，但他读了烂番茄的百分比，还把它做成海报发出来。选择性失明，看得见的只有对自己有利的那一栏。

有意思的是同一篇报道里他对AI的说法："只要它被当作工具而不是替代品，我不想替代人类，我想和人类一起工作。"这话说得挺体面。可就在几段之前，他刚刚示范了一次相反的动作——不是和批评者一起工作，是让批评者闭嘴。工具和替代品的区分，他给自己留了裁量权：有用的人留下，碍事的人进垃圾桶。"Out of touch. Obsolete. In the bin."三个词连着用，节奏很爽，爽的是权力，不是道理。

这不是一部电影的事。这是第三章那套东西的日常版：谁掌握认知入口，谁就能决定什么算"真实的反响"。票房是硬的，观众分是软的，影评人分是可以被重新归类为"无关"的。当一个导演开始给批评定价、给批评者定身份，他做的和所有武器化表达一样——不是回应事实，是制造一个对自己有利的事实。

真正被这句话扫到的人不在好莱坞。是那些写影评拿不到几个钱、还在认真拆一部片子哪里成立哪里不成立的人。他们没当成艺术家，也没打算当，他们做的是另一份工作。维塔提说他们可悲，是因为他需要他们可悲。

至于那78%——买票的观众里，有多少是冲着石黑一雄来的，有多少是冲着奥尔特加来的，有多少是看完觉得还行随手点了个高分，报道没写。一个首周末七十二万的片子，观众分的样本有多大，也没写。数字被举起来当盾牌的时候，通常是因为手里没有别的了。

Waititi's pushback isn't about the film. It's about who gets to speak. He calls critics "sad lost souls" who couldn't become artists, so they became critics instead. The move is precise: reclassify criticism as the resentment of failures, and the criticism stops counting. Define the speaker first, then make what they said irrelevant. That's the oldest entry point there is.

His evidence is 78 percent audience score against 28 percent critics score on Rotten Tomatoes. Look at what that comparison actually is. The audience score comes from people who bought tickets. The critics score comes from people who watch films professionally. Two different samples, never the same crowd. Framing it as the people versus the elite only works if you pretend the two pools are one electorate. The number that does describe the situation is elsewhere: an estimated $725,000 opening weekend across 880 North American theaters, outside the top 10, roughly $1.7 million worldwide. A film adapted from an Ishiguro novel, starring Jenna Ortega and Amy Adams, at that scale isn't misunderstood. It's barely seen.

"I haven't read a review of anything aimed at me or my peers in almost 15 years. But I do listen to the people." Those two sentences together are the whole operation. First declare yourself deaf to criticism, then declare yourself tuned to the audience, and criticism as a profession has been removed from his world while the audience is kept as his witness. He doesn't read reviews, but he read the Rotten Tomatoes percentages and put them on a poster. Selective blindness, seeing only the column that flatters him.

What makes it sharper is what he says in the same piece about AI: as long as it's used as a tool and not a replacement, he doesn't want to replace humans, he wants to work with them. Reasonable enough. Except a few paragraphs earlier he demonstrated the opposite instinct. Not working with the critics. Silencing them. Tool or replacement, he keeps the discretion: useful people stay, inconvenient people go in the bin. "Irrelevant. Out of touch. Obsolete. In the bin." Three words in a row, satisfying rhythm, and what's satisfying is the power, not the argument.

This is the everyday version of the machinery in Chapter 3. Whoever controls the cognitive entry point decides what counts as a real response. Box office is hard. Audience score is soft. The critics score can be reclassified as irrelevant. When a director starts pricing criticism and assigning identities to critics, he's doing what all weaponized expression does: not answering the facts, manufacturing a favorable one.

The people actually hit by that caption aren't in Hollywood. They're the ones writing reviews for almost nothing, still working out carefully where a film holds and where it collapses. They didn't fail to become artists. They weren't trying. They do a different job. Waititi needs them to be sad, because that's what makes his sentence land.

And that 78 percent: how many bought tickets for Ishiguro, how many for Ortega, how many just shrugged and clicked high afterward, the report doesn't say. How large the audience sample is for a film that opened to $725,000, it doesn't say either. Numbers get held up as shields when there's nothing else in hand.
