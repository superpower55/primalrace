旗帜、爱国主义与暴力的掩体Flags, Patriotism, and the Shield of Violence

国际 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-07 § 链接
用“爱国”定义的认知入口，本质是为直接暴力提供文化掩体。
Defining 'patriotism' as a cognitive entry point is simply creating a cultural shield for direct violence.

Ryan Bridge 和他的 Raise the Colours 玩了一套非常典型的 weaponized expression。他们把 Union Jack 和 St George’s flags 这些符号，从简单的国家标识重新定义为“团结与爱国主义”的认知入口。在元暴力的逻辑里，只要你抢占了“爱国”这个定义权，那么接下来的任何排外、骚扰甚至身体攻击，都可以被包装成是对“纯洁国家”的捍卫。

这种叙事极其阴险，因为它在 cultural 层制造了一个伪装：将直接暴力 (direct violence) 转化为一种“觉醒”的仪式。Farage 对 Bridge 的赞美——称其“唤醒了国家的大部分地区”——就是典型的共谋者姿态。政治精英通过给这种草根暴力背书，将种族歧视和骚扰合法化，使其看起来不像暴力，而像是一种“必要的爱国表达”。

但法律和证据（witness accounts and footage）撕开了这个掩体。14 项指控，包括种族加剧的骚扰和袭击，证明了这套“爱国主义”表达的真.最优解根本不是 unity，而是通过制造恐惧来确立一种基于种族等级的支配权。当旗帜被用来标记领地并威胁异己时，它就不再是符号，而是直接暴力的前奏。

这场博弈的讽刺之处在于，Bridge 试图通过扮演一个“爱国英雄”来获取社会政治资本，但这种假.最优解表达最终在 structural 层的法律制裁面前崩塌。不过，只要这种将“爱国”与“排外”绑定的认知入口依然畅通，下一个 Ryan Bridge 很快就会出现，因为这套叙事在很多共谋者心中依然是高效的武器。

Ryan Bridge and his 'Raise the Colours' group have executed a textbook case of weaponized expression. By rebranding the Union Jack and St George’s flags as entry points for 'unity and patriotism,' they attempted to hijack the narrative of national identity. Under the logic of meta-violence, once you seize the definition of 'patriotism,' any subsequent xenophobia, harassment, or assault can be repackaged as a defense of a 'pure' nation.

This narrative is insidious because it creates a cultural mask: it transforms direct violence into a ritual of 'awakening.' Nigel Farage’s praise—claiming Bridge 'woke up vast parts of this country'—is a classic posture of complicity. Political elites, by endorsing this grassroots aggression, legitimize racial harassment, making it look not like violence, but like a 'necessary patriotic expression.'

However, the law and the evidence—witness accounts and footage—have stripped away this shield. The 14 charges, including racially aggravated harassment and assault, prove that the actual optimal expression of this 'patriotism' was never unity, but the establishment of dominance through fear and racial hierarchy. When a flag is used to mark territory and threaten others, it ceases to be a symbol and becomes a prelude to direct violence.

The irony of this game is that Bridge tried to gain social and political capital by playing the role of a 'patriotic hero'—a fake optimal expression that eventually collapsed under the structural violence of legal sanctions. Yet, as long as the cognitive entry point linking 'patriotism' to 'exclusion' remains open, another Ryan Bridge will emerge, because this narrative remains a highly efficient weapon for many complicitors.
