性爱戏不是表演，是劳动Sex Scenes Aren't Performance. They're Labor.

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
亲密协调员在场，才让这场戏从表演变成工作。
An intimacy coordinator was on set. That's the only real story here.

一条关于《Wicker》的报道，通篇在夸两位演员拍床戏拍得开心。Colman 说自己本来觉得性爱戏很尴尬，这次不尴尬了，因为有幽默感，因为导演 Fischer 和 Wilson 是夫妻、会亲自示范。Skarsgård 说“性爱很棒”，然后解释：戏拍得很早，前两天就拍了，他还在摸索那身柳条人套装能动到什么程度，床上不灵活，只能换个方式再试。

值得说的不是他们有多放得开，是报道里那句一笔带过的话：整个过程中有 intimacy coordinator 参与。这句话才是这条新闻真正的信息量。Colman 的“不尴尬”不是凭空来的，是有一个人在片场把这件事从“两个演员在镜头前脱衣服”重新定义成“一项有流程、有边界、有协商的工作”。

第三章讲表达的武器化，色情产业是其中一个入口，它的逻辑是把身体变成产品，把表演变成剥削，演员的快感是演的，观众的兴奋是真的。好莱坞的床戏长期沿用同一套逻辑：导演喊开始，演员自己想办法。intimacy coordinator 这个工种的出现，是把这套逻辑往回掰了一点——它承认镜头前的身体接触是一种劳动，劳动就需要知情、同意、可以喊停。这不是道德洁癖，这是把定价权和边界还给干活的人。

但别急着把这条新闻当成胜利。报道里真正被讨论的是两位主演的舒适度，是 Skarsgård 穿着柳条套装怎么在床上翻身，是一个技术难题怎么被解决。这些当然值得说。可这条新闻从头到尾没有出现那个协调员的名字，也没有说她做了什么、怎么谈的、边界画在哪里。一个让整件事变得可接受的关键角色，在报道里是一个没有名字的括号。

这就是元暴力的日常形态：不是有人跳出来反对，是有人做了最关键的工作，然后从叙事里消失。Colman 和 Skarsgård 可以坦然说“性爱很棒”，因为有人替他们把不棒的部分提前处理掉了。

还有一层。这部片子的设定是一个孤独的女人被村落 ritual 挑去结婚，她不满，于是找篮子匠给自己编一个丈夫。一个被安排婚姻的女人，最后和一个编织出来的男人上了床。报道把它写成爱情故事，评论夸它“探讨归属、安全与快感”。归属和安全，恰恰是那个村落 ritual 从她身上拿走的东西。用一具柳条身体把这两样还给她，是童话，不是解法。

真正的问题不是床戏拍得好不好。是那个协调员的名字为什么不在稿子里，以及这部片子有没有意识到，它让一个女人逃离的安排婚姻的方式，是再定制一个男人。

The whole report is about how much fun two actors had shooting sex scenes. Colman says she usually finds them embarrassing, not this time, because there was humor and because the directors, Fischer and Wilson, are married and would demonstrate. Skarsgård deadpans that the sex was great, then explains: they shot those scenes in the first couple of days, he was still figuring out what the wicker suit allowed, you're not nimble in bed, so you try it another way.

The interesting part isn't how comfortable they were. It's the clause buried mid-paragraph: an intimacy coordinator was involved at every step. That's the actual news. Colman's lack of embarrassment didn't fall from the sky. Someone on that set redefined the thing from two actors taking their clothes off in front of a camera into work with a process, boundaries, and negotiation.

Chapter three treats porn as one entry point for weaponized expression: the body becomes product, performance becomes extraction, the actor's pleasure is acting, the viewer's arousal is real. Hollywood bed scenes ran on the same logic for decades. Director calls action, actors figure it out. The intimacy coordinator pushes back on that logic a little. It treats on-camera contact as labor, and labor requires informed consent and the right to call cut. Not prudishness. Just returning the boundary and the pricing power to the person doing the work.

Don't file this as a win yet. What the piece actually discusses is the comfort of two stars, and the technical problem of Skarsgård maneuvering in a wicker suit. Fair enough. But the coordinator is never named. The report doesn't say what she did, how she negotiated, where the lines were drawn. The person who made the whole thing tolerable is a parenthetical without a name.

That's meta-violence in its everyday form. Nobody stands up to object. Someone does the essential work, then disappears from the narrative. Colman and Skarsgård can say the sex was great because someone else handled the part that wasn't, in advance.

One more layer. The film's premise: a lonely woman is chosen for marriage in a village ritual she doesn't want, so she commissions a basketmaker to weave her a husband. A woman handed an arranged marriage ends up in bed with a man made of wicker. The report frames it as a love story; the review praises its meditation on belonging, security, and pleasure. Belonging and security are exactly what the ritual took from her. Giving them back through a woven body is a fairy tale, not a fix.

The question isn't whether the bed scene worked. It's why the coordinator has no name in the piece, and whether the film notices that its heroine escapes an arranged marriage by ordering a custom man.
