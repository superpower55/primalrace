“只是好玩”：元暴力在强奸现场的实时翻译“Just Fun”: The Real-time Translation of Meta-Violence at a Rape Scene

性别 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-15 § 链接
施暴者通过重新定义受害者的痛苦，将暴力伪装成“乐趣”。
The perpetrator weaponizes expression to redefine a victim's agony as 'pleasure'.

当 Russell Brand 在强奸受害者时说出“这只是好玩”(it's just fun)时，他完成了一次极其典型的 cultural violence 操作：他试图在暴力的发生瞬间，直接接管受害者的认知入口。

这不仅仅是简单的谎言，而是一种元暴力 (meta violence) 的实时翻译。在施暴者的叙事里，女性的哀求、疼痛和窒息被强行定义为“乐趣”或“正常”。这种行为的本质是：既然我无法通过共识获得你的身体，那么我就通过定义“什么是事实”来抹除你的主体性。他试图让受害者在被侵犯的同时，内化一套“你应该学会享受”的逻辑。这正是 weaponized expression 的最恶劣形式——将摧毁对方的意志包装成一种“引导”。

受害者在案发后感到 numb，并因为 Brand 变得越来越有名而保持沉默，这揭示了 structural violence 的运作机制。名声、权力与社会地位构成了一个巨大的共谋场域，让受害者在面对一个“成功者”时，潜意识里觉得自己的真实体验在对方的社会权重面前毫无价值。这种沉默不是因为恐惧，而是因为在男本位叙事的压制下，受害者的存在性被彻底边缘化了。

好在，这次庭审让这种“定义权”发生了易手。当这些细节被摆在法庭上，Brand 曾经用来掩盖暴力的那套“好玩”叙事，反而成了证明其主观恶意和操控心理的最有力证据。这场博弈的重点不在于他是否否认，而在于这种“定义事实”的权力正在被拆穿。

When Russell Brand told his victim "it's just fun" during the assault, he performed a textbook operation of cultural violence: attempting to hijack the victim's cognitive entry point at the very moment of violence.

This is more than a lie; it is a real-time translation of meta violence. In the perpetrator's narrative, the victim's pleas, pain, and suffocation are forcibly redefined as "fun" or "normal." The essence of this act is: since I cannot obtain your body through consent, I will erase your subjectivity by redefining "what is fact." He attempted to make the victim internalize a logic of "learning to enjoy it" while being violated. This is the most sinister form of weaponized expression—packaging the destruction of another's will as a form of "guidance."

The victim's subsequent numbness and her years of silence as Brand became "more and more famous" reveal the machinery of structural violence. Fame, power, and social status create a field of complicity, making the victim feel that her lived experience is worthless compared to the perpetrator's social weight. This silence is not merely fear, but the result of the victim's existence being marginalized under a masculine-centric narrative.

Fortunately, this trial marks a shift in the ownership of the "right to define." As these details are laid bare in court, the "fun" narrative Brand used to mask his violence has instead become the strongest evidence of his subjective malice and manipulative psychology. The core of this game is not whether he denies the charges, but that the power to manufacture "facts" is finally being dismantled.
