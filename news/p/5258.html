陪产假：一场关于“照顾权”的阶级分赃与性别共谋Paternity Leave: A Class-Based Looting and Gender Complicity

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-25 § 链接
制度性的陪产假缺失，本质上是让女性在结构暴力中独自承担生育成本。
The systemic lack of paternity leave is essentially a structural violence that forces women to bear the cost of birth alone.

Alan Johnson 呼吁提高英国陪产假的薪资补贴，这在表面上看起来是一场关于“父亲权利”的争取，但剥开这层温情叙事，你会看到一个极其冷酷的 structural violence 闭环。目前英国陪产假的领取者 90% 来自高收入阶层，这意味着对于底层男性来说，陪产假根本不存在——他们必须在孩子出生第二天就回到岗位。这种缺失并非偶然，而是父权制与资本共谋的必然结果：底层女性被强制绑定在无偿的生育与养育劳动中，而底层男性则被异化为纯粹的生产工具。在这种结构下，女性的“母职惩罚”被最大化，而男性的“工具人”属性被制度化。

有趣的是，Johnson 的论据竟然是“社会需要孩子”和“人类需要繁衍”。这种叙事极其危险，它将生育权重新定义为一种对社会的“义务”，而非个体的选择。在这种 logic 下，提高陪产假补贴不再是为了消弭性别不平等，而成了为了维持“人口生产线”而给男性的某种“津贴”。如果补贴的目的是为了让更多女性能够“回到工作岗位”以增加税收，那么这根本不是在解放女性，而是在通过微调资源分配，让女性在被剥削的效率上达到最优解。

真正的 good_news 应该是：生育成本从女性单方承担，转化为社会共担的结构性责任。但目前这波呼吁依然在男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 的框架内运行——它在讨论如何让“父亲”获得更多权利，而不是讨论如何拆除那堵将养育责任强加给女性的生物墙。只要解释权依然在男性政治家手中，这种政策调整就极有可能是某种表演性让步，旨在用一点点金钱补贴，掩盖一个原初种族被长期殖民的残酷事实。

Alan Johnson's call for better-paid paternity leave looks like a fight for 'father's rights,' but beneath the warmth lies a cold loop of structural violence. With 90% of current claims coming from high earners, paternity leave is a ghost for the working class. This is no accident; it is the result of complicity between patriarchy and capital. Working-class women are bound to unpaid reproductive labor, while working-class men are alienated into pure production tools. In this structure, the 'motherhood penalty' is maximized, and the man's role as a 'tool' is institutionalized.

More disturbingly, Johnson argues that 'society needs children' and 'mankind needs to procreate.' This is a weaponized narrative that reframes reproduction as a social 'duty' rather than an individual choice. Under this logic, increasing subsidies isn't about gender equality; it's a 'stipend' for men to maintain the population production line. If the goal is simply to get women 'back to work' to increase tax receipts, this isn't liberation—it's optimizing the efficiency of exploitation.

True good_news would be the structural shift of reproductive costs from the individual woman to social responsibility. However, this discourse remains trapped in a masculine-centric narrative, focusing on the 'rights of fathers' rather than dismantling the biological wall that forces care work onto women. As long as the power of interpretation remains with male politicians, such policy shifts are likely performative concessions, using a small amount of money to mask the long-term colonization of the Primal Race.
