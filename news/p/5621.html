朋克叙事下的伤口：谁在定义少女的‘混乱’？Wounds Beneath the Punk Narrative: Who Defines a Girl's 'Chaos'?

性别 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-17 § 链接
将女性的痛苦浪漫化为‘成长的混乱’，是最高级的文化暴力。
Romanticizing female suffering as 'teenage chaos' is a sophisticated form of cultural violence.

这部电影试图在 Stockport 的后朋克背景下勾勒一个少女的 self-portrait，但最耐人寻味的是评论者将其描述为“青春期混乱的研习”（a study in the chaos of being young）。在 masculine-centric narrative 中，少女的痛苦、被霸凌、被学校抛弃，往往被打包成一种带有美感的“混乱”或“阵痛”，从而消解了这些经历背后的 structural violence。

电影中的 Ann 试图通过模仿 Joy Division 来“灼烧”痛苦，这种表达方式本身就是一种博弈。她试图在一个由男性定义的音乐场景（music scene）中寻找存在感，但结果是她被 Tony Wilson 这种 supercilious 的男性权力持有者轻而易举地拒之门外。这种被排斥感不是偶然，而是因为在那个时代的认知入口里，女性只能是场景的背景或被消费的客体，而非创造者。

最令人不安的是关于“自杀”的叙事。电影对比了三个自杀者，只有 Ian Curtis 的死没有被“羞耻地掩盖”（shame-filled cover-up）。这揭示了一个残酷的 meta violence：男性的崩溃可以被转化为某种传奇或艺术符号，而女性（尤其是家庭内部）的崩溃则必须被掩盖，因为她们的痛苦不具备被公共空间承认的“价值”。

Ann 最终通过酒精和抑郁试图埋葬痛苦，这在很多评论者看来是 poignant 的，但在我看来，这是一个主体性在共谋环境下被挤压后的必然结果。当一个少女发现她的真.最优解表达无法在现实中获得公正的响应时，她只能选择在自我毁灭中完成某种病态的表达。

The film attempts to sketch a self-portrait of a young woman against the post-punk backdrop of Stockport, but the most telling part is the reviewer's description of it as "a study in the chaos of being young." In a masculine-centric narrative, a girl's pain, her bullying, and her expulsion from school are often packaged as an aesthetic "chaos" or "growing pains," effectively erasing the structural violence behind these experiences.

Ann's attempt to "cauterize" her agony by imitating Joy Division is a game of expression. She seeks existence within a music scene defined by men, only to be effortlessly shut out by power-holders like Tony Wilson. This exclusion is not accidental; in the cognitive portals of that era, women were meant to be backgrounds or objects of consumption, never the creators.

Most disturbing is the narrative of suicide. The film contrasts three deaths, noting that only Ian Curtis's was not a "shame-filled cover-up." This reveals a brutal meta-violence: a man's collapse can be transformed into a legend or an artistic symbol, while a woman's collapse—especially within the domestic sphere—must be hidden because her pain lacks the "value" to be acknowledged in public space.

Ann eventually buries her pain in depression and drinking. While critics find this poignant, I see it as the inevitable result of a subjectivity crushed under a system of complicity. When a girl finds that her true optimal expression receives no just response from reality, she is forced to complete a pathological expression through self-destruction.
