被删除的房客与被美化的“魔法”Deleted Tenants and the Glamour of 'Magic'

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-12 § 链接
历史的“包容性”如果只停留在叙事，就是一种文化层面的PR。
Inclusivity in history is just a cultural PR stunt if it only expands the narrative without challenging the monopoly of interpretation.

David Olusoga 觉得他的节目之所以成功，是因为他把视角锁死在一栋房子里，挖掘出了那些“普通人”的故事。他管这叫“历史的扩张” (expansion of history)，认为现在历史不再只关于“伟人”，而是变得更 inclusive 了。这听起来像个 good_news，但如果你用 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式去算，你会发现这里的 Actual 依然极其匮乏。

节目里提到的那个被警方委婉称为“名声不佳之屋” (house of ill-fame) 的女性，是全剧最有趣的 anomally。制作团队试图“锁定”她的故事却失败了。这种“难以捉摸” (elusive) 不是因为档案丢失，而是因为在那个时代的 structural violence 下，一个经营妓院的女性根本没有被允许留下任何关于“主体性”的表达。她的存在被简化为一个警方的记录，一个被定义为“不洁”的标签。这就是典型的元暴力 (meta violence)：解释权永远在警察、在法律、在男性书写者手里，而被掠夺者只能在历史的缝隙里留下一个被扭曲的投影。

Olusoga 赞美旧房子的“魔法” (magical)，这种浪漫化叙事其实是一种认知入口的 weaponization。当我们将贫民窟的岁月、被驱逐的苦难包装成“人类故事的拼图”时，我们实际上是在消费那些被结构性暴力抹除的人。如果一个房客在历史上被定义为“无名”或“卑贱”，那么这种“ inclusive”的挖掘，究竟是在还原正义，还是在用现代的审美去给过去的暴力打补丁？

真正的 inclusive 不应该是“我也愿意看看那些底层人”，而应该是追问：为什么在同一个空间里，有些人的生活被详细记录成史诗，而有些人的存在只能通过警方的“委婉语”来确认？这种解释权的垄断，直到今天依然在运作。

David Olusoga attributes the success of his show to the 'expansion of history,' claiming that history is no longer just about 'great men' but has become more inclusive. It sounds like a victory, but applying the formula Violence = Potential − Actual reveals a stark deficit in actual progress.

The woman who ran a 'house of ill-fame' is the most telling anomaly. The production team found her 'elusive.' This wasn't a lack of archives, but a result of structural violence: a woman in that position was never permitted to leave any expression of her own subjectivity. Her existence was reduced to a police record—a label of 'impurity.' This is meta-violence in its purest form: the power of interpretation remains with the police, the law, and the male chroniclers, while the exploited are left as distorted shadows in the cracks of history.

Olusoga romanticizes the 'magic' of old houses. This romanticized narrative is a weaponized cognitive entry. When we package the years of slums and the agony of eviction as 'human story puzzles,' we are consuming those erased by structural violence. If a tenant was defined as 'nameless' or 'vile' in their time, does this 'inclusive' digging restore justice, or does it simply apply a modern aesthetic patch to past violence?

True inclusivity isn't 'I'm also willing to look at the underclass'; it's asking why, in the same physical space, some lives were recorded as epics while others could only be confirmed through police euphemisms. This monopoly over the narrative is still operating today.
