囤货清单是一张阶级地图The Stockpile List Is a Class Map

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
能囤三天物资的人，才有资格被建议囤三天物资
Only those who can afford three days of supplies are told to stock three days of supplies.

政府的应急储备建议不是建议，是一次筛选。它假设你有地方放九升水，有钱多买三天的食物，有药房按时把药送到手上，有厕所可以冲。塔楼里的助产士 Keira Callaghan 说得直接：住在合租房单间里的家庭，囤九升水是「不现实」的。

这不是执行层面的小瑕疵。所谓「人人应囤积」的叙事，把一条只对部分人成立的路径包装成普遍适用的常识，于是做不到的人被默认为没准备好——责任从资源分配转移到了个人。加尔通那套算法在这里很清楚：本可以达到的准备状态，和实际能达到的，差出来的部分就是暴力。它不流血，它藏在房价、药房流程和有没有花园里。

Chris Rotheram 那段最锋利。他靠每月重复处方维持几种保命药物，诊所的政策是不到需要前一周不开新处方，药房再花一周处理，于是药永远卡在最后一刻。政府让他囤药，制度不让他囤药。这两个指令互相抵消，剩下的只有他每月的处方焦虑。这不是他不够配合，是结构在告诉他：你的命按流程走。

Peter Slessenger 问的那个问题没人敢答：三天不冲厕所怎么办。他住在公寓楼里，没有花园可以挖坑。应急叙事里永远有「囤水囤粮」这种体面的部分，没有排泄、没有经血、没有失能者的照护、没有婴儿的奶粉。这些被排除在「准备」之外的东西，恰好是照护劳动的主要构成，也恰好主要由女性承担。Callaghan 提出的方案——由地方议会设免费的物资中心——才是把准备当成公共卫生措施，而不是当成消费能力测试。

最后那条来信才点破整件事的性质：有人怀疑这轮囤货号召是为了在预算案前拉动经济。真假不论，逻辑是通的。恐惧可以变成销售额，而买不起的人连恐惧都要分期。

Hexham 的核掩体变成了麦当劳。掩体当年也不给他们用。

The government's emergency stockpile advice is not advice. It is a filter. It assumes you have floor space for nine litres of water, spare money for three extra days of food, a pharmacy that delivers on time, and a toilet that flushes. Keira Callaghan, a midwife in Tower Hamlets, says it plainly: for families in a single room in a shared house, storing nine litres per person is simply unrealistic.

This is not an implementation glitch. The narrative that everyone should stockpile dresses a path available to some as common sense, so those who cannot follow it are quietly reclassified as unprepared. Responsibility shifts from distribution to the individual. Run the Violence Triangle: the gap between the preparedness actually reachable and the preparedness people are told to reach is violence. It does not bleed. It lives in rent, pharmacy policy, and whether you have a garden.

Chris Rotheram's letter is the sharpest. He depends on a monthly repeat prescription for several life-preserving medications. His surgery will not issue a new one until a week before he needs it; the pharmacy then takes up to a week. His medicine always arrives at the last minute. The government tells him to stockpile medication; the system forbids him from stockpiling medication. The two instructions cancel out, and what remains is prescription anxiety every month. He is not failing to comply. The structure is telling him his life runs on its schedule.

Peter Slessenger asks the question nobody wants to answer: what happens to the toilet for three days. He lives in a block of flats and has no garden to dig a hole in. Emergency narratives always feature the respectable items, water and tins. They never feature waste, menstruation, care for the disabled, infant formula. Those excluded things are exactly what care labour is made of, and exactly what falls mainly on women. Callaghan's proposal, council-run stockpile hubs offering supplies free of charge, is what it looks like to treat preparedness as public health rather than as a means test for consumption.

The last letter names the whole thing: maybe the stockpiling call is there to boost economic activity before the budget. True or not, the logic holds. Fear converts into sales, and those who cannot afford supplies get to pay for their fear in instalments.

Hexham's nuclear bunker is a McDonald's now. It was never for them anyway.
