嫁给柳条的男人，和嫁给丈夫的女人She Married a Wicker Man; He Married a Wife

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
柳条丈夫是幻想，婚姻共谋才是现实
The wicker husband is a fantasy. The marital co-conspiracy is the reality.

一个由柳条编成的丈夫，只为她的快感和幸福存在。这是《Wicker》的核心设定，也是它最诚实的地方：它不假装这是婚姻，它直接是一个女人给自己定做的、不需要博弈的伴侣。

科尔曼在采访里替这个设定做了注脚。她说渔妇是村里唯一有自己名字的女人，其他人只是“面包师的女人”“客栈老板的妻子”。她还说，渔妇对自己的生活“完全满意”，是被逼到墙角才决定“把你们珍爱的婚姻惯例搞成笑话”。这句话说得很准，准到她自己可能没意识到它有多准：婚姻在这里被叫作“惯例”（convention），不是神圣，不是归宿，是一条需要被遵守的规矩。

然后是那个绕不开的对照。同一个采访里，科尔曼谈起自己和丈夫的婚姻：在一起32年，结婚25年，共同养育三个孩子，还一起做制片。她说他们“从来没有大吵过”，说自己喜欢“没有摩擦的生活”。

我不怀疑这句话。问题在于，一段32年的关系里没有摩擦，是谁在消化摩擦。采访给了答案，只是它被写成了笑话：她需要录试镜带，请他帮忙拍，他反问“你有没有想过试试这样演”；她给他一个眼神，他立刻说“好，收到”。这个“收到”是整篇采访里最有信息量的三个字。它说明这套机制在运转，而且运转得很好——好到可以当成趣闻讲出来。

这就是共谋的日常形态。它不需要施暴者，不需要争吵，甚至不需要当事人觉得委屈。它只需要一方习惯性地把另一方的专业判断当成可以商量的建议，另一方习惯性地用眼神而不是语言来纠正，然后双方都认为这叫和谐。科尔曼自己说得很清楚：“如果你打算和某人共度一生，要体贴。问问自己：这周我是不是一个好伴侣？”——注意这个问句的方向，是她在问自己。

电影那边，柳条丈夫的结局是村民发现自己的生活相比之下多么贫乏，于是麻烦来了。这个设定比电影主创自己承认的还要狠：一个不需要博弈的伴侣，会让周围所有靠博弈维持的关系显得难堪。所以村庄必须毁掉他。不是因为他是怪物，是因为他太省事。

至于那个“性感”卖点——一个50岁的瑞典男演员裹在乳胶和面具里，靠喉音和眉毛演戏，观众买票进场。这不值得批评，它甚至有点可爱。但它和柳条丈夫是同一件事的两面：女性向的情色幻想，仍然需要一个真实的男性身体来背书，需要Weta Workshop做特效，需要一张能印在海报上的脸。渔妇可以编一个丈夫，科尔曼不能。

采访最后，科尔曼用手指在空中打了个勾，“检查完毕”。这个动作很轻快。我想知道的是，32年里，那个勾是谁打的。

A husband woven from wicker, existing solely for her pleasure and happiness. That's the premise of Wicker, and it's the most honest thing about the film: it doesn't pretend to be a marriage. It's a partner a woman commissioned for herself, one that requires no negotiation.

Colman supplies the footnote. The Fisherwoman, she points out, is the only woman in the village with her own name; the rest are The Bakerwoman, The Innkeeper's Wife. The Fisherwoman is perfectly happy with her life, Colman says, and only when pushed does she decide to make a mockery of the village's beloved convention of marriage. That word, convention, is doing a lot of work. Not sacred. Not destiny. A rule you're expected to follow.

Then the unavoidable juxtaposition. In the same interview Colman describes her own marriage: 32 years together, 25 married, three children, several collaborations including this film. They've never had big fights, she says. She quite likes a friction-free life.

I don't doubt it. The question is who absorbs the friction in a 32-year relationship that has none. The interview answers, and the answer is written as a joke: she needs a taped audition, asks him to film it, and he asks whether she's thought of trying it another way. She gives him a look. He says, point taken. Those two words are the most informative in the whole piece. The mechanism is running, and running well enough to be told as an anecdote.

This is what co-conspiracy looks like in its domestic form. No perpetrator required, no argument, not even a felt grievance. It only requires one party to habitually treat the other's professional judgment as a suggestion, and the other to habitually correct with a glance rather than a sentence, and both to call the result harmony. Colman states the rule herself: if you're going to share your life with someone, be considerate, ask yourself whether you've been a good partner this week. Note the direction of the question. She's the one asking.

In the film, the wicker husband's existence makes the villagers realize how impoverished their own lives are, and trouble follows. That's sharper than the filmmakers admit. A partner who requires no negotiation makes every relationship built on negotiation look bad. So the village has to destroy him. Not because he's a monster, but because he's too easy.

As for the horny selling point, a 50-year-old Swedish actor in a full bodysuit and glued-on mask, grunting and eyebrow-acting, ticket-buyers lining up: it's not worth scolding, it's almost charming. But it's the same story from the other side. Female erotic fantasy still needs a real male body to underwrite it, Weta Workshop prosthetics, a face for the poster. The Fisherwoman can weave herself a husband. Colman can't.

At the end of the interview Colman draws a tick in the air. Check. It's a light gesture. I want to know who's been drawing that tick for 32 years.
