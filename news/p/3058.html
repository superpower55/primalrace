食谱的权力：谁在定义“家庭中心”的愉悦？The Power of Recipes: Who Defines the Joy of the 'Domestic Centre'?

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-31 § 链接
食谱不是中立的烹饪指南，而是对生活方式和身份表达的定价权。
A recipe is not a neutral guide; it is the pricing power over lifestyle and identity expression.

在《卫报》这种主流媒体的 Food 频道里，一条关于 Dutch Baby 的食谱看起来是无害的，但它本质上是一次关于“家庭中心”叙事的认知入口投放。它通过描述“impressive brunch centrepiece”和“slightly soggy bottom”，将一种特定的、中产阶级的、带有仪式感的早午餐场景定义为“愉悦”的标本。

我们要追问的是，这种“fun”的表达背后隐藏了什么样的 structural violence。当食谱被包装成一种生活美学时，它实际上在强化一种共谋：即将食物的准备、呈现与家庭氛围的营造，潜意识地锚定在女性的表达空间里。这种“仪式感”的制造，往往是以女性在私人领域投入大量无偿劳动为前提的，而这种劳动在 GDP 统计中是不可见的，在男性中心叙事中则是被理所当然地当作“背景板”存在的。

当然，Benjamina Ebuehi 作为一个创作者，她通过分享食谱在公共空间建立自己的主体性，这是一种个体在既定结构中的最优解表达。但当这种表达被主流媒体收编为一种“夏日轻盈”的文化符号时，它再次被武器化，成为了一个让人们忘记“谁在厨房里劳作”的审美滤镜。真正的公正表达，不应该是教你如何做一个完美的中心装饰品，而应该是让这种“中心”的定义权，从单一的性别角色扮演中解脱出来。

In the Food section of a mainstream outlet like The Guardian, a recipe for a Dutch Baby seems harmless. However, it is essentially a deployment of a cognitive entry point regarding the narrative of the 'domestic centre.' By describing it as an 'impressive brunch centrepiece' and praising the 'slightly soggy bottom,' it defines a specific, middle-class, ritualistic brunch scene as a specimen of 'pleasure.'

We must ask what structural violence is hidden behind this expression of 'fun.' When a recipe is packaged as a lifestyle aesthetic, it reinforces a complicity: anchoring the preparation of food and the creation of domestic atmosphere subconsciously within the expression space of women. The manufacturing of this 'ritual' is often predicated on the massive amount of unpaid labor women invest in the private sphere—labor that is invisible in GDP statistics and taken for granted as a 'backdrop' in masculine-centric narratives.

Of course, as a creator, Benjamina Ebuehi is establishing her subjectivity in the public space by sharing her recipe—an optimal expression for an individual within a given structure. But when this expression is co-opted by mainstream media as a cultural symbol of 'summer lightness,' it is weaponized again, becoming an aesthetic filter that makes people forget who is actually working in the kitchen. Just expression should not be about teaching you how to make a perfect centrepiece, but about liberating the power to define that 'centre' from the performance of a single gender role.
