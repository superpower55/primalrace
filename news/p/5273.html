板球场上的“王座”与被隐形的生物墙The 'Throne' on the Pitch and the Invisible Biological Wall

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
体育新闻的 serene 背后，是男性中心叙事的绝对垄断。
The 'serenity' of sports news is merely the absolute monopoly of masculine-centric narratives.

读这篇关于郡冠军赛 (County Championship) 的报道，最直观的感受不是竞技的紧张，而是一种令人窒息的 serene（宁静）。这种宁静来自一个事实：在这个巨大的叙事空间里，女性完全消失了。除了一个被当作“比喻”的体操运动员 Simone Biles，以及一个被描述为“正统王座”持有者的女性记者 Tanya，整篇报道是一个纯粹的 masculine-centric narrative（男性中心叙事）。

这就是典型的元暴力 (meta violence)。板球这种运动，以及围绕它的报道方式，构建了一个封闭的男性共谋场域。你看那些词汇：serene progress, baby-faced assassin, rightful throne。这些表达在构建一种关于“力量”、“掌控”和“继承”的男性神话。在这个空间里，男性的身体伤痛（如 Olly Stone 的腿筋受伤）被赋予了英雄主义的悲剧色彩，而女性的身体则被彻底排除在“竞技”这一定义之外。

最讽刺的是文末对 Tanya “正统王座”的提及。在这样一个男性垄断的结构里，给女性一个“报道”的席位，往往是一种表演性的让步。她坐在王座上，但她书写的是男性的战争，维护的是男性的规则。这种共谋让结构性暴力变得不可见——当你习惯了体育新闻里只有男性的名字，你就会潜意识地认为“体育”本身就是男性的属性，而女性的缺失被自然化为了“现状”。

这不仅仅是关于板球，这是关于解释权的垄断。当一个领域被定义为“纯粹的竞技”时，它实际上是在通过生物墙 (biological wall) 筛选掉异己，并用一套文化叙事将这种排他性包装成“传统”。

Reading this report on the County Championship, the most striking feeling isn't the tension of the game, but a suffocating sense of 'serenity.' This serenity stems from a simple fact: women are entirely absent from this vast narrative space. Aside from a gymnastic reference to Simone Biles and a female journalist, Tanya, described as holding a 'rightful throne,' the entire piece is a pure masculine-centric narrative.

This is meta violence in its textbook form. Cricket, and the way it is reported, constructs a closed field of masculine complicity. Look at the lexicon: 'serene progress,' 'baby-faced assassin,' 'rightful throne.' These expressions build a myth of 'power,' 'control,' and 'inheritance.' In this space, the physical pain of men (like Olly Stone's hamstring injury) is imbued with heroic tragedy, while the female body is completely excluded from the definition of 'athletics.'

The irony peaks at the mention of Tanya's 'rightful throne.' In a structure so dominated by men, granting a woman a 'seat' in reporting is often a performative concession. She sits on the throne, but she chronicles the wars of men and maintains their rules. This complicity renders structural violence invisible—when you are used to seeing only men's names in sports news, you subconsciously accept that 'sports' is a masculine attribute, and the absence of women is naturalized as the 'status quo.'

This isn't just about cricket; it's about the monopoly of the power to define. When a field is defined as 'pure competition,' it is actually using the biological wall to filter out the other, wrapping this exclusion in a cultural narrative of 'tradition.'
