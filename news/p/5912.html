烟花炸的是动物的神经The Fireworks Blast Animals' Nerves

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
噪音的代价，从来不由制造噪音的人支付。
The cost of noise is never paid by the people making it.

这条新闻里没有人在挨打，但暴力三角的差额清清楚楚：从120分贝降到110分贝，响度减半，被炸掉的那一半噪音，正是宠物、牲畜和野生动物年复一年被迫承受的部分。结构层在动——商业、创新、科学和贸易部关掉了咨询，提案是把更响的烟花只留给专业使用者，把消费级的上限压到110dB。这是真实的、具体的缩小，不是口号。

但注意这个缩小的形状。整篇报道里，站出来说话的是RSPCA、英国兽医协会、全国农民联盟、Redwings马匹收容所——全是替动物代言的组织。烟花炸响的那个夜里，被吓到的心脏、跑丢的狗、撞栏的马，没有一个能自己提交咨询意见。51,000份回应是替它们写的。这在加尔通的框架里叫文化层先行：先得有人替沉默的一方把痛苦叙述成痛苦，结构层才可能动。

而人的代价在这条新闻里几乎缺席。报道没有写谁在烟花声里睡不着，没有写创伤后应激的人，没有写夜班工人，也没有写那些住在控制区之外、被挤到更响的周边区域的人。Redwings 的负责人说得很准：苏格兰的“烟花控制区”制造了两套保护标准，把使用推向周边。也就是说，结构暴力没有消失，只是被重新分配了——从一个邮编挪到另一个邮编。

英国烟花协会的反驳值得认真对待：很多让人恼火的行为，比如在公共场所放、凌晨一点后放，本来就违法。问题从来不是法条不存在，而是执行不存在。现在把消费级产品的上限压下来，等于承认执法这条路走不通，于是改去管产品。这是次优解，但它是真的最优解吗？报道自己给了答案：给每个使用者发许可证的方案，因为“资源不足”被搁置，而且没有时间表。

这就是共谋的现场。政府不愿意投入执法资源，行业协会不愿意失去市场，双方在“更安静的烟花”这个折中上握手，各自都拿到了能对外交代的东西。兽医协会主席那句话说得克制但准确：更安静的烟花不是完整方案，闪光和不定的时间照样伤动物。折中方案被包装成解决方案，代价继续由不会投票的那一方支付。

荷兰全国禁令、捷克收紧、瑞士11月29日公投、德国早有购买窗口——方向确实在变。但方向不等于差额被填平。新西兰的上限是90dB，Redwings 要的也是90dB，英国提案给的是110dB。这20分贝的差距，就是这次“胜利”里还没被削掉的部分：它被留在了动物的耳朵里。

Nobody is being beaten in this story, but the Violence Triangle gap is plain: 120dB down to 110dB, loudness roughly halved, and that halved half is exactly what pets, livestock and wildlife have been absorbing year after year. The structural layer is moving — the Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade has closed a consultation proposing that louder fireworks be restricted to professional users and the consumer ceiling dropped to 110dB. That is a real, specific reduction, not a slogan.

But look at the shape of the reduction. Everyone quoted speaking up is an organisation speaking for animals: the RSPCA, the British Veterinary Association, the National Farmers' Union, Redwings Horse Sanctuary. On the night the bangers go off, the frightened hearts, the lost dogs, the horses into fences — none of them can submit a consultation response. The 51,000 responses were written on their behalf. In Galtung's terms, the cultural layer had to move first: someone had to narrate the animals' suffering as suffering before the structural layer could budge.

The human cost is almost absent here. The report does not mention who cannot sleep, does not mention trauma, does not mention night workers, does not mention the people living outside the control zones who get the displaced displays. Whitelegg at Redwings puts it precisely: Scotland's firework control zones create a two-tier system of protection and risk pushing use into surrounding areas. Structural violence did not disappear; it was redistributed — from one postcode to the next.

The British Fireworks Association's pushback deserves a hearing: much of what annoys communities — setting off fireworks in public, after 1am — is already illegal. The problem was never that the law didn't exist; it's that enforcement doesn't. Lowering the consumer ceiling is an admission that the enforcement road is closed, so the state goes after the product instead. That's a second-best solution. Is it the real optimum? The report answers itself: a licensing requirement for every user was shelved for lack of resources, with no indication of when it might return.

This is the co-conspiracy on display. The government won't fund enforcement; the industry won't lose market share; both shake hands on 'quieter fireworks' and each walks away with something to show. The BVA president says it with restraint but accurately: quieter fireworks are not a complete solution — flashes and unpredictable timing still hurt animals. A compromise gets packaged as a solution, and the bill keeps landing on the party that cannot vote.

A nationwide ban in the Netherlands, tighter rules in Czechia, a Swiss referendum on 29 November, Germany's narrow purchase windows — the direction is real. Direction is not the same as closing the gap. New Zealand's limit is 90dB; Redwings wants 90dB; the UK proposal offers 110dB. Those 20 decibels are the part of this victory that hasn't been cut: they stay in the animals' ears.
