全女公社，是出口还是陷阱An All-Female Commune Is a Hole, Not an Exit

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 IndieWire ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要凯莉·库恩主演的《解放》发布预告片，她在片中饰演中年女性丽塔，被莉莉·詹姆斯饰演的年轻灵性领袖吸引，离开家庭加入一个全女性公社；她的两个女儿意外到访后也被卷入，丽塔被迫在旧家庭与新生活之间选择。影片由盖·纳蒂弗执导，已在特柳赖德和多伦多电影节放映，Bleecker Street 定于 1 月 8 日院线上映。
The newsThe trailer for Guy Nattiv's "The Liberation" shows Carrie Coon as Rita, a woman in midlife who leaves her family to follow a magnetic young spiritual leader, played by Lily James, to an all-female commune. When her two daughters arrive and are drawn in too, Rita must choose between the family she left and the life she believes can save her. Bleecker Street releases the film in theaters on January 8.
全女公社是男权世界让出的一个洞，不是出口。
An all-female commune is a hole the patriarchal world left open, not an exit.

预告片和简介里，丽塔加入的是一个「全女性公社」。全女，这个词放在今天的电影宣发里，几乎等于自动贴上一张进步标签。但简介同时写明，带领这个公社的是「有魅力的年轻灵性领袖」里基——一个具体的人，有追随者，有让人离开家庭的说服力。全女公社，领袖是一个人，不是一种制度。

这正是第三章那个入口：宗教与偶像崇拜，靠无限美化加包装和距离运转。公社不是靠暴力把人留下的，它靠的是「她比以往任何时候都快乐」这句话，而这句话是丽塔自己说给女儿听的。真正稳固的控制不需要鞭子，是当事人自己把叙事内化成自我审查。全女只是这层叙事的外壳，外壳越进步，内化越顺滑。

再看丽塔离开的是什么。简介说她「被过去的重量纠缠」，IndieWire 的影评写，她一直觉得自己站在自己人生的外面。一个中年女人在原有生活里没有位置，然后出现一个告诉她「你属于这里」的人——这不是她软弱，这是她在打一场存在性战争，而公社看起来是她找到的最优解表达。影评说，影片「温和地戳破」了那个假设：她外面的生活并没有好那么多。戳破的对象选得很准，它质疑的不是公社，是外面。

问题在于，影片让丽塔在「她离开的家庭」和「她相信能拯救她的生活」之间做选择，这个二选一本身是假的。一个中年女人为什么只能在窒息的家庭和一个要她交出判断力的公社之间挑一个？这个二选一没有把任何一个选项做成真的出口，它只是把两个都不属于她的地方摆在她面前，然后让观众看她怎么输。这不是丽塔的困境，这是剧本给她的定价。

女儿们被卷进来，是这个故事里最诚实的一笔：一个靠「你属于这里」运转的团体，扩张靠的从来是里面的人去拉外面的人。丽塔拉进来的是自己的孩子。这是公社的真实成本第一次被写成具体的人。

影评说，也许丽塔在这个世界上根本没有位置。如果这句话成立，那这部片子拍的不是邪教，是一个女人被两个世界同时拒绝。全女公社不是出口，是男权世界让出来的一个洞，洞口写着「解放」。

The synopsis says Rita joins an "all-female commune." In today's film marketing, all-female is close to an automatic progressive label. But the same synopsis names the commune's leader: Ricki, a magnetic young spiritual figure with followers and the power to pull a woman away from her family. All-female commune, one leader. That is not a structure. That is a person.

This is the religion-and-idol entry from Chapter 3: infinite beautification plus packaging and distance. The commune does not keep people with force. It keeps them with the sentence "she's happier than she's ever been"—and Rita says that sentence to her own daughters. Real control doesn't need a whip. The subject internalizes the narrative and polices herself. All-female is the shell, and the more progressive the shell, the smoother the internalization.

Look at what Rita leaves. The synopsis says she is haunted by the weight of her past; IndieWire's review says she has long felt she was standing on the outside of her own life. A woman in midlife with no place in her existing life, then someone appears and tells her she belongs. This is not weakness. This is an existential war, and the commune looks like the optimal expression she found. The review says the film gently pokes holes in the idea that her outside life was so much better. The target is chosen precisely: what gets questioned is not the commune. It is the outside.

Then the film asks Rita to choose between the family she left and the life she believes can save her. That binary is fake. Why should a woman in midlife pick between a suffocating family and a commune that asks her to hand over her judgment? Neither option is built as a real exit. The film puts two places that don't belong to her in front of her and watches her lose. That is not Rita's dilemma. That is the script pricing her.

The daughters being drawn in is the most honest beat: a group that runs on "you belong here" expands through the people already inside pulling in the people outside. Rita pulls in her own children. For once, the commune's real cost is written as specific people.

The review says perhaps there is no place for Rita in this world. If that holds, the film is not about a cult. It is about a woman refused by two worlds at once. The all-female commune is not an exit. It is a hole the patriarchal world left open, with "liberation" written at the mouth.
