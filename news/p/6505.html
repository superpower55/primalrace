两个同名候选人，一场选举的元暴力Two Dan Sullivans and the Meta-Violence of an Election

其他 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要法官驳回了一项诉讼，该诉讼由特朗普支持，旨在若共和党现任参议员丹·沙利文落选则阻止阿拉斯加参议院选举结果认证。司法部表示将上诉。沙利文称会接受选举结果。
The newsA judge dismissed a Trump-backed lawsuit that sought to block certification of Alaska's Senate race if Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan loses. The Justice Department said it will appeal. Sullivan said he will accept the election results.
法官驳回的不是一场诉讼，是解释权的挪用
The judge didn't dismiss a lawsuit. He blocked a claim on interpretation.

这条新闻里，真正被驳回的东西比一场诉讼大得多。特朗普让司法部去起诉，要求在自己人可能输掉之前就阻止阿拉斯加参议院选举结果的认证——一个法官把这案子驳了。这不是程序新闻，这是元暴力在选举现场被截停了一次：谁有权定义什么叫"选民困惑"，谁就有权定义什么叫"公平选举"。

司法部的诉讼理由是选票上有两个叫 Dan Sullivan 的人，可能造成"选民困惑"。困惑这个词在这里是武器化的入口。选票上出现同名候选人，本身是选举制度早就处理过无数次的常规情况；把它升级成需要联邦司法部介入、需要在选举前不到两周起诉、需要预先阻止认证的理由，这不是在解决困惑，是在制造一个可以随时启动的否决权。输了就认证无效，赢了就不提——这个结构本身就是对选举结果的定价权争夺。

然后看共和党自己做了什么。华盛顿邮报报道，共和党在阿拉斯加的初选里也试过抬高那些不可能赢的民主党候选人，一个共和党 Super Pac 支持了两个根本不住在阿拉斯加的参议院候选人，还把他们夸成桑德斯式的"进步派"。同一个党，一边抱怨对手用同名候选人分流选票，一边自己用假进步派分流选票。这不是双重标准，这是同一套操作的两端：选举不是让选民表达，是让操盘手制造可能性。第三章说的"制造可能性的艺术"，在这里不需要任何宏大叙事，两个同名的人就够了。

Sullivan 在 CNN 上说他会接受选举结果。Jake Tapper 问他是不是只在赢的时候才接受，他说不是。同一场采访里他又说 Peltola"一直在对阿拉斯加人民撒谎"，"我保证她现在就在撒谎"。接受结果和指控对手犯罪可以同时进行，因为前者是给镜头看的，后者是给支持者听的。这不是矛盾，这是共谋者的标准配置：制度内的姿态要摆足，制度外的动员不能停。

Peltola 那边呢。纽约时报的调查说她的竞选团队招募了一个同名的人进这场选举，目的是从 Sullivan 那里分走选票。她否认自己知情，说不知道团队里有没有人参与。报道没有给出她知情的证据，也没有给出她不知情的证据。这里最该说的是：一个竞选团队如果真做了这件事，它就是在一个已经被武器化的场域里，用对方的武器打回去。这不叫公正的表达，这叫在存在性战争里选了一个代价最小的假最优解——短期可能有效，长期把"选举是可以被设计的"这件事又加固了一层。

法官的裁决缩小了一个具体的差额：联邦政府不能在选举结果出来之前就预设它无效。这是 structural 层的一次真实拦截，不是 PR 立法，不是表演性让步，是一个具体的否决权被拿掉了。但这条新闻里没有被缩小的部分是：两个党都在用同名候选人、假进步派候选人、诉讼和刑事指控作为选举工具，而选民被留在原地，被告知这就是民主。

Sullivan 说他保证 Peltola 现在就在撒谎。这句话不需要证据，因为它不是用来证明的，是用来定价的——给对手的可信度定价，给自己的支持者一个可以重复的口号。选举的认证可以被法官驳回，这种定价不会被驳回。

What got dismissed here is bigger than a lawsuit. Trump ordered the Justice Department to sue to block certification of Alaska's Senate race before his candidate could lose, and a judge threw it out. This is not a procedural story. This is meta-violence getting stopped at the election site: whoever gets to define "voter confusion" gets to define "a fair election."

The DOJ's argument was that two candidates named Dan Sullivan on the ballot could cause "voter confusion." Confusion is the weaponized entry point here. Same-name candidates are a routine situation election systems have handled many times. Upgrading it into grounds for federal intervention, a lawsuit filed less than two weeks before election day, and preemptive blocking of certification is not solving confusion. It is manufacturing a veto that can be triggered on demand. Lose, and certification is invalid. Win, and nobody mentions it. That structure is a fight over who gets to price the result.

Now look at what the Republicans did themselves. The Washington Post reported that Republicans tried to boost unviable Democratic candidates in Alaska's primaries too. A Republican Super Pac backed two Senate candidates who did not even live in Alaska and praised them as Bernie Sanders-style "progressives." The same party that complains about a namesake siphoning votes runs fake progressives to siphon votes. This is not a double standard. It is the same operation at both ends: elections are not for voters to express themselves, they are for operatives to manufacture possibilities. The art of manufacturing possibilities needs no grand narrative here. Two men with the same name will do.

Sullivan told CNN he would accept the election results. Jake Tapper asked if he would only accept them if he won, and he said no. In the same interview he said Peltola is "continually lying to the Alaskan people" and "I guarantee you, she's lying right now." Accepting results and accusing your opponent of crimes can happen simultaneously, because the first is for the camera and the second is for the base. This is not a contradiction. It is the standard configuration of a co-conspirator: perform institutional poise, never stop the extra-institutional mobilization.

And Peltola's side. The New York Times investigation reported that her campaign recruited another man named Dan Sullivan into the race to peel votes away from the incumbent. She denies knowing about it, saying she has "no idea" whether any of her staff were involved. The reporting offers no evidence she knew and no evidence she didn't. What needs saying is this: if a campaign really did that, it used the opponent's weapon inside an already weaponized field. That is not a just expression. It is the cheapest available fake-optimal expression in an existential war—maybe effective short-term, and it adds one more layer to the idea that elections can be designed.

The judge's ruling shrank a specific gap: the federal government cannot presume a result invalid before it exists. That is a real structural interception, not PR legislation, not a performative concession—a concrete veto was taken away. What was not shrunk is the rest: both parties using namesakes, fake progressive candidates, lawsuits and criminal accusations as election tools, while voters are left standing there being told this is democracy.

Sullivan says he guarantees Peltola is lying right now. That sentence needs no evidence because it is not meant to prove anything. It is meant to set a price—on his opponent's credibility, and on a slogan his supporters can repeat. Certification can be dismissed by a judge. That price-setting won't be.
