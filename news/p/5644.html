用削减救济金来“救”年轻人：一场典型的结构性诈骗Cutting Benefits to 'Save' Youth: A Classic Structural Scam

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-18 § 链接
将生存保障转化为就业筹码，是结构暴力对弱势者主体性的二次收割。
Turning survival security into a bargaining chip for employment is a secondary harvest of subjectivity by structural violence.

Alan Milburn 的这套逻辑是典型的 weaponized narrative：先定义一个“危机”（Neet crisis），然后提供一个看似充满希望的“解决方案”（employment support），最后揭晓代价——削减福利金（cut benefits）。这根本不是什么救赎，而是一场精准的结构性 scam。

从加尔通的暴力三角来看，这在 structural 层面上完成了一次极其阴险的置换。政府试图通过削减底线生存资源（Actual），来强迫年轻人进入一个由权力者定义、且未必能提供公正表达的就业市场（Potential）。当生存保障被作为筹码，所谓的“就业支持”就变成了某种形式的规训。Milburn 提到的“obligation”（义务）就是这套逻辑的真面目：如果你不接受我提供的、可能极其低质的就业机会，你就得面对生存资源的枯竭。

最讽刺的是，他将 Neet 状态定义为“健康危机”，却试图用削减福利这种增加生存压力的方式来解决。这种共谋逻辑极其恶劣：精神健康问题（mental distress）往往是结构性暴力（如贫困、不平等、缺乏主体性）的结果，而 Milburn 却想通过削减救济金来“激励”他们好起来。这就像是在告诉一个溺水的人，为了教他游泳，我们要先抽干他脚下的水。

这种叙事在 meta 层面上依然是男性中心叙事的延续——将人简化为“生产力单位”，认为一个人的价值仅在于其是否能被纳入资本主义的劳动循环。对于那些真正处于生物墙限制或严重心理创伤中的年轻人，这种“重新分配”意味着他们将被系统性地抛弃，因为他们无法提供 Milburn 想要的那个“最优解表达”：一个听话的、能干活的劳动力。

Alan Milburn's logic is a textbook example of a weaponized narrative: first, define a 'crisis' (the Neet crisis), then offer a hopeful 'solution' (employment support), and finally reveal the price—cutting benefits. This isn't salvation; it's a precise structural scam.

Applying the Violence Triangle, this is an insidious substitution at the structural layer. The government seeks to reduce baseline survival resources (Actual) to force young people into an employment market defined by power-holders, where Just Expressions are rarely found (Potential). When survival security becomes a bargaining chip, so-called 'employment support' transforms into a tool of discipline. The 'obligation' Milburn mentions is the true face of this logic: if you don't accept the potentially low-quality opportunities I provide, you face the depletion of your means of survival.

It is profoundly ironic that he defines the Neet state as a 'health crisis' while proposing to solve it by cutting benefits—a move that objectively increases survival stress. This complicity logic is malicious: mental distress is often the result of structural violence (poverty, inequality, loss of subjectivity), yet Milburn wants to 'incentivize' recovery by stripping away safety nets. It is like telling a drowning person that to teach them how to swim, we must first drain the water from beneath them.

At the meta level, this narrative continues the masculine-centric narrative—reducing human beings to 'units of productivity' and asserting that a person's value lies solely in their integration into the capitalist labor cycle. For those limited by biological walls or severe trauma, this 'redistribution' means systemic abandonment, as they cannot provide the 'optimal expression' Milburn craves: a compliant, productive worker.
