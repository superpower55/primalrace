一个演员的死，被写成了颂词An Actor's Death, Written as a Eulogy

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
讣告里没有一句提到他演过什么角色给谁定价
The obituary never says which narratives his face made legitimate

帕特里克·斯图尔特的死讯里，唯一具体的信息是死因：阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆，在他洛杉矶的家中，走完了一段被发言人称为"短暂而动人"的旅程。剩下的全是形容词。"温暖的怀抱""不可战胜的优雅""终其一生对我们的奉献"——这是一份公关稿，不是讣告。

我不否认他的表演。但讣告的写法本身就是一种表达，而表达是有选择的。发言人写下的每一句都在做同一件事：把一个人从具体的职业、具体的作品、具体在哪些叙事里站过什么位置，抬升成一个无差别的"他"——被爱、被怀念、被音乐和祈祷环绕的"他"。这个"他"没有性别，没有立场，没有演过什么。

《星际迷航》和《X战警》这两部作品，恰恰是公共叙事里被反复争夺的战场。一个是把"理性""秩序""文明"包装成宇宙尺度的远征叙事，一个是把"少数族群"的处境做成可以消费的奇观。斯图尔特在这两个战场里都是面孔，是那个被投射了"值得信赖的权威"的面孔。讣告把这张面孔收走了，收进"优雅"这个词里，不让任何人再追问这张面孔曾经为哪些叙事提供了合法性。

这不是斯图尔特一个人的问题。这是名人讣告这个文类的标准操作：把一个人的公共性抽干，只留下私人的温度。发言人说的"终其一生对我们的奉献"——对"我们"是谁？奉献了什么？报道一个字没写。这就是共谋的日常形态，不需要谁恶意，只需要所有人默契地不问。

阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆，两种病，一段"短暂"的旅程。报道没有写这段旅程有多长，没有写照护落在了谁身上。发言人用了"家人和朋友"，用了"温暖的怀抱"——照护这件事在讣告里永远是被动语态，好像它自己发生了。全球范围内承担照护的绝大多数是女性，这件事在悼词里连一个名字都换不来。

他演过的那两个宇宙，一个教人相信制度，一个教人同情异类。现在讣告教人相信优雅。三件事其实是一件事：把具体的权力关系，翻译成可以流泪的抽象词。

The only concrete fact in the announcement of Patrick Stewart's death is the cause: Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia, at his home in Los Angeles, after what his spokesperson called a "brief and poignant journey." The rest is adjectives. "Warm embrace." "Invincible grace." "A lifetime of devotion to us all." This is a press release, not an obituary.

I'm not disputing the performances. But the form of an obituary is itself an expression, and expression involves selection. Every line the spokesperson wrote does the same thing: it lifts a man out of his specific profession, his specific works, the specific positions he occupied inside specific narratives, and raises him into an undifferentiated "he" — loved, mourned, surrounded by live music and prayer. This "he" has no gender, no stance, no roles.

Star Trek and X-Men are precisely the battlegrounds where public narrative gets fought over. One packages "reason," "order," and "civilization" as a cosmic-scale expedition; the other turns the situation of a minority into a consumable spectacle. Stewart was a face in both battlegrounds — the face onto which "trustworthy authority" gets projected. The obituary withdraws that face, files it under "grace," and forecloses the question of which narratives that face once made legitimate.

This isn't about Stewart. It's the standard operation of the celebrity obituary as a genre: drain the public dimension, leave only private warmth. "A lifetime of devotion to us all" — who is "us"? Devotion to what? Not a word. This is what complicity looks like in its everyday form. No malice required. Only a shared, silent agreement not to ask.

Alzheimer's and Lewy body dementia. Two diseases, one "brief" journey. The report doesn't say how long that journey was, or whose hands did the caregiving. The spokesperson says "family and friends," says "warm embrace" — care in obituaries is always in the passive voice, as if it happened by itself. Worldwide, the vast majority of caregiving falls to women. In this eulogy that fact doesn't even earn a name.

The two universes he inhabited taught people to trust institutions, and to pity the alien. Now the obituary teaches people to trust grace. These are the same lesson three times over: translate concrete power relations into abstractions you can cry about.
