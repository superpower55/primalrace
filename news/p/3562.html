鸽子不扮演宠物，它在进行存在性博弈The Pigeon Refuses to Perform: An Existential Game of Agency

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
真正的关系不是扮演对方想要的角色，而是主体性的对峙。
True relationship is not about playing a role, but a confrontation of subjectivities.

这篇看似轻盈的散文，其实揭示了一场关于“表达”与“权力”的微型博弈。作者在渴望一个“Crow Bro”——一个能够通过互动、工具使用甚至某种程度上的“忠诚”来提供情绪价值的聪明伙伴。这种渴望本质上是对一种“可控且互惠”的社交关系的投射：我提供花生，你提供魅力与陪伴。这在某种程度上是人类习惯性的男性中心叙事逻辑：通过资源交换，将对方转化为某种符合预期的角色。

但那只名叫 Susan 的鸽子拒绝进入这个剧本。它不进入丈夫精心建造的“家”，不提供肩膀上的亲昵，不参与 TikTok 式的表演。它通过一种极端的、带有距离感的表达，确立了自己的主体性：它不需要你的爱，它只需要你作为农业供应商的服务，并且必须在它规定的时间、规定的距离内完成。

这是一种极其公正的表达 (Just Expression)。Susan 没有扮演一个“温顺的宠物”来换取更好的饲料，它在维持自己生物墙的同时，与人类达成了一项纯粹的经济契约。在这种关系中，没有一方在扮演他者认可的角色，因此没有主体性的死亡。作者感叹“也许我们得到了应得的朋友”，实际上是意识到自己在这场博弈中处于被动地位——她成了那个提供服务并保持“恭敬距离”的侍从。

很多人追求的“灵魂伴侣”或“完美宠物”，往往是要求对方在某种 weaponized 的浪漫叙事中扮演特定角色。而这只鸽子用它的傲慢告诉我们：真正的尊重，始于承认对方拥有拒绝被定义的权力。

This seemingly light essay actually reveals a miniature game of expression and power. The author longs for a "Crow Bro"—a sophisticated companion capable of providing emotional value through interaction and a certain kind of loyalty. This longing is essentially a projection of a "controllable and reciprocal" social relationship: I provide peanuts, you provide charisma and companionship. This is, in a sense, the typical masculine-centric narrative: using resource exchange to transform the other into a role that meets expectations.

But Susan the pigeon refuses to enter this script. She shuns the "homes" built by the husband, rejects the intimacy of perching on a shoulder, and ignores the performative demands of TikTok. Through an extreme, distant form of expression, she establishes her own subjectivity: she doesn't need your love; she only needs your services as an agricultural supplier, conducted on her terms and at her distance.

This is a truly Just Expression. Susan does not play the role of a "docile pet" to secure better feed; she maintains her biological wall while forming a pure economic contract with the human. In this relationship, neither party is playing a role recognized by the other, and thus there is no death of subjectivity. When the author muses that "maybe we get the bird friends we deserve," she is actually realizing her passive position in this game—she has become the servant who provides service while maintaining a "respectfully distant" stance.

Many people's pursuit of "soulmates" or "perfect pets" is often an attempt to force the other into a role within a weaponized romantic narrative. This pigeon, with its arrogance, reminds us that true respect begins with acknowledging the other's right to refuse to be defined.
