和平协议是政治表演，还是对暴力差额的真实缩减？Peace Treaty: Genuine Reduction of Violence or Political Performance?

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
和平协议若不触及权力结构的重新分配，仅是文化层面的叙事换皮。
A peace agreement is merely a narrative skin-swap if it doesn't touch the redistribution of power.

李在明呼吁用“和平体制”取代停战协定，听起来是 Actual 朝 Potential 走近了一步，但这种叙事在半岛这个高压博弈场里极其危险。我们需要问的是：这种所谓的“和平”是在缩减暴力差额，还是在为某种新的结构性共谋铺路？

从加尔通的暴力三角来看，目前的停战状态是典型的 structural violence。双方在边境线维持着一种极其脆弱的平衡，这种平衡本身就是一种暴力——它通过恐惧和军事对峙来定义双方的存在。李在明提出的“和平共存”试图在 cultural 层面上通过对话来稀释这种敌意，但北韩的金正恩已经通过宣布放弃统一、定义南韩为“敌对国家”完成了叙事上的切割。这说明，在元暴力的逻辑里，双方争夺的不是“和平”，而是对半岛定义权的垄断。

这种外交辞令往往是 weaponized 的。当一个领导人谈论“尊重对方体制”和“和平共存”时，他实际上是在进行一次存在性战争的博弈：试图通过降低对方的防御阈值，来换取自身政治资本的增加或安全成本的降低。如果这种对话不能触及核能力、资源分配以及意识形态的根本对立，那么所谓的“和平政权”不过是一场 PR 级别的表演，是 cultural violence 的高级版本——用“和平”的幻象掩盖结构性压迫的持续。

真正的 good_news 应该是：这种对话能否让那些被禁锢在体制内、在结构暴力中挣扎的底层个体获得真实的生存空间？如果和平协议最终只是统治者之间的共谋，而普通人的生存状态依然被定义在“敌我”的生物墙之内，那么这次差额并没有被缩小，只是被重新定价了。

Lee Jae-myung’s call to replace the armistice with a “peace regime” sounds like Actual moving toward Potential, but in the high-pressure game of the peninsula, this narrative is precarious. We must ask: is this “peace” reducing the violence gap, or paving the way for a new structural complicity?

From the perspective of the Violence Triangle, the current armistice is a textbook case of structural violence. Both sides maintain a fragile equilibrium on the border, and this equilibrium itself is violence—it defines existence through fear and military confrontation. Lee’s “peaceful coexistence” attempts to dilute this hostility at the cultural layer, but Kim Jong-un has already completed a narrative severance by abandoning reunification and labeling the South as a “hostile state.” This proves that in the logic of meta-violence, the struggle is not over “peace,” but over the monopoly of the right to define the peninsula.

Such diplomatic rhetoric is often weaponized. When a leader speaks of “respecting the other system,” he is engaging in an existential war: attempting to lower the opponent's defense threshold to gain political capital or reduce security costs. If these talks fail to address nuclear capabilities, resource distribution, and fundamental ideological contradictions, the so-called “peace regime” is nothing more than a PR performance—a sophisticated version of cultural violence that uses the illusion of peace to mask the persistence of structural oppression.

True good_news would be: can this dialogue grant real living space to the individuals trapped within these systems, those struggling under structural violence? If the peace treaty ends up being a mere complicity between rulers while the lives of ordinary people remain defined by the biological wall of “enemy vs. friend,” then the gap has not been narrowed—it has only been repriced.
