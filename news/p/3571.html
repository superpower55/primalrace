食谱里的油量与被定义的“正常”胃口Olive Oil Volume and the Definition of a 'Normal' Appetite

哲学 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
审美定价权不仅在时尚圈，也在决定你该吃多少油的厨房里。
The power to price aesthetics exists not only in fashion, but in the kitchen defining how much oil you should use.

一条关于白豆食谱的新闻，本质上是一次关于“标准”的微型博弈。NYT 的这份食谱要求用半杯橄榄油来烹饪 3-4 人份的豆子，这不仅是口味问题，而是一种被武器化的“中产阶级审美表达”。在某种特定的文化叙事里，大量油脂被等同于“地中海风情”或“高级感”，这种表达定义了什么是“正确的”烹饪方式。

有趣的是评论区出现了典型的博弈。有用户质疑油量，指出 2 汤匙就足够喂饱 6-8 人。这其实是两种存在性表达的碰撞：一方是掌控认知入口的权威媒体在定义“美味的基准”，另一方是基于实际生存经验的个体在尝试通过“事实”来拆穿这个 scam。当媒体把某种高耗能、高成本的烹饪方式标定为 5 星级标准时，它实际上在建立一种审美的筛选机制——如果你觉得油太多，那么你可能不属于这个“懂得享受”的阶层。

这种机制在生活的所有角落通用。从“老钱风”的穿搭到 NYT 的橄榄油，权力者通过定义“什么是好的/正常的”来获取定价权。大多数人习惯于在这样的标准下进行自我规训，认为自己的习惯是“不专业”的。但真正的最优解表达应该是：我的胃和我的钱包决定我放多少油，而不是由一个身在纽约的编辑来告诉我，半杯油才是“Phenomenal”。

A recipe for white beans is, in essence, a micro-game of 'standards.' The NYT recipe calls for a half cup of olive oil for 3-4 servings. This isn't just about taste; it's a weaponized expression of middle-class aesthetics. In a specific cultural narrative, excessive oil is equated with 'Mediterranean flair' or 'sophistication,' defining what constitutes 'correct' cooking.

Interestingly, a conflict emerges in the comments. A user questions the oil volume, noting that 2 tablespoons suffice for 6-8 people. This is a collision of two existential expressions: the authoritative media controlling the cognitive entry point to define the 'baseline of deliciousness,' versus an individual using lived experience to expose the scam. When a media outlet labels a high-resource cooking method as a 5-star standard, it establishes a filtering mechanism—if you think it's too oily, you simply don't belong to this 'refined' class.

This mechanism is universal. From 'Old Money' style to NYT's olive oil, those in power secure pricing power by defining what is 'good' or 'normal.' Most people internalize this, viewing their own habits as 'unprofessional.' But the true optimal expression is: my stomach and my wallet decide the oil, not an editor in New York telling me that a half cup is 'Phenomenal.'
