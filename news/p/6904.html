足球的激情，谁在付账Who Pays for the Passion

文娱 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《卫报》回顾了阿根廷超级德比（博卡青年对河床）的历史：1968年“12号门”事件中71名球迷被挤压致死；1994年作者采访La Doce头目巴里塔当天，赛后两名河床球迷被枪杀。自2013年起阿根廷联赛禁止客队球迷入场，2026年11月2日La Bombonera的德比因此色彩大减，门票炒到50万比索，博卡1比0取胜。
The newsThe Guardian revisits Argentina's Superclásico: 71 fans died at the 1968 Puerta 12 crush, and two River fans were shot dead after the 1994 derby the author attended. Since 2013 away fans have been banned, so Sunday's game at La Bombonera was a tamer spectacle at 500,000 pesos a ticket; Boca won 1-0.
被歌颂的狂热，是别人付了命的生意。
The frenzy being celebrated is a business someone else paid for with their life.

把「激情」和「死亡」写进同一段导语还带着欣赏的语气，这件事本身就是一种叙事暴力。作者自己都写了：爆炸、火箭、血与火，「Exciting, right?」——一个反问，然后接着写下去。1968年12号门，71个博卡球迷死在没完全打开的出口前，死因至今「much of what actually happened remains unknown」；1994年作者亲历的那个下午，赛后两名河床球迷被枪杀，墙上喷出「River 2 Boca 2」，把两条命换算成两个进球。这些不是激情，是直接暴力，而它被包装成一种「你死前必须体验一次」的景观，排在2004年那份榜单的第一名。

这份包装由谁完成，报道自己也交代了。俱乐部组织的烟花、统一发放的蓝黄雨伞、按秒放出的气球，都从La Doce手里收归官方——过去是黑帮走私进来的，现在是俱乐部自己放的。这不是暴力消失了，是暴力的组织权换了主人，从帮派转到俱乐部和媒体手里，形式从火并变成 spectacle。票价50万比索（约240英镑），当年女人和小孩免费进场。谁被请进来，谁被挡在外面，一目了然。

2013年起的客队禁令被写成「family-friendly」的进步：色彩少了，票好买了，记者和La Doce坐在同一高度，穿着博卡球衣骂裁判。这是一个典型的假.最优解表达——用移除对手来换取安全感和票房，代价是德比本身的意义被抽空。作者问得对：2018年那场解放者杯决赛被搬到马德里，「the soul of the Superclásico has remained, if it hadn't been sucked out already」。搬走的不只是一场比赛，是两群人正面相遇的可能性。而相遇，哪怕是敌意的相遇，才是表达。取消相遇不是和平，是另一种管理。

最刺人的一句在结尾：作者的儿子是河床球迷，现实中根本进不来。「I'd love to go to a Superclásico with my son, neither one of us banned by the other's tribe. Maybe one day.」一个母亲和儿子看同一场球，要等一个「maybe one day」。这就是结构暴力最温和也最彻底的样子——它不流血，它只是让某些人永远坐在外面。

至于那些死者：71个、两个，报道里都没有名字。La Doce的头目有名字，评论员有名字，流媒体新贵有名字，连烤肉摊的记者朋友都有名字。被挤压致死的人和被枪杀的人，只剩数字和涂鸦。元暴力在这里不是隐喻——解释权在谁手里，谁就决定哪些死亡算「事件」，哪些死亡算「背景」。

Putting “passion” and “death” in the same opening paragraph, and doing it with relish, is itself a narrative act. The author writes it out: rockets, flares, blood and fire — “Exciting, right?” — then keeps going. In 1968 at Puerta 12, 71 fans, mostly Boca, were crushed when an exit gate didn't fully open; “much of what actually happened remains unknown.” In 1994, the afternoon the author spent with La Doce's leader Barritta, two River fans were shot dead after the game, and graffiti went up reading “River 2 Boca 2,” converting two lives into two goals. That is not passion. That is direct violence dressed as a spectacle, ranked No 1 on a 2004 list of things you must do before you die.

The packaging has an owner, and the piece says so. The fireworks, the synchronized blue-and-yellow umbrellas, the balloons timed to the players' entrance — all of it used to be smuggled in by La Doce. Now the club organizes it. The violence didn't disappear; the right to stage it changed hands, from the gang to the club and the broadcasters, from a fight into a show. Tickets went for 500,000 pesos (£240). Women and children used to get in free. Who is invited in and who is kept out is not a mystery.

Since 2013, away fans have been banned, and the piece frames this as progress: more family-friendly, better ticket access, journalists seated level with La Doce in Boca shirts. This is a textbook false optimal expression — buy safety and gate revenue by removing the opponent, and pay with the meaning of the derby itself. The author asks the right question about 2018, when the Libertadores final was moved to Madrid: whether the soul of the Superclásico “has remained, if it hadn't been sucked out already.” What got moved wasn't just a match. It was the possibility of two tribes meeting face to face. Meeting, even hostile meeting, is expression. Cancelling the meeting isn't peace. It's management.

The sharpest line is the last one: the author's son is a River fan and could not, in any realistic sense, attend. “I'd love to go to a Superclásico with my son, neither one of us banned by the other's tribe. Maybe one day.” A mother and her son at the same game, waiting on a maybe. That is structural violence at its gentlest and most complete — no blood, just certain people permanently seated outside.

And the dead: 71, then two. No names in the piece. Barritta has a name. The commentator has a name. The streamer has a name. The family friend who supports River has a name. The crushed and the shot are numbers and graffiti. Meta-violence here isn't a metaphor — whoever holds the解释权 decides which deaths count as events and which count as backdrop.
