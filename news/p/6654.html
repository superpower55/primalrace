他尊重女人说不，这就是他的辩护He Respects It When a Woman Says No. That's the Defense.

性别 元暴力 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
强奸案的辩护词，本身就是一份元暴力样本
The closing speech is itself a sample of meta-violence.

Brand 的律师在结案陈词里说，他尊重女人说不，所以他就不是强奸犯。这句话值得单独拎出来看：它的逻辑是，一个男人只要在某个时刻接受了拒绝，他就不可能在另一个时刻构成侵犯。拒绝被当成一次性的开关，而不是每一次都必须重新取得的东西。

然后是那句被律师称作案件"极其奇怪的特征"的话：和 Brand 睡过的女性那么多，只有三个人说她们没有同意。这个数字被当成疑点抛给陪审团。它其实是一句自白：在这个辩护里，女性的数量本身就是不在场证明。睡过的女人越多，指控的比例越小，罪就越不像罪。性被换算成基数，侵犯被换算成概率。

律师还说，三名指控者事后表现得"正常"，如果他有罪，这根本不可能发生。这是把创伤的反应方式写进了罪与非罪的定义里。谁规定被侵犯的人必须当场崩溃、必须不像平常、必须让旁人一眼看出？一个能平静离开现场的女人，在这套逻辑里等于没有受到伤害。而受害者为什么延迟报案，报道里给了答案的一半：2023 年 Channel 4 的纪录片和《泰晤士报》的报道出来之后，这些女性才走向警察。律师把这叫做"污染了正义的水井"。

把公开报道说成污染司法的水源，等于说：女人听见别的女人开口，才敢开口，这件事本身是可疑的。可这正是"个人即政治"在法庭上的样子——私人的经历要借公共空间才获得被听见的资格。律师反对的不是程序，是这条通道存在。

他还引用了 1864 年纽曼的一句话来给"污染水井"这个说法找依据，一位神学家、哲学家的话，用来处理六名女性在十年间对同一名男性的指控。解释权的谱系在这里很清楚：一个维多利亚时代的男性神学家，被请来为 2026 年的一场强奸案定调。这不是修辞上的巧合，这是元暴力的日常运作方式——最"中立""文明"的法律语言，底下垫着的是几百年来由男性书写的权威。

还有一处更直接：律师说那名 1999 年的指控者不是"天真枯萎的花朵"。这句话的功能是把她从一个可能被侵犯的人，重新定价为一个有经验、应当自负其责的成年人。她的身体被拿来评估可信度，而不是被拿来评估发生了什么。

报道没写六名女性的名字，也没写她们在庭上如何描述那十年。我们读到的是律师怎么谈论她们，而不是她们说了什么。这个不对称本身就是材料：在这场审判里，被反复引用、反复解释、反复定义的是 Brand 的意图、Brand 的信念、Brand 是否"真的以为"对方同意。女性的同意只作为他主观状态的证据出现。

律师说，过着高度滥交的生活完全合法，哪怕你觉得它恶心、不道德，那又怎样，与你们无关。这句话是对的，而且它不该需要被辩护。问题在于，它被用来替换另一个问题：不是他睡了多少人，而是那三个人说没有同意的时候，发生了什么。前者被反复放大，后者被挤到角落。

判决还没出来。但辩护词已经先把一件事说清楚了：在这个法庭上，一个男人的"正常"可以证明他无罪，一个女人的"正常"可以证明她没被伤害。

Brand's barrister told the court that he respects it when a woman says no, and that this makes him someone who is not a rapist. Look at the logic: a man who accepted a refusal at one moment cannot have committed an assault at another. No is treated as a one-time switch rather than something that has to be obtained again every time.

Then the line the barrister called an "extremely curious feature": out of the many, many women Brand had sex with, only three say they did not consent. The number is offered to the jury as a doubt. It is also a confession: in this defense, the count of women is the alibi. The more women, the smaller the proportion of accusations, the less it looks like a crime. Sex becomes a denominator; assault becomes a probability.

The barrister also said the three complainants appeared "normal" afterwards, which simply cannot have happened if he were guilty. This writes the shape of trauma into the definition of guilt. Who decided that a woman who has been assaulted must fall apart on the spot, must stop seeming like herself, must be legible to bystanders as damaged? A woman who leaves calmly is, under this logic, a woman who was not harmed. And the report supplies half the answer to why they came forward late: the women went to police after Channel 4's documentary and the Times stories in 2023. The barrister calls this poisoning the wells of justice.

Calling public reporting a contaminant in the water supply of the courts amounts to saying that women hearing other women speak, and only then speaking themselves, is itself suspect. But this is the personal is political, staged in a courtroom: private experience needs public space before it can be heard at all. What the defense objects to is not procedure. It is the existence of that channel.

He opened by reciting an 1864 quote from the theologian and philosopher John Henry Newman to ground the phrase. A Victorian theologian, summoned to set the tone for a rape trial in 2026. This is not a rhetorical accident. It is how meta-violence works day to day: the most neutral, most civilized language of the law rests on centuries of authority written by men.

One more, blunter: the barrister said the 1999 complainant was no "naive and wilting flower." The function of that line is to reprice her, from a person who may have been assaulted into an experienced adult who should have known better. Her body is assessed for credibility, not for what happened to it.

The report does not name the six women, and does not tell us how they described those ten years in the witness box. What we read is the barrister talking about them, not them speaking. That asymmetry is the material: what gets cited, interpreted and redefined is Brand's intent, Brand's belief, whether Brand "genuinely thought" she was consenting. Women's consent appears only as evidence of his state of mind.

The barrister said living a highly promiscuous life is entirely lawful, however disgusting or immoral you find it, and who cares, it is irrelevant. That is true, and it should not need defending. The problem is that it is used to replace a different question: not how many women he slept with, but what happened when three of them say they did not consent. The first is amplified; the second is pushed into a corner.

There is no verdict yet. But the closing speech has already made one thing clear: in this courtroom, a man's normality can prove him innocent, and a woman's normality can prove she was never harmed.
