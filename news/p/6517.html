邮局少收一次信，选票就作废了One Fewer Mail Pickup, One Vote Erased

国际 结构层 · 文化层 ProPublica ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国邮政局削减成本、减少乡村地区邮件收取频次后，今年初选中因迟到被拒的邮寄选票在受影响地区翻倍甚至增至三倍。华盛顿州东部11个乡村县近2000张选票迟到，2022年约为600张。邮政局称11月3日大选将采取特别措施，但专家警告仍可能有选票被拒。
The newsAfter the U.S. Postal Service cut costs and reduced mail pickups in rural areas, ballots rejected for arriving late doubled or tripled in affected counties in this year's primaries. In 11 rural eastern Washington counties, nearly 2,000 ballots arrived late, versus about 600 in 2022. The Postal Service says special measures for the Nov. 3 election should prevent delays, but experts warn rejections remain likely.
削减成本省下的每一分钱，都从某个人的选票上扣掉。
Every dollar saved on rural mail is deducted from someone's ballot.

邮政局把偏远地区邮局的收信频次从一天两次砍到一天一次，省下的是钱，扣掉的是选票。ProPublica 的分析说得很清楚：受削减影响的县，因迟到被拒的选票率翻倍甚至翻三倍；没受影响的县没有这个变化。华盛顿州加菲尔德县今年有 19 张选票邮戳迟到，接近每十张一张，2022 年只有一张。这不是天气，不是意外，是一个可以被预测、被计算、被写进预算的后果。

机制值得看清楚。邮件不在本地邮局盖邮戳了，要送到区域处理中心才盖。收信从一天两次减到一次，等于把盖戳时间往后推了一天甚至更久。选举法要求邮戳在截止日前，选民按时把信投进邮箱，邮戳却盖在了截止日之后——选票作废。选民做对了每一步，制度在中间某一环把他删掉了。这是结构暴力最标准的形状：没有施暴者，只有流程；没有人被追责，只有人被剔除。

更值得看的是谁在付代价。ProPublica 的数据显示，延迟影响最大的是 2024 年投票给特朗普的地区。华盛顿州州务卿霍布斯说，他不认为邮政局的人意识到这会伤到自己的选民基础。这句话里有一种荒诞：削减乡村邮政服务的人，砍的是自己票仓的腿。但这不是反转，这是共谋者理论的老剧本——推动削减的人算的是成本账，没算选票账，因为选票从来不在他们的资产负债表上。乡村选民以为自己和决策者在同一边，其实只是同一边的耗材。

邮政局说 11 月 3 日大选有“特别措施”：选举前十天，选票单独分拣，当天送到选举办公室。听上去是解法。但全国选举官员协会的 Tammy Patrick 说，问题不是“会不会”有选票被拒，而是“有多少”。2024 年大选前试点时，邮政局督察长的审计就发现工作人员有时处理选票不当，原因是指令传达不清。特别措施依赖的是完美执行，而完美执行从来不是制度默认能给出的东西。

还有一层：这些变化是在 2024 年大选后被暂停、选后又恢复的。2024 年大选前 DeJoy 同意暂停 RTO 计划，只影响约 4000 个 ZIP code；选后恢复，现在扩展到近 20000 个。选举前暂停、选举后推进，这个时间点本身就是一种表达——它说明决策者知道这些改变会影响投票，只是选择在投票之后才让它生效。

选举官员在补救：增加投递箱，劝选民别用邮寄。Larsen 说她以前避免告诉选民怎么投票，现在最关心的是“拿到你的选票”。这是基层在替结构补漏。但补漏的前提是选民知道有漏。那些没看到新闻、按老习惯把选票投进邮箱的人，就是被这条新闻统计进去的那个数字。

邮政局省下的钱是真的：到 2025 财年末约 28 亿美元，比预期还少。代价也是真的：乡村居民等处方、等账单都变慢了。选票只是这份账单上最刺眼的一行——因为它把“服务变慢”翻译成了“权利消失”。

一张选票因为邮戳晚了一天而不算数，这不是行政失误，这是把“谁的声音能被计入”写进了物流时刻表。特别措施能救回多少张，取决于执行有多完美；而那些已经因为一次收信被砍掉而作废的选票，不会因为 11 月的特别措施复活。

The Postal Service cut mail pickups at rural post offices from twice a day to once. The money saved is real. So is the vote count that disappears. ProPublica's analysis is precise: in counties affected by the cuts, the rate of ballots rejected for arriving late doubled or tripled compared to 2022; counties where pickups stayed the same saw no such increase. In Garfield County, Washington, 19 ballots this year had late postmarks — nearly one in ten — against a single ballot in 2022.

The mechanism matters. Postmarks no longer happen at local post offices; mail has to reach a regional processing center first. Cut pickups in half and the postmark slips a day or more. Election law requires a postmark by the deadline. The voter drops the ballot in the box on time; the postmark lands after the deadline; the ballot is void. The voter did everything right. The system deleted them somewhere in the middle. This is structural violence in its purest form: no perpetrator, only process; no one held accountable, only people removed.

Look at who pays. ProPublica found the delays hit hardest in areas that voted for Trump in 2024. Washington Secretary of State Steve Hobbs said he doesn't think the people behind the changes realized how much it would affect their own voter base. There is a particular absurdity here: the people cutting rural mail are cutting the legs off their own voters. But this isn't a twist. It's the old co-conspirator script — the people pushing the cuts ran a cost calculation, not a ballot calculation, because ballots never appear on their balance sheet. Rural voters believed they were on the same side as the decision-makers. They were the material.

The Postal Service says the Nov. 3 general election has "extraordinary measures": for ten days before the election, ballots get separated and delivered the day they arrive. Sounds like a fix. But Tammy Patrick of the National Association of Election Officials says the question isn't whether ballots will be rejected, but how many. During the 2024 pilot, the inspector general's audit found staff sometimes mishandled ballots because instructions weren't clearly communicated. Extraordinary measures depend on flawless execution, and flawless execution is never what a system delivers by default.

One more layer: the changes were paused before the 2024 election and resumed after it. DeJoy agreed to pause RTO ahead of November 2024, leaving about 4,000 ZIP codes affected; after the election, the rollout resumed and now covers nearly 20,000. Pause before the vote, proceed after — the timing is itself an expression. It says the decision-makers knew these changes would affect voting and chose to let them take effect once voting was over.

Election officials are patching what they can: more drop boxes, telling voters not to trust the mail. Larsen says she used to avoid telling people how to vote; now her priority is getting the ballot at all. That's local government filling a hole the structure made. But patching only works if voters know the hole is there. The ones who didn't see the news and dropped their ballot in the mailbox out of habit are the numbers this story counts.

The savings are real: about $2.8 billion by the end of fiscal 2025, less than expected. The costs are real too: rural residents wait longer for prescriptions and bills. Ballots are just the sharpest line on that bill, because they translate "slower service" into "vanished right."

A ballot voided because a postmark landed a day late isn't an administrative error. It's a logistics timetable that decides whose voice gets counted. Extraordinary measures may rescue some ballots in November, depending on how perfectly they're executed. The ballots already voided by a single cut pickup will not be revived by any of it.
