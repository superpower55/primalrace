一支球队烂到底，城市还得掏钱A Bad Team, a Bigger Bill

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
球场上输的是比赛，谈判桌上赢的是补贴。
They lose on the pitch and win at the negotiating table.

DC United 的老板又一次出来道歉了。连续七年进不了季后赛——在一个每年至少放一半球队进季后赛的联盟里，七年里有五年是六成球队都能进。这不是运气差，这是把"我们不在乎"写进了运营手册。

然后他们向 DC 政府要几亿美元，翻修一座才用了八年的球场。逻辑很清楚：竞技层面的产出可以烂到创纪录，资本层面的索取一分不能少。输球是球迷的事，账单是纳税人的事。

报道里那串人事变动值得多看一眼。教练换了又换，最近一位是二十年带过十家俱乐部的瑞士人；体育部门交给一个从没进过管理层办公室的德国律师，维基百科上他最主要的身份是"厄齐尔的经纪人"，还写过几本讲足球和操控它的强势经纪人的书。一个卖故事的人被请来管一支球队，球队就继续成为一个故事。

这不是 MLS 独有的病。堪萨斯城赛季丢了八十个球，创联盟纪录；亚特兰大花两千两百万美元买来的人成了史上最大水货之一，八月赶紧租去柏林联；蒙特利尔球迷往场内扔烟火、砸坏看台通道，俱乐部反手禁止球迷入场。老板们轮流证明一件事：只要特许经营权还在升值，场上发生什么都可以是次要的。

蒙特利尔至少还有三十多年的城市记忆撑着一点微弱的希望。DC United 连这个都没有了，报道最后那句写得很准——禁止球迷进场，在 Audi Field 或许可以被视为一种爱的行为。

真正被定价的从来不是胜负。是一群人对一座城市的忠诚，被折算成谈判桌上可以抵押的筹码；是球迷几十年的记忆，被拿来给一次翻修预算做背书。老板道歉的时候，账已经在别处结清了。

DC United's ownership has apologized again. Seven straight seasons without playoffs — in a league that admits at least half its teams every year, and in five of those seven let in sixty percent or more. That is not bad luck. That is an operating manual that says we don't care.

Now they want hundreds of millions from the DC government to renovate an eight-year-old stadium. The logic is clean: competitive output can hit record lows, capital extraction holds steady. Losing is the fans' problem. The invoice is the taxpayers'.

The front-office churn is worth a second look. Coaches come and go; the latest is a Swiss journeyman who has guided ten clubs in twenty years. The sporting department went to a German-born lawyer who had never worked in a front office, whose Wikipedia entry leads with being Mesut Özil's agent, and who has written books about the high-octane world of football and the powerful agents who control it. A man who sells stories was hired to run a team, and the team stays a story.

This isn't an MLS-only disease. Kansas City has conceded eighty goals this season, a league record. Atlanta paid $22m for a designated player who became arguably the biggest bust in league history before being loaned out to Union Berlin in August. Montreal fans threw pyrotechnics onto the field and damaged concourses; the club responded by banning fans from the stands. Owners keep proving the same point: as long as the franchise keeps appreciating, what happens on the field is secondary.

Montreal at least has three decades of city memory holding up a faint hope. DC United doesn't even have that. The piece's closing line lands: barring fans from Audi Field could be viewed as an act of love.

What gets priced is never the win-loss record. It's the loyalty of people to a city, converted into collateral at the negotiating table; decades of memory used to underwrite a renovation budget. By the time the owner apologizes, the bill has already been settled somewhere else.
