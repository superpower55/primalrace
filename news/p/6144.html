被冻结的资金，解冻的电影The Films They Froze, and the Man Who Unfroze Them

文娱 结构层 · 元暴力 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
他举报了分钱的共谋者，于是共谋者冻结了他的电影。
He reported the people splitting the money. They froze his films.

曼切夫斯基在采访里说得很平：他向电影基金举报了两次腐败，2020 年一次，2022 年一次，一路捅到总理那里，然后他被列入黑名单，项目被冻结。已经拍完的《Kaymak》也一起被卡住。政府媒体发起抹黑攻势，对他提起刑事指控。最后反腐败委员会和国家审计署确认了他举报的内容，他赢了两次官司，钱才放出来。

这一段是整篇采访里最硬的部分，硬到不需要任何形容词。一个导演举报了「39 个项目拿了部分或全额资助却从未拍成，没人在乎，他们把经费分掉了」，代价是自己的项目全部停摆。这就是元暴力在具体制度里的运行方式：解释权不在谁对谁错，在谁握着钱袋和起诉书。

他说这是「新社会现实主义」（neo-social realism）：电影基金和电影节把「有信息量」变成一种行业要求、一种风格，创作者把自己压缩成一本小册子。他说得很不客气：这很他妈可悲。

这话值得停一下。他不是在反对有立场的电影，他反对的是把电影降格为信息的载体——「评判电影的标准变成了电影以外的东西：导演是谁、从哪来、什么生活方式、什么政治语境，一切都不是电影本身」。这是一个导演在说：表达被武器化了，武器化的方式是把表达变成传单。传单不需要美学，只需要站队。

然后他说了一句更狠的：「如果连美都没有，为什么要做艺术？」

这句话容易被误读成审美至上主义。不是。他拍《好人》——那部关于科查尼夜总会火灾的纪录片——是自己掏的钱，因为拿不到基金，也因为不想让这部片子有任何被政治化的可能。他去找那些被困在火里、在人群最底下、觉得自己正在烧的人，问他们：直面死亡是什么感觉。有人承认自己踩着别人往外爬。有人冲进火里好几次，不呼吸，伤了自己，救出别人。

这不是把苦难审美化，这是拒绝让苦难被别人拿去定价。他自己出钱，是为了不让这笔账落到共谋者手上。

所以「美」在这里不是装饰，是防守。当解释权被基金、被政府、被电影节的口味垄断时，作品本身的美学强度就是唯一没法被没收的东西。他们可以冻结你的账户、卡住你的发行、在政府媒体上抹黑你，但他们没法替你拍出那部片子。他说得很清楚：「他们想让我闭嘴，结果我拍了两部电影。」

采访里他提到母亲在他六岁半时去世，而他很多年拒绝承认这件事。他说母亲是他每部电影里都在的东西，不是计划好的，是渗进来的。这一段和腐败举报放在同一篇采访里，看起来是两件事，其实是同一件：一个人怎么处理「不被承认的事实」。母亲死了，他不承认；腐败发生了，制度不承认。他两次都选择了把不承认的东西摆到台面上，一次用电影，一次用举报。

报道没有写的是：那 39 个拿了钱没拍成的项目，钱到底去了谁的口袋，有没有人被追责。反腐败委员会的确认、审计署的确认、他赢的两场官司，都只解决了他一个人的资金。案子还在系统里走。

他赢了，因为他有威尼斯的金狮、有 HBO 的履历、有透明国际和威尼斯电影节的声援。一个没有这些的举报者，在同一套系统里会怎样，这篇采访没问，也没答。

Manchevski says it flatly in the interview: he reported corruption at the film agency twice, in 2020 and again in 2022, and it went all the way up to the prime minister. Then he was blacklisted and his projects were frozen. Kaymak was blocked too, even though it was finished. The government press ran a smear campaign. They filed criminal charges against him. In the end the Anti-Corruption Commission and the State Audit Office confirmed what he had reported, he won two court cases, and the money was released.

This is the hardest passage in the interview, and it needs no adjectives. A director reports that 39 projects were partially or fully funded and never made, that nobody cares, that they split the money — and the price is that his own projects stop. This is how meta-violence runs inside an actual institution: the解释权 does not belong to who is right, it belongs to whoever holds the purse and the indictment.

He calls the industry's condition neo-social realism: film funds and festivals have turned having a message into a requirement, a style, and creators compress themselves into a pamphlet. He is blunt about it — it's fucking sad.

Pause here. He is not arguing against films with a position. He is arguing against films demoted into vehicles for information: judging films by what surrounds the film, who the filmmaker is, where they come from, their lifestyle, the political context — everything but the film itself. A director saying: expression has been weaponized, and the weaponization is turning expression into a leaflet. A leaflet needs no aesthetics. It needs a side.

Then he says something harder: if there's no beauty, why do art?

That line is easy to misread as aestheticism. It isn't. He paid for Good People, the documentary about the Kočani nightclub fire, out of his own pocket — because he couldn't access the agency's funds, and because he didn't want any chance of the film being politicized. He went to the people who were trapped, at the bottom of the pile, who felt they were burning, and asked them what it is like to look death in the eye. One admitted he was clawing over other people to get out. Others went back into the fire, not breathing, hurting themselves, saving people.

This is not beautifying suffering. It is refusing to let suffering be priced by someone else. He paid himself so the bill would not land in the hands of the co-conspirators.

So beauty here is not decoration. It is defense. When the解释权 is monopolized by funds, by governments, by festival taste, the aesthetic force of the work itself is the one thing that cannot be confiscated. They can freeze your account, block your release, smear you in the government press — they cannot shoot the film for you. He says it plainly: they tried to shut me up, and instead I made two films.

He mentions that his mother died when he was six and a half, and that for years he refused to acknowledge it. Mothers are in every one of his films, not by plan, but by seepage. That passage sits in the same interview as the corruption reports, and it is the same thing: how a person handles a fact that is not being acknowledged. His mother died; he would not admit it. Corruption happened; the institution would not admit it. Both times he put the unacknowledged thing on the table — once with a film, once with a report.

What the interview does not say: where the money for those 39 unmade projects actually went, and whether anyone was held responsible. The commission's confirmation, the audit office's confirmation, his two court wins — all of it resolved only his own funding. The investigation is still moving through the system.

He won because he has a Golden Lion, a Wire credit, and support from Transparency International and the Venice Film Festival. What happens to a whistleblower in the same system without those, the interview does not ask, and does not answer.
