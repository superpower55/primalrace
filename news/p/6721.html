一部剧的体面，建在谁的身体上Whose Body Holds Up the Drawing Room

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Vulture ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要《镀金时代》第四季开播，Vulture 的订阅专栏逐场点评首集：Gladys 不知道自己已无法再怀孕，医生和未婚夫都没告诉她；Marian 与 Larry 一夜情后懊悔；剧集还引入 George 追查刺杀者的暗线。专栏同时记录了首映派对，并争论这部剧该不该拍得更性感。
The newsThe Gilded Age returns for season four, and Vulture's subscriber column recaps the premiere: Gladys is not told she can no longer bear children, Marian sleeps with Larry and regrets it, and George hunts his would-be assassin. The column also covers the premiere party and debates whether the show should be sexier.
他们夸这集终于有了人味，人味来自一个女人不知道自己身体里发生了什么。
They praise the show for finally giving people feelings. The feeling is a woman not knowing what happened inside her own body.

这篇专栏最诚实的一句话，是它自己没当回事的那句：Gladys 不知道关于自己器官的关键事实，而这会成为整季女性角色的主题——最有主权的女人也管不了自己的身体。然后她们继续聊裙子、聊派对、聊这剧该不该更性感。

医生知道，不说。未婚夫 Hector 知道，也不说。专栏里几个人轮流分配「该怪谁」，怪医生，怪 Hector，怪不到 Bertha 头上。这个分配讨论本身就承认了一件事：Gladys 的生育能力是一份信息，一份资产，握在几个男人手里，由他们决定什么时候交给她。她本人是最后一个知道自己身体的人。这不是剧情漏洞，这是那个年代的产权结构，而剧集把它当成一个可爱的桥段来消费。

更值得看的是专栏怎么处理 Marian。她跟 Larry 睡了一夜，懊悔，然后把 Larry 推开，被几位作者齐声吐槽「嫁给他不就完了」。同一段对话里，Emily 说这剧想讲的是「女人的身体如何被弄成有用的功能」，Olivia 说这两人毫无火花，赶紧收场。两种读法并列摆着，谁也不觉得冲突。这就是文化暴力最舒服的形态：它一边承认女人对自己的身体没有控制权，一边把这种失控包装成情节糖，让读者笑着咽下去。

真正把这套机制演出来的，是派对那一节。Cynthia Nixon「打扮成一把拖把」，Louisa Jacobson 西装露腹肌，Ashlie Atkinson 念了一句 Azealia Banks，Bonnie Milligan 找不到 Debra Monk，Dorinda Medley 说「女人是传奇！我们就是大地！」。这些句子很热闹，也很准确：一场关于十九世纪纽约上流社会的剧，它的首映礼本身就是上流社会的复刻，礼服、大理石楼梯、鱼子酱配鹌鹑蛋，谁被邀请、谁站在哪，全是表达，全是定价。HBO 的主管上台感谢纽约州的税收优惠，宣布这一季雇了两百个剧场工作者——这是整篇报道里唯一一处诚实的资源分配说明，也是最不被当回事的一处。

至于「这剧该不该更性感」那场辩论，问错了问题。它问的是要不要给观众看肉体，没问的是：这部剧从头到尾让谁的身体承担叙事的重量，又让谁来决定这个重量什么时候被揭开。Gladys 的子宫是剧情道具，Marian 的初夜是情节转折，George 的药瘾是「他看起来更性感了」。所有人都在被看，被看的方式不一样。

专栏结尾教人怎么吃洋蓟：先识别敌人，剥叶、蘸酱、刮肉，最后把心脏留给自己。写这段的人大概没意识到，这一集里被剥开、被刮、心脏被别人攥着的，正是 Gladys。

The most honest line in this column is the one nobody treats as serious: Gladys doesn't know a crucial fact about her own organs, and that sets the theme for every woman on the show — even the ones with the most agency don't control their bodies. Then everyone goes back to the hats, the party, and whether the show should be hornier.

The doctor knows and says nothing. Hector knows and says nothing. The column spends a paragraph assigning blame — the doctor first, then Hector, not Bertha, it wasn't her place. That allocation admits the thing it is trying not to say: Gladys's fertility is information, an asset, held by men who decide when she gets to hear it. She is the last person to learn about her own body. This isn't a plot hole. It's the property structure of the era, and the show serves it up as a charming beat.

Marian gets the same treatment. She sleeps with Larry, regrets it, pushes him away, and the panel collectively sighs: just marry him already. In the same exchange, Emily says the show is trying to grapple with how women's bodies were made functional, and Olivia says these two have no spark, wrap it up. Both readings sit side by side without friction. That is cultural violence at its most comfortable: it names the loss of bodily control and then candies it, so the reader swallows it smiling.

The party section is where the machinery shows. Cynthia Nixon dressed as a mop. Louisa Jacobson in a suit with exposed abs. Ashlie Atkinson rapping Azealia Banks. Dorinda Medley declaring that women are legends, that we are the earth. These lines are funny and they are precise: a show about Gilded Age New York throws a premiere that is itself Gilded Age New York — the gowns, the marble staircase, the caviar on quail eggs, who was invited and where they stood. Every bit of it is expression, every bit of it is pricing. The HBO head thanks New York State for its tax incentives and announces 200 theater workers employed this season. It is the only honest sentence about resource allocation in the whole piece, and the least examined.

The should-it-be-sexier debate asks the wrong question. It asks whether we get to see flesh. It doesn't ask whose bodies carry the story's weight, or who decides when that weight gets revealed. Gladys's uterus is a plot device, Marian's first night is a turn, George's laudanum is that he looks hotter than ever. Everyone is being looked at. Not in the same way.

The column closes with a guide to eating an artichoke: identify the enemy, pluck, dip, scrape, and claim the heart for yourself. Whoever wrote it didn't notice that in this episode the one being plucked, scraped, and held by the heart is Gladys.
