一支被低估的乐队，和一篇不追问的讣闻An Underrated Group, and an Obit That Asks Nothing

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
当纪念只谈歌单，解释权就留在了原处
When a tribute is only a playlist, the power to define stays put.

LaMonte McLemore 死了，Billboard 给出的回应是一份十五首金曲排行榜。这不是讣闻，是一次重新定价——把一支 1967 到 1973 年间有二十首单曲进 Hot 100 前四十、拿过两次格莱美年度制作奖的乐队，重新摆上货架，附一份带编号的清单。谁定的价，谁就有权说什么是重要的。

这篇稿子最诚实的一句话是它自己说出来的：摇滚名人堂因为“太流行”而忽视了他们。一个以“摇滚”为名的机构，用流派纯度当筛子，把一支黑人主唱、白人制作、跨 soul 与 jazz 的五人组合挡在门外几十年。筛子不是审美，筛子是权力给自己找的体面说法。品味区分阶级，这条规律在流行音乐工业里从没失效过，只是换了个词，叫“严肃性”。

更有意思的是它给出的补救：2021 年 Questlove 那部《Summer of Soul》让 1969 年哈莱姆文化节的影像重见天日，人们才想起来他们当年有多好。也就是说，一支乐队的价值要等另一位导演用镜头替它作证，才被承认。解释权从来不在被解释的人手里。

然后看它怎么谈这支乐队里的女人。Marilyn McCoo 被写成“金嗓子”，被写成那个时代最好的女声之一，和 Dionne Warwick、Gladys Knight 并列——紧接着补一句“她并不总得到应有的认可”。这句话是对的，但稿子自己没做到：它把《Wedding Bell Blues》的卖点讲成 McCoo 和 Billy Davis 录歌时在谈恋爱、发行时已结婚；把《If I Could Reach You》概括成一个女人想要承诺却只得到露水情缘，末句是“下次见”。这些歌被当成情感小电影来消费，唱歌的人被当成剧情的一部分。她的嗓子是材料，她的关系是花边。

再往下，是那句被当作趣闻写进去的话：Johnny Rivers 在首张专辑内页里夸他们有“强烈的视觉吸引力”。1966 年，一个制作人卖一组黑人歌手，先卖脸再卖声。这不是丑闻，这是行业常规，常规到可以印在内页上当赞美。

所以这份榜单真正在做什么？它把一个已经无法再发声的人，交给一套现成的评价机器：排名、峰值、制作人、趣闻。机器运转良好，产出可读性，也产出遗忘——它记住了《Aquarius/Let the Sunshine In》蝉联六周冠军，记不住是谁决定这支乐队该被记住多少。

McLemore 是 founding member，报道写了他 2 月 3 日去世，写了他 2001 年去世的队友 Ron Townson，写了乐队原名 The Versatiles。它没写的是：这份“被低估”的账，该由谁来还。榜单排完了，名人堂的门还关着。

LaMonte McLemore died, and Billboard's answer is a ranked list of fifteen hits. This is not an obituary. It is a re-pricing: taking a group that put twenty singles in the Hot 100 top 40 between 1967 and 1973 and won two Grammys for record of the year, and putting them back on the shelf with a numbered inventory attached. Whoever sets the price gets to say what matters.

The most honest line in the piece is the one it says itself: the Rock & Roll Hall of Fame ignored them because they were "so pop." An institution named after rock used genre purity as a sieve, and kept a five-piece with a Black lead singer and white producers, crossing soul and jazz, outside the door for decades. A sieve is not taste. A sieve is power finding itself a respectable word. Taste sorting class is a law that has never stopped working in the pop industry; it just changes its name to "seriousness."

More interesting is the remedy it offers: Questlove's 2021 documentary brought 1969 Harlem Cultural Festival footage back into view, and only then did people remember how good the group had been. A band's worth had to be vouched for by a director's camera before it counted. The power to explain never sits with the ones being explained.

Then watch how it talks about the woman in the group. Marilyn McCoo is called a golden voice, ranked alongside Dionne Warwick, Gladys Knight, Dusty Springfield and Karen Carpenter, and then handed the line that she "isn't always given the credit she deserves." True, and the piece doesn't do it either: it sells "Wedding Bell Blues" on the fact that McCoo and Billy Davis were dating when they recorded it and married when it came out; it summarizes "If I Could Reach You" as a woman who wants commitment and settles for an affair, last line "See you next time." The songs become little movies about feeling, and the singer becomes part of the plot. Her voice is material. Her relationship is color.

Further down is the anecdote offered as a compliment: Johnny Rivers, in the liner notes, praising the group for having not only a hit sound but "strong visual appeal." In 1966, a producer selling a Black vocal group sells the faces first and the sound second. That is not a scandal. That is industry standard, standard enough to print as praise.

So what is this list actually doing? It hands a man who can no longer speak over to a ready-made evaluation machine: rankings, peak positions, producers, trivia. The machine runs well. It produces readability, and it produces forgetting. It remembers that "Aquarius/Let the Sunshine In" held No. 1 for six weeks. It does not remember who decided how much this group should be remembered.

McLemore was a founding member. The piece notes he died Feb. 3, notes bandmate Ron Townson's 2001 death, notes the group's first name, The Versatiles. What it does not note is who owes the debt of "underrated." The list is finished. The Hall's door is still shut.
