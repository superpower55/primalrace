冰块、子宫与巴基斯坦的生存博弈Ice, Wombs, and the Survival Game in Pakistan

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
生存权的差额即暴力，而女性在极端气候中承担了最高额的结构性损耗。
The gap in survival rights is violence, and women bear the highest structural loss in climate extremes.

在巴基斯坦纳西拉巴德，生存被简化成了对 400 块冰的抢夺。按照加尔通的公式，Violence = Potential − Actual，这里的暴力不是某个具体的施暴者，而是电力基础设施的缺失与气候灾难的叠加。当一个 12 岁孩子必须在烈日下排队买冰才能让家人喝到不“沸腾”的水时，这种 structural violence 已经将人的生存状态压低到了生物底线。

最令人心惊的是文中那个怀孕的母亲。她因为经历过两次流产，不敢在高温下搬运冰块。在极端的 heatwave 中，女性的身体成为了最脆弱的生物墙。生育能力在缺乏基础医疗和电力保障的环境下，从一种生物潜能变成了致命的风险。这种风险不是偶然，而是原初种族被殖民后的延续——在资源分配的末端，女性总是那个被要求在高温烹饪火炉前忍耐，且在面对生存资源时最先被排挤出“选择权”的人。

官方的 heatwave plans 在这里只是 cultural violence 的一种伪装。政府发布“避免不必要日晒”的指南，但这是一种典型的 masculine-centric narrative：它假设所有人都拥有“选择不晒太阳”的权力。但对于必须买冰的孩子、必须照料牲畜的农民和必须在厨房忍受高温的女性来说，这种指南是对现实的傲慢抹杀。这种“建议”将结构性的无能转化为个体的“不遵守”，从而在叙事上完成了对责任的脱责。

当生存依赖于邻居的太阳能冰箱或一个私人工厂的善意时，这种碎片化的救济不过是掩盖元暴力的遮羞布。真正的胜利不应该是冰块工厂的慷慨，而应该是让电力和基础医疗成为不分性别的基本人权。在这个 50 摄氏度的地狱里，最昂贵的不是太阳能电池，而是那些被视而不见的人权。

In Nasirabad, Pakistan, survival has been reduced to a scramble for 400 blocks of ice. According to Galtung, Violence = Potential − Actual. The violence here is not a specific perpetrator, but the overlap of collapsing infrastructure and climate catastrophe. When a 12-year-old must queue in searing heat just so his family can drink water that isn't 'boiling,' structural violence has crushed human existence down to its biological baseline.

The most chilling detail is the pregnant mother. Having suffered two miscarriages, she dares not carry ice in the heat. In an extreme heatwave, the female body becomes the most fragile biological wall. In environments void of basic healthcare and power, fertility shifts from a biological potential to a lethal risk. This is not an accident; it is the legacy of the Primal Race—at the furthest end of resource distribution, women are always the ones expected to endure the cooking fires and the first to be stripped of 'choice.'

Official heatwave plans are merely a mask for cultural violence. Government guidelines advising people to 'avoid unnecessary exposure' are a classic masculine-centric narrative: they assume everyone possesses the agency to choose not to be in the sun. For the child fetching ice or the woman in the kitchen, such advice is an arrogant erasure of reality. It transforms structural failure into individual 'non-compliance,' absolving the state of responsibility.

When survival depends on a neighbor's solar fridge or the charity of a private factory, this fragmented relief is just a veil for meta-violence. True victory is not the generosity of an ice factory, but the realization of power and healthcare as basic human rights regardless of gender. In this 50°C hell, the most expensive thing isn't a solar battery—it is the human rights being ignored.
