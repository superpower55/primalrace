用棕榈油伪造的“奢华”：超市布里欧修的认知骗局The Luxury Scam: Chemical Simulations in Supermarket Brioche

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
当定义权被工业规模垄断，奢华就成了一种可量产的化学模拟物。
When the power of definition is monopolized by industrial scale, luxury becomes a mass-produced chemical simulation.

布里欧修 (Brioche) 在传统的认知入口里意味着鸡蛋与大量黄油的奢华堆砌。但在超市的货架上，这种“奢华”被彻底 weaponized 成了一种廉价的视觉符号。当你看到那些金灿灿、充满弹性的面包卷时，你消费的不是黄油，而是棕榈油、乳化剂和防腐剂组成的化学鸡尾酒。

这不仅是食品工业的 scam，更是一场关于“定义权”的博弈。工业资本通过控制认知入口，将“布里欧修”这个词从一种特定的生物学配比（高黄油含量）剥离，将其转化为一种“外观表达”：只要颜色够黄、口感够软，就可以被命名为布里欧修。在这种逻辑下，甚至完全不含黄油的产品也能堂而皇之地占据这个席位。这种对事实的篡改，让消费者在不知不觉中接受了“劣质替代品 = 正统”的设定。

最讽刺的是，在这种结构性暴力的背景下，评测者给出的最高赞誉竟然是“成分表相对简单”。这就像是在一个充满奴役的系统里，赞美某个奴隶主“偶尔会给奴隶多发一块面包”。当整个行业标准（the bar）被拉低到 UPF (超加工食品) 的泥潭里，所谓的“最佳”不过是在共谋者中挑选一个最不那么恶劣的骗子。

我们习惯于讨论口味和性价比，但真正应该追问的是：是谁在定义什么是“面包”？当真实的生物性食材被化学模拟物取代，且这种取代被冠以“奢华”之名时，我们失去的不仅是味觉，而是对真实世界的解释权。

Brioche, in the traditional cognitive entry, signifies a luxurious accumulation of eggs and copious butter. On supermarket shelves, however, this "luxury" has been weaponized into a cheap visual symbol. When you see those golden, springy buns, you aren't consuming butter; you're consuming a chemical cocktail of palm oil, emulsifiers, and preservatives.

This is more than a food industry scam; it is a game of definition. Industrial capital, by controlling the cognitive entry, detaches the term "Brioche" from its specific biological composition (high butter ratio) and transforms it into an "expression of appearance": as long as it's yellow enough and soft enough, it can be labeled as Brioche. Under this logic, products containing zero butter can legitimately occupy the seat. This manipulation of facts forces consumers to subconsciously accept that "inferior substitutes = the authentic."

The most ironic part is that in this context of structural violence, the highest praise given by reviewers is a "relatively simple ingredients list." It is like praising a slave owner for occasionally giving an extra piece of bread in a system of systemic servitude. When the bar is dragged down into the mud of UPF (Ultra-Processed Foods), the so-called "best" is merely picking the least offensive liar among a circle of complicity.

We are used to discussing flavor and value, but the real question is: who defines what "bread" is? When authentic biological ingredients are replaced by chemical simulations, and this replacement is branded as "luxury," we lose more than our taste—we lose the power to interpret reality.
