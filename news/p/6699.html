一部没人看的电影，和一圈人的体面A Movie Nobody Saw, and a Circle's Good Name

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要约翰·古德曼在播客中回应《Digger》遭遇的恶评，称这些评论「在上映前就把我们杀了」，并表示自己为这部由汤姆·克鲁斯主演、伊纳里图执导的电影感到骄傲。该片全球票房约5000万美元，而据报道制作预算超过1.25亿美元，但仍被视为颁奖季的潜在竞争者。
The newsJohn Goodman said on a podcast that the scathing reviews for Digger "kind of killed us before we opened," while defending the Tom Cruise and Alejandro G. Iñárritu film he is proud of. The movie has grossed about $50 million worldwide on a reported budget above $125 million, yet is still expected to be an awards contender.
票房输了，圈子里的定价权没输。
The box office lost. The circle's power to price things didn't.

古德曼说恶评「在上映前就把我们杀了」。这句话说反了：杀死这部片的不是影评，是它自己花掉的钱。据报道预算超过1.25亿，全球票房约5000万，观众用脚投票，比任何一篇影评都狠。

然后是那句最要紧的：「我为它骄傲，拍它的时候我很开心，那是我人生中美好的六个月。」六个月，对74岁的演员来说不短。但同一段话里，他承认自己开大排量车，所以支持这部片的环境 message 算是「伪君子」——他自己把话讲完了，不用我补。一部讲石油大亨闯祸的电影，主创一边开着大引擎车一边说这是「一个不错的警钟」。警钟敲给谁听？

真正值得看的是后半段名单：德尔·托罗说它「大胆、有野心」，哈撒韦在 Letterboxd 上写它「聪明、迷幻、有攻击性」，比尔·伯尔说「影评人疯了」。一个圈子在集体为一个票房失败的作品抬价。这不是阴谋，是共谋的日常形态——同行互评，互相确认「我们做的是重要的东西」，奖项季就是这套确认机制的年度结算。古德曼自己给了钥匙：「我给科恩兄弟做的每一部，一开始都扑了。」翻译一下：扑不扑不重要，重要的是后来谁说了算。

影评人当然不是无辜的裁判。但把「恶评」当成一部1.25亿的电影失败的原因，等于把观众的选择、发行的判断、预算的失控全部推给几篇文字。这是一种很方便的叙事：错不在我们，错在那些先开口的人。

报道里没写的是：这5000万票房背后，谁的钱亏了，谁的工作没了，那些在片场搬灯、做特效、跑宣发的人，他们的六个月有没有「美好」。古德曼的六个月被记住了，他们的没有。

Goodman says the reviews "kind of killed us before we opened." He has it backwards. What killed this film wasn't criticism; it was the money it spent. A reported budget above $125 million against roughly $50 million worldwide — audiences voted with their feet, harder than any review.

Then the line that matters: "I'm proud of it... It was a good six months out of my life." Six months is not nothing at 74. But in the same breath he calls himself a "big hypocrite" for loving a big-engine car while backing the film's environmental message. He finished the thought himself; I don't need to.

What's worth watching is the second half of the piece: del Toro calls it "daring, bold," Hathaway posts a glowing Letterboxd review, Bill Burr says the critics are out of their minds. A circle is collectively marking up a flop. Not a conspiracy — just the everyday form of complicity: peers reviewing peers, confirming to each other that what they make matters. Awards season is that confirmation mechanism settling its accounts. Goodman hands over the key himself: everything he did for the Coen brothers flopped at first. Translation: the flop doesn't matter; what matters is who gets to write the verdict later.

Critics are not innocent judges. But blaming "scathing reviews" for a $125 million film's failure shoves the audience's choice, the studio's judgment, the runaway budget onto a few paragraphs. It's a convenient story: the fault isn't ours, it's the people who spoke first.

What the report doesn't say: who lost the money behind that $50 million, whose jobs went with it, whether the six months of the people who rigged the lights and ran the press tour were also "good." Goodman's six months get remembered. Theirs don't.
