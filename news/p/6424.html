保密本身就是一种搜查Secrecy Is Itself a Search

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要美国两名民主党参议员指出，特朗普政府正以国会续期《外国情报监视法》第702条为前提，扣留外国情报监视法院七个月前的一项秘密裁定。该裁定认为情报机构用于筛选美国人通信的搜索工具构成搜查，须受法律限制，但法院仍批准该项目继续一年。参议员要求政府在11月6日前作出解释。
The newsTwo Democratic senators say the Trump administration is withholding a secret Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court ruling from seven months ago until Congress renews Section 702. The court found that tools used to pull Americans' messages from intercepted data amount to searches subject to legal limits, yet approved the program for another year. The senators demand answers by November 6.
不公开裁决，就是在用保密继续搜查你
Withholding the ruling is how the surveillance continues.

一个秘密法庭认定政府的搜查工具越界，要求重做；政府把这份裁决锁了七个月，然后拿它当筹码，逼国会先续期、再谈公开。这不是程序拖延，这是把解释权攥在手里当执法工具用。

报道里的事实很清楚：外国情报监视法院（FISA court）在三月认定，情报分析员用来从截获数据里捞出美国人通信的那些过滤工具，本身就算对美国人实施搜查，应当受同样的法律限制，并下令重建。法院同时批准该项目再运行一年。裁决从未公开，政府自己定的 180 天公开期限在 9 月 13 日到期，Wyden 和 Schiff 说期限已过，要求 11 月 6 日前给出解释和责任人姓名。

这里动的不是 direct 层，没有人被带走。动的是 structural 层和 meta 层：结构上，一个本应约束行政分支的司法认定被行政分支扣着不发；元层上，谁有权定义“这算不算搜查”这件事，被扣留裁决的人单方面占住了。加尔通的公式在这里很好用：Potential 是法院已经写下的那份限制，Actual 是公众看到的七份事实表，里面写着 98.9% 合规率、写着拦下维也纳 Taylor Swift 演唱会的恐袭图谋，一个字不提法院的认定。差额就是暴力。

那七份事实表里的一处细节值得盯住：它们说 FBI 审计员会复核每一次此类搜查。而 FBI 局长 Kash Patel 去年五月关掉了内部审计办公室，把审计员并进了另一个部门。同一批材料还漏掉了 2025 年之前通过“参与者过滤器”跑的查询——政府自己的隐私监督机构说那些查询“无法追踪”。合规率的分母，是由被审计的一方自己划的。

更早的信号已经出现过：去年解密的另一份 FISA 法院意见描述过类似的“参与者过滤器”，2025 年 2 月司法部国家安全司告诉法院，这个工具一直在跑没人统计过的搜查，FBI 次月关掉，后来又重启。今年这份裁决走得更远，把过滤工具直接定性为搜查。于是你明白政府为什么不肯公开：一旦公开，国会辩论的基础就不是“98.9% 合规”，而是“法院说你们的工具违法，你们还在用”。

共谋在这里不需要阴谋论。Cotton 和 Warner 四月催过 15 天内公开，Blanche 七月书面回答“Yes”，然后没有然后；Cotton 办公室不回应，白宫、国家情报总监办公室、司法部都不回答 WIRED 的问题。被问到会不会在国会投票前公开时，Clayton 只写政府正在“努力尽快解密”。这不是某个人撒谎，是一整套制度在默契地把“公开”换算成“续期的筹码”。

真正被定价的是国会自己。Durbin 在参议院说，议员们去安全室读完这份裁决，“你对 FISA 的 role 和美国人权利的看法一定会被影响”。也就是说，投票的人里有相当一部分是在信息不全的情况下投票的。Wyden 还提到一份多届政府都依赖、都拒绝解密的机密解释，他说一旦公开，美国人会震惊于它被藏了这么久。

公民自由团体这次找对了杠杆：Demand Progress 和另外 22 个组织，从 ACLU 到保守派的 Project for Privacy and Surveillance Accountability，联名要求国会不要在政府扣留法定信息期间续期。跨意识形态的联盟能成立，恰恰说明这不是左右之争，是“谁有权定义事实”的问题。

报道最后一句埋着下一步：倡导者担心政府把续期塞进年度国防授权法案，2023 年就是这么干的，四个月延期搭着国防法案过。如果这次也这样，公开裁决的期限就会被并进一个没人会为它单独投票的包裹里。

法院给的当前批准到明年三月到期。在那之前，真正的问题不是这份裁决会不会公开，而是：一个连自己法院的认定都敢扣着不发的机构，凭什么要求别人相信它会在三月之后守规矩。

A secret court found the government's search tools out of bounds and ordered them rebuilt. The government has sat on that ruling for seven months and is now using it as leverage to make Congress renew the program first and talk about disclosure later. This is not procedural delay. It is the machinery of interpretation held as an enforcement tool.

The facts in the report are plain. In March, the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court held that the filters analysts use to pull Americans' messages out of intercepted data amount to searches of Americans, subject to the same limits, and ordered them rebuilt. The court approved another year of surveillance anyway. The ruling has never been made public. The administration's own 180-day deadline for release passed on September 13. Senators Wyden and Schiff say the deadline is blown and have demanded an explanation and the names of responsible officials by November 6.

Nothing here moves at the direct layer. No one is led away. What moves is structural and meta: structurally, a judicial finding meant to bind the executive is being withheld by the executive; at the meta layer, the power to define whether something counts as a search has been seized unilaterally by the party being searched. Galtung's formula fits neatly. The potential is the limit the court already wrote down. The actual is the seven agency fact sheets the public gets to see, citing a 98.9 percent compliance rate and a foiled terrorist plot against a Taylor Swift concert in Vienna, with not one word about what the court found. The gap is the violence.

One detail in those fact sheets deserves a hard look. They say FBI auditors review every such search. FBI director Kash Patel closed the bureau's Office of Internal Auditing last May and moved its auditors into another division. The same materials also omit queries run through the participants filter before 2025, searches the government's own privacy watchdog says are not possible to track. The denominator of the compliance rate is drawn by the party being audited.

The earlier signal was already there. A FISA court opinion declassified last year described a similar participants filter. In February 2025 the Justice Department's National Security Division told the court the tool had been running searches no one counted. The FBI shut it off the next month and switched it back on later that year. This year's ruling goes further, treating the filtering tools themselves as searches. Which is exactly why the government won't release it: once it is out, the congressional debate stops being about 98.9 percent compliance and starts being about a court saying your tools are unlawful while you keep using them.

No conspiracy theory is needed for the collusion here. Cotton and Warner pressed for release within 15 days in April. Blanche answered yes in writing in July. Then nothing. Cotton's office did not respond. The White House, the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, and the Justice Department did not answer WIRED's questions. Asked before his confirmation whether he would ensure the opinion was public before a vote, Clayton wrote only that the government was working arduously to declassify it. This is not one person lying. It is an entire apparatus quietly converting disclosure into renewal leverage.

The real price is being charged to Congress itself. Durbin told the Senate in June that once members read the ruling in a secure room, their opinion of FISA's role and Americans' rights will be impacted for sure. That means a substantial share of the people voting are voting on incomplete information. Wyden has also warned about a classified interpretation of Section 702 that multiple administrations have relied on and refused to declassify. When it comes out, he said, Americans will be stunned it took so long.

The civil liberties groups have found the right lever this time. Demand Progress and 22 other organizations, from the ACLU to the conservative-backed Project for Privacy and Surveillance Accountability, are telling congressional leaders to refuse reauthorization while the administration withholds legally mandated information. A coalition spanning that ideological range is itself the tell: this is not a left-right fight. It is a fight over who gets to manufacture the facts.

The last line of the report points to the next move. Advocates expect the administration may try to attach renewal to the annual defense authorization bill, the way Congress extended Section 702 for four months in 2023. If that happens again, the deadline for disclosure gets folded into a package no one will vote on separately.

The court's current approval runs out in March. Before then, the question is not whether the ruling will eventually be released. The question is why anyone should believe an agency that withholds its own court's findings will observe any limit after March.
