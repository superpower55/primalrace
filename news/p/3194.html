被定义的“不友好”与男本位审美的快照The 'Unfriendliness' of Agency and the Male-Centric Gaze

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-03 § 链接
所谓“不友好”，不过是女性拒绝扮演协作客体时的主体性溢出。
What is labeled as 'unfriendly' is simply the overflow of female agency refusing to be a compliant object.

这篇文章本质上是一次典型的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 的权力回顾。摄影师 Gered Mankowitz 用一种极其轻盈的口吻，将一个世纪的流行文化偶像物化为他的“作品”和“素材”。在这些描述中，男性偶像（如 Elton John, Jimi Hendrix）被定义为“幽默”、“迷人”或“简单的拍摄对象”，而女性偶像的价值则被锚定在她们如何配合他的“创意”上。

最讽刺的细节在于对 Sade 的评价——“并不特别友好” (wasn’t particularly friendly)。在男本位叙事中，女性的“友好”通常意味着一种低成本的协作，即扮演一个顺从的、可被塑造的客体。Sade 表现出保留、冷淡，甚至对占用录音时间的厌恶，这在本质上是她在捍卫自己的主体性，拒绝在博弈中让渡自己的存在性。然而，在摄影师的视角里，这种主体性的觉醒被简化为一种性格缺陷，一个关于“不友好”的负面标签。

而对于其他女性，如 Suzi Quatro 的“纯真”与“保护姿态”的对比，或者 Annie Lennox 的“纯粹美丽”与男伴“紧绷”的对比，全部落入了第三章所说的“武器化表达”陷阱：通过定义某种特定的审美标签（如 understated punk glamor），将女性身体转化为一种可消费的符号。这种叙事将女性的成功简化为“被相机宠爱”，完全抹去了她们在一个由男性主导的产业中为了生存而进行的艰苦的存在性战争。

这场回顾赛中，摄影师是唯一的定义者，而这些被拍摄的女性，即便在画面中看起来自信，但在叙事层面上，她们依然是共谋机制下的客体。所谓的“标志性肖像”，其实就是一次次成功的、将主体性剥离并替换为男性审美最优解的快照。

This article is a textbook exercise in masculine-centric narrative. Photographer Gered Mankowitz recounts six decades of pop culture as a series of 'trusting relationships,' but the subtext is the objectification of artists into 'materials' for his vision. While male icons like Elton John or Jimi Hendrix are described as 'humorous' or 'charming'—essentially easy to manage—the female subjects are valued based on how well they cooperated with his 'concepts.'

The most telling detail is the description of Sade as 'not particularly friendly.' In a patriarchal framework, 'friendliness' from a woman is often a code for low-cost compliance—the willingness to act as a passive object. Sade’s reserve and her resentment at having her recording time interrupted were acts of asserting her agency. Yet, in this narrative, her refusal to be a compliant tool is reduced to a personality flaw. Her subjectivity is framed as a lack of friendliness.

Furthermore, the descriptions of Suzi Quatro’s 'innocence' or Annie Lennox’s 'unadorned beauty' fall into the trap of weaponized expression. By attaching labels like 'understated punk glamor,' the photographer transforms the female body into a consumable symbol. The narrative suggests they were 'adored by the camera,' conveniently erasing the existential war these women fought to survive in a male-dominated industry.

In this retrospective, the photographer remains the sole definer. The 'iconic portraits' are not captures of truth, but snapshots of a successful process: stripping away the subject's agency and replacing it with the optimal expression demanded by the male gaze.
