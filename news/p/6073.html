AI 吃掉的不是岗位，是练手的机会AI Didn't Take the Job. It Took the Practice.

中国科技 结构层 · 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要IBM首席人才、领导力与文化官阿帕娜·奈尔表示，入门级员工应重点培养批判性思维和判断力。IBM 9月发布的调查显示，57%的首席人力资源官认为批判性思维在AI时代重要，而员工中这一比例为49%；48%的首席人力资源官看重在不确定与模糊情境下的判断力，员工中仅29%。
The newsAparna Nair, IBM's chief talent, leadership and culture officer, says entry-level workers should focus on building critical thinking and judgment. In an IBM survey published in September, 57% of CHROs considered critical thinking important in the AI era, versus 49% of employees; 48% of CHROs valued judgment under uncertainty, versus just 29% of employees.
判断力不是听讲座长出来的，是被工作喂出来的。
Judgment isn't taught. It's fed by work that AI now eats.

IBM 的首席人才官说，入门级员工要练两项技能：critical thinking 和 judgment。这话没错。问题在她自己接着说的那句：过去人是靠实际做工作来建立判断的，现在这部分活儿 AI 干了。

于是判断力的练习场被抽走了，然后公司回头要求你自带判断力。这是一笔很干净的账：成本从雇主那里挪到了求职者身上，还包装成一句成长建议。

看调查数字就更清楚。1500 名 CHRO 和 8800 名员工，57% 的 CHRO 认为 critical thinking 重要，员工里只有 49%；judgment under uncertainty 是 48% 对 29%。高管比员工更看重这两种能力，差了将近二十个百分点。四分之三担心这件事的员工说 AI 已经在侵蚀自己的技能，最常点名的就是 critical thinking。

也就是说：员工已经感觉到自己在被削，而雇主的回应是把标准提高。

然后是解决方案。Zapier 的招聘经理建议你在 LinkedIn 上发帖谈行业观点，证明你在批判性思考；或者在面试和 take-home 里解释你怎么决定哪些自己做、哪些交给 AI。这是在教人表演判断力，不是教人拥有判断力。表达在这里变成了一次定价行为：你要向未来的雇主展示一种能力，而这种能力的养成条件恰好被同一批雇主拆掉了。

“专家是靠大量 at-bats 练出来的”，这句是全文最诚实的一句。at-bats 就是上场机会。入门级岗位之所以是入门级，正因为它提供低风险的上场机会。现在机会被 AI 接管，练习被跳过，却要求结果先到位。

这套逻辑和“做不了小芯片就宣布新定律”是同一个动作：能力没变，评价方式变了。以前看你做过什么，现在看你有没有一种无法被验证、只能靠自述和发帖证明的素质。尺子换了，能力没换。

最后，谁在为这条新闻付代价，报道里写得很清楚：那些担心自己技能正在退化的员工。他们的名字一个也没有出现。

IBM's chief talent officer says entry-level workers need two skills: critical thinking and judgment. Fine. Then she says the quiet part: people used to build judgment by actually doing the work, and that work is no longer there because AI is doing it.

So the practice ground gets removed, and the company turns around and asks you to arrive with the product. That's a clean transfer: the cost moves from the employer to the applicant, and gets rebranded as career advice.

The survey numbers make it plainer. Out of 1,500 CHROs and 8,800 employees, 57% of CHROs call critical thinking important versus 49% of employees; judgment under uncertainty is 48% versus 29%. Executives value these skills more than the people who have to have them, by nearly twenty points. Meanwhile three in four worried employees say AI has already eroded their skills, most often naming critical thinking.

Employees can feel themselves being hollowed out. The employer's response is to raise the bar.

The fix on offer: post on LinkedIn to show you think critically, or explain in an interview which tasks you'd hand to AI and why. That teaches the performance of judgment, not judgment. Expression becomes a pricing move, a demonstration of a capacity whose training conditions the same employers just dismantled.

"Expertise is built by lots and lots of at-bats" is the most honest line in the piece. At-bats means getting to swing. Entry-level jobs exist because they offer low-stakes swings. Now the swings are automated away, the practice is skipped, and the result is demanded up front.

It's the same move as declaring a new law when you can't make the small chip: capability unchanged, the yardstick changed. Once they asked what you'd done. Now they ask whether you possess a quality that can't be verified, only narrated.

And who pays? The employees who say their skills are eroding. Not one of them is named.
