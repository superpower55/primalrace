甜甜圈与多洛米蒂：算法制造的景观骗局Krapfen and the Dolomites: The Landscape Scam Manufactured by Algorithms

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
人们追逐的不是甜点，而是由算法定价的“存在性证明”。
People aren't chasing pastries; they are chasing an 'existential proof' priced by algorithms.

这根本不是关于 Krapfen 的美食新闻，而是一次典型的认知入口被武器化的集体癔症。在多洛米蒂山脉 2300 米的高处，数百人排队等待的不是食物，而是一张能够被社交媒体算法认可的“入场券”。

这就是典型的 weaponized expression。博主们通过将“奶油甜点”与“壮丽山景”进行视觉绑定，制造出一种虚假的、被精心包装的“最优解表达”：只要你出现在这个坐标，拿着这个产品，拍这张照片，你就在某种阶级或审美序列中获得了确认。在这种叙事中，多洛米蒂山脉被剥夺了其作为自然存在的属性，被降格为一张巨大的、服务于自拍的背景板。

最讽刺的是这种共谋机制：排队的景象本身成为了新的视觉符号，进一步诱导更多人加入。人们在潜意识中达成了一种共谋——通过参与这场毫无意义的排队，来证明自己处于某种“流行”的中心。这种行为本质上是主体性的死亡，他们不再是探索自然的个体，而是算法喂养下的生物插件，执行着“看到 $ ightarrow$ 迁移 $ ightarrow$ 拍照 $ ightarrow$ 上传”的指令集。

当山地社区协会抱怨“山脉不是游乐场”时，他们其实是在面对一种 meta-violence。算法定义了什么是“值得去的”，从而垄断了对自然美学的解释权。真正的自然体验被这种 image-driven 的快餐叙事彻底稀释。在这种博弈中，赢家只有那些通过流量变现的 influencer 和平台，而代价则是被践踏的生态和被异化的游客。

This is not a food story about Krapfen; it is a collective hysteria triggered by the weaponisation of cognitive entry points. At 2,300 metres in the Dolomites, hundreds of people aren't queuing for food, but for an 'entry ticket' validated by social media algorithms.

This is a classic case of weaponised expression. By binding 'creamy pastries' with 'panoramic views,' influencers manufacture a fake 'Optimal Expression': if you stand at this coordinate, hold this product, and take this photo, your identity is confirmed within a certain social or aesthetic hierarchy. In this narrative, the Dolomites are stripped of their essence as nature and degraded into a giant backdrop for selfies.

The most cynical part is the mechanism of complicity: the sight of the queue itself becomes a new visual symbol, enticing more people to join. People subconsciously conspire to prove they are at the centre of a 'trend' by participating in this meaningless line. This is the death of subjectivity; they are no longer individuals exploring nature, but biological plugins fed by algorithms, executing a script of 'see $ ightarrow$ migrate $ ightarrow$ photo $ ightarrow$ upload.'

When mountain associations complain that 'the mountains are not a playground,' they are facing a form of meta-violence. Algorithms define what is 'worth visiting,' thereby monopolising the interpretation of natural aesthetics. The authentic experience of nature is completely diluted by this image-driven fast-food narrative. In this game, the only winners are the influencers and platforms monetising traffic, while the cost is a trampled ecosystem and alienated tourists.
