被数字化抹除的个体与系统的“已知缺陷”The Erased Individual and the System's "Known Issue"

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
当系统将个体简化为数据标签，生物墙就成了行政暴力的漏洞。
When a system reduces individuals to data tags, the biological wall becomes a loophole for administrative violence.

一个典型的 structural violence 样本：英国内政部试图用 digitise 替代复杂的行政管理，结果却在数字化过程中抹除了个体的独立存在。Nidia Webb 被系统误认为她的双胞胎姐妹，这在技术层面上被轻描淡写地称为“known issue”，但在存在性维度上，这是一种严重的身份剥夺。当一个人的合法居留权被绑定到另一个生物体上时，她在系统眼中已经不再是一个独立的主体，而是一个可替代的、模糊的标签。

最令人作呕的是内政部那套典型的 PR 话术：用“1000万人的成功案例”来掩盖数万人的潜在危机。这是一种典型的数字暴力——用统计学上的“大概率正确”来合法化对个体的具体伤害。对于被困在机场的人来说，那 1000 万个成功案例没有任何意义，因为在这一刻，她的 Potential（合法回国的权利）与 Actual（被困机场的现状）之间的差额，就是由系统制造的暴力。

这不仅仅是一个技术 Bug，而是一场关于解释权的博弈。内政部和承运公司在玩一个 blame game，互相推诿责任，而个体在其中被彻底客体化。从 Windrush 丑闻到现在的 eVisa 乱象，逻辑始终如一：国家机器在追求管理效率的 an optimal expression 时，习惯性地牺牲掉那些处于边缘或特殊状态（如双胞胎）的个体。在这种叙事里，个体的身份确证被让渡给了算法的判定，而算法的错误被定义为“罕见情况”。

我们要追问的是，当一个人的存在被数字化之后，谁拥有定义“我是谁”的最终解释权？如果确认身份需要依赖于一个充满 Bug 的数据库，那么所谓的“合法地位”不过是一场随时会被撤销的施舍。

A textbook case of structural violence: the UK Home Office attempted to replace complex administration with digitisation, only to erase individual existence in the process. Nidia Webb was mistaken for her twin sister—a situation dismissed as a "known issue" technically, but an existential theft of identity. When a person's legal status is linked to another biological entity, they cease to be an independent subject in the eyes of the system and become a blurred, replaceable tag.

The most repulsive part is the Home Office's typical PR narrative: using the "10 million successful cases" to mask the potential crisis of tens of thousands. This is digital violence—using statistical "probability" to legitimise concrete harm to individuals. For someone stranded at an airport, those 10 million successes are irrelevant; the gap between her Potential (the right to return home) and her Actual (being stranded) is the violence manufactured by the system.

This is more than a technical bug; it is a struggle over the power of interpretation. The Home Office and carriers engage in a blame game, shifting responsibility while the individual is completely objectified. From the Windrush scandal to the current eVisa chaos, the logic remains the same: in the pursuit of an optimal expression of administrative efficiency, the state habitually sacrifices individuals in marginal or unique states. In this narrative, the confirmation of identity is surrendered to algorithmic judgment, and algorithmic error is defined as a "rare case."

We must ask: once existence is digitised, who holds the final interpretation of "Who am I"? If proving one's identity depends on a buggy database, then so-called "legal status" is nothing more than a precarious favor that can be revoked at any moment.
