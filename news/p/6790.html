抽签不是公平，是换个方式排队A Lottery Is Just a Queue With Better Manners

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要Target 上线 Pokémon 卡牌抽签系统：在指定时间窗口内登记意向，截止后随机抽取，中签者再在期限内自行完成付款，中签商品不可退换。首轮测试的是一款 14.99 美元的 Destined Rivals 三包套装，11 月 4 日截止。此前 Target 常在午夜后上架新品，粉丝熬夜仍常被机器人毫秒级抢空。
The newsTarget has launched a lottery system for Pokémon cards: fans register interest within a set window, winners are drawn at random, and buyers then complete payment before a deadline; winning items cannot be returned or exchanged. The first test is a $14.99 Destined Rivals three-pack blister, closing Nov. 4. Previously, Target dropped new products after midnight on weekdays, and fans who stayed up were routinely beaten by bots buying everything in milliseconds.
抢不到的人和黄牛之间，只多了一道抽签
Between the fan who loses and the scalper, there is now one extra draw.

Target 把「谁手快谁得」换成了「谁被抽中谁得」。别急着叫好。这不是把货分给了更需要它的人，这是把「抢」这个动作从毫秒级挪到了登记窗口里，抢的人一个没少，只是换了个姿势。

原来的机制是什么？工作日午夜后上架，粉丝熬几个小时，机器人毫秒内清空。熬夜是一种成本，而且是单方面压在粉丝身上的成本：你付出睡眠，换来一个几乎为零的中签率。这套机制从来不打算让货到达想要它的人手里，它只负责让货在最短时间内离开货架。粉丝的熬夜不是 bug，是这套流程默认可以消耗的东西。

抽签改了什么？它承认了「先到先得」在机器人面前已经失效，于是把分配权收回到自己手里。这是零售商在给自己止损，不是在给玩家发福利。报道写得很清楚：更长的登记窗口意味着黄牛有更多时间注册更多账号。Target 没有公布任何针对多账号的验证机制，报道里也没有。所谓公平，目前只是一个还没被测试过的假设。

真正被改动的是付款那一步。不自动扣款，中签者有期限自己付，中多件可以只挑一件。这一条对真人有利，对黄牛也无害——黄牛本来就不缺账号，缺的是货。

再看那句「不可退换」。抽中的东西不能退不能换，没抽中的等补货再抽一次。风险全在买家这边，库存压力全在买家这边，Target 只负责开奖。这不像是在解决稀缺，更像是在把稀缺重新包装成一次有仪式感的分配，顺便让每一次落空都显得像运气问题，而不是供给问题。

报道里没有一个粉丝的名字，没有一句玩家对这套系统的说法。整篇新闻的主体是系统本身：窗口多长、什么时候截止、能不能退。熬夜的那群人从头到尾只是被安排的对象。抽签把「你抢不到」翻译成了「你没被抽中」，前者指向机器人和备货，后者指向你自己。这就是它最精巧的地方：它没有减少任何人的损失，只是让损失看起来像是中立的。

Target has replaced "fastest finger wins" with "whoever gets drawn wins." Don't applaud yet. This doesn't put cards in the hands of people who want them more; it moves the grabbing from milliseconds into a registration window. The same people are grabbing, just in a different posture.

The old mechanism: drops after midnight on weekdays, fans staying up for hours, bots clearing the shelf in milliseconds. Staying awake was a cost, and a cost loaded entirely onto fans: you pay in sleep for a near-zero chance to spend money. That system was never designed to get product to the people who wanted it. It was designed to get product off the shelf as fast as possible. The all-nighters weren't a bug; they were fuel the process was happy to burn.

The raffle changes one thing: it admits that first-come-first-served has already lost to bots, so the retailer takes allocation back into its own hands. This is a retailer cutting its own losses, not handing fans a gift. The report says it plainly: a longer entry window means scalpers get more time to spin up more accounts. Target has announced no multi-account verification, and the report describes none. Fairness here is an untested assumption.

What actually changed is the payment step. No auto-charge, winners pay by a deadline, and someone who wins multiple times can pick just one. That helps real people. It also costs scalpers nothing — they were never short on accounts, only on stock.

Then the line about no returns or exchanges. Win it and you're stuck with it; lose and you wait for restock and enter again. The risk sits with the buyer, the inventory pressure sits with the buyer, and Target just runs the drawing. This doesn't look like solving scarcity. It looks like repackaging scarcity as a ceremonial allocation, so that every miss reads as bad luck rather than a supply problem.

Not one fan is named in the report, not one player is quoted about the system. The subject of the story is the system itself: window length, deadline, return policy. The people who stayed up are, start to finish, only the arranged. The raffle translates "you couldn't beat the bots" into "you weren't drawn" — the first points at bots and stock, the second points at you. That's the clever part: it doesn't reduce anyone's loss, it just makes the loss look neutral.
