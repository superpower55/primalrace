福利诈骗是种族叙事的旧配方Welfare Fraud Is the Old Racist Recipe

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要明尼苏达州选情胶着：民主党参议员克洛布彻以7到9个百分点领先，副州长弗拉纳根与共和党对手塔福亚仅差一两个百分点，均在民调误差范围内。共和党抓住「Feeding Our Future」福利诈骗案猛攻，将索马里社区被捕者的照片放进广告，并把总检察长埃里森列为重点目标。报道称，该案由拜登政府司法部于2022年9月首次起诉47名被告，涉案金额2.5亿至3亿美元。
The newsMinnesota's races are tightening: Senator Klobuchar leads by 7 to 9 points, while Lieutenant Governor Flanagan is just one or two points ahead of Republican Michelle Tafoya, both within the margin of error. Republicans are hammering Democrats over the Feeding Our Future welfare fraud case, running ads featuring arrested Somali defendants and targeting Attorney General Keith Ellison. The case was first charged by Biden's Justice Department in September 2022 against 47 defendants, with $250–300 million stolen.
「诈骗」只是包装，里面装的是黑人的脸
「Fraud」 is just the wrapper. Inside is a Black face.

共和党在明尼苏达打的那张牌，不是「诈骗」，是「黑人」。报道写得很直白：诈骗网络的许多主谋是白人，共和党广告里放的却全是索马里社区被捕者的照片，一个白人都没有。这套配方在美国选举里用了快一百年，换过移民、换过福利女王、换过「超级掠食者」，这一次叫 Feeding Our Future。

所以先看清机制在哪一层。直接层是那些被起诉、被判刑的人，他们确实偷了钱，2.5亿到3亿美元，用不存在的餐食喂不存在的孩子。结构层是监管失灵，州审计长办公室其实只管审计城市，跟这种案子没关系，但选民照样把账算到他头上。文化层才是真正在动的地方：把「诈骗」这个词和一张张黑脸焊在一起，让选民在投票站里想起的不是账本，是肤色。元层在最后收尾——谁有权力决定「诈骗」这个故事讲成什么样子，谁就有权力决定这次选举讲什么。

报道里有个细节值得停一下。一位罗斯蒙特的女性说，选民反感的是共和党把诈骗案里的被捕者和「我们的索马里邻居」捆在一起。也就是说，这套种族叙事在明尼苏达没有完全奏效，至少没有像共和党期待的那样奏效。但报道同时说，民主党内部一个资深幕僚承认「他们打到了真问题」，另一个 strategist 直接说「如果民主党输了，就是因为诈骗，没别的」。这两句话放在一起，才是这次选举真正的结构：共和党用种族化的方式讲一个真实存在的治理失败，民主党没有办法把「治理失败」和「种族叙事」拆开回应，只能希望选民自己分得清。

分得清吗？看看弗拉纳根的初选。她以双位数差距击败克雷格，竞选纲领是全民医保和强硬反对 ICE，在双子城之外也赢了。这说明明尼苏达选民不是要更温和的政策，是要政府真的做事。报道里那句「选民不是愤怒于有人偷政府的钱，是感到被冒犯」，比任何民调都准。被冒犯的是「政府该帮人」这个共识被辜负了，不是「黑人拿了我的钱」。

但这里有一个报道没有回答的问题，也是这次选举里最该被追问的地方：Feeding Our Future 案最早是拜登的司法部在 2022 年 9 月起诉的，47 名被告，案子一直在推进。特朗普政府的 Operation Metro Surge 打着「清除诈骗」的旗号进来，结果 ICE 去骚扰拉丁裔居民，邻居出来作证，Renee Good 被杀，Alex Pretti 被杀，六名联邦助理检察官辞职抗议，包括福利诈骗调查的主控检察官。诈骗案的起诉反而慢下来了。也就是说，那个被共和党拿来当竞选武器的「诈骗」，恰恰被共和党自己的表演性执法拖慢了。

这笔账没有人算。共和党不算，因为算了对他们不利；民主党不敢算，因为一算就要承认自己治理失败；选民算不清，因为广告里只有脸，没有时间线。

最后说回明尼苏达本身。报道里写，圣保罗一个街区看到的州旗比作者在双子城住十年看到的都多，咖啡店里插着一本袖珍宪法，门上贴着执法部门不得入内的告示，Good 和 Pretti 的纪念处还有新鲜的花。一个朋友听说作者来写「Metro Surge 的后果」，反问：「后果？它从来没结束。」

这句话是这次选举里最诚实的一句。诈骗案会判完，选举会结束，但那个把「诈骗」和一张黑脸焊在一起的叙事不会结束，它只是等下一次换个名字再出来。而明尼苏达的选民这一次要回答的，不是要不要惩罚诈骗，是能不能在惩罚诈骗的时候不顺手把邻居也惩罚了。

The card Republicans are playing in Minnesota isn't 「fraud.」 It's 「Black.」 The reporting says it plainly: many of the embezzlement ringleaders were white, but the grainy images in Republican ads are all of arrestees from the Somali community, not one white face among them. This recipe has been used in American elections for nearly a century—against immigrants, against welfare queens, against 「superpredators.」 This time it's called Feeding Our Future.

So look at which layer is actually moving. The direct layer is the people charged and sentenced; they did steal money, $250 to $300 million, billing for nonexistent meals for imaginary children. The structural layer is regulatory failure—the state auditor's office audits cities, it has nothing to do with this kind of case, but voters blame him anyway. The cultural layer is where the real action is: welding the word 「fraud」 to a set of Black faces, so that what a voter recalls in the booth isn't a ledger but a complexion. The meta layer closes the loop—whoever gets to decide how the fraud story is told gets to decide what this election is about.

One detail in the piece deserves a pause. A woman from Rosemont says voters were disgusted by Republicans lumping the fraud arrestees together with 「our Somali neighbors.」 So the racial narrative hasn't fully landed in Minnesota, at least not the way the GOP hoped. But the piece also quotes a senior Democratic staffer admitting 「it's a real problem that they're hitting on,」 and a strategist saying flatly, 「If Democrats lose, it's because of the fraud. That's it, nothing else.」 Put those two together and you have the actual structure of this race: Republicans are racializing a real governance failure, and Democrats have no way to answer the governance failure without also answering the racial narrative, so they hope voters will sort it out themselves.

Will they? Look at Flanagan's primary. She beat Craig by double digits on Medicare for All and forceful opposition to ICE, and she swept counties outside the metro too. That result doesn't square with an electorate hungry for moderation. The line in the piece that beats any poll: voters aren't angry that people stole from the government, they are offended. What's offended is the consensus that government is there to help people—not the sense that Black people took their money.

But there's a question the piece leaves open, and it's the one this election should be asking: the Feeding Our Future case was first charged by Biden's Justice Department in September 2022, 47 defendants, and it was moving. Then Operation Metro Surge came in under the pretext of rooting out exactly this kind of fraud, ICE shifted to harassing Latino residents, neighbors came forward as witnesses, Renee Good was murdered, Alex Pretti was murdered, six federal assistant U.S. attorneys resigned in protest, including the lead prosecutor on the welfare fraud investigations. The fraud prosecutions slowed down. The very fraud Republicans are running on was delayed by Republicans' own performative enforcement.

Nobody is counting that cost. Republicans won't, because it cuts against them. Democrats can't, because counting it means admitting governance failed. And voters can't, because the ads show faces, not timelines.

One last thing about Minnesota itself. The piece notes more state flags on a single block in St. Paul than the author saw in ten years living in the Twin Cities, a pocket Constitution propped by the espresso machine, a sign on the door saying law enforcement isn't allowed on the premises, fresh flowers still at the memorials for Good and Pretti. A friend, told the author was in town to cover the 「aftermath」 of Metro Surge, was incredulous: 「Aftermath? It's never ended.」

That's the most honest line in the whole election. The fraud case will finish. The election will end. But the narrative that welds 「fraud」 to a Black face won't end—it will just wait for its next name. What Minnesota voters have to answer this time isn't whether to punish fraud. It's whether they can punish fraud without punishing their neighbors along with it.
