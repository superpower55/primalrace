从十二楼滑下来，山还在那儿They Rappelled Down a Billboard. The Mountain Is Still There.

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
把珠峰搬上广告牌，是为了卖票，不是为了纪念
Turning Everest into a billboard is selling tickets, not honoring the dead.

一条十二层楼高的广告牌，两个人从上面滑下来，宣传一部关于珠峰的纪录片。这不是登山，这是营销。把极限运动变成一场垂直的行为艺术，挂在日落大道上，给551块银幕做路演。

Morrison说广告牌"大得难以置信"。他背上还绑着滑雪板，从直角上滑下来当然不在计划里，因为直角不是山。这个动作本身没有任何风险，他自己也说"一点都不怕"。真正让他怕的东西，电影里拍过了：2022年9月，他的伴侣Hilaree Nelson在Manaslu雪崩中遇难，就在和他一起滑雪下山的途中。

这里有一个值得停下来的地方。Chin说，Nelson死后他们"一直在和Jim保持沟通"，一边悲伤一边拍摄。Vasarhelyi问的是：一个刚刚变得更痛苦的故事，要怎么去尊重它。这个问题很诚实，但答案已经写好了——继续拍，继续完成那个目标，把Nelson的故事"留在世上"。悲伤被记录，被剪辑，被发行，被拿来当首映礼上的问答素材。

Morrison在Q&A上讲，第一次看片时满屋子的焦虑和紧张，看完他抱住Chin，两个人都哭了。他说"我亲身经历过，Jimmy，我全都经历过"。这句话是真的，但它现在被放在威尼斯、特柳赖德、多伦多的放映厅里，被反复播放。一个女人的死，成了这部片子的情感引擎。

这不是在指责Morrison。他失去了伴侣，他用自己唯一会的方式继续活下去——去完成他们一起没做完的事。问题在于，这套叙事被产业接住了。极限运动的浪漫化，是把"冒生命危险"包装成"对人类的贡献"，Morrison自己也是这么说的："推动边界、成就伟业，对人类是好事。"他还拿上太空做类比，说那也不安全，很多人冒着风险帮一个人上去。

这个类比暴露了整件事的结构。有人负责冒险，有人负责拍摄，有人负责发行，有人负责在广告牌下面拍视频发新闻。风险被分配给身体，收益被分配给叙事的所有者。Nelson的身体留在了Manaslu，她的故事现在属于National Geographic。

从十二楼滑下来不危险，危险的是那个把危险卖成浪漫的机器。它需要一个死去的女人，一个悲伤的男人，一座不可能的山，和一块足够大的广告牌。

Two men rappelled down a twelve-story advertisement on the Sunset Strip to promote a documentary about Everest. This is not mountaineering. This is marketing, dressed up as a vertical stunt, hung over Sunset Boulevard to sell 551 screens.

Morrison said the billboard was "huge." He had his skis strapped to his back, and skiing down a right angle, naturally, wasn't in the plan—because a right angle is not a mountain. The descent carried no risk. He said so himself: it wasn't something he was afraid of. The thing that actually frightened him was already filmed: in September 2022, his partner Hilaree Nelson was killed in an avalanche on Manaslu, skiing down the mountainside with him.

There is a place worth stopping here. Chin said they were "in communication with Jim the whole time" as he grieved, documenting throughout. Vasarhelyi asked how you honor a story that just got a lot more painful. That question is honest. The answer was already written: keep filming, finish the goal, keep Nelson's story alive. Grief gets recorded, edited, distributed, and turned into Q&A material at a premiere.

Morrison told the audience that the first time he watched the film, the room was full of anxiety and tension. When it ended, he hugged Chin and both of them cried. He said, "I lived it, Jimmy. I lived all of it." That sentence is true. It is also now playing in Venice, Telluride, and Toronto. A woman's death has become the emotional engine of the film.

This is not an accusation against Morrison. He lost his partner and is continuing in the only way he knows—finishing what they started together. The problem is that the industry caught this narrative and held it. The romanticization of extreme sport packages "risking your life" as "a contribution to humanity." Morrison said it himself: pushing the envelope, striving to do great things, is good for humanity. He compared it to going to space—also unsafe, with many people risking a lot to help one person get there.

That analogy exposes the structure. Some people take the risk. Some people film it. Some people distribute it. Some people shoot a video under the billboard and file a news item. Risk is assigned to bodies; the return is assigned to whoever owns the narrative. Nelson's body stayed on Manaslu. Her story now belongs to National Geographic.

Rappelling down twelve stories is not dangerous. The dangerous thing is the machine that sells danger as romance. It needs a dead woman, a grieving man, an impossible mountain, and a billboard big enough to hold them all.
