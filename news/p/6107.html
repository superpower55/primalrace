把否决权写进法案里Write the Veto Into the Statute

其他 结构层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要南达科他州选民周二将表决一项由共和党州议会推动的措施：若国会下调目前90%的联邦匹配拨款，该州将自动废除医疗补助扩张。四年前，该州选民曾通过公民提案批准扩张。目前已有十个州通过此类「触发法」，犹他州今年三月最新加入。
The newsSouth Dakota voters on Tuesday will decide on a Republican-led ballot measure that would automatically repeal Medicaid expansion if Congress lowers the current 90 percent federal matching rate. Four years ago, voters there approved expansion through a citizen-led measure. Ten states have already passed such trigger laws, most recently Utah in March.
选民投过一次票，议会就把那次投票变成可撤销的。
Voters passed it once; the legislature made that vote revocable.

南达科他州这周要投的那条法案，核心不是医保，是投票权本身。四年前选民自己投票通过了 Medicaid 扩展，现在共和党主导的州议会推一条 trigger law：只要华盛顿把联邦匹配比例降到 90% 以下，扩展自动废除。Adam Searing 说得直白——到时候州里不会有辩论，也不由议员决定。等于把四年前那次公投的结果，预先作废了。

这是结构暴力最标准的一种写法：不动用警察，不通过新的禁令，只是把"如果钱少了就自动关门"这句条件句塞进法条。将来真触发的时候，没有人需要为断掉几十万人的医保负责，因为"不是我们决定的，是联邦先动的"。责任被写进了机制里，从人身上转移走了。

已经有十个州通过了同类法律，最近一个是今年三月的犹他。俄克拉荷马、西弗吉尼亚的共和党议会也在推。这些州几乎全都设定成"联邦匹配资金再降一点就触发"——也就是说，它们等的不是某一次具体的削减，而是任何一个削减。Heritage Foundation 和 Paragon Health Institute 一直在鼓吹砍联邦匹配比例，砍下去，这些闸门就一起落下。

真正值得盯的是这条链子的方向。特朗普 2025 年那份被叫作"One Big Beautiful Bill Act"的预算案里，Medicaid 被削了近一万亿美元，加了工作要求，减了服务和资金。联邦层面 Rick Scott 想把匹配比例压到 90% 以下的修正案没成功，但州议会已经先动了。上面砍不动，就先在下面把门框装好。

Searing 说 trigger law 至少比联邦那套削减"没那么阴"——因为它承认自己在握着方向盘，承认会把人踢出保险。这话对，但也只对一半。承认了又怎样？南达科他的农村人口占比很大，Murphy 说连锁反应会压在医疗机构、医院和整个医疗系统上。承认之后，这些地方照样关门。

选民投过一次票，议会就把那次投票改写成可撤销的。这不是医保政策的争论，是解释权在谁手里的问题：谁有权决定一次公投算不算数。答案已经写在那十个州的法律里了。

The ballot measure South Dakotans vote on this week isn't really about Medicaid. It's about who gets to count a vote. Four years ago, voters passed Medicaid expansion themselves. Now a Republican-led legislature is pushing a trigger law that would automatically repeal expansion the moment Congress drops federal matching payments below 90 percent. Adam Searing puts it plainly: there will be no debate in the state, and it won't be up to the legislators.

This is structural violence in its cleanest form. No police, no new ban—just a conditional clause slipped into statute: if the money falls, the door closes by itself. When it triggers, nobody has to own cutting hundreds of thousands of people off coverage, because it wasn't our decision, the feds moved first. Responsibility gets written into the mechanism and lifted off the people.

Ten states have already passed versions of this, most recently Utah in March. Oklahoma and West Virginia legislatures are pushing too. Nearly all of them are set to fire on any further drop in matching funds—so they aren't waiting for one specific cut, just for any cut. The Heritage Foundation and the Paragon Health Institute have been advocating for exactly that cut, and when it comes, all these gates drop together.

Follow the direction of the chain. Trump's 2025 budget bill—the One Big Beautiful Bill Act—cut nearly $1 trillion from Medicaid, added work requirements, trimmed services and funds. At the federal level, Rick Scott's amendment to push matching below 90 percent failed. So the states moved first. Can't cut from above? Install the doorframe below.

Searing grants that trigger laws are less deceptive than the federal cuts, because at least they admit: we're holding the steering wheel, and we'll cut people off if Washington doesn't pay. True, and only half true. Admitting it doesn't change it. South Dakota has a substantial rural population, and Murphy says the ripple will hit providers, hospitals, whole health systems. The admission arrives, and the hospitals still close.

Voters passed it once; the legislature rewrote that vote as revocable. This isn't a Medicaid argument. It's a question of who holds the power to decide whether a referendum counts. The answer is already sitting in ten states' statutes.
