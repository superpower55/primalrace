两个“救世主”的权力分赃与存在性共谋The Power-Sharing and Existential Complicity of Two 'Saviors'

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-28 § 链接
外交辞令是元暴力的遮羞布，本质是男性权力者的资源互换。
Diplomatic rhetoric is the veil of meta-violence; essentially, it's a resource exchange between masculine power-holders.

这场所谓的“背靠背”会面，是一次典型的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 闭环。Trump, Netanyahu, Zelenskyy，三个男人在椭圆形办公室里扮演着“救世主”与“决策者”的角色。在这种叙事中，乌克兰的领土、加沙的废墟、黎巴嫩的血泊，全部被抽象成了他们博弈桌上的筹码。这就是元暴力的运作方式：将具体的人及其痛苦客体化，转化为权力的数字和外交的 leverage。

Netanyahu 与 Trump 之间那种“complicated”的关系，本质上是两个 Alpha Male 在争夺谁才是那个能定义现实的人。Trump 宣称“没有我就没有以色列”，这是极端的权力表达，试图通过抹除对方的主体性来确立自己的绝对地位。而 Netanyahu 试图通过提供情报来维持 Trump 的参与，这是一种典型的共谋策略——通过喂食对方需要的“权力快感”来换取生存资源。

最讽刺的是，这种所谓的“积极且高效”的闭门会议，其 Potential 与 Actual 之间的差额（Violence）被完全抹去了。在围栏之外，抗议者在呐喊，但在权力中心，他们讨论的是导弹授权和框架协议。对于这些决策者来说，战争不是为了解放，而是一场关于“谁在掌控”的存在性战争。他们通过定义什么是“正义”和“安全”，在文化层 (cultural layer) 武器化叙事，从而让结构性的屠杀和掠夺看起来像是在进行“外交努力”。

当人权被简化为“防火墙”和“拦截弹”的授权时，这就是最彻底的元暴力：解释权被垄断，而真正的受害者——那些被定义为“附带损害”的女性与儿童——在他们的叙事中根本没有表达空间。

This so-called 'back-to-back' meeting is a textbook closed-loop of masculine-centric narrative. Trump, Netanyahu, and Zelenskyy play the roles of 'saviors' and 'deciders' in the Oval Office. In this narrative, Ukrainian territories, the ruins of Gaza, and the blood-soaked soil of Lebanon are all abstracted into bargaining chips on their table. This is how meta-violence operates: objectifying concrete human beings and their suffering, converting them into numbers of power and diplomatic leverage.

The 'complicated' relationship between Netanyahu and Trump is essentially two Alpha Males fighting over who gets to define reality. Trump's claim that 'Without me, there would be no Israel' is an extreme expression of power, attempting to establish absolute dominance by erasing the other's subjectivity. Netanyahu's attempt to keep Trump involved by providing intelligence is a classic strategy of complicity—trading the 'pleasure of power' to secure survival resources.

The most ironic part is that in these 'positive and productive' closed-door meetings, the gap between Potential and Actual (Violence) is completely erased. Outside the fences, protesters are screaming, but at the center of power, they discuss missile licenses and framework deals. For these decision-makers, war is not about liberation, but an existential war over 'who is in control.' By defining 'justice' and 'security,' they weaponize narratives at the cultural layer, making structural slaughter and plunder look like 'diplomatic efforts.'

When human rights are reduced to authorizations for 'firewalls' and 'interceptors,' this is the ultimate meta-violence: the monopoly of interpretation, where the real victims—the women and children defined as 'collateral damage'—have no space for expression in their narrative.
