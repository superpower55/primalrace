他们先杀记者，再杀报馆They Killed the Journalists, Then the Newsroom

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
解释权被没收时，连'战争'两个字都要打星号
When the state owns the story, even the word 'war' becomes asterisks.

《新报》关门的真正含义不是一家报纸停刊，而是一个国家把解释权收归己有之后，最后一个还在外面说话的入口被焊死了。

先看最直白的证据：Sokolov 的声明里，"war"这个词被三个星号代替。不是编辑部胆小，是俄罗斯法律要求所有人把入侵乌克兰叫作"特别军事行动"，否则后果自负。这就是文化暴力最赤裸的样子——它不需要说服你相信什么，它只需要规定你不能说什么。当一套叙事被写进刑法，事实就不再是事实，而是被许可的措辞。

但俄罗斯这套机器比"禁言"更精细。它是一层一层剥的：2022年3月停印纸质版，9月吊销出版许可，网站被封锁，然后记者 Oleg Roldugin 在四月被捕，罪名是"非法访问计算机信息"，目前软禁候审；上个月 Sokolov 自己的家也被搜查，同一桩案子。到了这个月，房东通知他们11月9日前搬离莫斯科市中心的办公室——那间办公室里有 Politkovskaya 的纪念博物馆，展品现在要被拆下来收进仓库。与此同时，所有捐款平台被关闭。

看清楚这个顺序：先拿走法律身份，再拿走钱，再拿走房子，最后拿走记忆。这不是随机的骚扰，这是一套完整的拆除工序。Politkovskaya 2006年在自家公寓楼梯间被枪杀，报社前后有六名记者被暗杀，Muratov 2021年拿诺贝尔和平奖，2022年4月在火车上被泼红漆、视力受损——美国官员说这是俄情报机构下的令。直接暴力杀身体，结构暴力断生路，文化暴力抹掉"这里曾经有人说过话"的痕迹。三层同时运转，缺一层这套系统都不成立。

最值得说的是他们怎么撑了四年半。纸质版停了，就出999份的杂志——因为法律规定印数低于1000份不需要许可。网站被封，就继续在境外运作。版面被要求填官方说法，他们就印空白。这是"制造可能性的艺术"的防守版本：当对方垄断了认知入口，你就在缝隙里凿出一个个小孔，让读者看见孔的形状本身就是censorship的证据。Kostyuchenko 说这是"奇迹"，但她紧接着说，这不只是奇迹，是"每天的选择"——在很害怕的时候仍然去上班。

Prusenkova 说"有时候你的敌人会赢"。这句话是准确的，不是修辞。Violence = Potential − Actual：一个社会本可以有的信息流通、本可以被追究的腐败、本可以被看见的权利侵害，现在全部落空，差额就是这场暴力的体积。这差额不是抽象的，它由六具尸体、一个被泼漆的诺贝尔奖得主、一个软禁中的记者、一间要被清空的纪念室构成。

但有一件事报道里没有答案：谁在共谋。房东是谁？捐款平台是被谁下令关的？Roldugin 案子的检察官是谁？这些名字一个都没出现。不是记者没查，是这套系统运作的方式就是让执行者隐身在"法律""租约""平台合规"这些中性词后面。元暴力最高效的形态，从来不是有人举着枪说"闭嘴"，而是一整套看起来像正常行政流程的东西，把一间开了三十年的编辑部请出大门。

Sokolov 写的是"一个国家和社会正在被系统性地碾碎"。碾碎之后剩下的是什么？是一整个民族只能用星号谈论正在发生的战争，是年轻人从小读到的是被删改过的历史，是 Politkovskaya 的名字从她自己的办公室里被搬走。Prusenkova 说同事们大概还会想办法继续做下去，"毕竟我们不会干别的"。这句话听着像倔强，其实是这个系统还没碾到的那一小块地方——它还在，但它能撑多久，取决于还有没有人记得那三个星号原本该是什么词。

Novaya Gazeta closing is not a newspaper going out of business. It is the last open cognitive entry point in a country being welded shut.

Start with the most literal evidence: in Sokolov's statement, the word "war" is replaced by three asterisks. Not because the editors are timid, but because Russian law requires everyone to call the invasion of Ukraine a "special military operation" or face the consequences. This is cultural violence in its purest form — it does not need to convince you of anything, it only needs to dictate what you cannot say. Once a narrative is written into criminal code, facts stop being facts and become permitted phrasing.

But the Russian machine is more precise than a gag order. It peels layer by layer: print edition stopped in March 2022, publishing license revoked that September, website blocked, then journalist Oleg Roldugin arrested in April on a charge of "unlawful access to computerised information" — he is under house arrest awaiting trial — and last month Sokolov's own home was searched as part of the same case. This month the landlords wrote to say staff must vacate their long-standing office in central Moscow by 9 November. That office houses a memorial museum to Politkovskaya. The exhibits will be taken down and put into storage. Meanwhile every donation platform has been shut down.

Look at the order: take the legal identity, then the money, then the building, then the memory. This is not random harassment. It is a complete demolition sequence. Politkovskaya was gunned down in her own stairwell in 2006; six Novaya journalists have been assassinated over the years; Muratov won the Nobel peace prize in 2021 and was doused with red paint on a train in April 2022, damaging his eyesight — US officials say Russian intelligence ordered it. Direct violence kills the body, structural violence cuts the lifeline, cultural violence erases the trace that someone once spoke here. All three layers run at once. Remove any one and the system doesn't hold.

What deserves the most attention is how they lasted four and a half years. Print was banned, so they published magazines with a print run of 999 copies — because the law exempts publications under 1,000. The website was blocked, so they kept running it from outside. When ordered to fill the page with official language, they printed blank white spaces instead. This is the defensive version of the art of manufacturing possibilities: when the other side monopolises the cognitive entry point, you carve small holes in the seams, and the shape of the hole itself becomes evidence of censorship.

Kostyuchenko called it a miracle, then immediately corrected herself: not just a miracle, but the daily choice of people who kept going to work even when it was very scary.

Prusenkova said: "Sometimes it happens that your enemies win." That is accurate, not rhetorical. Violence = Potential − Actual. The information that could have circulated, the corruption that could have been exposed, the rights abuses that could have been seen — all of it now unrealised, and the gap is the volume of this violence. The gap is not abstract. It is made of six bodies, a Nobel laureate with paint in his eyes, a journalist under house arrest, and a memorial room about to be emptied into storage boxes.

One thing the report does not answer: who is complicit. Who is the landlord? Who ordered the donation platforms shut? Who is the prosecutor on Roldugin's case? Not one name appears. Not because the reporters didn't look, but because this system operates by letting its enforcers hide behind neutral words — "law," "lease," "platform compliance." Meta-violence at its most efficient never looks like a man with a gun saying shut up. It looks like a normal administrative process escorting a thirty-year-old newsroom out the door.

Sokolov wrote that a country and a society are being systematically crushed. What remains after the crushing? A nation that can only discuss an ongoing war in asterisks. Young people raised on edited history. Politkovskaya's name removed from her own office. Prusenkova says her colleagues will probably find some way to keep working — "after all, we don't know how to do anything else." That sounds like defiance. It is actually the one patch this system has not yet crushed. It is still there. How long it stays depends on whether anyone still remembers what those three asterisks were supposed to spell.
