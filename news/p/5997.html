换把尺子，碳就埋住了Change the Ruler, Bury the Carbon

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 ProPublica ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
证据不够，就投票制造证据。
When evidence runs out, vote on it and call the vote evidence.

2005年奥维耶多那场会，科学家们做的事说出来很简单：证据不够，那就投票。匿名问卷，问一圈专家“你估计地底下能存多少、能存多久”，然后把结果写成“99% 的 CO2 可以在地下留一千年”。投完票，问卷烧掉，正式报告里一个字不提。这不是评估文献，这是用专家的直觉替文献填空，再把填空的结果印成 431 页的权威。

IPCC 的规矩是评估已有研究，不是自己做研究。所以这一手等于把“我们不知道”重新命名为“我们一致认为”。加尔通的公式在这里被倒过来用：先把 actual（现有证据）藏起来，再把 potential（专家猜的上限）写成 actual，差额就被抹平了。抹平差额的那支笔，比任何一台捕集设备都更有效地改变了气候政策。

谁在推动这场投票，报道写得很清楚：沙特阿拉伯和其他石油出口国向联合国施压要求写这份报告，壳牌、雪佛龙、沙特阿美的研究人员坐进了写作组。石油公司要的从来不是减排，是“继续烧”的许可证。Occidental 的 CEO 在休斯敦说得很直白，直接空气捕集“将帮助我们的行业延续下去”。报告给了他们这句话的学术背书。

然后现实开始还账。In Salah 的 CO2 从一英里外的废井里呼啸而出，压力把裂缝顶穿了 3000 英尺泥岩层的四分之一，2012 年项目悄悄关停。Snøhvit 把容量高估到只剩三年就得搬家。Gorgon 号称世界最大，2025 财年只注入了收集量的四分之一，剩下的排进大气，成了澳洲大陆上最大的工业碳污染源。从 1996 年至今，全球只建成 12 个大型地质封存项目，加起来埋的碳不如一座电厂一年排的。

Benson 把这些叫“探测的胜利”。In Salah 是技术失败、监测成功——这话不算全错，但监测成功的意思是：气体去了模型说它不会去的地方。模型错了，而模型正是那份报告卖出去的东西。

这里最值得看的不是某个人有没有被收买，报道也明说没有证据显示她篡改结论。值得看的是共谋的结构：ExxonMobil 出 1 亿美元给斯坦福，资助方对研究项目有否决权；Benson 后来当上这个中心的主任，再后来进白宫，再后来在加州空气资源委员会的听证会上，又一次引用 2005 年那句“99% 一千年”。一个被反复引用的数字，源头是一场烧掉的问卷。资助不规定结论，资助规定的是哪些问题值得问、哪些答案值得重复。

后果是结构性的，而且不可逆。气候模型把 CCS 当成既定前提，于是“如何减少排放”变成了“如何事后清除”；巴黎协定的 1.5 度、各国的 net zero 承诺、拜登的通胀削减法案里的补贴，全部建立在这个前提上。到了今天，联合国说没有碳清除就到不了 1.5 度——一个从未被验证的技术，现在成了唯一的退路。这才是武器化的完整闭环：先制造“可行”，再让世界依赖它，最后让质疑它等于放弃气候目标。

加州今年五月开注的那个项目，两个月注入量是宣称产能的 7%，公司已经在申请另外八个。CARB 设定的目标是 2045 年每年埋一亿吨，超过人类历史上所有地质封存的总和。Benson 被问到能不能做到，第一次说“非常困难”，第二次说“如果我们能挥一挥魔杖”。

魔杖没有。烧掉的那份问卷倒是还在起作用。

What happened in Oviedo in 2005 is easy to state: the evidence was thin, so the scientists took a vote. An anonymous survey of experts' best guesses about how much CO2 the planet could hold and for how long, and out of it came the sentence that 99% would likely stay underground for a thousand years. Then they burned the survey and never mentioned it in the official report. That is not assessing the literature. That is filling the literature's blanks with expert intuition and printing the blanks as 431 pages of authority.

The IPCC's job is to assess existing research, not conduct new research. So this move renamed "we don't know" as "we agree." Galtung's formula runs in reverse here: hide the actual (the evidence that exists), write the potential (the experts' upper guess) as the actual, and the gap disappears. The pen that erased that gap did more for climate policy than any capture unit ever built.

Who pushed the vote is in the reporting: Saudi Arabia and other oil exporters pressured the U.N. to write the report, and researchers from Shell, Chevron and Saudi Aramco sat in the author group. The industry never wanted abatement. It wanted a license to keep burning. Occidental's CEO said it plainly in Houston: direct air capture "is going to be the technology that helps to preserve our industry over time." The report gave that sentence an academic spine.

Then reality sent the bill. At In Salah, CO2 whistled out of an abandoned well nearly a mile away, pressure drove cracks a quarter of the way up a 3,000-foot mudstone seal, and the project was quietly scrapped in 2012. Snøhvit overestimated its capacity so badly it had to relocate after three years. Gorgon, billed as the world's largest, injected a quarter of the CO2 it collected in the year ending 2025 and vented the rest, becoming the largest single industrial carbon source on the continent. Since 1996, just 12 large-scale storage projects have been built worldwide; together they bury less than one power plant emits in a year.

Benson calls In Salah a technical failure but a triumph of detection. That is not entirely wrong, but detection succeeding means the gas went where the model said it could not. The model was wrong, and the model was the product.

The interesting question is not whether one person was bought; the reporting says there is no evidence she skewed her findings. The interesting question is the structure of complicity. ExxonMobil put up $100 million for Stanford's center, and sponsors held veto power over which projects got funded. Benson later ran that center, later went to the White House, and later, testifying before California's air board, cited the same line again: 99% over a thousand years. A number repeated for two decades, sourced to a survey that was burned. Funding does not dictate conclusions. It dictates which questions get asked and which answers get repeated.

The consequences are structural and effectively irreversible. Climate models folded CCS in as a premise, so "how do we cut emissions" became "how do we clean up afterward." The Paris 1.5 target, national net-zero pledges, the subsidies in the Inflation Reduction Act — all rest on that premise. Today the U.N. says 1.5 is unreachable without carbon removal. An unproven technology has become the only exit. That is the full weaponization loop: manufacture feasibility, make the world depend on it, then make doubting it equal abandoning the climate goal.

California's project began injecting this May and has run at 7% of advertised capacity, while the company applies for eight more. The state's target is 100 million tons a year by 2045, more than everything ever stored geologically in human history. Asked whether that was possible, Benson said first that it was very challenging, and second, "if we could wave a wand."

There is no wand. The burned survey is still doing its work.
