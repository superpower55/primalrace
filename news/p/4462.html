被剥夺的子宫与被收割的种族Stolen Wombs and Harvested Races

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
殖民暴力的蓝图始于对生育力的掠夺与对母职的强行定义。
The blueprint of colonial violence begins with the plunder of fertility and the forced definition of motherhood.

《Little Bird》这部剧触碰的是加拿大历史上最阴森的 structural violence：Sixties Scoop。所谓的“儿童福利”，本质上是一场国家机器发起的、针对原住民的种族清洗。当国家决定谁有资格成为“母亲”，谁的孩子必须被“拯救”并移交给白人家庭时，它在执行一种极端的 meta violence —— 剥夺原住民女性作为生育主体的定义权。

这种暴力逻辑与原初种族（Primal Race）的形成完全同构。殖民者首先将原住民女性的身体客体化，将其生育力视为可以被重新分配的资源，而非主体性的延伸。通过强制分离孩子，国家实际上是在物理层面上切断一个种族的生物墙与文化传承。这不是什么“福利”，而是一场以文明之名行使的、针对子宫的殖民战争。

有趣的是，同一份电视指南里还出现了《The Great British Sewing Bee》在讨论麦当娜的锥形胸罩。这种极端的对比恰恰揭示了文化层面的 weaponization：在西方主流叙事中，女性身体的“性化”被包装成时尚与自由的表达，而原住民女性身体的“剥夺”则被掩盖在历史的尘埃里。前者是消费主义的 scam，后者是结构性暴力的血泪。

好新闻是这种历史被拍成了剧并引发愤怒，但真正的胜利不在于观众的 seethe with anger，而在于那些被强行定义的“养父母”与“被拯救者”能否在权力结构中倒戈，承认这场名为福利的抢劫。

The drama *Little Bird* touches upon the most sinister structural violence in Canadian history: the Sixties Scoop. What was termed "child welfare" was, in essence, a racial cleansing orchestrated by the state. When the state decides who is fit to be a "mother" and which children must be "rescued" and transferred to white families, it is executing a form of meta violence — stripping Indigenous women of their agency over their own reproductive subjectivity.

This logic is perfectly isomorphic to the constitution of the Primal Race. Colonizers first objectified the bodies of Indigenous women, treating fertility as a resource to be redistributed rather than an extension of the self. By forcibly removing children, the state physically severed the biological wall and cultural transmission of an entire race. This was never "welfare"; it was a colonial war waged against the womb under the guise of civilization.

It is telling that the same TV guide features *The Great British Sewing Bee* discussing Madonna's cone bra. This contrast reveals the weaponization of cultural narratives: while the "sexualization" of the female body is packaged as fashion and liberation in mainstream Western discourse, the "deprivation" of Indigenous female bodies is buried in history. The former is a consumerist scam; the latter is structural violence.

It is a small victory that this history is dramatized to provoke anger, but true victory lies not in the audience's outrage, but in whether the "adoptive parents" and "rescued" children within the power structure can defect and acknowledge this robbery disguised as welfare.
