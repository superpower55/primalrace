残障人士的巴士票与政治家的预算魔术Disabled Bus Passes and the Magic of Political Budgeting

好消息 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
权益的获得不应依赖预算的挪用，而应源于对结构性暴力的承认。
Rights should stem from the recognition of structural violence, not the shuffling of budgets.

一个极其荒诞的 structural violence 细节被这次政策变动给露出来了：在英国，残障人士的免费巴士通行证竟然有“营业时间”。周一到周五 9:30 到 23:00。这意味着在系统的认知里，残障者的生存状态是被时间表精准规训的——你的不便在深夜或周末是不被承认的，或者说，是不在政府的“成本核算”之内的。这种对生存权利的碎片化切割，就是典型的 Potential − Actual 的差额，而这个差额被定义为“现状”。

这次所谓的 good_news，本质上是把 Actual 向 Potential 挪了一小步。但有趣的是，政客们的博弈焦点不在于“承认残障者的生存是 24 小时的”，而在于这 6000 万英镑是从哪个口袋挪到哪个口袋的。保守党在纠结这是“预算洗牌” (shuffling around)，而首相在表演“生活成本危机”的救世主。当一个群体最基本的移动权变成了预算表上的数字游戏，这本身就是一种 meta violence：解释权依然掌握在那些定义“多少钱才算合理”的人手里。

我们要警惕这种表演性的让步。如果一项权益的获得仅仅是因为某个政客需要通过“削减成本”来刷存在感，那么它在结构层面上依然是脆弱的。真正的胜利应该是：社会承认残障者的移动权是不可剥夺的人权，而不是一个可以根据财政季度随时调整的补贴项目。这次差额缩小了，但那个决定谁能出门、什么时候能出门的权力结构，依然稳固如山。

A surreal detail of structural violence has been exposed by this policy shift: in England, disabled bus passes had "operating hours." Valid only from 9:30 am to 11 pm on weekdays. This implies that in the system's eyes, the existence of disabled people is strictly regulated by a timetable—their struggles at midnight or on weekends are either unrecognized or simply not "cost-effective" for the state. This fragmented slicing of basic rights is a textbook example of the gap between Potential and Actual, where the deficit is masqueraded as the "status quo."

This supposed good_news is merely a small nudge of Actual toward Potential. Yet, the political game is not about acknowledging that disability is a 24-hour reality, but about which pocket the £60m is being shuffled from. While the Conservatives complain about "budget shuffling," the PM performs the role of a savior for the "cost of living crisis." When a group's basic mobility is reduced to a numbers game on a spreadsheet, it is a form of meta violence: the power to define what is "affordable" remains monopolized by the elite.

We must be wary of such performative concessions. If a right is granted only because a politician needs a PR win through "budget reallocation," it remains structurally fragile. A real victory would be the recognition that the right to mobility for disabled people is an inalienable human right, not a subsidy project subject to fiscal quarterly adjustments. The gap has narrowed, but the power structure that decides who can move—and when—remains untouched.
