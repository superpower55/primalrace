用自然之声掩盖的结构性悲剧Natural Sounds as a Mask for Structural Tragedy

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-20 § 链接
当艺术试图用“自然的魔力”对冲悲剧时，它在消解暴力的真实性。
When art uses 'natural magic' to offset tragedy, it erases the authenticity of violence.

这篇文章读起来像是一次典型的中产阶级审美远足：脱掉鞋子，在森林里采集苔藓和树干的声音，试图用所谓“原始的魔力” (Rough magic) 来构建一个剧场空间。这种对“自然”的迷恋，本质上是一种文化层面的逃避。作曲家 Dom Coyote 坦言，他希望用音乐带来的“惊奇感” (wonder) 来对冲故事中“绝对的悲剧” (absolute tragedy)。

这是一个危险的信号。在《Lanny》的故事里，一个男孩失踪，一个村庄在恐惧中自我撕裂，一个家庭分崩离析。这不是什么森林神话，而是一场典型的结构性暴力 (structural violence) 演习：当一个群体面对未知威胁时，他们会迅速制造一个“替罪羊” (scapegoat) 来承接所有愤怒，从而维持内部脆弱的秩序。这种机制在现实世界中被无数次复制——无论是种族清洗还是对边缘群体的污名化。

当艺术创作者试图用“大自然的律动”或“非训练的嗓音”来包装这种残酷时，他们实际上是在进行一种叙事武器化。他们把一个关于权力、恐惧和排挤的政治寓言，转化为一种关于“自然与人类关系”的审美体验。这种做法将观众的注意力从“为什么村庄会如此轻易地转向暴力”转移到了“森林的声音是否像邦戈鼓”这种无关痛痒的感官讨论上。

真正的“公正表达”不应该是用美学去对冲悲剧，而应该是让悲剧在剧场中保持其刺人的原样。如果音乐的作用是让观众感到“惊奇”从而缓解痛苦，那么这种艺术就成了共谋者。它在潜意识里告诉我们：只要包装得足够浪漫，结构性的残酷也可以被消费成一种“神圣的体验”。

This piece reads like a typical middle-class aesthetic excursion: taking off shoes, harvesting sounds from moss and trunks, and attempting to construct a theatrical space through so-called 'Rough magic'. This obsession with 'nature' is, in essence, a cultural escape.

Composer Dom Coyote admits he hopes the 'wonder' produced by the music will 'counteract the absolute tragedy' of the tale. This is a red flag. In the story of Lanny, a boy vanishes, a village tears itself apart in fear, and a family is destroyed. This is not a forest myth; it is a classic exercise in structural violence: when a group faces an unknown threat, they rapidly manufacture a 'scapegoat' to absorb all the rage and maintain a fragile internal order. This mechanism is replicated countless times in reality, from ethnic cleansing to the stigmatization of marginalized groups.

When creators attempt to wrap this cruelty in 'natural rhythms' or 'untrained voices', they are engaging in the weaponisation of expression. They transform a political allegory about power, fear, and exclusion into an aesthetic experience of 'the relationship between nature and humans'. This shifts the audience's focus from 'why the village turned to violence so easily' to trivial sensory debates over whether a tree sounds like a bongo drum.

Just Expressions should not use aesthetics to offset tragedy; they should allow the tragedy to remain piercingly raw. If the role of music is to provide 'wonder' to alleviate pain, the art becomes a complicity. It subconsciously tells us that as long as cruelty is packaged romantically, structural violence can be consumed as a 'sacred experience'.
