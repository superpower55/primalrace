所谓的“社交技巧”，不过是权力等级的礼貌翻译Social 'Tactics' are Just Polite Translations of Power Hierarchies

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
表达空间的争夺从来不是礼貌问题，而是权力博弈。
The struggle for expression space is never about politeness; it is about power.

这篇文章把 Forbes 提供的“高效沟通技巧”当成某种社交救星，试图教人们如何在生活中体面地让对方 STFU。但这种讨论本身就陷入了一个巨大的 scam：它把对表达空间的霸凌，包装成了“效率”和“社交艺术”。

在加尔通的暴力三角里，这属于典型的 cultural violence。当一个所谓的“领导者”或“社交掌控者”决定谁该闭嘴、谁的 tangent（离题）是不必要的、谁的细节是 superfluous（多余的）时，他实际上在行使一种定义权。谁拥有定义“什么是冗余信息”的权力，谁就掌控了这场博弈的定价权。

Forbes 提供的这三套话术，本质上是权力等级的礼貌翻译。在办公室里，这种权力来自 structural violence（职位等级）；而在生活中，这种“技巧”则变成了某种隐形的阶级筛选。那些能够熟练运用这些话术将他人“引导”出对话空间的人，实际上在通过操纵认知入口来确立自己的主体性，而代价是对方主体性的暂时死亡。

最讽刺的是，这种“技巧”在不同性别间的运作逻辑完全不同。当一个男性使用这些技巧时，通常被视为“果断”或“掌控力”；而当女性尝试同样的操作时，往往会被贴上“不友好”或“刻薄”的标签。这种双标正是 meta violence（元暴力）的体现——男性中心叙事定义了什么是“高效的领导力”，而将女性的同类行为定义为“情绪化”。

所谓的“公正表达”应该是双方真.最优解的碰撞，而不是一方利用话术技巧，在不让对方感到尴尬的前提下，悄悄地剥夺对方的表达权。

This article treats Forbes' 'efficient communication tips' as a social savior, attempting to teach people how to politely tell others to STFU. But this discussion is a scam: it packages the bullying of expression space as 'efficiency' and 'social art.'

In Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is classic cultural violence. When a so-called 'leader' or 'social controller' decides who should shut up, whose tangents are unnecessary, or whose details are superfluous, they are exercising the power of definition. Whoever defines what constitutes 'redundant information' controls the pricing power of the game.

These three phrases from Forbes are essentially polite translations of power hierarchies. In the office, this power stems from structural violence (job rank); in daily life, these 'tactics' become a form of invisible class screening. Those who skillfully use these phrases to 'route' others out of the conversation are asserting their subjectivity by manipulating the cognitive entry point, at the cost of the other person's temporary existential death.

The irony is that these tactics operate differently across genders. When a man uses them, he is seen as 'decisive' or 'in control'; when a woman does the same, she is often labeled 'unfriendly' or 'sharp.' This double standard is a manifestation of meta violence—the masculine-centric narrative defines what 'efficient leadership' looks like, while defining the same behavior in women as 'emotional.'

Just Expressions should be a collision of both parties' true optimal expressions, not one party using tactical maneuvers to quietly strip away the other's right to express, all while ensuring the victim doesn't feel 'embarrassed.'
