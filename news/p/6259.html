凌晨四点半的清场The 4:30am Sweep

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要葡萄牙里斯本警方周日凌晨进入罗西奥广场，驱散住房抗议者的帐篷营地，此前一天数千人游行反对一项租赁法案。抗议者搭起50多顶帐篷，警方称其非法并发出三次警告。上周类似行动中，里斯本有25人被捕、波尔图6人被捕。
The newsPortuguese police entered Lisbon's Rossio Square early Sunday to clear a tent camp of housing protesters, a day after thousands marched against a rental bill. Protesters had pitched more than 50 tents, which police called illegal. The previous week's operations led to 25 arrests in Lisbon and six in Porto.
房租翻倍是市场，帐篷越界是违法
Doubled rents are the market; a tent is illegal.

葡萄牙警察周日凌晨四点半进入里斯本罗西奥广场，用盾牌和警棍清走了五十多顶帐篷。动手之前，指挥官 Iuri Rodrigues 给了三次警告，理由是帐篷“非法搭建”，抵抗者可能面临违抗罪指控。一个星期的间隔，同样的广场，同样的动作：上周日里斯本抓了 25 人、波尔图抓了 6 人，住房组织 Casa para Viver 说两轮行动共 60 人受伤，警方的定性是“适度”。

“非法”这个词在这里用得很省事。帐篷是非法的，因为它是被搭起来的；房价从 2017 年翻了一倍多、新租约租金几乎翻倍、全国缺 30 万套住房——这些都不叫非法，叫市场。葡萄牙央行自己算出来的那个缺口，等于整个里斯本的住房存量，它不是被谁搭出来的，所以不需要在凌晨四点半被拆掉。加尔通那个公式在这里很好用：Violence = Potential − Actual，30 万套住房的差额就是暴力，只不过它慢、它弥散、它被写成统计数字，所以不算事件；而五十顶帐篷是事件，有画面，有狗，有警棍，可以被清走。

真正该看清的是这轮清场在为什么服务。周六有几千人上街，要求政府撤回一份租房法案——那份法案让驱逐更容易，并取消新租约的租金上限。周日凌晨帐篷就被拆了。中间隔了不到一天。这不是治安问题的时间表，这是立法的时间表。法案要过，广场上就不能有人。法律在这里不是中立的裁判，它是被争夺的产物本身：谁能让“非法”这个词落在帐篷上而不是落在法案上，谁就赢了一半。

议员 Pedro Delgado Alves 说广场是平静的，没有任何理由动用“武力、身体暴力或警棍冲锋”。他说得对，但他说的仍然是程序问题—— peaceful、justified、proportionate，全是程序词。程序词的问题是它默认了清场这件事本身可以成立，只争论手法。而 30 万套住房的缺口不会因为一次“适度”或“不适度”的清场改变一个数字。

值得注意的是报道里谁没有名字。受伤的 60 人没有名字，被逮捕的 31 人没有名字，凌晨四点半被狗和盾牌围住的几百人没有名字。有名字的是警官、是议员、是组织。这不是记者的疏忽，这是这类事件的常规结构：被清走的人以人数出现，执行清场的人以职务出现。

最后一点，也是最容易被放过的一点：这场抗议针对的法案还没通过。也就是说，凌晨四点半的警棍不是打在已经生效的法律上，是打在一个还没被写进法条、但已经需要被保护的东西上。帐篷可以拆，租金上限拆不掉之后，人们还得住在那里。

At about 4:30am on Sunday, Portuguese police moved into Lisbon's Rossio Square with shields and batons and cleared more than fifty tents. Commander Iuri Rodrigues gave three warnings first: the tents were erected illegally, and anyone resisting removal could face disobedience charges. Same square, one week apart, same operation: 25 arrests in Lisbon and six in Porto the previous Sunday, 60 people injured across both operations according to the housing group Casa para Viver, and the police's own word for it is "proportionate."

"Illegal" is doing a lot of quiet work here. The tents are illegal because they were put up. House prices more than doubling since 2017, rents on new leases nearly doubling, a nationwide shortage of 300,000 homes — none of that is illegal. That's the market. The Bank of Portugal's own estimate of the gap equals Lisbon's entire housing stock, and nobody stormed it at dawn, because it wasn't erected by anyone.

Galtung's formula fits: Violence = Potential − Actual. The 300,000-home gap is violence. It's just slow, diffuse, and written as a statistic, so it doesn't count as an event. Fifty tents are an event. They have images, dogs, batons. They can be cleared.

What this sweep is actually serving matters more than how it was conducted. Thousands marched on Saturday demanding the government withdraw a rental bill that would make evictions easier and remove rent caps on new leases. The tents came down before dawn on Sunday. Less than a day. That isn't a public-order timetable. That's a legislative one. The bill has to pass, so the square has to be empty. Law here isn't a neutral referee; it's the thing being fought over. Whoever gets the word "illegal" to land on the tents instead of the bill wins half the fight.

Lawmaker Pedro Delgado Alves said the square was peaceful and nothing justified "a police intervention involving force, physical violence or baton charges." He's right, and he's still arguing procedure — peaceful, justified, proportionate. Procedural language concedes that the clearing itself was legitimate and only disputes the technique. A 300,000-home gap doesn't move by one unit whether the sweep was proportionate or not.

Notice who has no name in this report. The 60 injured have no names. The 31 arrested have no names. The hundreds dispersed at 4:30am have no names. The names belong to the commander, the lawmaker, the organization. That's not a reporter's oversight; it's the standard architecture of this kind of story. The cleared appear as a number. The clearers appear as a rank.

And the part easiest to let slide: the bill hasn't passed yet. The batons at 4:30am weren't enforcing a law already on the books. They were protecting something that hasn't been written into statute yet but already needs defending. Tents can be dismantled. Once the rent caps are gone, people still have to live there.
