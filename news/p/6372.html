书单里的女人都在等一个男人Every Woman on This List Is Waiting for a Man

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
十本书，一半女主角的终点是跟男人谈恋爱
Ten books, and half the heroines end up in a romance they didn't choose.

Polygon 这份十一月科幻奇幻书单，我数了一下：十本书里，至少四本的核心设定是把一个女人放进一段她没选的婚姻或关系里，然后让故事从那里开始。

第八本最直白。私生女 Mirelle 被当作保护边境的筹码嫁出去，婚后被关进自己城堡的地牢。这是第一章的素材：她的表达——出身、性别、身体——在故事开始前就已经被别人定价完毕。她唯一能指望的，是同在地牢里的一个神秘男人。一个被囚禁的女人，出路是另一个男人，这个叙事模板比任何一本奇幻小说都古老。

第四本，精灵公主在洛杉矶当私家侦探，查的是针对好莱坞女演员的魔法袭击。听起来像女人在查案，但简介里紧接着出现的是：她得解开一个诅咒，对象是“下一任 Seelie 宫廷的国王”。女演员被袭击是案情，国王的诅咒是主线。

第六本把 Mad Max 和 Snowpiercer 套上奇幻皮，女主角 Lirien 二十年前用魔法毁了世界，现在要赎罪。赎罪叙事给女性角色的定价方式是：她造成的灾难必须由她亲手偿还，而男性角色造成的灾难通常叫“成长”。

第九本最值得说。TJ Klune 的书，主角 Mike 在一个田园小镇里爱他的书店工作，给当地女性俱乐部主持读书讨论，然后和餐厅服务员 Sean 的关系“准备进入下一阶段”。这个设定里，女性俱乐部是背景板，是 Mike 生活里一个温馨的装饰。故事真正的钩子是 Mike 开始出现暴力记忆，小镇变得诡异。女人的存在是为了让男人的世界看起来正常、温暖、值得被拯救。

这不是在说这些书都坏。类型小说的读者要的是逃逸，而逃逸的路线是被市场验证过的。问题在于，验证过的路线里，女性的逃逸方向几乎总是通向一段关系，而男性的逃逸方向通向一个世界。

书单本身也是一次表达。Polygon 选这十本，排序、措辞、卖点，全都在告诉读者什么样的故事值得期待。第十本里那个被绑架、回来失去二十四小时记忆的初级军官，性别在简介里没写——这种时候没写，通常就是男的。

十本书里有一本让我停了一下：第五本，Tanya Huff 写的魔法高中，主角 Bex Olynyk 用 they 指代。老师知道，一个学生在开学第一天就展现出超常力量，意味着反派一定会出现。这个故事里没有等待被拯救的女人，只有一个得在反派来之前把课上完的学校。它排在第五，不前不后。

I counted the ten books on Polygon's November sci-fi and fantasy list. At least four of them start by putting a woman inside a marriage or a relationship she did not choose, and then let the story run from there.

Number eight is the plainest. Mirelle, a king's bastard daughter, is used as a bargaining chip to protect the border, wedded to a conqueror, and imprisoned in the dungeon of her own castle. This is chapter-one material: her expression — birth, sex, body — was priced by other people before the story began. Her only hope is a mysterious man locked in the same dungeon. A captive woman whose exit is another man. That template is older than any fantasy novel on this list.

Number four: a faerie princess working as a PI in Los Angeles, hunting whoever is attacking up-and-coming actresses. Sounds like a woman running an investigation. But the next sentence is about breaking a curse on the man meant to be the next king of the Seelie Court. The attacked actresses are the case; the king's curse is the plot.

Number six puts a fantasy skin on Mad Max and Snowpiercer. Lirien broke the world with magic twenty years ago and now seeks redemption by waging war against the night. Redemption narratives price women this way: the disaster she caused must be repaid by her own hand. When a male character causes a disaster, we call it an arc.

Number nine deserves a closer look. TJ Klune's book: Mike loves his bookstore job in an idyllic town, runs literature discussions for the local women's club, and is about to take his relationship with Sean, a diner waiter, to the next level. The women's club is set dressing, a warm prop in Mike's life. The real hook is that Mike starts having violent memories and the town turns strange. Women exist here to make a man's world look normal, warm, worth saving.

I am not saying these books are bad. Genre readers want escape, and the escape routes are the ones the market has already validated. The problem is that the validated routes for women almost always lead into a relationship, while the validated routes for men lead into a world.

The list itself is an act of expression. Polygon chose these ten, ordered them, framed them, and told readers what kind of story is worth anticipating. In number ten, the junior officer who is abducted and returned with no memory of twenty-four hours — the blurb does not give a gender. When it does not, it is usually a man.

One book made me pause: number five, Tanya Huff's magic high school, where the protagonist Bex Olynyk is referred to as they. The teachers know that a student who shows up on day one with surprising power means a villain is bound to follow. No woman waiting to be rescued here — just a school that has to finish its lessons before the confrontation arrives. It sits at number five. Not first, not last.
