从“拥有街道”到被管理：狂欢节的驯化与反驯化From 'Owning the Streets' to Being Managed: The Domestication of Carnival

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
当自发秩序被管理逻辑取代，表达就从存在的确证变成了表演的仪式。
When spontaneous order is replaced by management logic, expression shifts from an affirmation of existence to a ritual of performance.

这篇回忆录揭示了一个典型的 Violence Triangle 演变过程。早期的 Notting Hill Carnival 是一个纯粹的 Expression 场域，加勒比移民通过在伦敦街道上重建家乡的活力，完成了一次身份的确证。对于后 Windrush 一代来说，Sound Systems 的出现更是将这种表达从“复刻传统”升级为“定义当下”——他们不再需要扮演父母辈的移民，而是在伦敦的街头通过 Reggae 和 Soul 寻找自己的真.最优解表达。

这种“拥有街道”的感觉，本质上是 Actual 状态向 Potential 状态的一次剧烈逼近。在那个警察骚扰和种族歧视是 structural violence 常态的年代，这种短期的、自发的“空间占领”是对元暴力的反向操作。它创造了一个临时的、互不掠夺的共谋场域，让边缘群体在公共空间中拿回了定义“什么是快乐”的解释权。

但 1976 年的动乱成为了一个转折点。随后而来的 overpolicing 和媒体对“犯罪”叙事的武器化，将 carnival 从一个自组织系统变成了被管理的对象。当一个事件从“由我们发起”变成“被施加于我们”，它就进入了 cultural violence 的驯化阶段。秩序（Order）在这里成了掩体，掩盖的是对自发性（Spontaneity）的剥夺。这种“有序”实际上是 structural violence 的升级版：它允许你狂欢，但必须在规定的时间、规定的区域，在监管的注视下进行。

好在，新一代人的回归再次尝试找回那种“欢乐的无政府状态”。这说明只要生物墙和身份认同的基础还在，对主体性的追求就不会停止。真正的胜利不是获得一个被批准的节日，而是重新夺回那个可以让任何人只要出现就能贡献本质的、不被定义的公共空间。

This memoir reveals a classic evolution of the Violence Triangle. The early Notting Hill Carnival was a pure field of Expression, where Caribbean immigrants affirmed their identity by reconstructing the vibrancy of home on London's streets. For the post-Windrush generation, the emergence of Sound Systems upgraded this expression from 'replicating tradition' to 'defining the present'—they no longer had to play the role of their parents' immigrant experience, but found their true optimal expression through Reggae and Soul in the heart of London.

This feeling of 'owning the streets' was essentially a drastic move of Actual state toward Potential state. In an era where police harassment and racism were the norms of structural violence, this short-term, spontaneous 'occupation of space' was a reverse operation against meta-violence. It created a temporary, non-predatory field of complicity, allowing marginalized groups to reclaim the power of interpretation over 'what happiness is' in the public sphere.

However, the 1976 riot served as a tipping point. The subsequent overpolicing and the weaponization of 'criminality' narratives by the media transformed the carnival from a self-organizing system into a managed object. When an event shifts from being 'done by us' to 'done to us,' it enters the domestication phase of cultural violence. 'Order' here became a cover, masking the deprivation of spontaneity. This 'orderliness' is actually an upgraded version of structural violence: you are allowed to revel, but only at the prescribed time, in the prescribed area, under the gaze of surveillance.

Fortunately, the return of the new generation is once again an attempt to reclaim that 'joyful anarchy.' This proves that as long as the biological wall and identity foundations remain, the pursuit of subjectivity will not cease. True victory is not obtaining a sanctioned holiday, but reclaiming a public space where anyone, simply by showing up, can contribute to the true essence of the event without being predefined.
