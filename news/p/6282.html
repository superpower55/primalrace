撞钢琴的人，和写讣告的人The Man Who Hit the Piano, and the Man Who Wrote the Obituary

文娱 文化层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
一句'不疼不长进'，把死亡写成了职业精神
'No pain, no gain' turns a death into a work ethic.

贝尔ushi（John Belushi）撞向立式钢琴，两三次，全身承受那台巨大乐器的反冲，琴键发出刺耳的乱响。克里斯托弗·劳埃德（Christopher Lloyd）问他有没有事，他答：No pain, no gain。这篇报道把这句话当作他一生的座右铭来引用，然后紧接着写：1982年3月5日，他死于明显的用药过量，33岁，尸体在Chateau Marmont被发现。

这两段之间的距离，就是整篇文章假装不存在的东西。报道写他为了拍《Goin' South》每周末还得赶回纽约录《周六夜现场》，"put himself through some stress to accomplish"——把自己塞进某种压力里去完成它。这不是轶事，这是工作条件。一个33岁死掉的人，被回忆的方式是：他多拼。把身体当耗材的行业，用"敬业"给耗材镀一层金，然后耗材死了，金层还在，继续给下一批人看。

劳埃德讲这个故事的语境是他自己的回忆录。回忆是真诚的，我不怀疑。但真诚的回忆和一篇报道之间隔着一道编辑选择：这篇稿子从头到尾没有一句问，是什么让一个人觉得撞钢琴撞到骨头里、然后说"不疼不长进"是值得被记住的人生格言。它引用这句话两次，一次在 stunt 现场，一次在得知死讯的那一刻——第二次引用把这句话变成了墓志铭，而写墓志铭的人没有停下来。

这就是文化暴力最日常的形态：它不需要谁出来说"死得好"，它只需要把"豁出去"写成美德，把代价写成花絮，把33岁写成"英年早逝"然后翻页。男性身体在这套叙事里从来不是被保护的，是被征用的——你越不保护自己，你越是一个"真男人"，越值得一篇报道。贝尔ushi 是这套叙事的执行者，也是它的产品。

劳埃德回忆说，得知死讯时他想起了那句"不疼不长进"。这是一个老人对朋友的哀悼，里面有真东西。但同一句话被印在娱乐版上、配上"他全情投入"的副标题，就变成了另一种东西：一份招工广告。

John Belushi took a punch and went flying backwards into an upright piano. He did it two or three times, absorbing the full impact of the massive instrument, the keys jangling each time. Christopher Lloyd asked if he was okay. Belushi said: No pain, no gain. This article quotes that line as his life motto, and then, in the next paragraph, reports that he died of an apparent drug overdose on March 5, 1982, at 33, his body found at the Chateau Marmont.

The distance between those two paragraphs is what the piece pretends isn't there. It notes that while shooting Goin' South he had to be back in New York every weekend for SNL, that he "put himself through some stress to accomplish" it. That isn't an anecdote. That's a working condition. A man who died at 33 is remembered here for how hard he went. An industry that treats bodies as consumables gilds the consumable with the word dedication, and when the consumable dies, the gilding stays, on display for the next batch.

Lloyd is telling this story in his own memoir. The recollection is sincere; I don't doubt it. But sincere recollection and a published article are separated by an editorial choice, and this one never once asks what makes a person believe that crashing into a piano hard enough to feel it in his bones, then calling it no pain no gain, is a line worth putting on a headstone. The piece quotes it twice — once at the stunt, once at the moment Lloyd learned of the death — and the second quote turns it into an epitaph. The person writing the epitaph doesn't stop.

This is cultural violence in its most ordinary form. Nobody has to say the death was good. All it takes is framing going all out as a virtue, the cost as a bit of color, the age 33 as a tragedy and then turning the page. In this narrative the male body is never something to protect; it is something to requisition. The less you shield yourself, the more of a real man you are, the more you deserve a write-up. Belushi was both the enforcer of that story and its product.

Lloyd says that when he heard the news, he thought back to that line from Durango. That is an old man mourning a friend, and there is something real in it. But the same sentence, printed in an entertainment section under a subtitle about how he gave his all, becomes something else: a recruitment ad.
