在“温情”的剧本里，被系统遗忘的 Ricky 们The Warmth Scam: Romanticizing the Systemic Abandonment of the Rickys

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
电影将系统性暴力转化为温情叙事，是最高级的文化共谋。
Converting systemic violence into heartwarming narratives is the ultimate form of cultural complicity.

Sam Neill 的回忆录式访谈充满了典型的好莱坞/新西兰式温情：傀儡猪的趣闻、临场发挥的生日歌、演员之间像亲戚般的羁绊。这种叙事试图告诉我们，即便是在一个“失败的寄养系统” (failing system of foster care) 中，只要遇到了一个像 Hec 这样沉默但深情的成年男性，破碎的孩子也能被治愈。

但这正是典型的 cultural violence。电影将 Ricky Baker 这样一个被结构性抛弃的个体，放入一个“公路电影”的浪漫化模版中，通过个体之间偶然的 emotional bond 来抵消 structural violence 的残酷。当 Julian Dennison 提到他身边有很多像 Ricky 一样被系统抛弃的亲戚和朋友时，这本该是一次关于制度性暴力的控诉，但在电影的整体叙事中，这种痛苦被转化为了一种“成长痛”和“幽默感”。

最讽刺的是，这种“治愈”的逻辑依然是 masculine-centric 的：一个沉默的男人（Hec）通过某种粗粝的父权式关怀，赋予了孩子存在感。而那个真正代表系统暴力的社会福利官（Rachel House 饰演的角色），在电影中被简化为一个提供喜剧冲突的追逐者。系统性的盘剥被解构成了个体的误会或效率低下，从而让观众在走出电影院时感到一种虚假的安慰。

这种温情叙事是最高级的共谋。它让人们相信，面对一个崩坏的制度，我们不需要去拆除它，只需要在其中寻找一个“好心人”即可。而那些没能遇到 Hec 的 Ricky 们，依然在那个被 spray paint 标记的、布满地雷的现实世界里孤独地死去。

Sam Neill’s nostalgic interview is drenched in the typical cinematic warmth: anecdotes about puppet pigs, improvised birthday songs, and kinship between actors. This narrative suggests that even within a "failing system of foster care," a broken child can be healed as long as they encounter a silent, soulful man like Hec.

This is exactly how cultural violence operates. By placing an individual structurally abandoned like Ricky Baker into a romanticized "road movie" template, the film uses an accidental emotional bond to offset the cruelty of structural violence. When Julian Dennison mentions cousins and friends who mirrored Ricky’s struggle, it should have been an indictment of institutional failure. Instead, in the movie's narrative, this pain is converted into "growing pains" and "humor."

The irony is that this "healing" logic remains strictly masculine-centric: a silent man (Hec) grants the child a sense of existence through a rugged, patriarchal form of care. Meanwhile, the child welfare officer—the actual face of the system's violence—is reduced to a comedic antagonist in a chase sequence. Systemic oppression is downgraded to individual misunderstanding or inefficiency, leaving the audience with a fraudulent sense of comfort.

This heartwarming narrative is a sophisticated act of complicity. It convinces us that we don't need to dismantle a broken system; we just need to find a "good person" within it. Meanwhile, the Rickys who never find their Hec continue to perish in a real world that is as riddled with landmines as the ancestral lands mentioned in the film.
