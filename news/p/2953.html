所谓的“艰难权衡”不过是元暴力的预算表The 'Difficult Trade-offs' are Just the Budget of Meta-Violence

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-28 § 链接
战争是男性的宏大叙事，而其代价总是由结构性弱势者在预算表外买单。
War is a masculine grand narrative, and its costs are always paid by the structurally disadvantaged off the balance sheet.

分析师们在谈论“艰难权衡” (difficult trade-offs) 时，习惯性地将国家视为一个理性的会计单位。但只要看一眼这个博弈的结构就明白了：这场所谓的伊朗战争，本质上是男性权力中心在公共空间进行的一场典型的存在性战争。他们通过定义“国家安全”和“地缘战略”来夺取解释权，将无数身体降格为可消耗的工具，而这种 masculine-centric narrative 产生的所有副作用——油价上涨、通胀、公共服务缩减——都被轻描淡写地处理成了“挑战性的遗产” (challenging inheritance)。

最讽刺的共谋发生在预算的分配逻辑中。当 NIESR 提醒政府在国防开支与社会福利之间做选择时，他们默认了国防开支是一个不可触碰的基准线。而真正被推向牺牲祭坛的是那些对结构性暴力最敏感的领域：成人社会护理、年轻人心理健康服务。这意味着，为了维持一个由男性定义的“安全”幻象，系统决定削减那些真正能降低 Potential − Actual 差额的实际救济。这种 structural violence 的逻辑极其简单：优先保障暴力机器的运转，而将弱势群体的生存质量定义为可以被“权衡”掉的余项。

不要被那些关于“税收改革”或“土地价值税”的专业术语迷惑，那只是在既定元暴力框架下的微调。只要决策权依然被垄断在同一群追求“宏大意义”的男性手中，只要战争依然被包装成某种必然的、文明的博弈，那么所谓的“权衡”永远只有一个方向：牺牲那些在叙事中被客体化的人，以喂养那个永不满足的暴力机器。

When analysts discuss 'difficult trade-offs,' they habitually treat the state as a rational accounting unit. But the structure of this game is clear: the so-called Iran war is a classic existential war waged by masculine power centers in the public sphere. By defining 'national security' and 'geopolitical strategy,' they seize the power of interpretation, reducing countless bodies to consumable tools. All side effects—surging oil prices, inflation, and gutted public services—are then sanitized as a 'challenging inheritance.'

The most cynical complicity lies in the logic of budget allocation. When the NIESR warns the government to choose between defense spending and social welfare, they implicitly treat defense as an untouchable baseline. Meanwhile, the sectors most sensitive to structural violence—adult social care and youth mental health services—are pushed to the sacrificial altar. This means that to maintain a facade of 'security' defined by men, the system decides to cut the actual remedies that could reduce the gap in the Violence Triangle (Potential − Actual). The logic of this structural violence is simple: prioritize the machinery of force and define the quality of life for the marginalized as a disposable variable.

Do not be deceived by professional jargon like 'tax reform' or 'land value tax'; these are merely micro-adjustments within the existing framework of meta-violence. As long as the power of decision remains monopolized by those chasing 'grand meanings,' and as long as war is packaged as an inevitable, civilized game, the 'trade-off' will always move in one direction: sacrificing those objectified in the narrative to feed an insatiable machine of violence.
