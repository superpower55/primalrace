州长选举的实质是堕胎权The Governor Races Are Really About Abortion

其他 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
联邦层面丢了的东西，州长手里还攥着一半
What was lost federally is now held, state by state, in one chair.

这条新闻讲的是美国中期选举里的州长选战：36 个州改选州长，其中 19 个是空缺席位，现任州长或者任期到头，或者主动不选。报道把州长的权力列了一长串：提预算、任命官员、签法案或者否决法案、任命选举官员、和联邦政府谈钱谈条件。

但真正让州长这个位置变得要紧的，是 2022 年最高法院推翻了宪法层面的堕胎权。从那以后，堕胎合不合法落到各州自己定，已经有 13 个州的议会通过了全面或者接近全面的堕胎禁令。报道引了古特马赫研究所的说法：大约有十几个州长用行政令去“减轻”这类禁令造成的伤害。北卡罗来纳就是例子——州长支持堕胎权，议会不支持，州长只能靠行政令挡一挡。

这就是结构暴力最典型的运作方式：一项权利被从宪法层面拿掉，剩下的每一层都变成战场，而每一层的胜负取决于谁坐在那把椅子上。州长能签也能否决，能任命也能撤换，能执行也能拖着不执行。选票投给谁，投的其实是这条权利在你所在的州还剩下多少。

报道里没有出现任何一个正在承受这项政策后果的女性。没有名字，没有年龄，没有因为禁令而被迫跨州、被迫继续妊娠、被迫等并发症严重到“够格”才被接诊的人。整篇文章谈论堕胎权的方式，是把它当作一个“议题”，一个州长可以“影响”的领域，一个可以用来判断候选人立场的标尺。人不在里面。

这不是报道的疏忽，这是政治新闻的常规写法：制度是主体，人是背景。可堕胎禁令的代价从来不是抽象地落在“州”这个单位上，是落在具体的身体上。谁付这个代价，报道没写。

另一件报道写了却没展开的事：州长任命选举官员，州长签或者否决改变投票规则的立法。也就是说，这一批州长不只决定接下来几年本州的法律，还决定接下来几年本州怎么投票、谁来计票、规则对谁有利。

还有一层。报道花了不少篇幅讲州长是通往总统的跳板，至少 17 位前总统当过州长，纽森被广泛猜测是 2028 年民主党提名的热门人选，马里兰的摩尔、宾州的夏皮罗也在这个周期里竞选连任，被当作未来总统竞选的预演。

于是州长选举同时是两件事：对本州居民来说是未来四年的生活条件，对候选人来说是全国舞台的入场券。这两件事不一定冲突，但也不一定一致。一个州长为了全国曝光而推的政策，和一个州长为了本州居民而推的政策，未必是同一个。报道把这两件事并排写出来，没有问它们什么时候会打架。

36 个州，19 个空缺席位，13 个已经有禁令的州，十几个用行政令挡了一下的州长。这些数字背后是一个被拆散的权利，正在被切成五十份，逐份投票。

投完这一轮，还有下一轮。

This is a story about the governor races inside the US midterms: 36 states elect a governor, 19 of those races have no incumbent on the ballot, either term-limited or opting out. The piece lists what a governor can do — propose budgets, appoint officials, sign or veto bills, appoint election officials, negotiate with Washington over funding.

What makes the office matter right now is that in 2022 the Supreme Court ruled there is no constitutional right to abortion. Since then it has been up to each state, and 13 state legislatures have passed complete or near-total bans. The piece cites the Guttmacher Institute: about a dozen governors have issued executive orders to mitigate the harm. North Carolina is the example — a governor who supports abortion rights, a legislature that does not, and an executive order as the only thing in between.

This is structural violence doing exactly what it does: a right is removed at one level, and every level below becomes a battlefield, decided by who sits in the chair. A governor signs or vetoes, appoints or removes, enforces or drags. The vote is a vote on how much of that right survives in your state.

Not one woman currently living under these bans appears in the piece. No name, no age, no one who crossed a state line, no one kept pregnant, no one who had to get sick enough to qualify for care. Abortion is discussed as an issue, a domain a governor can influence, a marker for where a candidate stands. The people are not in it.

That is not an oversight, it is how political journalism is written: institutions are the subject, people are the backdrop. But the cost of a ban never lands on the abstraction of a state. It lands on a body. Who pays it — the piece does not say.

Another thing the piece reports without unfolding: governors appoint election officials, and sign or veto legislation that changes voting rules. This crop of governors will not only decide the law for the next few years, but how the state votes and who counts, and whose turnout the rules favor.

Then there is the ladder. At least 17 former presidents were governors. Newsom is widely speculated to be a frontrunner for 2028. Moore and Shapiro are running for re-election this cycle and are being read as previews of presidential bids.

So a governor race is two things at once: for residents, the conditions of the next four years; for the candidate, a national stage. Those two things are not always in conflict, and not always aligned. A policy a governor pushes for national exposure and one pushed for the people of the state are not the same policy. The piece sets them side by side and never asks when they collide.

36 states, 19 open seats, 13 states with bans already in place, a dozen governors with executive orders in between. Behind those numbers is a right that has been dismantled and cut into fifty pieces, each one voted on separately.

After this round, there is another.
