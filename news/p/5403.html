用“包容”掩盖的阶级定价权与精英共谋Class Pricing and Elite Complicity Masked as 'Inclusivity'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-04 § 链接
所谓的“包容性”往往是特权阶层在失去垄断后，为了维持解释权而进行的叙事换皮。
所谓的 'inclusivity' is often just a narrative skin-swap by elites to maintain interpretive power after losing a monopoly.

RHS（皇家园艺学会）这次拿到了230万英镑的补偿金，面对1300万英镑的损失，这笔钱在结构层面上更像是一种“安抚金”而非真正的救济。有趣的是，RHS 的掌门人 Clare Matterson 在接受采访时，熟练地将园艺与 NHS（国民医疗服务体系）的减压、社区赋权等公共叙事绑定。这是一种典型的武器化表达：通过将一个精英俱乐部的利益诉求，包装成对全民健康和“地方自豪感”的贡献，从而在政治博弈中换取更多资源。

最讽刺的环节在于她对切尔西花展（Chelsea Flower Show）的辩护。面对“觉醒”（woke）的指责和资助者的流失，她将其定义为“园艺界的奥运会”，并轻描淡写地将批评者归类为“对流行事物的习惯性抱怨”。这种叙事试图抹除一个事实：切尔西花展本质上是关于审美定价权和阶级筛选的场域。它定义了什么是“高级”的园艺，谁拥有进入这个圈层的门票。当这个精英场域开始引入环保和慈善叙事时，它并不是在进行真正的权力让渡，而是在通过更新“文化符号”来维持其在 21 世纪的合法性。

至于所谓的“社区园艺”和“城市展会”，这不过是 RHS 在意识到原有的精英模型面临危机后，尝试扩大认知入口的 PR 策略。将目光转向阳台花园和盆栽，并不是因为他们突然关心底层民众的生存空间，而是因为在土地所有权被高度垄断的英国，通过定义“微小空间的审美”来获取更多追随者，是成本最低的扩张方式。这依然是一场关于“谁来定义美”的存在性战争，而 RHS 试图在扮演一个“包容者”的同时，继续把持着解释权。

The RHS receiving £2.3m in compensation against a £13m loss is, structurally, more of a 'consolation prize' than actual relief. What's fascinating is how Clare Matterson skillfully binds the interests of a horticultural club to public narratives like NHS pressure reduction and community empowerment. This is a classic weaponisation of expression: packaging elite demands as contributions to public health and 'pride in place' to extract more resources from the political game.

The most ironic part is her defense of the Chelsea Flower Show. Facing accusations of being 'woke' and losing funders, she frames it as the 'Olympics of the horticultural world' and dismisses critics as merely complaining about 'anything popular.' This narrative attempts to erase a fundamental truth: Chelsea is a site for class screening and the pricing of aesthetic power. It defines what 'high' gardening is and who holds the ticket to that circle. By integrating eco-friendly and charitable narratives, the elite field isn't yielding power; it's simply updating its cultural symbols to maintain legitimacy in the 21st century.

As for the 'community gardening' and 'urban shows,' these are merely PR strategies to expand their cognitive entry points after realizing the old elite model is failing. Shifting focus to balcony gardens isn't a sudden concern for the living space of the underclass, but a low-cost expansion strategy in a country where land ownership is heavily monopolized. By defining the 'aesthetic of tiny spaces,' they acquire more followers. This remains an existential war over 'who defines beauty,' and the RHS is simply trying to maintain its interpretive power while performing the role of an 'inclusive' leader.
