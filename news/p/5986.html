产后身体不是段子Postpartum Bodies Are Not Punchlines

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
把产后损伤讲成笑话，是让它继续不可说。
Turning postpartum damage into a joke keeps it unspeakable.

一条关于产后身体的坦白，被写成了一条段子。Jennifer Lawrence 在 Call Her Daddy 上说，生完两个孩子之后「你的阴道会恢复，你的肛门不会」，主持人 Alex Cooper 追问会发生什么，她说等能进到一个暖和的地方再讲，「我会展示给你看」。娱乐媒体把这段剪成标题，读者笑完就划走了。

笑点在哪里，机制就在哪里。产后的肛门问题——痔疮、肛裂、盆底肌劳损、括约肌损伤——是大量女性真实承受的东西，报道自己也列了这几项。它被放进一个「过来人给准妈妈的忠告」的框架里，用玩笑包装，效果是让听的人既知道了、又不用真的去想。这就是文化暴力最省力的形态：它不否认损伤存在，它只是把损伤调成一个可以被消费的音调。

更值得看的是这段对话里谁在承担解释的成本。Cooper 怀着第一个孩子，问的是「我该怎么办」，Lawrence 的回答是「没有回头路」「那东西会掉出来」。一个即将生育的女性，从另一个刚生育完的女性那里，得到的全部信息是一句玩笑加一句「到时候告诉你」。这不是她的错，这是整个社会把产后身体放进私密和羞耻里的结果——它没有公共语言，只能在播客的暖气片旁边私下交换。

报道里还有一段更重的，被放在后面：第二次生产后她经历了产后抑郁，在电梯里对一个妈妈说「我每天焦虑得像被老虎追」，那个人抱了她，给了她号码。这才是真实的产后：身体损伤、精神崩塌，靠陌生人临时结成的「mom community」接住。而这个 community 之所以必要，是因为结构没有接住她们。

所以别把这条当成名人私事跳过。生育的身体代价被讲成笑话，和它被讲成神圣牺牲，是同一枚硬币的两面：两种说法都让它停在「个人经验」里，不进入医疗资源分配、产假制度、产后康复覆盖这些需要被追问的地方。Lawrence 说「没有回头路」，这句话对她是自嘲，对制度是一份免罪书。

她讲了自己不是好妈妈的愧疚，讲了「你会有本能」这句话怎么骗了她。这些都是真话。真话被剪成标题，就只剩下肛门那一个词在传播。谁在定价这段表达，谁在受益于它停留在段子层面——报道没写，但答案不难找：所有希望产后损伤继续待在私密领域的人。

A confession about a postpartum body got written up as a punchline. On Call Her Daddy, Jennifer Lawrence said that after two kids "your vagina will bounce back, your butthole won't." Host Alex Cooper asked what happens, and Lawrence said she'd tell her when she could get somewhere warm — "I'll show you." Entertainment media clipped it into a headline, and readers laughed and scrolled on.

Where the joke is, the mechanism is. Postpartum anal problems — hemorrhoids, anal fissures, pelvic floor strain, sphincter strain — are things a huge number of women actually live with; the report lists them itself. It gets packaged as an experienced mother's warning to a pregnant one, wrapped in humor, and the effect is that the listener now knows and still doesn't have to think about it. This is cultural violence at its cheapest: it doesn't deny the damage, it just retunes it into something consumable.

Look at who is paying the cost of explanation here. Cooper is expecting her first child and asks what to expect. Lawrence answers: there's no going back, that thing is coming out. A woman about to give birth gets, from a woman who just did, one joke and a promise to explain later. That's not her failure. It's what happens when the postpartum body is kept private and shameful — it has no public language, so it gets exchanged quietly next to a studio radiator.

There's a heavier passage further down, buried: after her second birth Lawrence had postpartum depression, and in an elevator told another mother she felt chased by a tiger every day. The woman hugged her and gave her her number. That's the real postpartum — body damage, mental collapse, caught by a makeshift mom community. That community is necessary precisely because the structure doesn't catch them.

So don't file this under celebrity private life and move on. Birth's bodily cost told as a joke, and told as sacred sacrifice, are two sides of one coin: both keep it inside "personal experience," away from who gets medical resources, parental leave, postpartum rehab. "There's no going back" is self-mockery from her and a get-out-of-jail card for the system.

She also said she never felt like a good mom, and that "you'll have instincts" was a lie. Those are true things. Clipped into a headline, only the one word about her butthole travels. Who priced this expression, who benefits from it staying a joke — the report doesn't say, but the answer isn't hard: everyone who wants postpartum damage to stay private.
