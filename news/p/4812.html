摇滚的复兴还是数字资本主义的自慰？Rock's Revival or Digital Capitalism's Masturbation?

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
当‘被好生活杀死’成为一种审美，反叛就成了特权阶级的装饰品。
When 'being killed by a good life' becomes an aesthetic, rebellion is just an accessory for the privileged.

John Harris 在《卫报》里把 Geese 描绘成这个不安时代的‘完美原声带’，试图给摇滚乐的回归赋予某种世代性的神圣感。但剥开那些关于‘权力反叛’和‘焦虑表达’的叙事，这不过是一场典型的 cultural violence 包装下的阶级自嗨。

最讽刺的段落在于：这群私立学校出身的年轻人，在舞台上对着数千人高唱‘我被一个相当不错的生活给杀死了’ (I’m getting killed by a pretty good life)。这哪里是反叛？这简直是数字资本主义时代最奢侈的表达——一种‘特权者的忧郁’。当真正的 structural violence 正在剥夺底层人群的生存空间时， privileged class 的年轻人通过扮演‘焦虑的金丝雀’来确立自己的艺术身份。这种表达不是为了打破墙，而是为了在墙内装饰一个名为‘反叛’的房间。

文中提到的‘认知入口’非常典型：通过模仿 70 年代滚石乐队的造型，利用怀旧的视觉符号去套现当代的焦虑。这种‘武器化’的表达精准地捕捉了年轻一代对‘不可能之境’的挫败感，但它提供的解药是自毁式的——‘花钱做个脑叶切除术’。这种叙事将系统性的压迫简化为个人情绪的波动，把政治性的绝望转化为一种可消费的‘艺术摩擦’。

至于那句‘我在你身上看到了我自己’，这不过是特权群体内部的共谋 (complicity)。他们通过共享一种‘被文明生活压抑’的伪痛点，在音乐节的草坪上完成了一次群体性的身份确证。这种所谓的‘爆发’，在本质上依然在男本位的摇滚叙事框架内运行：一个极具才华的男性主唱，带领着他的乐队，在舞台上通过愤怒的表达来夺取解释权。这不是在挑战权力，而是在练习如何成为下一个权力的持有者。

John Harris in The Guardian frames Geese as the 'perfect soundtrack' for an age of disquiet, attempting to sanctify the return of rock with a sense of generational destiny. But strip away the narratives of 'power rebellion' and 'anxiety expression,' and what remains is a classic case of class narcissism wrapped in cultural violence.

The most ironic part is this: a group of privately educated youths screaming 'I’m getting killed by a pretty good life' to thousands of fans. This isn't rebellion; it's the ultimate luxury of the digital capitalist era—the 'melancholy of the privileged.' While structural violence strips the bottom class of their basic survival, the privileged class uses the persona of the 'anxious canary' to establish an artistic identity. This expression isn't about breaking the wall; it's about decorating a room called 'Rebellion' inside that wall.

The 'cognitive entry' here is textbook: using 70s Rolling Stones aesthetics to cash in on contemporary anxiety. This weaponised expression precisely captures the frustration of a generation facing an 'impossible world,' but the cure it offers is self-destructive—'paying for a lobotomy.' Such narratives reduce systemic oppression to individual emotional fluctuations, transforming political despair into a consumable 'artistic friction.'

As for the line 'I see myself in you,' it's nothing more than complicity among the elite. By sharing a pseudo-pain of being 'oppressed by civilized life,' they complete a collective confirmation of identity on a festival lawn. This so-called 'explosion' still operates within the masculine-centric narrative of rock: a talented male frontman leading his band, seizing the power of interpretation through angry expressions. This isn't challenging power; it's practicing how to become the next holder of it.
