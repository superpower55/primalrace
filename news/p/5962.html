当众夸臀与司法追杀是同一套Praise Her Ass, Raid Her House

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
同一个女人，能被夸臀，也能被查家。
The same woman can be flattered or investigated. Same system.

特朗普在密歇根集会上当众夸 Nicki Minaj 的屁股，舔嘴唇，让她站起来。台下欢呼。这不是失态，这是定价。

同一个政府，这一个月把司法部对准了他那些黑人女性政敌。文章写得很清楚：突袭众议员 Ilhan Omar 的家，逮捕参议员 Louise Lucas。一边是突袭和逮捕，一边是当众夸臀。看起来是两件事，其实是一件事：黑人女性的身体和存在，由他决定是奖赏还是惩罚。顺从的，被夸；不顺从的，被查。

这套机制在书里叫表达的武器化——不是封住你的嘴，是规定你只有哪几种表达能被听见。Nicki Minaj 在台上被夸的，不是她的说唱，不是她的政治立场，是她的身体。她站起来，微笑，挥手。这是一个完美的最优解表达：她拿到了总统的公开背书，代价是她的主体性被压缩成一个部位。她比 Baartman 有更多能动性，文章自己说了。但能动性不等于自由，只能说明这次交易是她自己签的字。

文章引了数据：四年里特朗普在 Truth Social 上至少 50 次说别人“低智商”，其中 60% 涉及黑人公众人物和议员，名单里有 Kamala Harris、Jasmine Crockett、Maxine Waters、Ilhan Omar、Rashida Tlaib、Al Sharpton、Letitia James、Donna Brazile。夸臀和骂低智商，是同一套评价体系的两端。

Baartman 的部分不是修辞装饰。她被关在笼子里，被命令坐下站起，“像马戏团的动物一样”。她死后，Cuvier 做了她身体的石膏模，把她的大脑和生殖器泡在福尔马林里，在巴黎人类博物馆展出到 1974 年——她死后 159 年。这不是历史轶事，这是这套定价机制的原始版本：黑人女性的身体是可以被观看、被展示、被保存、被占有的物件。特朗普在集会上让 Minaj 站起来给人群看，和那个“训练师”命令 Baartman 坐下站起，中间隔了两百多年，隔着一个“自愿”。

文章提到 Minaj 可能接任白宫新闻秘书，白宫通讯主任 Steven Cheung 发推直接引用了这个传闻。一个被总统当众点评臀部的女人，去当政府的发言人。这不是讽刺，这是这套体系的完整闭环：她的身体是入场券，她的嘴是工具。

文章最后说 Minaj 在“助纣为虐”。这个判断对，但停得太早。真正的问题是：为什么一个黑人女性要在“被突袭”和“被夸臀”之间选？这个选择题本身就是暴力。夸臀的那一端看起来是奖赏，其实是同一种所有权的另一种行使方式。

报道没写的是：那些被突袭、被逮捕的黑人女性，她们的名字在集会的欢呼声里一个字都没有出现。

Trump stood at a Michigan rally and talked about Nicki Minaj's ass. Licked his lips. Told her to stand. The crowd hooted. This is not a lapse. This is a price tag.

The same administration spent the past month siccing the DOJ on his Black female political opponents. The raid on Rep. Ilhan Omar's home. The arrest of Sen. Louise Lucas. Two things, apparently. Actually one: a Black woman's body and existence get sorted by him into reward or punishment. Compliant, you get praised. Noncompliant, you get investigated.

In the book this is the weaponization of expression. Not silencing you—deciding which of your expressions are allowed to register. What got praised on that stage was not Minaj's rapping, not her politics. It was her body. She stood, smiled, waved. A textbook optimal expression: she got a presidential endorsement, and the price was her subjectivity compressed into a body part. She has more agency than Baartman—the article says so itself. Agency is not freedom. It only means she signed the deal herself.

The article cites the data: over four years Trump called people "low IQ" at least 50 times on Truth Social, and 60 percent of those instances involved Black public figures and legislators—Kamala Harris, Jasmine Crockett, Maxine Waters, Ilhan Omar, Rashida Tlaib, Al Sharpton, Letitia James, Donna Brazile. Praising the ass and calling them stupid are two ends of one evaluation system.

Baartman is not rhetorical decoration. Locked in a cage, ordered to sit and stand "in a similar way that circus animals are ordered." After she died, Cuvier reportedly made a cast of her body, pickled her brain and genitals in formaldehyde, and put them on display at the Paris Museum of Man until 1974—159 years after her death. This is the original version of the pricing mechanism: a Black woman's body as an object to be viewed, displayed, preserved, owned. Trump telling Minaj to stand so the crowd can look is the same command, two hundred years later, with a "voluntary" stapled on top.

The article notes the rumor that Minaj might become White House press secretary, and that Communications Director Steven Cheung tweeted a direct reference to it. A woman whose ass the president just appraised in public, going on to speak for the government. Not irony. The closed loop: her body is the ticket, her mouth is the tool.

The article concludes that Minaj is aiding and abetting. True, but it stops too early. The real question is why a Black woman has to choose between being raided and being praised. The choice itself is the violence. The praise end looks like a reward. It is the same ownership exercised differently.

What the article doesn't say: the names of the Black women who were raided and arrested did not appear once in that crowd's cheering.
