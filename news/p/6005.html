僵尸游戏里唯一被消费的是谁In Zombie Games, Who's Really Being Consumed

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要万圣节之际，一篇回顾电子游戏中可操作僵尸历史的文章提到，新动作游戏《Stupid Never Dies》由前卡普空制作人小林裕幸创立的新工作室 GPTRACK50 推出。文章称，这款游戏围绕一个名叫 Davy 的僵尸展开多元宇宙冒险，但作者表示自己不太喜欢它的开局部分，并认为其玩法缺乏僵尸的感觉。
The newsA Halloween piece reviewing playable zombies in video games discusses Stupid Never Dies, the first game from GPTRACK50, the new studio of former Capcom producer Hiroyuki Kobayashi. The game follows a zombie named Davy on a multiverse adventure, but the author says they don't much like its opening hours, finding the gameplay lacking in zombie feel.
僵尸不好玩，是因为它太像被规训的人
Zombies aren't fun because they look too much like disciplined people

一篇讲"扮演僵尸好不好玩"的文章，写了一千多字，没有一次提到僵尸是从哪来的。

僵尸这个形象的原型是海地的甘蔗种植园。被贩卖到加勒比海的非洲人，在奴隶制下不被承认为人，死了也不算死透，于是民间流传出这样一种恐惧：人可以被变成一具只会干活、没有意志、不会说话的躯体。它从一开始就不是怪物，是劳动力。这个来源在游戏里被反复擦掉，只剩下一个可以爆头、可以套上交通锥、可以被"英雄"踩着拍照的靶子。文章开头那段写得很生动——"被羞辱""被拴在绳子上""存在就是为了当主角杀的第一样东西"。写得很对，但它把这件事当笑话讲。

然后文章问：有没有一款游戏真的为这些可怜的家伙讨回了公道？

这个问题本身就问错了。因为僵尸在游戏里的功能从来不是"被讨回公道"，而是被无限量供应。主角一路"枪杀成千上万，甚至几十万"同类，这个数字在报道里是当修辞用的，可它描述的正是一种取之不尽的资源：不需要名字、不需要背景、不需要死得有尊严。谁在共谋？设计关卡的人、写数值的人、买游戏的人，还有写这类怀旧稿的人。回报很明确——一个永远不会枯竭的敌人库，一个不需要为死亡负责的屠杀场。

《Stupid Never Dies》是这个逻辑的最新样本。主角 Davy 为了复活一个他"一见钟情于她冻僵的尸体"的女孩，穿越多元宇宙。报道自己都写了这句设定，然后轻轻放过。一个连脑子都不转的男性僵尸，驱动力是占有另一具身体；而游戏给他的能力是不断变形、不断变强、不断吞食。这不叫为僵尸平反，这叫把僵尸塞回男性主角的模板里，再贴上"荒诞搞笑"的标签。

真正有意思的是报道最后那句抱怨：玩家会尽快离开 Davy 的默认形态，因为那个形态"只会挥爪和呕吐"，不好玩。也就是说，僵尸形态被设计成一个需要被逃离的起点。游戏嘴上说让你扮演僵尸，实际上奖励你尽快不做僵尸。这才是这条新闻里最锋利的地方——它无意中承认了，在这个产业里，"僵尸"是一个必须被超越的弱位，谁停留在这个位置，谁就不好玩。

至于那个被复活的女孩，全篇没有一句她自己的话。她的尸体是动机，她的复活是奖品。僵尸被消费，她也一样，只是她连被爆头的戏份都没有。

A thousand words on whether it's fun to play a zombie, and not one mention of where the zombie comes from.

The figure's prototype is the Haitian sugarcane plantation. Africans sold into the Caribbean were not recognized as persons under slavery, and death didn't fully count either, so a fear took shape: a person can be turned into a body that only works, has no will, and doesn't speak. It was never a monster. It was labor. In games that origin is erased over and over, leaving a target that can be headshot, cone-hatted, and posed over by a "hero" with a camera. The opening of this piece describes it vividly—humiliated, leashed, existing to be the first thing the protagonist kills. Vivid, and delivered as a joke.

Then the piece asks: has any game ever really done these poor rotting saps justice?

Wrong question. The zombie's function in games was never to be given justice; it's to be supplied without limit. The protagonist guns down "thousands, if not hundreds of thousands" of them—a number used here as rhetoric, but it describes exactly a resource that never runs out: no names, no backstory, no dignified death required. Who's complicit? The level designers, the people tuning the numbers, the people buying, and the people writing nostalgic pieces like this one. The payoff is clear: an inexhaustible enemy pool, a killing field where nobody has to answer for the dead.

Stupid Never Dies is the latest sample of that logic. Davy, the hero, sets out across the multiverse to revive a girl he had a "love at first glimpse of her frozen corpse" experience with. The piece writes that sentence down and lets it pass. A male zombie whose brain doesn't work is driven by possessing another body, and the game gives him the power to keep transforming, keep growing, keep devouring. That isn't rehabilitating the zombie. It's stuffing the zombie back into the male-protagonist template and labeling it wacky.

The sharpest thing here is the reviewer's own complaint: players will leave Davy's default form as fast as possible, because in it he can only swipe and puke—not fun. The zombie form is designed as a starting point to be escaped. The game says you play a zombie while rewarding you for stopping being one as soon as possible. That's the admission buried in the review: in this industry, "zombie" is a weak position you must transcend, and whoever stays there isn't having fun.

As for the girl to be revived, she never gets a line. Her corpse is the motive; her resurrection is the prize. The zombie is consumed, and so is she—except she doesn't even get a headshot scene.
