逃避主义的避难所，还是被定价的欲望？Escapism Sanctuary or Priced Desire?

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-18 § 链接
当女性的欲望被定义为‘trash’，这种鄙视本身就是元暴力的快感。
The dismissal of female desire as 'trash' is the pure pleasure of meta-violence.

这篇关于 Romantasy（浪漫奇幻）溯源的文章揭露了一个极其稳定的结构：从12世纪到21世纪，女性通过构建一个‘非人’的超现实空间来安置自己的欲望。为什么必须是精灵？因为在那个空间里，生物墙被暂时拆除，或者被重新定义。当现实中的女性被要求贞洁、顺从且在家庭结构中处于从属地位时，‘精灵性爱’成为了一个绝佳的认知入口——因为它被定义为‘不作数’的，从而让女性在不破坏现实身份（如处女或贤妻）的前提下，完成一次主体性的实验。

但我们要警惕这种‘自由’。文章提到，很多 Romantasy 倾向于描写‘非自愿’或‘强迫性’的邂逅，理由是这能减轻女性的负罪感。这在本质上是一种假.最优解表达。在这种叙事里，女性依然在扮演一个‘被动’的角色，通过将权力交给一个‘迷人且危险’的男性（即便他是精灵），来换取一种安全的快感。这不过是将父权结构的权力博弈搬到了水晶宫殿里，并没有真正消弭元暴力，而是在潜意识中完成了对‘被支配’的内化。

最讽刺的是那些批评者。无论是14世纪的乔叟还是现代的文学评论家，他们对这类作品的鄙视（dismiss）并非基于文学质量，而是基于一个简单的逻辑：因为这是女性在生产和消费。当男性写充满性爱和暴力的《权力的游戏》时，那是‘宏大叙事’；当女性写性感女王和精灵骑士时，那是‘smut’（色情垃圾）。这种评价体系的差异，正是男性中心叙事对解释权的垄断。他们通过定义什么是‘严肃文学’，成功地将女性的欲望定价为‘低端’，从而在文化层面上维持一种认知上的优越感。

This exploration of Romantasy roots reveals a structural constant from the 12th century to today: women constructing a 'non-human' surreal space to house their desires. Why fairies? Because in that realm, the biological wall is temporarily dismantled or redefined. When real-world women are demanded to be chaste and subservient, 'fairy sex' becomes a perfect cognitive entry—it was historically deemed 'not to count,' allowing women to experiment with subjectivity without destroying their social identity as virgins or wives.

However, we must be wary of this 'freedom.' The article notes a trend toward 'non-consensual' or 'coercive' encounters to alleviate guilt. This is a classic fake optimal expression. In such narratives, the woman still performs a 'passive' role, trading her agency for a 'safe' thrill provided by a 'dangerous' male (even a fairy one). It is merely relocating the power dynamics of the masculine-centric narrative to a crystal palace; it does not eliminate meta-violence but internalizes the pleasure of being dominated.

The most ironic part is the critics. From Chaucer to modern journalists, the dismissal of the genre is not about literary merit, but about who produces and consumes it. When men write sex and violence in Game of Thrones, it is a 'grand narrative.' When women write sexy queens, it is 'smut.' This disparity in evaluation is the monopoly of the masculine-centric narrative over the right to interpret. By defining what constitutes 'serious literature,' they price female desire as 'low-end,' maintaining a cultural superiority through the weaponisation of taste.
