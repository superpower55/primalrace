谁在定义“搞怪”：被消费的边缘表达与特权掩体Defining the 'Mess Around': Consuming Marginal Expression as a Privilege Shield

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-31 § 链接
当“离经叛道”成为被雇佣的娱乐产品，它就成了结构性暴力的温和遮羞布。
When 'rebellion' becomes a hired entertainment product, it serves as a mild veil for structural violence.

一个拿着拖把在英联邦运动会上跳舞的男人，被媒体包装成“打破常规”的快乐使者。叙事逻辑很完整：一个内向的舞者通过扮演一个“愚蠢”的清洁工，告诉世界“搞怪也没关系”。这种叙事在 cultural 层面上极其讨巧，它用一种低成本的 whimsical（古怪）感，制造了一种假象——即在这个高度规训的社会里，主体性可以通过这种“被允许的越轨”而获得释放。

但我们需要拆穿这个 scam。CJ Miller 不是在反抗规训，他本身就是 Games 雇佣的“娱乐工具”。他的“离经叛道”是经过编排的 choreograph，他的“打破常规”是合同的一部分。当一个人的 flamboyant ass 被定义为“娱乐项目”时，这种表达不仅没有挑战结构，反而通过将“边缘身份”（清洁工、舞者、Queer 气质）转化为一种可消费的快感，消解了真实身份在结构中面对的 Potential − Actual 的差额。这是一种典型的武器化叙事：用个体的“快乐”掩盖了职业阶级与权力结构的僵化。

最讽刺的共谋发生在媒体与观众之间。BBC 随口给拖把起名 Doris，观众在屏幕前感到“被治愈”，这种 chemistry 本质上是对边缘表达的一种俯视。人们爱的是那个“被允许在特定时间内扮演疯子”的表演者，而非那个在现实中可能被视为异类、被排挤的真实个体。这种“好新闻”的本质是：系统通过在边缘地带开一个小口子，释放一点点名为“幽默”的压力，从而让整体的 structural violence 显得更加不可见且温和。

A man dancing with a mop at the Commonwealth Games is packaged by the media as a messenger of joy who 'breaks the rules.' The narrative is tidy: an introverted dancer, by playing a 'stupid' cleaner, tells the world it's OK to mess around. At the cultural layer, this is a clever scam. It uses a low-cost sense of whimsy to create an illusion that subjectivity can be liberated through this 'permitted transgression.'

But let's dismantle this. CJ Miller isn't resisting discipline; he is a tool hired by the Games for entertainment. His 'rebellion' is choreographed; his 'breaking rules' is a contractual obligation. When a flamboyant ass is defined as an 'entertainment act,' this expression doesn't challenge the structure. Instead, by converting marginal identities—cleaners, dancers, Queer aesthetics—into consumable pleasure, it erases the actual gap of Potential − Actual violence these identities face in reality. This is the weaponization of narrative: using individual 'happiness' to mask the rigidity of class and power structures.

The most cynical complicity lies between the media and the audience. The BBC casually names the mop 'Doris,' and viewers feel 'healed.' This chemistry is essentially a gaze of condescension toward marginal expression. People love the performer who is 'allowed to be a madman' for a limited time, not the real individual who might be marginalized or excluded in a non-performance setting. This 'good news' is actually a mechanism: the system opens a tiny vent for 'humor' to release pressure, making the overall structural violence invisible and seemingly benign.
