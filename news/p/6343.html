柳条男人不是厌男，是省事The Wicker Man Isn't Misandry, It's Convenience

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
她要的不是非人，是一个不用她伺候的男人
She doesn't want a non-human. She wants a man who needs no upkeep.

这篇专栏问的是：女人是不是受够了男人，所以宁可去操一只柳条篮子。它自己给出的答案已经摆在文中了——Skarsgård 演的柳条人"是男人形状的，会修东西，穿粗花呢裤子，当然，还有阴茎"。连那些更离谱的物恋小说，据 New York 杂志统计，"绝大多数也是把人类女性和雄性生物、雄性物件配对"。所以这不是逃离男性，这是给男性换了个材质。

真正被换掉的是维护成本。原文写得很清楚：柳条丈夫不需要"mankeep"，不会增加家务负担，最多拿湿布擦一擦、上点家具蜡。这句话是整篇的钥匙。女人幻想的不是没有男人的世界，是一个不需要她做情绪劳动、不需要她收拾、不需要她哄、还能修东西、还有阴茎的男人。也就是说，她们把婚姻里那笔一直由女方垫付的账，单独拎出来，做成了一个可以买回家的物件。

这不是什么新东西，这是最优解表达在幻想市场上的报价。现实里的 heterosexual relationship 让她们累，她们没有去改规则，没有去要求男人分担，而是把"一个不添麻烦的雄性"做成了商品化的欲望对象——篮子、精灵、牛头人、 mothman，配方一样，只是材质越来越便宜。假的最优解表达就是这样运作的：不去动那张桌子，只换桌上摆的东西。代价是主体性继续留在原地，幻想结束后，那个穿粗花呢的还是会要求你做饭。

专栏作者还搬来一个数据给自己壮胆：英国 93.7% 的女性认同自己是异性恋，所以"异性恋已死"的报道被夸大了。这个数字恰恰是问题本身，不是问题的解答。在一个被训练了几十年的异性恋叙事里，绝大多数人当然会这么回答——这不是欲望的证明，这是规训的完成度。用认同率来反驳异性恋疲惫，等于用体温计证明发烧不存在。

更值得看的是这篇稿子怎么处理它自己摸到的东西。它提到"heterofatalism"、提到男性不愿承诺、提到 manosphere 撑腰的厌女，然后立刻拐进圣诞蛋糕和"Stir-up Sunday 有了新含义"。这是文化暴力最熟练的一手：把结构性的疲惫翻译成一个可爱的段子，让读者笑着离场，什么也没带走。作者甚至替读者把结论写好了——这是"逃避主义"，是希望生活更轻、更性感、更傻一点。轻、性感、傻，三个词把一整代人对不平等分工的倦怠，打包成了一次周末消遣。

所以柳条篮子告诉我们的不是女人厌男，是女人已经学会了在不动结构的前提下给自己止痛。止痛有效，账单照付。真正的问题不是为什么她们想要一只不会添乱的篮子，而是为什么"不添乱"在异性恋关系里已经成了需要靠幻想才能获得的东西。

The column asks whether women are so sick of men that they'd rather sleep with a wicker basket. It answers itself: Skarsgård's wicker husband is "man-shaped, fixes stuff, wears tweedy trousers and, of course, has a penis." Even the wilder object erotica, per New York magazine, "overwhelmingly pair human women with male creatures and objects." This isn't an escape from men. It's a man in a different material.

What actually gets swapped out is maintenance. The piece says it plainly: you don't need to "mankeep" a woven spouse, and he won't add to the domestic load — at most a wipe with a damp cloth and some furniture polish. That sentence is the key to the whole thing. The fantasy isn't a world without men. It's a man who fixes things and has a penis and does not require her emotional labor, her cleaning, her soothing. She has taken the part of marriage that women have always subsidized and turned it into an object she can bring home.

This is not new. It's optimal expression quoting a price on the fantasy market. Real heterosexual relationships exhaust them, so instead of changing the rules or demanding that men share the load, they turn "a male who causes no trouble" into a commodified desire-object — basket, elf, minotaur, mothman, same recipe, cheaper materials each time. This is how fake optimal expression works: leave the table alone, rearrange what sits on it. The cost is that subjectivity stays exactly where it was. The fantasy ends, and the man in tweed still wants dinner.

The columnist props herself up with a statistic: 93.7% of UK women identify as heterosexual, so reports of heterofatality are exaggerated. That number is the problem, not the answer. After decades of heterosexual narrative training, of course nearly everyone says yes — that's not proof of desire, it's a measure of how thoroughly the training took. Citing identification rates to refute heterosexual exhaustion is like using a thermometer to prove there's no fever.

More telling is what the piece does with what it touches. It names heterofatalism, men's reluctance to commit, manosphere-enabled misogyny — then swerves into Christmas cake and a pun about Stir-up Sunday. This is cultural violence at its most practiced: translate structural exhaustion into a cute bit, let the reader leave smiling, carrying nothing. The author even writes the conclusion for us — it's "escapism," a wish for life to be lighter, sexier, sillier. Light, sexy, silly: three words that package a generation's fatigue over unequal labor into a weekend diversion.

So the wicker basket doesn't tell us women are done with men. It tells us women have learned to dull the pain without touching the structure. The painkiller works. The bill still comes due. The real question isn't why they want a basket that causes no trouble — it's why "causes no trouble" has become something you can only get from a fantasy.
