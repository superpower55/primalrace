她活下来了，然后呢She Survived. Now What?

其他 结构层 · 直接层 The Cut ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
一次失败的处决，暴露的不是技术失误，是谁有权决定一个人该不该痛
A botched execution doesn't reveal incompetence. It reveals who gets to decide how much pain is acceptable.

Christa Pike 没死成。九月三十日，田纳西州的处决室里，两剂 pentobarbital 推下去，她还活着。被送到医院，插管，上呼吸机，十天后出院，回到她已经待了三十年的监狱。她说她的身体在痛，手臂痛，手痛，喉咙痛，说话仍然困难，痛的不只是身体，还有心智和灵魂。

州政府的说法会是“执行过程出现意外”。意外这个词在这里承担了全部的工作：它把一次由国家发起的、以杀死一个人为目的、并且已经着手实施的行为，翻译成了一场医疗事故。护士找了超过一个小时才建立静脉通路，律师怀疑针插错了位置，负责的医生 Mark Fowler 说他对自己的工作问心无愧，他“不是一个邪恶、恶毒的人”。没有人被追责，因为在这个框架里，没有人在施暴，只有人在失误。

这就是 structural violence 最干净的样本：暴力不需要一个愤怒的施暴者，只需要一套流程、一个签名、一份“我按规程办事”的自我陈述。Galtung 的公式在这里很好用，Violence = Potential − Actual。Pike 本可以不被这样对待——要么被依法处决，要么被依法减刑——而她实际得到的，是两剂没杀死她的药、十天的医院、以及回到死囚牢房继续等下一次。这个差额不是天灾，是有人签字画押做出来的。

值得注意的还有那条时间线。1996 年判死刑，她是田纳西州自 1819 年以来第一个面临死刑的女性。三十年。人权组织在她行刑前请求减刑，没有成功。行刑失败之后，她的律师转而请求州长 Bill Lee 把刑期改为终身监禁。也就是说，她现在活着的每一天，都取决于一个政客愿不愿意承认这套机器已经坏了。她的命被挂在别人的行政决定上，这本身就是暴力。

报道里没有写 Colleen Slemmer 的家人这三十年是怎么过的，也没有写他们怎么看这次失败的行刑。这是一个缺席，值得指出：受害者的声音在这类报道里常常只作为判决依据出现一次，然后就消失了，剩下的篇幅全给了制度的技术细节和施暴者的自我辩护。这不是要替谁开脱，而是说，当一套杀人机器连“杀死”都做不好的时候，它同时辜负了两边的人。

Fowler 医生说他不邪恶、不恶毒、不冷漠、不无能。四个否定，一个都没回答真正的问题：为什么一个人被国家弄成半死，然后被送回牢房，继续等？

Christa Pike did not die. On September 30, in a Tennessee death chamber, two doses of pentobarbital went in and she was still alive. She was taken to a hospital, intubated, put on a ventilator. Ten days later she was discharged and returned to the Nashville prison where she has been held for thirty years. She says her body hurts, her arms hurt, her hands hurt, her throat hurts, she still struggles to speak. The pain, she says, is not only physical.

The state will call this an accident. That word does all the work here: it translates a state-initiated act whose entire purpose was to kill a person, and which was already underway, into a medical mishap. The IV team struggled for over an hour to set a line. Her lawyers suspect the needle went in wrong. The doctor who oversaw it, Mark Fowler, says he stands by his work and his decisions, that he is not evil, vicious, unscrupulous, uncaring, or incompetent. No one is held responsible, because in this frame no one was doing violence — people were merely making errors.

This is structural violence at its cleanest: violence that needs no angry perpetrator, only a procedure, a signature, and a self-statement of "I followed protocol." Galtung's formula applies neatly. Violence = Potential − Actual. Pike could have been lawfully executed, or lawfully commuted. What she actually got was two doses that failed, ten days in a hospital, and a return to death row to wait for the next attempt. That gap was not weather. Someone signed for it.

Note the timeline. Sentenced to death in 1996, the first woman to face the death penalty in Tennessee since 1819. Thirty years. Human rights organizations asked for commutation before the scheduled execution; they failed. After the botched attempt, her lawyers are now asking Governor Bill Lee to commute the sentence to life. Which means every day she is alive now depends on whether a politician is willing to admit the machine is broken. A life hung on an administrative decision is itself a form of violence.

The reporting does not say how Colleen Slemmer's family has spent these thirty years, or what they make of the failed execution. That absence is worth naming: the victim's voice appears once, as the basis of a sentence, then vanishes, while the remaining space goes to procedural detail and the self-defense of the man who ran the procedure. This is not an argument for anyone's absolution. It is an observation that when a killing machine cannot even kill, it fails both sides.

Dr. Fowler says he is not evil, not vicious, not uncaring, not incompetent. Four denials, and not one of them answers the actual question: why was a person left half-killed by the state, then sent back to her cell to wait?
