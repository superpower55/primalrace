谁在为女性的身体账单买单？Who Pays the Bill for Women's Bodies?

好消息 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
职业病名单的缺失，就是一种结构性的性别暴力。
The absence of breast cancer from occupational disease lists is a form of structural violence.

一个法国空姐把乳腺癌打成了“职业病”，这在航空业是个首例。听起来是法律的胜利，但把视角拉高一点，你会发现这其实是一次对 structural violence 的迟到补课。在法国，乳腺癌不在官方职业病清单里，这意味着绝大多数女性在面对这种病痛时，必须在法律的黑洞里独自搏斗两年半，才能换来一个“这不是我的错”的认定。

为什么乳腺癌不在清单里？因为制定清单的逻辑通常是 masculine-centric。在传统的职业病定义中，人们倾向于关注工厂的化学污染、矿山的粉尘——那些典型的“男性工作场景”带来的损伤。而像夜班导致的内分泌紊乱、长期处于被动吸烟环境这种弥散性的、与女性高频从业岗位绑定的风险，被默认为一种“生活方式”或“个体体质”问题。这种认知的缺失，本质上就是一种元暴力：它通过定义“什么是职业病”，直接决定了谁的痛苦可以被量化为赔偿，谁的病痛必须被内化为自认倒霉。

空姐 Sophie 的胜利在于她打破了这种共谋。她不仅赢回了提前退休的权利，更重要的是，她通过法律手段强行把女性身体的损耗从“私领域”拉到了“公领域”。当法院承认夜班和电离辐射与乳腺癌的关系时，它实际上是在承认：女性在职场中承担的生物性代价，应当由雇主和制度买单，而不是由女性的生命质量来买单。

这场胜利是 good_news，但刺点在于：如果一个国家最致命的女性癌症需要个体通过两年的诉讼才能被认定为职业病，那么在那个清单被更新之前，还有多少女性在被结构性地地消耗？

A French flight attendant successfully had her breast cancer recognised as an occupational disease—a first for the aviation industry. While framed as a legal win, it is actually a belated correction of structural violence. In France, breast cancer is not on the official list of occupational diseases, meaning most women must fight through a legal black hole for years just to prove they aren't solely to blame for their own illness.

Why is it missing? Because the logic of these lists is masculine-centric. Occupational hazards are typically defined by factory chemicals or mine dust—damages linked to traditional "masculine" workspaces. Conversely, risks tied to female-dominated roles, such as endocrine disruption from night shifts or passive smoking, are dismissed as "lifestyle choices" or "individual disposition." This gap in recognition is meta-violence: by defining what counts as an occupational disease, the system decides whose suffering is compensable and whose is internalized as bad luck.

Sophie’s victory lies in breaking this complicity. She didn't just win early retirement; she forcibly dragged the biological toll of women's labor from the private sphere into the public record. When the court linked night work and radiation to cancer, it acknowledged that the biological cost women pay in the workforce should be billed to the employer and the state, not to the woman's quality of life.

This is good_news, but the sting remains: if the deadliest cancer for women in a country requires a two-year lawsuit to be recognised as work-related, how many other women are being structurally consumed while we wait for the list to be updated?
