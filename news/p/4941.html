冻结账户里的利息：一场关于“人道主义”的金融共谋Interest in Frozen Accounts: A Financial Complicity of 'Humanitarianism'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-11 § 链接
当救济金变成生息资产，人道主义就成了金融博弈的遮羞布。
When relief funds become interest-bearing assets, humanitarianism becomes a veil for financial gambling.

一个极其荒诞的事实：被冻结在巴克莱银行账户里的切尔西出售款，在过去几年里凭空产生了 1.75 亿英镑的利息。分析师戏称这是“全球最赚钱的足球生意”。但这根本不是什么生意，而是一场典型的 structural violence。在 Potential（资金本可用于救济乌克兰受害者）与 Actual（资金在银行账户里躺着生息）之间，巨大的差额就是对受害者的暴力。

这场博弈的精彩之处在于所有参与者都在扮演“正义”的角色。英国政府在 perform 它的强硬与人道，声称优先级是“最脆弱的群体”；阿布拉莫维奇在 perform 他的慷慨，承诺捐款但又在细节上通过 offshore companies 玩弄定义权，试图区分“全部款项”还是“利润部分”。而银行则在沉默中通过管理这些冻结资金获得实际收益。这是一个完美的共谋场域 (complicity field)：政府需要一个政治正确的姿态，寡头需要一个谈判的筹码，金融机构需要资产规模。

最令人作呕的武器化叙事是“人道主义”。当资金被冠以“救济”之名，它就获得了一种神圣的掩体，使得这笔钱在法律争端中被无限期拖延而无需承担道德压力。而此时，那些被承诺救助的受害者在现实中承受着 direct violence，他们的生命被量化成了银行账单上的利息增长率。这就是元暴力的运作方式：掌控解释权的权力中心，通过定义“如何正确地救助”，事实上剥夺了被救助者获得救济的权利。

这场“最赚钱的足球生意”揭示了一个真相：在权力博弈面前，受害者的痛苦只是一个可以被用来对冲风险的会计科目。

An absurd reality: the frozen proceeds from the sale of Chelsea FC have generated at least £175m in interest. Analysts jokingly call it the 'most profitable football business in the world.' But this is no business; it is a textbook case of structural violence. The gap between the Potential (funds that could have aided Ukrainian victims) and the Actual (funds sitting in a bank account accruing interest) is, in itself, an act of violence against the victims.

What makes this game fascinating is how every player performs 'justice.' The UK government performs its toughness and humanitarianism, claiming the 'most vulnerable' are the priority; Abramovich performs generosity, pledging donations while manipulating the definition of 'proceeds' versus 'profits' through a network of offshore companies. Meanwhile, the bank silently profits from managing these frozen assets. This is a perfect complicity field: the government needs a politically correct posture, the oligarch needs a bargaining chip, and the financial institution needs asset volume.

The most sickening weaponized narrative here is 'humanitarianism.' By labeling the funds as 'relief,' they are granted a sacred shield, allowing the money to be indefinitely delayed by legal disputes without any real moral cost. Meanwhile, the victims, facing direct violence in reality, see their lives quantified as interest rates on a bank statement. This is how meta-violence operates: the centers of power, by defining 'how to correctly provide aid,' effectively deprive the victims of the aid itself.

This 'most profitable football business' reveals a grim truth: in the face of power games, the suffering of victims is merely an accounting entry used to hedge risk.
