美人计是一门生意A Beauty Contest Was Her Audition

其他 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
她的悼词写的是美貌，不是演技
Her obituary remembers the face, not the actress.

一篇讣闻写完了 Elke Sommer 的一生，从头到尾最重的词是 bombshell。

她出生在柏林，牧师女儿，战时随家人疏散到巴伐利亚，原本想当翻译。1958 年在意大利赢了一场选美，被德西卡看见，然后就有了后面的戏。这条路径值得看清楚：她不是被导演从舞台上看中的，是被一场选美比赛筛选出来的。表达在这里先被定价——长相先于语言、先于她想当翻译的那个愿望，替她决定了职业。

她确实有本事。会多国语言，1961 年就拍了第一部英语片，1963 年被列为“世界上最令人兴奋的六位女性”之一。但讣闻里这些能力都排在“bombshell image”后面，作为点缀出现。报道说好莱坞“made much of her bombshell image”——这个说法很客气，实际机制是：一个行业发现某种表达能卖钱，就把它固定下来，反复生产。她在《黑夜怪枪》里“leaned heavily into her sexpot image”，一个被怀疑杀人的女佣。这不是她的选择被记录，是市场对一种表达的收购被记录。

她后来去拍英国性喜剧，《Percy》和《Percy's Progress》，还有《Carry on Behind》。一个拿过金球奖新人奖、和保罗·纽曼演对手戏的演员，中年之后进入的是这个片种。这不是个人品味问题，是定价体系的问题：这个行业给女性的表达定的价，随年龄折旧，而折旧之后唯一还有市场的表达是性。她于是继续供应。

报道里还有一段：她和 Zsa Zsa Gabor 因为一档电视节目结怨，最后因诽谤获赔 330 万美元。两个被同一种机制定价的女人，被安排在同一档节目里，然后互相开火。观众看的是“两个女明星吵架”，机制看的是收视率。这场官司她赢了，但赢的是一场被设计好的戏里的一句台词。

她结过两次婚，第二任丈夫 2026 年去世。报道没写她晚年怎么过，只写她“concentrated on painting”。这句话读起来像退场，其实也可能是她终于拿回了自己的表达——不再由选美比赛和片厂替她决定她是谁。

问题是，讣闻没有写这一层。它写的是一个美人活到 85 岁，死于心力衰竭。美貌是她的入场券，也是她的墓志铭，中间那个会几国语言、想当翻译的 Elke Schletz，始终没有出现在标题里。

The obituary runs the length of a life and the heaviest word in it is bombshell.

She was born in Berlin, a pastor's daughter, evacuated to Bavaria during the war, intending to become an interpreter. In 1958 she won a beauty contest in Italy, was spotted by De Sica, and the rest followed. Look at that path closely: she wasn't discovered on a stage. She was filtered out by a beauty pageant. Expression got priced before language did—her face decided her profession before her wish to translate ever got a hearing.

She had real gifts. She worked in several languages, made her first English-language film in 1961, and was billed as one of the six most exciting women in the world in 1963. But in the obituary those abilities trail behind the bombshell image, appearing as decoration. The report says Hollywood made much of her bombshell image—a polite way of describing the mechanism: an industry finds that a certain expression sells, fixes it in place, and reproduces it. In A Shot in the Dark she leaned heavily into her sexpot image as a maid suspected of murder. What's recorded there isn't her choice. It's the market's purchase of an expression.

Later she moved into British sex comedies, Percy and Percy's Progress, then Carry on Behind. An actor who had won a Golden Globe for most promising newcomer and played opposite Paul Newman ends up in that genre in middle age. That isn't a matter of taste. It's a pricing system: what this industry pays for a woman's expression depreciates with age, and after the depreciation the only expression still on the market is sex. So she kept supplying it.

There is also the feud with Zsa Zsa Gabor, which ended with $3.3m in defamation damages. Two women priced by the same mechanism, booked onto the same TV show, set to fire at each other. The audience saw two stars fighting; the mechanism saw ratings. She won the lawsuit, but what she won was a line in a script someone else had written.

She married twice; her second husband died in 2026. The obituary doesn't say how she spent her last years, only that she concentrated on painting. That reads like an exit, but it might also be the moment she finally took her expression back—no longer letting a pageant and a studio decide who she was.

The obituary doesn't say that part. It says a beauty lived to 85 and died of heart failure. Beauty was her ticket in and her epitaph, and the woman in between—the one who spoke several languages and wanted to be an interpreter—never makes it into the headline.
