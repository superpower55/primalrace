省下的钱不会变成内容The Savings Won’t Become Content

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要迪士尼在多个部门筹备新一轮裁员，涉及电视网络与电影制片厂，部分团队被要求实现最高20%的年度成本节约。自3月18日Josh D’Amaro接任CEO以来，公司已多轮裁员，包括4月1000个岗位、7月数百人，以及9月底和上周的数百人。公司全球员工超过23万。
The newsDisney is preparing another round of layoffs across divisions including TV networks and the movie studio, with some teams asked to hit up to 20% in annual cost savings. Since Josh D’Amaro became CEO on March 18, the company has cut 1,000 roles in April, several hundred in July, and hundreds more in late September and last week. Global headcount is just over 230,000.
裁员的成本被记在员工账上，节约的收益被记在增长账上
The cost of the cuts lands on workers; the benefit lands on growth.

迪士尼这轮裁员最值得看的不是裁多少人，是「20% 年度成本节约」这个指标怎么被造出来的。管理层先定一个节约目标，再把目标拆给各团队主管，主管再去决定谁走。数字在前，人在后。这套顺序本身就是结构暴力：先有账，再有人被从账上划掉。

D’Amaro 在财报会上说得很清楚：团队「更聪明地工作」，省下的资本就能「激进地再投资」到新内容和下一代体验。翻译一下，就是同一笔钱从工资单挪到内容预算，挪的过程里没有谁问过被挪走的人。Walden 在 Bloomberg 的会上把原因推给科技行业，「技术盯上了我们的生意，我们必须生存、繁荣、增长」。可报道里没有一处写到技术真的替代了哪个岗位，只写到裁的是电视网、制片厂、技术、HR、Imagineering。技术是叙事，账是事实。

这套叙事的功能是让裁员看起来像进化，而不是分配。文化暴力在这里干的活，就是让结构层的资源转移显得自然、正确、甚至值得鼓掌。读者看到「重塑组织」四个字，脑子里浮现的是效率，不是某个在 Glendale 的 Imagineering 员工上周收到的那封邮件。

还有一层没人提：这些被裁掉的人里，谁在承担照护责任、谁刚休完产假回来、谁的岗位在「成本中心」这个标签下最先被划进可节约的那一栏，报道一个字都没有。不是记者漏了，是这类数据从来不在公司的披露范围里。缺席本身就是材料。

省下来的 20% 会变成新内容，新内容会需要新的、更便宜的人来做。循环闭合，账平了。

The number worth looking at in this round of Disney layoffs isn’t how many people go. It’s how the 20% annual cost-savings target gets manufactured. Management sets a savings goal, hands it down to team managers, and the managers decide who leaves. The number comes first, the people come after. That order is structural violence: first the ledger, then the names struck from it.

D’Amaro said it plainly on the earnings call: when teams work smarter, the freed capital can be reinvested aggressively in new content and next-generation experiences. Same money, moved from payroll to content budget, and nobody in the move asks the people being moved.

Walden, at Bloomberg’s conference, blamed the tech industry: technology set its sights on our business, and we must survive and thrive. But the report never shows technology actually replacing a single role. What it shows is cuts in TV networks, the movie studio, tech, HR, Imagineering. Technology is the narrative. The ledger is the fact.

The job of that narrative is to make layoffs look like evolution rather than distribution. This is cultural violence doing its usual work: making a structural transfer of resources look natural, correct, even applause-worthy. The reader sees reshape our organization and pictures efficiency, not the email someone at the Glendale Imagineering campus got last week.

One layer goes unmentioned: among those cut, who carries caregiving responsibilities, who just returned from parental leave, whose job sat in the cost-center column first. The report says nothing. That isn’t an oversight. That data was never in the disclosure scope to begin with. The absence is the material.

The 20% becomes new content. The new content needs new, cheaper people to make it. The loop closes. The books balance.
