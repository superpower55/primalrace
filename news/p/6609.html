导弹被拦下了，医院没有The Missile Was Intercepted. The Hospitals Weren't.

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
被拦截的导弹上头条，被围困的医院不上。
An intercepted missile makes the headline. A besieged hospital doesn't.

这条新闻里真正被拦下的只有一枚导弹。塔伊兹的120家卫生中心已经停运，另有66家接近关停，这件事没有被拦截，也不会被拦截。

加尔通的公式在这里很好用：也门本可以达到的状态，和它现在实际的状态，中间那道差额就是暴力。这道差额不是导弹造成的，是封锁造成的。胡塞武装从十月初切断通往塔伊兹的补给路线，食物、燃料、药品进不去，一座约一百万人的城市就这么被掐着。过去两周，至少23辆卡车在绕路时损坏或翻覆。这些卡车司机不是士兵，他们是在试图把药送进一座正在关门的城市，然后连人带车翻在山路上。

直接层的暴力是可见的：导弹、无人机、空袭、荷台达拉斯伊萨港码头的4名伤者、巴吉勒区石油公司设施的3名伤者。这些数字有出处，有画面，有发言人。结构层的暴力是缓慢的：120家卫生中心停运，66家濒临停运，这是没有施暴者面孔的死亡，它不会上头条，因为它没有事件。

而文化层在干什么？沙特民防部门发布警报，警告阿布哈、海米斯穆谢特、塔伊夫、奈季兰可能面临危险，然后警报解除。同一份警报里还有一条：警告居民不要拍摄、发布或分享与导弹和无人机袭击有关的信息。这条禁令比任何一枚导弹都更精确地说明了这场战争里谁掌握解释权。导弹可以被拦截，画面不可以被传播。

也门政府军的发言人纳齐利说，24小时内进行了1272次“打击行动”，打死约500名胡塞武装人员，摧毁或瘫痪近170辆车辆、装备或重型武器。半岛电视台自己注明无法独立核实。1272、500、170，这三个数字被原样播出，它们和“120家卫生中心停运”并排放在同一篇报道里，读者被邀请去相信前者是战况，后者是背景。

共谋不需要密谋。只需要把“打击行动”写成主动语态，把“卫生中心停运”写成被动语态；只需要让一方有发言人和数字，让另一方只有“附属媒体”和“报道称”；只需要在警报里顺手禁止拍摄。这就是元暴力在战争报道里的日常形态：不是谁在撒谎，而是谁被允许把话说完。

塔伊兹省长呼吁紧急援助，一位叫努尔·丁·曼苏里的委员会成员告诉半岛电视台23辆卡车的事。这两个名字在报道里出现了一次，然后就没了。他们不是交战方，他们是在计算一座城市还能撑多久的人。

一枚导弹被拦截，值得写进标题。一座城市的医院在关门，值得写进第七段。这就是这篇报道的排序，也是这场战争的排序。

差额还在扩大：66家卫生中心还没关，23辆卡车还没到。它们会不会被算进下一枚被拦截的导弹的新闻里，取决于有没有人继续数。

Only one missile was intercepted in this story. In Taiz, 120 health centres have already stopped operating and another 66 are close to shutting down. Nobody intercepted that, and nobody will.

Galtung's formula fits cleanly here: the gap between what Yemen could be and what it is, that gap is the violence. The gap isn't made of missiles. It's made of a siege. The Houthis cut supply routes into Taiz in early October, and food, fuel and medicine stopped going in. A city of about one million people is being held by the throat. In the past two weeks, at least 23 trucks were damaged or overturned while trying to find alternative routes. Those drivers aren't soldiers. They were trying to deliver medicine to a city that is closing down, and they went off the mountain roads with their trucks.

Direct violence is visible: missiles, drones, air strikes, four wounded at the Ras Isa port dock, three at an oil company site in Bajil. These numbers have sources, images, spokespeople. Structural violence is slow: 120 health centres down, 66 about to go. That's death without a perpetrator's face. It doesn't make headlines because it isn't an event.

And what is the cultural layer doing? The Saudi Civil Defence authority issued alerts warning of potential danger in Abha, Khamis Mushait, Taif and Najran, then lifted them. The same alert carried another line: residents were warned against filming, publishing or sharing information related to missile and drone attacks. That prohibition explains this war's distribution of interpretive power more precisely than any missile. Missiles can be intercepted. Images cannot be circulated.

Yemeni army spokesman Colonel Majed Abdullah al-Nazili said government forces conducted 1,272 "targeting operations" in 24 hours, killed roughly 500 Houthi fighters and destroyed or disabled nearly 170 vehicles, equipment or heavy weaponry. Al Jazeera notes it could not independently verify the claims. The 1,272, the 500, the 170 run as written, placed in the same article as the 120 closed health centres, and the reader is invited to treat the first set as the war and the second as background.

Complicity doesn't require a conspiracy. It requires active voice for "targeting operations" and passive voice for "health centres stopped operating." It requires one side with a spokesman and numbers, the other with "Houthi-affiliated media" and "reported." It requires slipping a filming ban into a public alert. This is meta-violence in its everyday wartime form: not who is lying, but who gets to finish a sentence.

The governor of Taiz called for urgent help. A committee member named Nour al-Din al-Mansouri told Al Jazeera about the 23 trucks. Those two names appear once and are gone. They aren't belligerents. They are the people calculating how much longer a city can hold.

A missile intercepted belongs in the headline. A city's hospitals closing belongs in paragraph seven. That is this article's ordering, and it is this war's ordering.

The gap is still widening: 66 health centres haven't closed yet, 23 trucks haven't arrived. Whether they get counted depends on whether anyone keeps counting.
