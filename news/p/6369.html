一万个游戏，没人多下一单Ten Thousand Games, Not One More Download

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
AI 把表达的成本压到零，却没有多造出一个读者
AI drove the cost of expression to zero and created not one new reader.

itch.io 每天新建的游戏页面从八月的一千多天前的三千涨到六千，下载和购买没有跟着翻倍。这是第三章最干净的一次演示：表达被武器化，靠的不是哪一个坏人的阴谋，是产能本身。

表达原本是稀缺的，因为稀缺，它才有价值，才构成身份的确证。一个人花两年做一款游戏，这两年就是他的票，投进去，换一个被看见的位置。现在生成式 AI 把这张票的成本压到接近零，六千个页面里大部分是 AI 做的，意味着六千次投票，投的是同一个东西：没有人的时间在里面。

真正被抢走的不是销量，是可见性。报道说得很清楚，itch.io 本来就是学生作品、game jam、慈善包里高知名度独立游戏共存的地方，它的价值恰恰在于门槛低到几乎任何人都能上架。现在这个低门槛被反向利用了——门槛低本来是给没有资源的独立开发者开的门，现在成了批量生产的入口。Steam Next Fest 的 demo 被淹没或者被顶替，同一个机制换了个平台再演一遍。

平台自己也在共谋，只是共谋得很被动。itch.io 要求开发者披露是否使用 AI，这是把责任推给填写表格的人；一个每天新增六千页面的系统，没有能力逐个核实，披露就成了一种仪式。报道给出的唯一出路是平台“真正下定决心”限制 AI 内容——这句话本身就是承认，现在的规则制定者没有这个意愿，因为页面数、流量、上架量对平台是好看的指标，对独立开发者是灾难。指标好看和生态健康，从来是两件事。

这件事里没有直接的施暴者，只有一层很安静的结构暴力：资源（注意力）的分配机制被改了，改的方式是让所有人都更难被看见，而改的人不需要出面。

被削掉的是独立开发者用时间换来的那点差额。他们本来可以靠手艺在一个小平台上被找到，现在要在一个六千分之一的位置上被找到。这个差额没有被任何一方补上，也没有人负责。

六千个页面里，有多少是有人真的想让人玩的东西，报道没写。这个数字大概也没人统计得出来——这正是它最方便的地方。

itch.io's daily game pages went from around 3,000 in August to over 6,000 by October 30. Downloads and purchases did not double. This is the cleanest demonstration of Chapter 3: expression weaponized not by a villain's plot but by sheer capacity.

Expression used to be scarce, and scarcity gave it value—it was the confirmation of existence. Someone spends two years on a game; those two years are their vote, cast to earn a place where they can be seen. Generative AI pushes the cost of that vote toward zero. Most of the 6,000 pages are AI-made: 6,000 votes cast for the same thing—no human time inside.

What's being stolen isn't sales. It's visibility. The report is precise: itch.io was built for student projects, game jams, high-profile indies in charity bundles—its value lay in a barrier low enough that almost anyone could publish. That low barrier was a door opened for developers without resources. Now it's an entrance for mass production. Steam Next Fest demos get drowned out or displaced—same mechanism, different storefront.

The platform is complicit, passively. itch.io asks developers to disclose AI use, which outsources responsibility to whoever fills out a form. A system gaining 6,000 pages a day cannot verify them one by one; disclosure becomes ritual. The report's only way out is platforms "really putting their foot down"—an admission that the current rule-makers don't want to. Page counts and traffic look good for the platform and are a disaster for indie devs. Good metrics and a healthy ecosystem have never been the same thing.

No direct perpetrator here. Just a quiet structural violence: the allocation of attention was rewritten so that everyone is harder to see, and whoever rewrote it never had to show up.

The gap being erased is the one indie developers earned with time. They used to be findable on a small platform through craft. Now they have to be found in one slot out of six thousand. Nobody fills that gap. Nobody is responsible for it.

How many of those 6,000 pages contain something someone actually wants played—the report doesn't say. Probably no one can count it. That's exactly what makes it convenient.
