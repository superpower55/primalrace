猛犸象的骨骸与文明的幻觉Mammoth Bones and the Illusion of Civilization

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
自然灾害是直接暴力，而将其视为“意外”则是结构性共谋。
Natural disasters are direct violence; treating them as 'accidents' is structural complicity.

多瑙河和莱茵河的干涸，在新闻叙事里被处理成一场关于“能源危机”和“猛犸象化石”的奇观。但如果套用加尔通的暴力三角，这就是一次典型的 structural violence 集中爆发。所谓的“气候紧急状态”不是一个突发事故，而是一个由工业文明、资本扩张和男性中心主义驱动的长期掠夺结果。Potential（一个可持续的生态系统）与 Actual（干涸的河床与停摆的核电站）之间的差额，就是这个时代最巨大的暴力。

有趣的是，当核电站因为冷却水不足而不得不停机，匈牙利和罗马尼亚的领导者们在 X 上呼吁“克制”和“减少用电”。这种叙事将危机定义为“此时此刻”的资源短缺，而巧妙地抹去了对过去一个世纪以来环境破坏的解释权。这就是典型的 cultural violence：用“自然灾害”的词汇掩盖“结构性毁灭”的事实，让人们在恐慌中寻找临时的最优解，而不是质疑那个制造危机的系统。

而那个被意外挖掘出的猛犸象骨骸，成了这场灾难中最讽刺的注脚。它像一个沉默的证人，提醒我们所谓的“文明”不过是在原初种族——自然与生物多样性——的尸骸上建立的临时营地。当水退去，露出的不仅是二战的炸弹，更是这个男性中心叙事下，对自然资源进行无止尽 a-priori 剥削后的真实底色。我们习惯于将自然视为可被定价、可被调度、可被牺牲的客体，直到这个客体通过一次干涸，把我们所有人的存在性战争直接推向了停电的绝境。

The drying of the Danube and the Rhine is framed in news narratives as a spectacle of 'energy crises' and 'mammoth fossils.' But through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this is a textbook eruption of structural violence. The 'climate emergency' is not a sudden accident, but the result of a long-term plunder driven by industrial civilization, capital expansion, and a masculine-centric narrative. The gap between the Potential (a sustainable ecosystem) and the Actual (parched riverbeds and stalled nuclear plants) is the most immense violence of our era.

It is telling that as nuclear plants shut down due to lack of cooling water, leaders in Hungary and Romania call for 'restraint' and 'reduced energy use' on X. This narrative defines the crisis as a temporary resource shortage, skillfully erasing the accountability for a century of environmental destruction. This is classic cultural violence: using the vocabulary of 'natural disaster' to mask 'structural destruction,' forcing people to seek short-term optimal expressions while ignoring the system that manufactured the crisis.

The unearthed remains of a woolly mammoth serve as the most ironic footnote. It stands as a silent witness, reminding us that our so-called 'civilization' is merely a temporary camp built upon the corpses of the Primal Race—nature and biodiversity. As the water recedes, what emerges are not just WWII bombs, but the raw reality of an a-priori exploitation of natural resources under a masculine-centric narrative. We are accustomed to treating nature as an object to be priced, scheduled, and sacrificed, until that object, through a single drought, pushes our existential war directly into a blackout.
