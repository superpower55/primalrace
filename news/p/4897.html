荒野的浪漫，是殖民者的认知入口The Romance of Wilderness as a Colonial Entry Point

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-10 § 链接
所谓的“纯净荒野”，通常是抹除原住民主体性后的叙事伪装。
The so-called 'pure wilderness' is often a narrative disguise used to erase indigenous subjectivity.

这篇文章读起来像是一次典型的中产阶级精神洗礼：Gore-Tex 冲锋衣、冰川融水、以及在北极圈尽头寻找某种“纯粹”的朝圣感。作者在文中试图通过引用向导的话来反思“荒野”（wilderness）这个词的误导性，承认这里是萨米人（Sami）生活了数千年的家园。但这种反思极其廉价，因为它被包裹在一种极其危险的“浪漫爱”叙事里——对自然与远方的迷恋。

最讽刺的细节在于那座 Alkavare 小教堂。作者在赞美其绝美风景的同时，轻描淡写地提到这座教堂在 18 世纪是“强制基督教化”和“根除萨米萨满信仰”的工具。在加尔通的暴力三角里，这就是典型的 cultural violence（文化暴力）在为 structural violence（结构暴力）洗地。曾经的强制洗脑和文化殖民，在两百年后变成了现代徒步者眼中一个“充满灵性的打卡点”。

这种叙事将殖民史“景观化”了。当作者感叹这里是“欧洲最狂野的朝圣之路”时，他实际上是在消费一个被强行剥夺了主体性的族群所留下的伤痕。对于萨米人来说，这里的每一块石头可能都记录着被殖民的痛苦，但在认知入口被掌控的旅游叙事中，这些痛苦被过滤成了“文化价值”和“世界遗产”。

这本质上是一次认知层面的 weaponization（武器化）。通过将殖民遗迹包装成“荒野探索”和“精神朝圣”，原本的元暴力（meta-violence）被稀释成了某种具有异域风情的审美体验。当一个群体地盘上的血泪被定义为另一个群体的“冒险乐园”时，这种表达本身就是一种持续的暴力。

This piece reads like a typical middle-class spiritual cleanse: Gore-Tex jackets, glacial water, and a quest for 'purity' at the edge of the Arctic Circle. The author attempts a superficial reflection on the term 'wilderness,' acknowledging that the land has been shaped by the Sami people for millennia. However, this reflection is cheap, wrapped in a dangerous romantic narrative of nature and far-off lands.

The most ironic detail is the Alkavare chapel. While praising its beauty, the author mentions in passing that the chapel was a tool for 'forced Christianisation' and the 'eradication' of Sami shamanistic beliefs in the 18th century. In Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is a textbook example of cultural violence legitimizing structural violence. What was once forced brainwashing and colonial erasure has become, centuries later, a 'spiritual landmark' for modern hikers.

This is the 'spectacularization' of colonial history. When the author describes this as 'Europe’s wildest pilgrimage route,' he is consuming the scars of a race whose subjectivity was forcibly stripped away. For the Sami, every stone may record the pain of colonization, but in a tourism narrative where the cognitive entry points are controlled, this pain is filtered into 'cultural value' and 'World Heritage.'

Essentially, this is the weaponisation of expression. By packaging colonial ruins as 'wilderness exploration' and 'pilgrimage,' the original meta-violence is diluted into an exotic aesthetic experience. When the blood and tears of one group's land are defined as another's 'adventure playground,' the expression itself becomes a form of enduring violence.
