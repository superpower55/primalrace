Ofsted 的“海鸥模式”：一场关于权力凝视的表演Ofsted's 'Seagull Mode': A Performance of Power and Gaze

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-08 § 链接
将“检查”改为“评估”只是词汇游戏，除非权力结构从凝视变为共情。
Changing 'Inspection' to 'Evaluation' is a word game unless the power structure shifts from gaze to empathy.

Ofsted 的这场所谓“改革”再次证明了结构层暴力的顽固：它试图通过微调评分系统来平息愤怒，但本质上，它依然在维持一种 top-down 的凝视权力。两位写信者的描述极其精准——一方将其比作“海鸥”，飞进来制造噪音然后留下排泄物；另一方将其比作“警察搜查”，在怀疑中审视记录。这根本不是教育质量的评估，而是一场关于“服从”与“标准”的权力博弈。

这种“检查” (Inspection) 机制是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。它追求的是绝对的量化、等级化的判定以及一种不可挑战的权威结论。在这种叙事里，学校、教师和学生被客体化为被审视的样本，而检查员则扮演着那个掌控解释权的上帝。当一个机构被评为“需要改进”时，其造成的不是专业上的指引，而是 staff morale 的崩塌和精神健康的损毁。这种从 structural 层传导至 direct 层的压力，正是元暴力在教育系统中的具体投射。

有趣的是，文中提到的“自我评估” (Self-evaluation) 模式其实提供了一种 Just Expression 的可能：让身在其中的主体参与定义什么是“成就”，将权力从外部的凝视者移交给内部的实践者。但我们要警惕，如果这种模式被 Ofsted 这种权力机器简单地“采纳”为一种新的管理工具，它极有可能演变成另一种更隐蔽的共谋——强迫学校在自我审查中扮演“优秀”的角色，以换取一个体面的等级。真正的 good_news 不应该是检查员变得“有礼貌”，而应该是解释权本身的换手。

The proposed overhaul of Ofsted proves the tenacity of structural violence: it attempts to soothe anger by tinkering with grading systems, yet it maintains a top-down gaze of power. The letters describe it perfectly—one as a 'seagull' that flies in, makes noise, and leaves guano; the other as a 'police search' conducted under suspicion. This is not an assessment of educational quality; it is a power game of 'obedience' and 'standards.'

This 'Inspection' mechanism is a textbook masculine-centric narrative. It craves absolute quantification, hierarchical judgment, and an unchallengeable authoritative conclusion. In this narrative, schools, teachers, and students are objectified as samples, while inspectors play the role of the god who monopolizes the right of interpretation. When an institution is rated 'requires improvement,' the result is not professional guidance, but the collapse of staff morale and mental wellbeing. This pressure, flowing from the structural layer to the direct layer, is the concrete projection of meta-violence within the education system.

Interestingly, the 'Self-evaluation' model mentioned offers a possibility for Just Expression: shifting the power to define 'achievement' from the external gazer to the internal practitioners. However, we must be wary. If this model is simply 'adopted' by a power machine like Ofsted as a new management tool, it may evolve into a more insidious form of complicity—forcing schools to perform 'excellence' through self-censorship to secure a decent grade. True good_news is not when inspectors become 'courteous,' but when the right of interpretation actually changes hands.
