围城不只是断水断粮A Siege That Rewrites People

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
先改身份，再改课程，最后改人。
First cut the water. Then rewrite the curriculum. Then remake the people.

沙姆桑把塔伊兹的处境说成“回到石器时代”，这个说法太客气了。石器时代的人至少不用在胡塞武装的路障前，看着每天三万五千立方米的水需求被砍到一千立方米。围城从来不是只为了断水断粮。

看这位省长列出来的东西：水、氧气、燃料、面粉、透析。这些是直接暴力在起作用，身体一层一层地被削掉。但真正让这套直接暴力站得住的是后面那段话——被占领村庄换了新的宗教领袖，教育和宗教课程被改写，居民被要求出示对宰德派教义的忠诚、签表声明自己加入了胡塞武装，每个村子还有一批孩子不经父母同意被送上萨达前线。

水断了，人活不下去；课程改了，活下来的人也不再是原来的人。这两件事是同一场战争的两个阶段。围城是手段，改身份才是目的。沙姆桑自己说得很清楚：“这不是一场军事战争，而是一场复仇战争”，“他们开启的不是战争，而是一场身份革命”。一个省长能说出这句话，说明他看得懂对方在做什么。

胡塞领导人的回应是另一套叙事：“路是通的，食物、药品、人道援助都能进，连沙特支持的团体都能进，谁想核实塔伊兹没有被围，可以自己去看。”这是第三章里最标准的武器化操作：夺取“什么是事实”的制造权。八百多万人困在城里，路障在胡塞手里，移动网络在胡塞手里，连市民之间的通话都被监听，然后他说“欢迎来核实”。核实的前提是能进去、能自由走动、能说话不被记录。这些前提全被同一只手掐着，所谓“开放核实”就是一句自证清白的话术。

人权观察的贾法尔尼亚提到“12年之久的围城”，这个时间尺度是关键。12年里，轰炸、饥饿、阻断援助，国际法被违反到什么程度，报道里只用了一个词：scant。塔伊兹不是今天才被围的，它是被围了12年之后，才轮到水、氧气和透析一起见底。

沙姆桑的诉求也很直白： solidarity statements 没用，要的是援助机构、包括联合国，能真正把物资送进城。这句话里藏着一个尴尬：一个被围城市的省长，在国际媒体上喊话，能做的只是请人来送水。而胡塞那边，只要路障不动，谁来都进不去。

报道没写的是，这12年里国际社会到底谈成了什么、又执行了什么。一个“月长”的围城被拿出来讲，可它前面站着12年。真正的问题不是这次围城有多新，而是为什么围了12年，塔伊兹的水还要靠每天一千立方米来计量。

The governor says the Houthis are sending Taiz "back to the stone age." That's too generous. Stone age people didn't have to watch their daily water need of 35,000 cubic metres get cut to 1,000 at a checkpoint.

Look at what he lists: water, oxygen, fuel, wheat, dialysis. That's direct violence doing its work, stripping the body layer by layer. But what holds that direct violence up is the paragraph after it — captured villages handed to new religious leaders, education and religious curricula rewritten, residents required to show loyalty to the Zaydi religion and sign forms affirming they joined the Houthis, and children from each village ordered to the frontlines in Saada without their parents' consent.

Cut the water and people die. Rewrite the curriculum and the survivors are no longer the same people. These are two stages of one war. The siege is the method; changing identity is the goal. Shamsan says it himself: "This is not a military war but a war of vengeance," and "they are not starting a war but a revolution in identity." A governor who can name what the other side is doing is not confused about the stakes.

The Houthi leader's answer is the other narrative: "The way is open for food, medicine, and humanitarian aid to enter the city... Anyone who wants to verify that there is no siege on the city of Taiz can go to the city and see." This is the textbook weaponisation move — seize the power to manufacture what counts as fact. More than 800,000 people are inside, the roads are in Houthi hands, the mobile networks are in Houthi hands, calls are being eavesdropped on, and then comes the invitation to come verify. Verification requires access, movement, and the ability to speak without being recorded. The same hand controls all three preconditions. "Come and see" is a self-issued certificate of innocence.

Niku Jafarnia of Human Rights Watch mentions "the 12-year-long siege of Taiz." That timescale is the point. Twelve years of bombing, starvation, and blocked aid, with, in the report's word, scant adherence to international law. Taiz was not besieged today. It was besieged for 12 years before the water, oxygen, and dialysis all ran out at once.

Shamsan's ask is blunt: statements of solidarity are of no use; what's needed is access for aid agencies, including the UN, to deliver supplies. Inside that sentence is the awkward truth — a governor of a besieged city, speaking to international media, can only ask people to come bring water. The Houthis, as long as the roadblocks stay, don't have to let anyone in.

What the report doesn't say is what the international community actually negotiated and enforced across those 12 years. A "month-long" siege is being reported, but it stands on top of 12 years. The real question isn't how new this siege is. It's why, after 12 years, Taiz still measures its water in a thousand cubic metres a day.
