用一次性烧烤掩盖的生态殖民Ecological Colonialism Masked by Disposable Barbecues

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
拒绝命名问题是最高级的武器化表达，旨在用微观琐事消解结构性暴力。
Refusing to name the problem is the ultimate weaponized expression, using trivialities to dissolve structural violence.

Andy Burnham 的沉默不是由于迟钝，而是一场精心设计的认知博弈。当第五次热浪席卷英国，一个首相在 Cobra 会议后将注意力引导至“一次性烧烤” (disposable barbecues)，这在表达逻辑上是一个极其阴险的置换：他试图将一个 structural violence（结构性暴力）——由化石燃料驱动的气候危机，降维成一个 individual behavior（个人行为）——民众乱扔烧烤架导致山火的问题。

这就是典型的 weaponized expression。通过拒绝给危机“命名”，Burnham 实际上在夺取解释权。只要不承认 climate crisis 是 driving cause，政府就可以在接下来的 Rosebank 和 Jackdaw 油气田钻探决策中，继续扮演一个“理性、务实”的经济管理者，而非一个在生态种族灭绝面前失职的共谋者。他用微观的道德指责（指责民众用烧烤架）来掩盖宏观的利益勾兑。

有趣的是，外交大臣 Ed Miliband 却在社交媒体上大谈国家安全。这种政府内部的叙事撕裂，恰恰暴露了他们正在进行的共谋博弈：一个人负责在国际舞台上维持“进步派”的人设，另一个人则在权力中心通过沉默来保护化石燃料资本的利润。这种“好警察/坏警察”的配合，让 Potential（本可达到的低碳转型）与 Actual（继续钻探）之间的差额被进一步拉大。

不要被这种“敦促首相发声”的叙事感动。真正的 victory 不是首相在压力下说了几句正确的话，而是他是否停止对北海油田的授权。如果命名之后依然钻探，那么这种“发声”不过是另一种形式的 cultural violence——一种表演性的让步，旨在安抚大众，好让权力者能更安静地完成对地球的最后一次掠夺。

Andy Burnham's silence is not a lapse in judgment, but a calculated cognitive game. As the fifth heatwave grips the UK, the Prime Minister's decision to focus on "disposable barbecues" after a Cobra meeting is a sinister displacement of expression: he is attempting to downgrade a structural violence—the fossil-fuel-driven climate crisis—into a matter of individual behavior—citizens mishandling BBQ grills.

This is the essence of weaponized expression. By refusing to "name" the crisis, Burnham is seizing the power of interpretation. As long as the climate crisis is not acknowledged as the driving cause, the government can continue to pose as "rational and pragmatic" economic managers while authorizing drilling in the Rosebank and Jackdaw fields, rather than being exposed as complicitors in an ecological genocide. He uses micro-moralizing to mask macro-collusion.

Notably, Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband is simultaneously framing the crisis as a "national security issue" on social media. This narrative fracture within the government reveals a strategic complicity: one official maintains the "progressive" persona for the international stage, while the other ensures the profits of fossil fuel capital through strategic silence. This "good cop/bad cop" routine only widens the gap between the potential of a green transition and the actual reality of continued extraction.

We must be wary of the narrative that simply "urging the PM to speak" is a solution. True victory is not a Prime Minister uttering the right words under pressure, but the actual cessation of North Sea drilling. If the naming is followed by drilling, such "expression" is merely another form of cultural violence—a performative concession designed to pacify the public while the powerful finalize the plunder of the planet.
