用强奸作为边境管理的注脚，是最高级的文化暴力Using Rape as a Footnote for Border Control is the Ultimate Cultural Violence

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-04 § 链接
将女性身体的破碎作为政策辩护，本质是元暴力的共谋。
Using the shattering of female bodies to justify policy is a complicity in meta-violence.

内政大臣 Shabana Mahmood 的这段表述极其阴险：她承认了几乎所有通过小船抵达英国的女性都遭受过强奸，但她把这个事实当作了“边境失控”的证据，而非“制度性谋杀”的指控。在她的叙事里，强奸是犯罪团伙的行径，而政府只是一个无奈的观察者。这正是典型的文化暴力 (cultural violence) —— 通过定义施暴者为“外部犯罪分子”，将政府在 structural 层面上关闭安全通道、逼迫女性进入掠食者领地的责任给抹除了。

这是一种极其恶劣的共谋 (complicity)。政府一边在公共空间表演对女性受难的“承认”，一边在制度层面通过关闭家庭团聚等 safe routes，将女性身体直接推向那些必然会发生强奸的路径。在这种博弈中，女性的身体成了一个被定价的筹码：她们必须先被摧毁，才能获得一个被讨论的资格。这种“承认”不是救赎，而是一种 weaponized 的叙事，旨在证明目前的边境管理多么“艰巨”，从而为更严酷的管控提供合法性。

最讽刺的是，Mahmood 试图用自己的穆斯林身份和被极右翼厌恶的处境来建立一种“受害者同盟”，试图以此掩盖她作为权力执行者在元暴力 (meta violence) 中的角色。当一个女性权力持有者在谈论其他女性被强奸时，如果她不追问“是谁关闭了她们的安全通道”，那么她就成了这套男性中心叙事的共谋者。在这种逻辑下，女性的苦难被简化成了边境管理的一个数据点，而真正的暴力——那种由法律和政策制造的、让女性不得不面对强奸的结构性暴力——在这次“诚实”的采访中被彻底隐形了。

Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood’s statement is profoundly insidious: she acknowledges that nearly all women arriving by small boats have been raped, yet she presents this horror as evidence of "border chaos" rather than an indictment of "institutional murder." In her narrative, rape is the act of criminal gangs, while the government is merely a helpless observer. This is classic cultural violence — by defining the perpetrators as "external criminals," the government erases its own structural responsibility for closing safe routes and forcing women into the path of predators.

This is a vile form of complicity. The government performs an "acknowledgment" of female suffering in the public sphere, while simultaneously closing safe routes like family reunions, effectively pushing women's bodies directly into territories where rape is inevitable. In this game, the female body is a priced chip: they must first be broken to earn the right to be discussed. This "recognition" is not rescue; it is a weaponized narrative used to prove how "challenging" border management is, thereby legitimizing even harsher controls.

The irony peaks when Mahmood attempts to use her Muslim identity and the hatred she receives from the far-right to build a "victim alliance," masking her role as an executor of power within the framework of meta-violence. When a female power-holder speaks of other women being raped, if she fails to ask "who closed their safe routes," she becomes a co-conspirator in the masculine-centric narrative. Under this logic, female suffering is reduced to a data point for border security, while the true violence — the structural violence created by laws and policies that make rape an inevitability — is rendered invisible by this "honest" interview.
