票房榜不写谁的身体在付账The Box Office Never Says Whose Body Is Paying

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
一部恐怖片的400M，是另一群人的身体被反复定价
A horror film's $400M is another group's bodies, priced over and over

《Resident Evil》全球票房到400M了，报道说它“never been walking dead at the box office”。一部恐怖片能拍到第七周还在66个市场2万1千多块银幕上收钱，中国贡献71.4M，是这部片子在中国的恐怖片历史第三。这个数字本身没什么可骂的，可它值得看一眼：恐怖片是少数几个把女性的尖叫、流血、被追逐的身体做成标准件、再卖给全球观众的片种，它卖得越好，说明这套标准件的模具越成熟。

再看《Other Mommy》，报道专门写了它在德国、荷兰、奥地利、瑞士、捷克开画的成绩，跟M3GAN、Smile、The Black Phone挨个比。一部非系列恐怖片能拿到“今年非系列恐怖片开画最大”的名头，靠的是万圣节这个档期。万圣节是什么？是一年一度允许人们扮演他者、扮演恐怖、扮演女人的夜晚。这套装扮的生意做得越大，越说明扮演是被许可的、被定价的、被限定在特定日期的。

报道里最值得看的一句是“Halloween puts a damper on the domestic box office, but that’s not necessarily the case around the world”。同一个节日，英国是大档期，西班牙和法国就不行，法国还“still impacted by protests”。墨西哥和拉美过的是亡灵节，也影响观影。节日不是自然现象，是被安排出来的时间表，谁在这一天出门、谁在这一天被挡在家里，票房数字就是这张时间表的读数。

《Clayface》拿了周末第一，22.8M，制作成本净44M，全球累计83M。报道列了它的分市场：英国6.1M、澳大利亚2.5M、墨西哥2.5M、法国2.4M、巴西1.7M、意大利1.7M、西班牙1.4M、沙特1.4M、德国1.3M、阿联酋1.2M、台湾949K、韩国891K。这张表看着像地理，其实是购买力排序。沙特和阿联酋排在德国前面，不是因为那里的人更爱看电影，是因为那里的票价和汇率让同样的上座率换算成更多的美元。票房榜从来不是“谁看了”，是“谁的钱更值钱”。

《The Odyssey》庆祝的是环球影业国际累计票房历史第一，1.17B，超过《Furious 7》的1.16B。报道写它“continues to take victory laps”。一部电影跑了十二周还在中国收300K，在比利时第十六周还能拿第一。这种长尾不是口碑的自然结果，是发行方一块银幕一块银幕谈下来的排片。银幕是有限的，一部片子占着第十二周的厅，就有另一部片子进不来。

整篇报道里没有一个人。没有观众，没有影院员工，没有因为法国抗议而没能出门的人。只有数字、市场、银幕数。这不是记者的疏忽，这是票房报道的文体本身：它把人换算成美元，然后宣布这就是全部事实。

Resident Evil has hit $400M worldwide, and the trade press notes it has "never been walking dead at the box office." A horror film still collecting in its seventh weekend across 66 markets and 21,500+ screens, with China contributing $71.4M — the third-highest horror gross in that market ever. There is nothing scandalous about the number itself. What is worth a look is what the genre does: horror is one of the few categories that manufactures women's screaming, bleeding, pursued bodies into a standard part and sells it to a global audience. The better it sells, the more mature the mold.

Then Other Mommy, whose opening weekends in Germany, the Netherlands, Austria, German-speaking Switzerland and Czech Republic/Slovakia the report compares one by one against M3GAN, Smile, The Black Phone. A non-franchise horror film earning "the biggest opening for a non-franchise Horror this year" — on Halloween. Halloween is the one night a year when playing the other, playing the monstrous, playing the woman, is licensed. The bigger that costume business gets, the clearer it is that the playing is permitted, priced, and confined to a date.

The most telling line in the piece: "Halloween puts a damper on the domestic box office, but that's not necessarily the case around the world." The same holiday is a major frame in the UK, not so much in Spain and France — France still "impacted by protests." Mexico and Latin America have Día de los muertos, which also shapes attendance. A holiday is not weather. It is a schedule someone arranged. Who goes out that day and who is kept home is what the ticket number reads.

Clayface took the weekend with $22.8M against a net $44M production, $83M running worldwide. The territory list: UK $6.1M, Australia $2.5M, Mexico $2.5M, France $2.4M, Brazil $1.7M, Italy $1.7M, Spain $1.4M, Saudi Arabia $1.4M, Germany $1.3M, UAE $1.2M, Taiwan $949K, Korea $891K. This reads like geography; it is purchasing power sorted. Saudi Arabia and the UAE ahead of Germany is not because more people there love movies — it is because ticket prices and exchange rates turn the same attendance into more dollars. The box office chart never says who watched. It says whose money is worth more.

The Odyssey is celebrating the biggest international cume of all time for a Universal release, $1.17B, past Furious 7's $1.16B. It "continues to take victory laps." Twelve weekends in, still $300K from China; sixteenth weekend in Belgium, still No. 1. That tail is not the natural reward of word of mouth. It is screens, negotiated one by one. Screens are finite. A film holding a hall in its twelfth weekend is a film another title cannot enter.

There is not a single person in this report. No audience, no cinema staff, no one in France kept home by the protests. Only numbers, markets, screen counts. That is not the reporter's oversight. It is the genre of box office coverage itself: convert people into dollars, then declare that the whole of the facts.
