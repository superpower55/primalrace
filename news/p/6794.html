明星替烂片挡刀Stars Take the Bullet for a Flop

文娱 文化层 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要由阿加多·冈萨雷斯·伊纳里图执导、汤姆·克鲁斯主演的气候变化讽刺片《Digger》上映后票房惨淡，影评褒贬不一。演员约翰·古德曼近日将影片失利归咎于影评人，称他们「提前把片子埋了」「几乎毁了我们」；此前已有曼迪·帕廷金、本·阿弗莱克、比尔·伯尔等名人公开为该片辩护。
The newsAlejandro G. Iñárritu's climate-change satire Digger, starring Tom Cruise, opened to mixed reviews and a tiny box office. Actor John Goodman now blames the critics, saying they buried the film before release and that it "kinda killed us." Mandy Patinkin, Ben Affleck and Bill Burr have already publicly defended the movie.
神坛上的片子摔了，先怪写影评的人
When the sacred film falls, blame the people who write about it.

古德曼说影评人「提前把片子埋了」。可一部电影在上映前被埋，埋它的不是几篇影评，是发行方砸下去的钱和排片。影评人手里没有铲子。

这是第三章那个入口的标准操作：把一部作品从「商品」升格为「信仰」，再让信众去清算不跪的人。帕廷金说这是他看过最伟大的电影之一，阿弗莱克求大家别再欺负一部享尽好莱坞一切优势的电影，伯尔在播客里骂影评人是「化石傻瓜」。三个人，三种说法，同一个动作：把评价权从观众手里夺回来，交给「懂的人」。

「Digger Hive」这个词本身就是证据。一部上映一个月、票房惨败的电影，居然有了「蜂巢」。蜂巢不讨论作品，蜂巢护教。护教的前提是先有神。而神是被供上去的，供的人越多，越没人敢说那句「没那么好笑」。

最妙的是古德曼那句「这伤到我了」。一个演员的伤，被摆到台面上，成了要求观众闭嘴的理由。可真正被伤到的是谁？是买了票进场、出来觉得被骗的普通人。他们的表达没有播客，没有红毯，没有「这伤到我了」的镜头。他们的评价，就是影评。

影评人当然会错，会势利，会跟着风向走。但把一部片子的死因写成「影评人太刻薄」，等于宣布：作品不需要面对观众，只需要面对信徒。这套逻辑在宗教里叫护教，在商业里叫公关，在存在性战争里叫——输不起。

所以《Digger》到底死在哪，报道没写。写的是谁在替它哭丧，哭得最大声的那几个，恰好都不需要自己买票。

Goodman says the critics buried Digger before release. But what buries a movie before release isn't a handful of reviews. It's the money behind it and the screens it got. Critics don't hold the shovel.

This is the standard move from Chapter 3: promote a product into a faith, then send the congregation after anyone who won't kneel. Patinkin calls it one of the greatest films he's ever seen. Affleck begs people to stop bullying a movie that enjoyed every advantage Hollywood could offer. Burr calls critics the real Fossil Fools. Three people, three sentences, one action: take the power of judgment away from the audience and hand it to those who "understand."

The phrase "Digger Hive" is itself the evidence. A film a month into release, with a disastrous box office, somehow has a hive. A hive doesn't discuss the work. A hive defends the shrine. And a shrine needs a god first. The more people who bow, the fewer who dare say "not that funny."

The best part is Goodman's line about being hurt. An actor's wound gets placed on the table and becomes the reason the audience should shut up. But who was actually hurt? The ordinary person who bought a ticket, walked out, and felt conned. Their expression has no podcast, no red carpet, no camera on the phrase "this hurt me." Their review is the review.

Critics are wrong sometimes. They're snobs, they follow the wind. But writing a film's death certificate as "critics were too mean" announces that a work no longer has to face the audience, only the faithful. In religion that's called apologetics. In business, PR. In an existential war, it's called being a bad loser.

So where Digger actually died, the report doesn't say. It says who is mourning loudest, and the loudest mourners happen to be the ones who never had to buy a ticket.
