从IS的屠刀到气候的绞索：暴力从未消失，只是换了形状From IS Blades to Climate Nooses: Violence Never Ends, It Only Shifts Shape

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
暴力不是某个恐怖组织的专利，而是 Potential 与 Actual 之间永恒的差额。
Violence is not the monopoly of terror groups, but the eternal gap between Potential and Actual.

很多人在读这条新闻时会产生一种错觉：IS 走了，战争结束了，现在面对的是一个中立的、自然的“气候危机”。这是一种极其 naive 的认知陷阱。按照加尔通的暴力三角，Violence = Potential − Actual。当 Yazidi 社区的女性被 IS 绑架、屠杀，那是 direct violence（直接暴力）；而当这些村庄在战后十年依然没有重建，没有基础服务，没有水，导致人们无法回归时，这就是典型的 structural violence（结构暴力）。

所谓的“气候危机”在伊拉克北部不是一个孤立的自然现象，它是结构暴力的放大器。一个被战争摧毁、被政府忽视、资源分配极度不均的地区，在面对全球气候变暖时没有任何 buffer。水位的下降、土地的干涸，本质上是生存资源的进一步被剥夺。当一个 19 岁的年轻人 Smain 面对被烧毁的菜园时，他面对的不是一个“尘卷风”，而是一个被武器化了的生存环境。在这种环境下，生存本身变成了一场残酷的博弈，而这里的居民在博弈中毫无筹码。

最令人心惊的是这种暴力的联动：文化层面的元暴力（meta violence）让这些边缘群体在国际叙事中被简化为“战争受害者”，然后被遗忘。当世界在讨论“解放中东女性”时，现实是这些女性在战后不仅要面对创伤，还要在干涸的土地上承担沉重的养育和生存压力。她们的主体性在 IS 的屠刀下被践踏，在结构性的匮乏中被消磨。这不再是某个恐怖组织的暴行，而是一场由战争、制度性忽视与全球生态崩塌共同完成的、针对原初种族的慢速清洗。

Many readers will fall into a naive trap: believing that since IS is gone and the war has ended, they are now facing a neutral, natural "climate crisis." This is a fundamental misunderstanding of the Violence Triangle. Violence = Potential − Actual. When Yazidi women were abducted and slaughtered by IS, that was direct violence. But when these villages remain unreconstructed a decade later—void of basic services and water—preventing people from returning, we are seeing textbook structural violence.

In northern Iraq, the "climate crisis" is not an isolated natural event; it is an amplifier of structural violence. A region devastated by war and neglected by the state, with systemic resource maldistribution, has zero buffer against global warming. Falling water tables and parched lands are, in essence, the further stripping of survival resources. When 19-year-old Smain watches his vegetable garden burn, he isn't just fighting a "dust devil," but a weaponized environment. Survival here has become a brutal game where the locals hold no chips.

Most chilling is the linkage of these violence layers: meta-violence at the cultural level reduces these marginalized groups to mere "war victims" in global narratives, and then forgets them. While the world performs discussions on "liberating Middle Eastern women," the reality is that these women must navigate both trauma and the crushing burden of survival on arid land. Their subjectivity was trampled by the blades of IS and is now being eroded by structural scarcity. This is no longer the atrocity of a single terror group, but a slow-motion cleansing of the Primal Race, executed by the synergy of war, systemic neglect, and global ecological collapse.
