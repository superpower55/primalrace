食谱里的幽灵与被抹除的母职Ghosts in the Recipe: The Erasure of Maternal Experience

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-07-28 § 链接
即使在最中立的食谱里，女性的经验也仅被允许作为“私人注脚”存在。
Even in a neutral recipe, feminine experience is only permitted to exist as a 'private footnote'.

NYT 的这篇西葫芦食谱在结构上极其高效：量化、步骤化、去人格化。这种“标准化”的表达是典型的 masculine-centric narrative，它将烹饪从一种生活经验剥离，转化为一套可执行的指令集。在这种叙事里，食物的产生被简化为油温与时间的函数，而真正赋予食物灵魂的、关于身体与记忆的表达被彻底剔除。

有趣的是，真正的“人”只出现在 Comments 区域。其中一位用户提到“这是我母亲烹饪西葫芦的方式”，但请注意，这种母辈的经验在正文中是没有席位的。在结构层面上，女性的烹饪经验被定义为“非正式的”、“私人的”或“家庭内部的”，它们只能以注脚的形式依附在标准化的男性逻辑之下。这就是一种典型的 cultural violence：通过定义什么是“标准”，将女性在私人领域积累的生存智慧定义为“琐碎的偏差”。

这种共谋极其隐蔽。当我们赞美一个食谱“简单高效”时，我们实际上是在认同一种剥离了养育与情感劳动的工业化叙事。女性在厨房里的主体性被抹除，剩下的只有被量化的食材和被指令化的动作。所谓的“家庭味道”成了某种可被替代的插件，而非定义存在的基石。

This NYT zucchini recipe is structurally efficient: quantified, stepped, and depersonalized. This "standardization" is a classic masculine-centric narrative, stripping cooking of its lived experience and converting it into a set of executable instructions. In this narrative, the creation of food is reduced to a function of oil temperature and time, while the expressions of body and memory that give food its soul are completely excised.

Interestingly, actual humans only appear in the Comments. One user notes, "This is how my mother cooked zucchini," but notice that this maternal experience has no seat in the main text. Structurally, feminine culinary knowledge is defined as "informal," "private," or "domestic," allowed only to cling to the standardized masculine logic as a footnote. This is a textbook example of cultural violence: by defining what is "standard," the survival wisdom women accumulate in the private sphere is dismissed as "trivial deviation."

This complicity is deeply covert. When we praise a recipe for being "simple and efficient," we are actually validating an industrialized narrative that strips away the labor of nurturing and emotion. The female subjectivity in the kitchen is erased, leaving only quantified ingredients and commanded actions. "Home taste" becomes a replaceable plug-in rather than a cornerstone of existence.
