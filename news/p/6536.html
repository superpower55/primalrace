69条命，换不来一个刹车69 Lives, and No One Hit the Brake

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要西班牙上周发生高速列车相撞事故，造成69人死亡，其中包括4名美国公民，以及加拿大、日本、阿根廷、墨西哥、突尼斯和捷克公民各1人。事故发生在马德里以南约130公里的乌尔达村附近，一列Renfe列车撞上停驶的Iryo列车。目前除3人外，遇难者身份均已确认。
The newsA high-speed train collision in Spain last week killed 69 people, including four US citizens and one each from Canada, Japan, Argentina, Mexico, Tunisia and the Czech Republic. The crash occurred near Urda, about 130km south of Madrid, when a Renfe train hit a stationary Iryo train. All but three victims have been identified.
安全系统的失败不是天灾，是有人签字放行的结构暴力
A safety system's failure is not an accident. Someone signed off on it.

西班牙这起高铁相撞，69人死亡，是欧洲本世纪最致命的铁路事故之一。报道写得很清楚：自动安全系统失效，63公里路段上的所有列车停运，然后一个控制中心批准Renfe的列车继续行驶——而另一列车还停在那里。这不是天气，不是恐怖袭击，是一个坐在控制台前的人，在一个安全系统已经失效的状态下，按下了放行。

结构暴力的定义是没有具体施暴者的、嵌在制度里的暴力。但这次有一个具体的动作：授权恢复运行。问题在于，这个动作之所以能发生，是因为制度允许一个人在安全系统失效时凭判断放行。加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual，69个人本可以活着到达目的地，差额就是这69条命。这个差额不是自然产生的，是制度设计里留了一个口子，让“继续运行”比“停下来等”更容易被选中。

同一国家，同一年，第二次高铁相撞。一月安达卢西亚的事故死了46人。报道说这次事故“raised fresh questions about the country's rail safety”——问题是新的，事故不是。两次事故之间，结构没有改变。这就是结构暴力的运作方式：它不制造事件，它制造事件可以重复发生的条件。

还有一个时间点不能忽略：11月29日大选，桑切斯要争取连任。报道把这个背景放在最后一段，像是在说“顺便一提”。但69条人命和一场选举之间的距离，恰恰是文化暴力最能发挥作用的地方——事故会被转化为“安全问题”的政治筹码，而不是“谁批准了放行”的追责问题。

报道里没有出现的是：那个控制中心里做出授权决定的人是谁，他依据什么规则，这个规则是谁写的。受害者的国籍被一一列出——美国、加拿大、日本、阿根廷、墨西哥、突尼斯、捷克——像一份外交清单。而决定那列车继续开的人，没有名字。

人权即女权在这里的用法是：当制度把“继续运行”设为默认选项，把“停下来”设为需要额外理由的例外，它就已经在分配谁的生命可以被冒险。69个人付了这个差额。下一次，制度还是那个制度。

Sixty-nine people are dead in Spain, one of Europe's deadliest rail accidents this century. The Guardian's account is precise: an automatic safety system failed, all trains on a 63km stretch were halted, and then a control centre authorised the Renfe service to resume its journey while the other train was still standing there. Not weather. Not a attack. A person at a console, in a system that had already failed, pressing go.

Structural violence is usually defined as violence without a visible author, embedded in institutions and resource allocation. This time there is a specific act: the authorisation to resume. The reason that act could happen is that the system permits one person to override a failed safety mechanism on judgement alone. Galtung's formula: Violence = Potential − Actual. Sixty-nine people could have arrived alive. The gap is the sixty-nine. That gap was not natural. It was designed into a system where "keep running" is easier to choose than "stop and wait."

Same country, same year, second high-speed collision. Forty-six died in Andalusia in January. The report says this crash "raised fresh questions about the country's rail safety." The questions are new. The crash is not. Between the two, the structure did not change. That is how structural violence works: it does not manufacture events, it manufactures the conditions under which events repeat.

One date cannot be ignored: the 29 November general election, with Sánchez seeking to extend an eight-year premiership against unfavourable polls. The report places this in its final paragraph, as an aside. But the distance between sixty-nine bodies and an election is exactly where cultural violence does its work — the crash becomes a "safety issue" for political leverage, not a question of who authorised the train to move.

What the report does not contain: the name of the person in the control centre who gave the authorisation, the rule they followed, who wrote that rule. The victims' nationalities are listed one by one — US, Canada, Japan, Argentina, Mexico, Tunisia, Czech Republic — like a diplomatic inventory. The person who let the train keep moving has no name.

The human-rights-is-women's-rights point here: when a system makes "keep running" the default and "stop" the exception requiring justification, it is already allocating whose life may be risked. Sixty-nine people paid that gap. Next time, the system will be the same system.
