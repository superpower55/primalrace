从塑料袋到 90 英镑皮包：怀旧是资本最廉价的收割机From Plastic Bags to £90 Totes: Nostalgia as Capital's Cheapest Harvester

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
所谓的“生活碎片”被定价，本质是资本在收回它曾经随意抛弃的认知入口。
Pricing 'life fragments' is simply capital reclaiming the cognitive entries it once discarded.

把超市塑料袋当成“社交历史符号”来收藏，甚至将其复刻成 90 英镑的奢侈皮包，这简直是当代最荒诞的 weaponized nostalgia。资本最擅长的 scam 就是：先通过结构性暴力（structural violence）把一个东西定义为“廉价、琐碎、可抛弃”，然后在它消失后，通过文化叙事（cultural violence）将其重新定义为“纯真、真实、有温度的记忆”，最后以高价卖回给消费者。

这就是典型的认知入口夺取。M&S 和 Aries 的这次合作，本质上是在制造一种“假.最优解表达”：让年轻人通过购买一个昂贵的皮包，来扮演一个“懂得欣赏历史碎片”的文化精英。这种表达不需要你真正理解 80 年代的阶级结构或消费习惯，只需要你支付 90 英镑，就能在社交媒体的博弈中获得一个“有品味”的身份标签。

最讽刺的是，那些被赞美为“结实”的旧塑料袋，其实是那个时代工业垄断和资源浪费的物证。现在，这些物证被洗白成了“令人安心的图腾”。当一个原本免费且被厌恶的塑料垃圾变成了 status symbol，这说明资本已经完成了对“平庸”的定价权垄断。它告诉我们：即便你生活中的最底层碎片，只要我愿意，我都能把它变成一种特权。

这场怀旧游戏里没有真正的“真实”，只有被精心计算过的“复古”。当你觉得自己在通过一个塑料袋连接过去时，你其实只是在为资本的一次成功套现买单。

Collecting supermarket carrier bags as 'symbols of social history' and then recreating them as £90 luxury leather totes is a textbook example of weaponized nostalgia. The ultimate scam of capital is this: first, use structural violence to define something as 'cheap, trivial, and disposable,' and then, once it's gone, use cultural violence to redefine it as 'pure, authentic, and warm memory'—only to sell it back at a premium.

This is a blatant seizure of cognitive entries. The collaboration between M&S and Aries manufactures a 'fake optimal expression': allowing young people to perform the role of a 'cultured elite who appreciates historical fragments' by buying an expensive bag. This expression doesn't require an actual understanding of the class structures or consumption habits of the 80s; it only requires £90 to secure a 'tasteful' identity tag in the existential war of social media.

The irony is that those 'sturdier' old bags are actually evidence of industrial monopoly and resource waste from that era. Now, these artifacts are whitewashed into 'reassuring totems.' When a once-free and hated piece of plastic becomes a status symbol, it proves that capital has achieved monopoly over the pricing of 'mediocrity.' It tells us that even the lowliest fragments of your life can be turned into a privilege, provided capital decides to do so.

There is no genuine 'authenticity' in this nostalgia game, only a carefully calculated 'vintage' aesthetic. When you feel you are connecting to the past through a plastic bag, you are simply paying for a successful cash-out by capital.
