三岁小孩成了流量矿A Three-Year-Old Becomes the Product

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
一个三岁孩子被当成商品，成年人全都在抽成。
A toddler was turned into inventory, and every adult took a cut.

这条新闻里最该被追问的不是"谁报了警"，而是：一个三岁的小孩，凭什么已经有九十万 Instagram 粉丝、一百二十万 TikTok 粉丝，和一个超额完成的 GoFundMe。

报警那部分是个烟雾弹。组织者也是黑人，他告诉警察这孩子推了别的孩子、家长在跟其他家庭争吵，他按 i9 Sports 的规则轮换球员让每个人都有机会上场；911 录音里他抱怨一个家长跟着他、骂他、直播这场对峙。警察没目击争执，按民事纠纷处理，讨论了退款，没有人被捕。真相没有那么戏剧性，但这不重要——重要的是这段素材被剪成了"他们因为一个三岁小孩太强就报警"，然后被投进算法。

这里动的是文化层，机制是第三章那套：认知入口被夺走，叙事被剪成最能激怒人的形状，然后分发。母亲自己在视频里说得很清楚："我们在这些人面前演一场戏，全程 TikTok 直播，几千人在看，他出名了。"这句话不是被记者逼出来的，是她自己说的。她知道自己站在镜头前，她知道观众要什么。一个三岁的孩子，被安排成这场表演的道具，而他自己不知道。

然后是第二条流水线。Roc Nation 提出要把他飞到西班牙去"体验一次有冲击力的运动经历"；周一的帖子里，他的形象被塞进十几个 AI 生成的"广告"，卖麦片、卖含糖饮料、卖运动装备。这不是他发的，他大概连字都不认识。他的主页上挂着两个链接：一个商务邮箱，一个众筹——众筹说明写着这一切是为了"保护他对足球的热爱"，到周三晚上已经筹到四万九千多美元，目标三万。热爱被换算成了美元，然后这笔钱归谁，报道没写。

LeBron、Tiger Woods、Freddy Adu 的教训摆在那里，没人学。因为教训不赚钱，而流量赚钱。

还有一件报道点到却没展开的事：那天早上在场上踢球的其他孩子，他们的影像可能被直播给了几千个陌生人，而他们的父母从没同意过这种曝光。这些孩子连名字都没有，在这条新闻里他们是背景板，是"被三岁天才过掉的人"。他们的表达被别人的镜头征用了，没有人问过他们。

结尾那句"成年人和他们的机器让他失望了"说得客气。机器不会失望，机器只结算。真正的问题是：这条流水线上每一个环节都有人在抽成——父母、平台、经纪公司、AI 广告的投放者——而这个三岁的小孩，是唯一一个没有账户的人。

The question this story should raise isn't who called the police. It's why a three-year-old already has 900,000 Instagram followers, 1.2 million on TikTok, and an overfunded GoFundMe.

The police angle is a decoy. The organizer, who is also Black, told officers the boy had been pushing other kids and that his parents had become argumentative; he was rotating players so everyone got a turn, per i9 Sports' stated philosophy. On the 911 call he complained about a parent following him, calling him names, livestreaming the confrontation. Officers witnessed no dispute, treated it as a civil matter, discussed a refund. No arrests. The truth is less dramatic than the clip — which is exactly why the clip won.

What moved here is the cultural layer, and the mechanism is straight out of chapter three: the cognitive entry point gets seized, the narrative gets cut into its most enraging shape, and the algorithm distributes it. The mother says it herself on camera: we're putting on a show in front of these people, it's all on TikTok Live, thousands are watching, he's famous. Nobody put those words in her mouth. She knew where she was standing and what the audience wanted. A three-year-old was cast as the prop, and he had no idea.

Then the second assembly line. Roc Nation offers to fly him to Spain for an impactful athletic experience. Monday's post stuffs his likeness into a dozen AI-generated ads selling cereal, sugary drinks, athletic gear. He isn't posting these. His profile carries two links: a business email and a fundraiser claiming the whole thing is about protecting his passion for soccer — past $49,000 against a $30,000 goal as of Wednesday night. Passion converted into dollars, and who collects them, the report doesn't say.

The lessons of LeBron, Tiger Woods and Freddy Adu are sitting right there and nobody learns them, because lessons don't monetize and traffic does.

One thing the piece notices and drops: the other children on the field that morning, whose images may have been livestreamed to thousands of strangers without their parents ever consenting. They have no names in this story. They are scenery, the kids the prodigy dribbled past. Their expression was conscripted by someone else's camera, and nobody asked.

The closing line about grownups and their machines letting him down is polite. Machines don't feel disappointment; they settle accounts. The real problem is that every link in this chain takes a cut — parents, platforms, agencies, the people placing AI ads — and the three-year-old is the only one without an account.
