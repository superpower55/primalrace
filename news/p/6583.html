疏散三万人，谁在名单外Thirty Thousand Evacuated, Who's Left Off the List

其他 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
预警不是暴力，预警到不了的人才算。
A warning isn't violence. A warning that never arrives is.

这条新闻里没有反派，只有一道算术：预计撤离三万人，周一已经撤走两千。剩下的两万八不是没被通知，是还没轮到。灾难的暴力从来不在风速里，在谁先被装上车、谁得自己找路。

报道写得很干净：低洼地带、渔民不许出海、学校停课、六支国家救援队加七支邦级救援队提前部署。这些都是真的动作，不是表演。但所有动作都指向同一个前提——人被当作可以搬运的物件来统计。能搬走的搬走，搬不走的留在原地等风。上一场气旋 Arnab 已经死了六个人，毁了几十间房和庄稼，一周之内又来一场，这片海岸的恢复期只有七天。

最值得看的一句是首席部长说的：估计有六千到八千根电线杆会损坏，对供电构成严重威胁。电线杆有预估数字，人只有“约三万人”这个整数。停电会被清点、上报、抢修，被疏散的人被清点完就消失在统计里。等风过去，谁家的房子没了、谁在安置点待了多久、渔民的船还能不能下水，这些不会出现在下一份通报里。结构暴力就是这样运作的：它不制造灾难，它决定灾难之后哪些损失算损失。

报道没有写安置点在哪里、容纳多少人、有没有分性别分家庭的空间。一场风灾之后，女性在拥挤的避难所里面对的是什么，从来不是气象部门的指标。这不是报道的失职，是这类报道默认的盲区——灾害被写成风速和电力的故事，而不是人的故事。

风会过去，电线杆会重新立起来。那两万八千人里，有多少人根本不在任何一份名单上，报道没写，也没人问。

There is no villain in this story, only arithmetic: some 30,000 people to be evacuated, 2,000 moved by Monday. The remaining 28,000 were not uninformed. They simply hadn't been reached yet. The violence of a disaster is never in the wind speed. It's in who gets loaded onto a truck first, and who has to find their own way out.

The reporting is clean: low-lying areas, fishermen barred from the sea, schools closed, six national rescue teams plus seven state teams deployed in advance. These are real actions, not theater. But every action rests on the same premise—people are counted as objects to be moved. Move what can be moved, leave the rest to wait for the wind. Cyclone Arnab already killed six people and destroyed dozens of homes and crops last Wednesday. This coastline got seven days to recover.

The line worth staring at is the chief minister's: an estimated 6,000 to 8,000 electrical poles could be damaged, posing a severe threat to power supply. The poles get a projected number. The people get a round 30,000. Blackouts will be counted, reported, repaired. The evacuated get counted once and then vanish from the statistics. After the storm passes, whose house is gone, how long people sat in shelters, whether the fishermen's boats can still go out—none of that appears in the next bulletin. This is how structural violence works: it doesn't create the disaster. It decides which losses after the disaster count as losses.

The report doesn't say where the shelters are, how many they hold, whether space is divided by gender or family. What a woman faces in a crowded shelter after a storm has never been a meteorological indicator. This isn't the reporter's failure. It's the blind spot the genre defaults to—disasters written as stories about wind speed and electricity, not about people.

The wind will pass. The poles will go back up. How many of those 28,000 were never on any list at all—the report doesn't say, and nobody asks.
