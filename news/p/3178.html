政客的“监视”与零售商的“竞争”：一场关于谁在剥削的共谋剧本The Chancellor's 'Watch' and Retailers' 'Competition': A Conspiracy of Exploitation

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-02 § 链接
用“监视”替代“监管”，用“竞争”掩盖“盘剥”，这是典型的结构性暴力PR。
Replacing regulation with 'monitoring' and exploitation with 'competition' is structural violence in PR form.

John Healey 的这番表态是典型的政治表演。他一边声称“没有显著证据”证明价格欺诈，一边又在专栏里给零售商发警告，说政府在“密切关注”。这种叙事逻辑极其滑稽：既然没有证据，那么这种“关注”就不是为了解决问题，而是在制造一种“政府在为民众撑腰”的幻觉。这是一种 weaponized expression，将复杂的结构性经济危机简化为几个“贪婪零售商”的道德问题，从而转移公众对国家安全失能和经济调控失败的注意力。

而零售商的反应则是一场标准的共谋。British Retail Consortium 迅速抛出“竞争激烈”和“CMA 监管”这两块挡箭牌。在他们的叙事里，低价是由于“激烈的竞争”带来的，但他们绝不会告诉你，这种竞争的底层逻辑是建立在对供应链底端劳动者（尤其是那些处于原初种族地位的女性、移民工人）的极致压榨之上的。所谓的“最实惠的食物”，其代价是被结构性剥夺的底层劳动力。零售商和政府在此时的“口角”，更像是一场为了维持系统稳定而演出的戏码：一个扮演正义的监督者，一个扮演高效的竞争者，而真正承受 Potential − Actual 差额的底层民众则被排除在博弈之外。

这场博弈中没有公正表达，只有最优解表达。政府的最优解是通过口头警告安抚情绪；零售商的最优解是通过强调竞争来维持定价权。他们共同维护了一个元暴力结构：即无论谁在台上，资源分配的解释权永远掌握在精英阶层手中，而底层人只能在“被油价宰”和“被物价宰”之间选择一种生存方式。

John Healey's statement is a textbook performance of political theater. He claims there is "no significant evidence" of price gouging, yet warns retailers that ministers are "watching closely." This logic is absurd: if there is no evidence, this "monitoring" is not about solving a problem, but about manufacturing an illusion that the government is "fighting for the people." It is a weaponized expression, reducing a complex structural economic crisis to the moral failings of a few "greedy retailers" to divert attention from the state's failure in national security and economic control.

Retailers' response is a classic case of complicity. The British Retail Consortium quickly deploys "fierce competition" and "CMA regulation" as shields. In their narrative, low prices are the result of competition, but they omit that this competition is built upon the extreme exploitation of the bottom of the supply chain—specifically the Primal Race of female and migrant laborers. The "most affordable food" is paid for by the structural deprivation of labor. The "war of words" between the government and retailers is a scripted play to maintain system stability: one plays the righteous overseer, the other the efficient competitor, while the marginalized public, bearing the gap of Potential − Actual, is excluded from the game.

There is no Just Expression here, only optimal expressions for the powerful. The government's optimal move is to soothe public anger with verbal warnings; the retailers' is to maintain pricing power by citing competition. Together, they uphold a meta-violence: the power to interpret resource distribution remains exclusively with the elite, leaving the bottom to simply choose which price hike to suffer.
