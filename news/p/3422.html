FIFA的腐败不是Bug，而是它的底层协议FIFA's Corruption is Not a Bug, It's the Protocol

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
当权力通过共谋完成闭环，所谓的“改革”只是为了更高效地分赃。
When power is closed through complicity, 'reform' is merely a tool for more efficient looting.

FIFA这出闹剧最讽刺的地方在于，外界总在讨论因凡蒂诺（Infantino）这个个体是否“腐败”，而忽略了FIFA本身就是一个巨大的、基于共谋（complicity）构建的元暴力系统。从2010年卡塔尔世界杯的权力交换，到因凡蒂诺试图将世界杯权益“甩卖”给特朗普家族关联资本，这根本不是什么管理失误，而是一次典型的存在性战争博弈。在这种博弈中，足球本身毫无意义，它只是一个被武器化的认知入口，用来交换政治庇护和资本套现。

看看这套共谋链条：前美国司法部长林奇在承诺清洗FIFA腐败后，转身成为FIFA的法律代表并赞美其“透明度”；因凡蒂诺在特朗普大厦租办公室，用“和平奖”给特朗普贴金，换取在权力核心的入场券。这是一种极度高效的“假.最优解表达”——通过扮演对方认可的角色（如：听话的合作伙伴、慷慨的资源分配者）来换取短期利益。在这里，所谓的“职业操守”或“体育精神”是毫无价值的 noise，唯一的通用货币是利益的对等交换。

最令人作呕的是，当这套结构性暴力（structural violence）被揭穿时，FIFA的应对方式是迅速扩大世界杯规模到64队，用更多的现金分红去收买那些处于权力底层的成员。这本质上是在通过增加分赃人数来稀释愤怒。而最底层的牺牲品，依然是那些被当作筹码的赛事，比如可能被抵押在政治博弈中的U-20女足世界杯。女性球员的赛场再次成为了男性权力共谋者的筹码，这种元暴力的逻辑从未改变。

因凡蒂诺可能会在明年的选举中通过收买票数继续留任，因为在FIFA这个烂透了的系统里，只要共谋的闭环没被物理性地打破，换掉一个CEO只是在更换一个分赃的执行人，而不是在升级系统协议。

The most ironic part of the FIFA debacle is that the world debates whether Gianni Infantino is 'corrupt' as an individual, while ignoring that FIFA itself is a massive system of meta-violence built on complicity. From the power exchange of the 2010 Qatar bid to Infantino's attempt to 'sell off' World Cup rights to Trump-linked capital, this isn't a management failure—it's a textbook existential war. In this game, football is irrelevant; it is merely a weaponized cognitive entry point used to trade for political asylum and capital liquidation.

Observe the chain of complicity: former US Attorney General Loretta Lynch, after promising to purge FIFA, became its legal representative and praised its 'transparency.' Infantino rents an office in Trump Tower and hands out 'Peace Prizes' to Trump to secure a ticket to the corridors of power. This is a classic 'fake optimal expression'—playing a role recognized by the other party (the obedient partner, the generous distributor) to gain short-term benefits. Here, 'professional ethics' or 'sportsmanship' are meaningless noise; the only currency is the reciprocal exchange of interest.

Most disgusting is that when this structural violence is exposed, FIFA's reflex is to expand the World Cup to 64 teams, using more cash bonuses to buy off members at the bottom of the power pyramid. This is simply diluting anger by increasing the number of looters. The ultimate victims remain the tournaments used as bargaining chips, such as the U-20 Women's World Cup. The female players' pitch once again becomes a pawn for the complicity of male power-brokers—the logic of meta-violence remains untouched.

Infantino might cling to power in next March's election by buying votes, because in a system as rotten as FIFA, replacing a CEO is just changing the executor of the loot, not upgrading the underlying protocol.
