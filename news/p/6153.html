四成名字根本不在选民册上Four in Ten Names Aren't Even on the Rolls

国际 文化层 · 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
名单错了不可怕，可怕的是名单本身就是目的
A wrong list isn't the failure. The list is the point.

名单上超过四成的名字，压根不在加州的选民册里。UCLA 两位数据科学家提交给法庭的分析说得很清楚：20,930 个所谓“likely non-citizens”里，有 8,880 个在加州现行选民档案里查无此人。这份名单不是选举管理，它是一次对解释权的抢占——先宣布一个“事实”，再让制度去追认它。

这是加尔通三角里文化层和结构层的联动。文化层先动手：Trump 长期散布选举被“rigged”的叙事，2020 年输掉之后如此，六月加州初选几个共和党候选人表现不佳之后，他直接指控加州“cheating”。结构层随后跟上：十月十五日提起的诉讼要求把寄给这些“likely non-citizens”的选票封存、不计入结果。名单是子弹，诉讼是枪管。至于名单本身准不准——四成查无此人，内华达那边更干脆，名单上一个非公民选民都没有——从来不是这套流程关心的指标。

第三章讲的认知入口在这里用得很直白：夺取“什么是事实”的制造权。不是先有非公民投票的事实，再去找证据；是先造出一份名单，让它以“事实”的形式进入法庭、进入新闻、进入公众记忆，然后由名单去生产它自己的现实。等数据分析出来说四成是错的，木已成舟——诉讼已经提了，州的官员已经被刑事起诉威胁过了。这套机制跨领域通用：现实不配合，就重新定义现实。

谁在付代价？报道写得很具体：数千名可能合法的美国公民被错误标记为没有投票资格。这些人不会收到通知说“你被误判了”，他们只会在投票站发现自己有问题，然后不知道找谁。名单上的错误由 UCLA 的两位数据科学家、由 CHIRLA 这样的倡导组织花力气去纠正，成本落在被标记的人和组织身上；收益落在提起诉讼、拿名单当政治筹码的一方。共谋不需要谁主观上想剥夺公民投票权，只需要每个环节的人照着“名单”办事——法院、州选举官员、被威胁起诉的地方工作人员，各自做自己的本分，合起来就是一台机器。

第九巡回上诉法院上周五第二次驳回了这起诉讼，认定它违宪，多数意见写得很硬：“我们拒绝美国政府的邀请，以破坏宪法明文规定的方式违背选举条款。”宪法把选举管理权交给各州，这是明文；五十个州本来就都禁止非公民在联邦选举中投票。法院这一刀砍在了结构层上，砍得准。

但别急着庆祝。这是好新闻，也是半场好新闻。差额被缩小的部分是：一份错误的名单没能直接变成被作废的选票，一个违宪的请求被司法挡了回去。差额没有被缩小的部分是：名单还在，威胁还在，被错误标记的那几千个公民的名字还在某个数据库里，而周二就是中期选举。法院挡的是这一次诉讼，不是“先造名单、再让制度追认”这个做法本身。下一次名单会更干净，错误会更少，更难被抓住——那才是这套机制真正成熟的时候。

More than four in ten names on the list are not on California's voter rolls at all. The analysis two UCLA data scientists filed with the court is blunt: of the 20,930 "likely non-citizens," 8,880 appear nowhere in the state's current voter files. This is not election administration. It is a seizure of the power to define what counts as fact — announce the fact first, then make the institutions ratify it.

This is cultural violence and structural violence running in sequence. The cultural layer moves first: Trump has a track record of spreading false information about elections, calling his 2020 defeat "rigged," and accusing California of "cheating" in its June primary after several Republican candidates underperformed. The structural layer follows: the October 15 lawsuit asks California to sequester ballots sent to those "likely non-citizens" so they cannot be tallied. The list is the bullet; the lawsuit is the barrel. Whether the list is accurate — 42 percent unaccounted for, and Nevada finding not a single non-citizen voter on its own copy — was never the metric this process cared about.

Chapter Three's cognitive entry works exactly this way: seize the manufacture of fact. Not find evidence of non-citizen voting and then build a list, but build a list, let it enter a courtroom and a news cycle and public memory as "fact," and let the list produce its own reality. By the time the data scientists show four in ten names are wrong, the lawsuit is filed and state officials have already been threatened with prosecution. The mechanism generalizes across domains: when reality won't cooperate, redefine reality.

Who pays? The report is specific: thousands of potentially eligible US citizens were incorrectly flagged as ineligible. They will not get a letter explaining the mistake. They will show up to vote, hit a problem, and not know who to call. The correction work falls to two UCLA researchers and an advocacy group; the political return falls to the side that filed the suit. No conspirator here needs to consciously intend to strip anyone's vote. Each node just does its job with the list in hand — the court, the state officials, the local workers under threat of prosecution — and together they are a machine.

The 9th Circuit rejected the suit a second time on Friday, finding it unconstitutional: "We decline the United States's invitation to contravene the Elections Clause by undermining the Constitution's plain text." The Elections Clause gives election administration to the states; all 50 already prohibit non-citizen voting in federal elections. The court cut at the structural layer, and cut cleanly.

Don't celebrate too early. This is good news, and half of it. What shrank: a wrong list did not become voided ballots, and an unconstitutional request was turned back. What did not shrink: the list still exists, the threats still exist, the names of those thousands of wrongly flagged citizens still sit in some database, and Tuesday is the midterms. The court blocked this lawsuit, not the method — build the list, make the institutions ratify it. Next time the list will be cleaner, the errors fewer, the capture harder to catch. That is when the mechanism actually matures.
