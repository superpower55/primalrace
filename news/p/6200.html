法庭之争，谁在定义法治Who Gets to Define the Rule of Law

国际 元暴力 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要波兰总统纳夫罗茨基周六在总统府与部分政党代表举行危机磋商，总理图斯克领导的公民联盟未出席。此前周五，宪法法庭首席法官希维琴科夫斯基被停职，引发争议。截至周六下午，磋商未取得突破。
The newsPolish President Nawrocki held crisis talks with party leaders at the presidential palace on Saturday; PM Tusk's Civic Coalition stayed away. The talks followed Friday's suspension of Constitutional Tribunal chief judge Swieczkowski. No breakthrough emerged by Saturday afternoon.
解释权的争夺被写成了法治危机
A fight over interpretive power dressed up as a rule-of-law crisis.

波兰这场宪法法庭危机，最值得看的不是谁对谁错，而是「法治」这个词被双方同时拿去当武器。图斯克说解职院长是为了「恢复法治」，法庭官方账号在X上把政府这一手叫「政变」。同一件事，两个名字。谁赢了这一轮，谁就有权决定以后这件事叫什么。

这就是解释权的争夺。法庭院长斯维奇科夫斯基是法律与公正党2015到2023年执政期间任命的，被指控利用职权阻止议会选举新法官。图斯克的说法是法官不中立、解职「痛苦」但必要。可纳夫罗茨基自己也在阻挠法庭的人事提名，而他是个右翼人物。两边都在动人事，两边都说对方在破坏独立。

关键在于，这不是「法治 vs 反法治」，这是两套政治力量在抢同一个入口：谁来定义什么是中立的法官。法律与公正党任命的法官被说成不中立，那图斯克任命的就是中立的？报道里没有任何一方给出中立法官的判定标准，只有谁有权任命。

真正被挤到边上的是那些在法庭外站了好几周的法律与公正党支持者，还有那些既没去总统府、也没被邀请去的普通波兰人。他们在这场「内部安全威胁」的叙事里，只是背景板。总统说这是安全威胁，总理说这是民主存亡，两个人都需要一个足够大的词，好让各自的动作显得不像权力斗争。

美国大使说「波兰最不需要的是升级成更糟的对抗」。这句话本身没错，但它出现的位置很有意思：一个外国大使在提醒双方冷静，而欧盟委员会在催总统接受新法官提名。外部力量也在挑边，只是挑得客气。

所以这场危机没有「法治的胜利」这一层。它只在元层动了一下：解释权从一群人手里往另一群人手里挪，挪的过程被包装成原则之争。等挪完了，新的解释者会写下一版「法治」的定义，然后等着下一轮被同样的话术掀翻。

The most telling thing about Poland's constitutional court crisis isn't who's right. It's that both sides are using the phrase "rule of law" as a weapon at the same time. Tusk says removing the court's chief judge is a "painful" but necessary step to restore the rule of law. The court's own X account called the government's move a "coup d'etat." Same event, two names. Whoever wins this round gets to decide what it's called from now on.

This is a fight over interpretive power. Chief judge Bogdan Swieczkowski was appointed under the Law and Justice government that ruled from 2015 to 2023, and is accused of using his position to block parliament from electing new judges. Tusk's line is that the judges aren't neutral and the removal was necessary. But President Nawrocki, himself a right-wing figure, has also blocked nominations to the court. Both sides are moving personnel. Both say the other is destroying independence.

The point is that this isn't "rule of law versus anti-rule of law." It's two political forces grabbing for the same entrance: who gets to define what a neutral judge is. Law and Justice appointees are called non-neutral. So Tusk's appointees are neutral? Nowhere in the report does either side offer a standard for neutrality that isn't just who holds appointment power.

Pushed to the margins are the Law and Justice supporters who've been standing outside the court for weeks, and the ordinary Poles who weren't at the presidential palace and weren't invited. In the narrative of a "threat to internal security," they're set dressing. The president needs a big enough word to make his move look like something other than a power play. So does the prime minister.

The US ambassador says the last thing Poland needs is a confrontation that spirals into something worse. True enough. But notice where that line lands: a foreign ambassador urging calm while the European Commission pushes the president to accept new judge nominations. Outside powers are picking sides too. They're just politer about it.

So there's no "victory for the rule of law" layer here. Something moved at the meta level: interpretive power shifting from one set of hands to another, with the shift packaged as a clash of principles. Once it's done, the new interpreters will write the next definition of "rule of law" and wait for the same script to come back around.
