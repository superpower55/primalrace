孤独是被挑选的布景Solitude Is a Chosen Set

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《卫报》图片栏目介绍摄影师 Nicolas Rachline 的新书《Elsebound》，书中照片受 Edward Hopper、Brassaï 及电影《Blade Runner》影响，拍摄空旷街道与独坐的人物。他的个展将于 2026 年 11 月 19 日至 2027 年 1 月 23 日在巴黎 Oana Ivan 画廊举行。
The newsThe Guardian's picture gallery features Nicolas Rachline's new book Elsebound, whose painterly photographs of empty streets and solitary figures draw on Edward Hopper, Brassaï and Blade Runner. A solo exhibition runs at Oana Ivan Gallery in Paris from 19 November 2026 to 23 January 2027.
把空旷拍成美，先得有人替你清场
To photograph emptiness as beauty, someone first clears the room for you.

Rachline 说他一直在找「desired solitude」——被渴望的孤独。这句话很诚实：孤独不是他撞见的，是他想要的。想要孤独的人走进一间空餐厅，坐到最里面的角落，面朝墙壁。这个动作本身是选择，不是处境。

他拍的那些人「caught between day and night, as if suspended」，悬在昼夜之间。悬置是一种特权。真正被悬置的人——等不到班次的、付不起下一顿的、夜里不敢回家的——不会成为这种照片的主体，因为她们不够安静。摄影师的镜头要的是「timeless elegance」，而优雅的前提是这个人暂时没有别的事要做。

有意思的是他自己承认了这一点。拍那间暗下来的餐厅时，他请值夜班的门房再多开一分钟灯。一个夜里还在上班的人，被用来给一张关于「时间静止」的照片打光。照片里没有他。这就是第三章说的那种入口：美学的入口。品味不是无辜的，它靠排除来成立——把谁框进去，把谁留在框外，是一套筛选机制，只是这套筛选不流血，所以没人叫它暴力。

再看那些参照系：Hopper、Brassaï、film noir、Blade Runner、Paris, Texas。全是男性作者的名字，全是男性凝视训练出来的城市夜晚。他说这些影像成了他的「secret school」，教的是一门「aesthetic of loss」的课。问题不在于他喜欢这些，问题在于这套课程被当成普遍的人类经验在传播——孤独、忧郁、城市的诗意——而实际上它是一种非常特定的、有闲阶级的、白种男性的观看位置。他说「It's them – or me」，好像摄影师和被摄者共享同一种孤独。不共享。他在相机后面，可以走；她们在画面里，走不了。

这不是要骂 Rachline。他拍得确实好，那间布鲁塞尔的 Le Variétés 他熬了很多个夜晚才拍成，这种笨功夫值得尊重。我想说的是这类照片流通时被赋予的位置：它被读成「关于当代生活的孤独」，而不是「一个有余裕的人对孤独的审美化」。前者是普遍真理，后者是一个立场。把立场说成真理，就是元暴力最日常的运作方式——不是谁在撒谎，是解释权在谁手里，谁就有资格把自己的偏好命名为人类的处境。

顺便说，这组图里唯一被点出名字的具体人物是 Gena Rowlands，因为「the subject's blond hair」。一个女演员的名字被用来给一个匿名女人的金发命名。照片里的人没有名字，参照物有名字，而且那个名字属于另一个被观看的女人。

展览开到明年一月。开幕那天晚上，巴黎还会有值夜班的人。

Rachline says he is after “desired solitude.” That word is honest: the solitude wasn't found, it was wanted. A person who wants to be alone walks into an empty restaurant, takes the back corner, faces the wall. That's a choice, not a condition.

The people he photographs are “caught between day and night, as if suspended.” Suspension is a privilege. The actually suspended — waiting for a shift that never comes, short on the next meal, afraid to walk home at night — don't end up as subjects in these pictures, because they aren't quiet enough. The lens wants “timeless elegance,” and elegance requires that a person have nothing else to do for a moment.

He admits as much. Shooting the darkened restaurant, he asked the night porter to keep the lights on one more minute. A man still working at night is used to light a photograph about time standing still. He isn't in the frame. This is the aesthetic entrance: taste isn't innocent, it works by exclusion — who gets framed, who stays outside — and because this filtering draws no blood, nobody calls it violence.

Then the references: Hopper, Brassaï, film noir, Blade Runner, Paris, Texas. All male authors, all city nights trained by a masculine gaze. He calls these images his “secret school,” teaching an “aesthetic of loss.” The problem isn't that he loves them; it's that the curriculum gets circulated as universal human experience — loneliness, melancholy, the poetry of cities — when it is a very specific viewing position: propertied, white, male. He writes “It's them – or me,” as if photographer and subject share one solitude. They don't. He stands behind the camera and can leave; they are inside the frame and cannot.

This isn't a hit on Rachline. The pictures are good, and the nights spent getting Le Variétés right deserve respect. What I'm pointing at is the slot this kind of work gets placed in: read as “about the loneliness of contemporary life” rather than “one comfortable person's aestheticization of loneliness.” The first is a universal truth; the second is a position. Calling a position a truth is how meta-violence works day to day — not someone lying, but whoever holds the interpretive monopoly gets to name their own preference the human condition.

One more thing: the only named person in the whole set is Gena Rowlands, because of “the subject's blond hair.” An actress's name is used to title an anonymous woman's hair. The woman in the photo has no name; the reference does, and that name belongs to another woman who was looked at.

The show runs until January. On opening night, Paris will still have people working the night shift.
