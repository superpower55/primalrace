一部恐怖片的预算里没有鬼No Ghosts in This Budget

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Deadline ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
低成本不是美学，是分配结果
Low budget is not an aesthetic. It's a distribution outcome.

这条新闻里真正值得看的不是三部恐怖片，是那个数字：每部预算低于一百万美元。

报道写得很清楚，Hashmi 的策略是「高概念、商业化、低于一百万美元」，靠的是他拍短片练出来的省钱本事——少场景、少资源、共享剧组。这话说得漂亮，但把预算表翻开看，省下来的从来不是均匀的。钱在哪里被砍，人在哪里被砍，是同一张表。

低成本恐怖片在行业里向来有另一个名字：最容易被当成「谁都能干」的类型。它便宜，因为它可以靠一个尖叫的女人、一间屋子、一个夜晚撑完。Hashmi 的片单恰好把这条线摆得整整齐齐：一部发生在迪拜一个夜晚的六段式合集，一部改编自他 2020 年短片、讲绑架小女孩的《Cinder》，还有一部他自己定义为「女性主义附身复仇恐怖片」的《Latching》，发生在卡拉奇，一个夜晚。

三个项目，两个明确写「一个夜晚」，一个写「一夜之内」。省的是场景、灯光、场次、剧组的时间。这些东西省下来，代价落在谁身上，报道没写。它也不会写——制作成本的账本从来不记这个。

《Latching》被叫做「女性主义」，这是全篇最需要停一下的地方。一个男性导演，用「女性主义」给一部附身复仇片贴标签，然后把它放进一个每部低于一百万美元的片单里。这两件事不矛盾，但也不互相担保。类型片里被反复使用的女性身体——被附身、被侵害、被复仇驱动——是恐怖片最省钱的燃料之一，因为它不需要解释，观众已经会怕了。「女性主义」在这里可以是真的，也可以是一个让预算和卖点同时成立的词。报道没有告诉我们它指的是哪一种，只告诉我们 Hashmi 会编剧也会执导。

他讲的行业逻辑本身没错：可持续的电影工业不是靠融资拍一部贵的，是靠拍能回本、能养下一部的片子。这话在任何一个新兴市场都成立。但「可持续」这个词在报道里只指向投资回收，不指向谁在这套低成本里被反复征用。迪拜、卡拉奇、塞尔维亚，三个拍摄地，三种成本结构，一个共同点：便宜的地方拍吓人的事。

所以这条新闻的正确读法不是「又有一个独立导演起步了」，而是：一套被验证过的低成本类型公式，正在被复制到一个新的市场。公式里的每个变量都是钱，包括那个被写成「女性主义」的变量。

《Latching》最后拍成什么样，才是这句话的答案。在那之前，「女性主义」只是一个还没兑现的预算项。

The number worth reading in this announcement isn't three horror features. It's the one under a million dollars, per film.

Hashmi's strategy is stated plainly: high concept, commercially oriented, sub-$1M, drawing on what he learned making shorts with few locations and shared crews. Fine. But open the budget and see what gets cut first. Money is never trimmed evenly, and neither are people.

Low-budget horror has another name in this industry: the genre anyone can supposedly do. It's cheap because a screaming woman, one room, and one night can carry it. Hashmi's slate lines up neatly along that edge: an anthology of six stories across one night in Dubai; Cinder, expanding his 2020 short about a barber facing a kidnapper who has taken a young girl; and Latching, which he calls a feminist possession-revenge horror film, set over a single night in Karachi.

Three projects, two explicitly one night, one "over the course of a single harrowing night." What's saved is locations, lighting, shooting days, crew time. Who absorbs that saving is not in the report. It never is. Production ledgers don't have a line for it.

"Feminist" is the word to stop on. A male director labels a possession-revenge film feminist and slots it into a slate capped below a million dollars. Those two facts don't contradict each other, and they don't guarantee each other either. The female body being entered, violated, and avenged is one of horror's cheapest fuels, because it needs no setup — the audience already knows how to be afraid of it. "Feminist" here can be true, or it can be a word that makes the budget and the pitch work at once. The report tells us Hashmi wrote and will direct it. It doesn't tell us which kind of true.

His industry logic is sound: a sustainable film industry isn't built on financing one expensive film, it's built on films that recover their money and fund the next. True in any emerging market. But "sustainable" in this report points only at return on investment, never at who gets repeatedly drafted into the low-cost version. Dubai, Karachi, Serbia — three locations, three cost structures, one constant: cheap places to shoot frightening things.

So the correct reading isn't "another indie director launches." It's that a proven low-budget genre formula is being ported into a new market. Every variable in that formula is money, including the one labeled feminist.

What Latching actually becomes is the answer to that sentence. Until then, "feminist" is a budget line not yet paid out.
