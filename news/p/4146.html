民主不能被俄罗斯绑架，但可以被“战争状态”绑架Democracy isn't held hostage by Russia, but by the 'State of War'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
当“紧急状态”成为权力续期的遮羞布，民主就成了被武器化的叙事。
When 'emergency' becomes a veil for power extension, democracy is weaponised into a narrative.

Fedorov 的发声撕开了一个极其典型的权力博弈样本：在战争叙事中，谁拥有定义“时机”的权力，谁就拥有了定义“现实”的权力。Zelenskyy 维持的不是简单的战时体制，而是一种基于“紧急状态”的 structural violence。在这种结构中，专业主义被 personal loyalty 替换，决策过程被黑盒化，而所有对权力合法性的质疑，都被轻而易举地贴上“给敌人递刀子”的标签。

这本质上是一场关于“认知入口”的争夺战。Zelenskyy 的叙事是：战争 $\rightarrow$ 戒严 $\rightarrow$ 暂停选举 $\rightarrow$ 权力集中。而 Fedorov 试图通过“民主不能被俄罗斯绑架”这个 weaponized concept 来反击，试图将“举行选举”重新定义为一种抵抗行为，而非干扰行为。这种博弈的残酷在于，当一个国家处于 total war 状态时，任何关于民主程序的讨论都会被简化为“实用主义”与“理想主义”的对立，而实用主义永远是权力掌控者的最优解表达。

最讽刺的是，外部力量（如 Trump）对选举的呼吁，在此时反而成了内部权力斗争的筹码。当民主变成了一种被外部操纵或内部利用的工具时，它就不再是公正的表达，而成了另一种形式的武器。Fedorov 的支持率在民调中上升，证明了社会对“黑盒决策”的厌倦，但这种不满能否转化为实际的 structural 改变，取决于共谋者——那些在议会和军队中维持现状的人——是否意识到，一个失去合法性支撑的权力中心，在面对长期战争时才是最大的风险。

Fedorov's challenge exposes a classic power game: in a wartime narrative, whoever controls the definition of 'timing' controls reality. Zelenskyy is maintaining more than just a wartime regime; he is presiding over a structural violence rooted in the 'state of emergency.' In this structure, professionalism is replaced by personal loyalty, decision-making is black-boxed, and any questioning of legitimacy is easily branded as 'aiding the enemy.'

This is essentially a struggle over the cognitive entry point. Zelenskyy's narrative flows from War $\rightarrow$ Martial Law $\rightarrow$ Suspended Elections $\rightarrow$ Centralised Power. Fedorov attempts to counter this by weaponising the concept that 'democracy cannot be held hostage by Russia,' trying to redefine holding elections as an act of resistance rather than interference. The cruelty of this game is that in a total war, any discussion of democratic process is reduced to a binary between 'pragmatism' and 'idealism'—and pragmatism is always the optimal expression for those in power.

Most ironically, external demands for elections (e.g., from Trump) have become chips in an internal power struggle. When democracy becomes a tool for external manipulation or internal leverage, it ceases to be a Just Expression and becomes another weapon. The rise in Fedorov's polls proves a societal exhaustion with 'black-box' governance, but whether this transforms into structural change depends on the complicity of those in parliament and the military. They must realize that a power center lacking legitimacy is the greatest risk in a prolonged war.
