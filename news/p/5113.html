用 750 万美元购买的“消失权”The $7.5 Million Purchase of the 'Right to Disappear'

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-18 § 链接
暴力不是失控，而是通过精准定价实现的结构性外包。
Violence is not a loss of control, but a structural outsourcing achieved through precise pricing.

这是一场极其典型的 Violence = Potential − Actual 的操作。对于这些被驱逐者来说，Potential 是法律曾认定的“免于被酷刑的权利”，而 Actual 是被蒙头殴打、推下楼梯以及在赤道几内亚的破旧酒店里等待消失。中间的差额，就是由美国政府用 750 万美元买单的暴力。

这件事最恶心的地方在于它揭示了一种极其高效的 structural violence：外包。美国政府通过与赤道几内亚签署协议，将“驱逐”这个动作从法律监管的白盒，移交给一个以酷刑著称的独裁政权黑盒。当 ICE 声明“个体一旦离开监管就不再由其负责”时，他们实际上是在声明：我已经完成了对暴力的定价，并将其外包给了最擅长执行暴力的人。在这种逻辑下，人权不再是底线，而是一个可以被抵消的成本项。

而那些殴打受害者的警员大喊“手机在哪里”，则揭示了这场博弈中唯一的认知入口——表达。在被定义为“空白”且“不存在”的囚徒身份中，手机是他们唯一能与外界建立连接、确证自身存在的工具。夺走手机，就是夺走他们作为人的表达权，让他们在物理和叙事上同时消失。当警员说“我就是法律”时，他其实是在扮演元暴力的执行者：在这个由金钱和权力勾兑的共谋场域里，解释权被完全垄断，受害者被剥夺了定义自己痛苦的权利。

这不仅是人道主义灾难，这是一次精准的共谋。美国政府提供资金，独裁政权提供黑狱，双方共同完成了对一个特定族群的“清除”。最讽刺的是，其中一名受害者因为同性恋身份在埃及面临迫害，结果他被送到了另一个同样视其为异类的地狱。这种“安全第三国”的叙事，本身就是最大的 scam。

This is a textbook case of Violence = Potential − Actual. For these deportees, the Potential was the legally recognized 'right to be free from torture,' while the Actual is being bound, bagged, beaten, and left to rot in a run-down hotel in Equatorial Guinea. The delta between the two is the violence purchased by the US government for $7.5 million.

The most repulsive part of this is the efficiency of its structural violence: outsourcing. By striking a deal with an authoritarian regime notorious for torture, the US administration moved the act of 'deportation' from a legally monitored white box to a diplomatic black box. When ICE claims they are 'no longer responsible' once an individual leaves their custody, they are effectively stating that they have priced the violence and outsourced its execution to those most proficient at it. In this logic, human rights are no longer a baseline, but a cost item to be offset.

The police shouting 'Where is the phone!' during the beatings reveals the only remaining cognitive entry point in this game: expression. For prisoners defined as 'blanks' who 'don't exist,' a phone is the only tool to establish a connection with the outside world and confirm their own existence. Seizing the phone is seizing their right of expression, erasing them physically and narratively. When the officer screams 'I am the law,' he is performing the role of meta-violence: in this field of complicity between money and power, the right to interpret reality is fully monopolized.

This is not just a humanitarian catastrophe; it is a precise act of complicity. The US provides the funding, the dictatorship provides the black site, and together they execute the 'cleansing' of a specific group. The ultimate irony is the gay man from Egypt who faced persecution at home, only to be outsourced to another hell that views him with the same hatred. The narrative of a 'safe third country' is the biggest scam of all.
