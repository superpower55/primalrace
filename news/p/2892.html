工业化养鸡：一场关于蛋白质的生物殖民骗局Industrial Poultry: A Biological Colonization Scam

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
用生态多样性的坍塌换取廉价蛋白，本质是结构性暴力对未来的预支。
Trading ecological diversity for cheap protein is structural violence pre-paying for a future catastrophe.

270 亿只鸡，占据全球鸟类生物量 70%。这个数字不是农业奇迹，而是一个巨大的生物学 scam。当我们习惯于在超市买到廉价的、快速生长的“超级鸡”时，我们实际上是在参与一场针对地球生态系统的暴力拆迁。按照加尔通的公式，这里的暴力差额就是：本可维持的物种隔离与生态平衡 − 工业化强行压缩后的病原体温床。

这项研究揭示了典型的 structural violence。工业化养殖通过极高密度的空间压缩和抗生素的常规化投喂，强行将野生鸟类与家禽的生物墙拆除。这种“细菌进化大熔炉”制造出的 Frankenstein monster bugs，是资本为了追求规模效应而制造的外部性成本。抗生素耐药性的增加，就是这种结构性暴力在 direct 层面的预演——当药物失效，这种暴力将直接作用于每一个个体的身体。

更讽刺的是这种共谋机制：政府在社区呼吁停止工厂化养殖的同时，却在考虑放宽规划规则以建设更多设施。这是一种典型的元暴力逻辑——定义什么是“经济增长”，定义什么是“食品安全”，从而将一个疾病温床包装成“可负担的蛋白质供应”。在这种叙事中，生物多样性的丧失和公共卫生风险被定义为“必要的代价”，而这种定价权牢牢掌握在工业资本和政策制定者手中。

我们不能在一种通过摧毁生物隔离墙来获取效率的系统里寻找安全感。当 100 倍的菌株交换率成为现实，所谓的“把鸡煮熟就没事”不过是文化层面的安慰剂，试图掩盖一个事实：我们正在通过工业化养殖，为下一次全球性生物危机递交投名状。

27 billion chickens, accounting for 70% of all bird biomass on Earth. This isn't an agricultural miracle; it's a massive biological scam. While we enjoy the convenience of "super-growing" chickens, we are participating in a violent demolition of Earth's ecosystem. In terms of Galtung's formula, the violence here is the gap between the potential for species isolation and the actual reality of an industrial pathogen cauldron.

This study exposes a textbook case of structural violence. By enforcing extreme spatial compression and routine antibiotic administration, industrial farming has forcibly demolished the biological walls between wild birds and poultry. This "evolutionary cauldron" producing Frankenstein monster bugs is simply an externality of capital's pursuit of scale. The rise of antimicrobial resistance is the direct layer of this violence manifesting—once drugs fail, the violence will act directly upon the human body.

The complicity here is particularly cynical: governments are considering relaxing planning rules to build more factory farms even as communities scream for them to stop. This is meta-violence in action—monopolizing the definition of "economic growth" and "food security" to rebrand a disease-ridden facility as "affordable protein." In this narrative, the collapse of biodiversity is dismissed as a "necessary cost," with the pricing power held firmly by industrial capital and policymakers.

We cannot find security in a system that derives efficiency by destroying biological barriers. With a 100-fold increase in strain exchange, the cultural narrative that "just cook the chicken" is merely a placebo, masking the fact that we are signing a pact with the next global biological crisis.
