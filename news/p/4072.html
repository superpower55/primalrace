在马背上夺回被抹除的身体Reclaiming the Erased Body on Horseback

好消息 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
好新闻不是温情，而是 Actual 朝 Potential 走近了一步。
Good news is not about warmth, but the actual narrowing of the gap between Potential and Actual.

很多所谓的“女性艺术展”容易陷入一种表演性的温柔，但 Ayana V Jackson 这次在 Arles 的展览真正触及了 meta 层的暴力。她处理的不是简单的“女性被遗忘”，而是针对原初种族——黑人与混血女性——在历史叙事中被系统性 erasure（抹除）的暴力。

在元暴力的逻辑里，黑人女性的身体被定义为“牲畜”或“工具”，她们的主体性在殖民者的历史书写中被彻底清零。这种 structural violence 导致了一个结果：即便她们曾是邮递员、战士或骑手，她们在档案中的表达也被简化为 phenotype（表型）的标签。当一个群体被剥夺了定义自身事实的权力，她们在历史中就是不存在的。

Jackson 采用的“equestrian embodiment”（骑马具象化）是一次极具攻击性的表达。马在殖民叙事中是权力与征服的符号，而她让黑人女性重新骑上马，这不仅是视觉上的致敬，而是在博弈中夺回解释权。她通过“critical fabulation”（批判性虚构）填补档案的空白，用身体的在场去对抗历史的缺席。这是一种典型的“真.最优解表达”：不请求主流叙事的接纳，而是直接通过重构事实来制造可能性。

最深刻的刺在于《Intimate Justice》系列。历史记录了黑人女性的劳作与受难，却从未记录她们的“休息”与“内省”。因为在男性中心叙事中，客体是不需要内省的。记录静谧，就是对元暴力最精准的反击。这次差额的缩小，是用艺术手段在文化层撕开了一个口子，让那些被定义为“工具”的身体重新拥有了 interiority（内心世界）。

Many 'women's art exhibitions' fall into a trap of performative gentleness, but Ayana V Jackson's exhibition in Arles strikes at the heart of meta-violence. She isn't just dealing with 'forgotten women,' but the systemic erasure of the Primal Race—Black and mestiza women—from historical narratives.

Under the logic of meta-violence, the bodies of Black women were defined as 'beasts of burden' or 'tools.' Their subjectivity was zeroed out in the bookkeeping of colonizers. This structural violence ensures that even when they were mail carriers, soldiers, or riders, their expression in archives was reduced to mere phenotype labels. When a group is stripped of the power to define their own facts, they cease to exist in history.

Jackson's use of 'equestrian embodiment' is an aggressive act of expression. In colonial narratives, the horse is a symbol of power and conquest; by placing Black women back on horseback, she isn't just paying tribute, she is seizing the right of interpretation. Through 'critical fabulation,' she fills the archival voids, using physical presence to combat historical absence. This is a true optimal expression: not begging for acceptance from the mainstream narrative, but creating new possibilities by reconstructing facts.

The sharpest point lies in the 'Intimate Justice' series. History recorded the labor and suffering of Black women, but never their 'rest' or 'introspection.' In a masculine-centric narrative, objects are not granted the luxury of interiority. To record silence and peace is the most precise riposte to meta-violence. This reduction of the gap is a cultural strike, allowing bodies once defined as 'tools' to finally reclaim their own interiority.
