The newsHelen Mirren and Alden Ehrenreich star in A Talent for Murder, now in theaters. Adapted from the 2014 play Switzerland, the film follows author Patricia Highsmith and a young New York publisher. Mirren plays Highsmith; Ehrenreich plays both the publisher and Tom Ripley.
一个女演员被允许演天才，前提是天才得是个怪人
An actress may play a genius, if the genius is first made a freak.

海伦·米伦爱刀。这是这篇采访最抓人的一句，也是整篇报道唯一没被追问的一句。

一个在修道会学校读书的女孩，每天坐公交，盯着街对面刀店的橱窗看。刀柄、配重、钢。她说得那么清楚，清楚到你能看见那个女孩。然后采访就滑到了下一个问题：你怎么准备这个角色。

没人问那把刀对她意味着什么。也没人问，一个女演员到了这个年纪，为什么会说“我们演员，我们爱我们的道具”。

Patricia Highsmith 被写成一个怪人：爱刀，爱枪，隐居，难搞。米伦演她，报道说她看了 YouTube 上仅有的几段采访，“谢天谢地有 YouTube”。一个写书的人，留下的影像资料少到需要感谢一个视频网站。这不是怀旧，这是档案的分配问题——谁被记录，谁被反复拍摄，谁只剩下几段“表现良好”的电视访谈。

然后看 Ehrenreich 那条线。他演 Tom Ripley，去看了《怒海沉尸》，因为 Highsmith 本人喜欢那一版。他说，因为阿兰·德龙“太好看了”。米伦接了一句：“太美了。”

这段对话是整篇报道里最诚实的地方，也是它最不诚实的地方。一个男演员演一个骗子杀手，准备工作是确认原作者脑子里那张脸够不够漂亮；一个女演员演那个原作者，准备工作是找几段她“表现良好”的影像。一个被反复重拍、反复选角、反复讨论“哪一版最帅”的角色，和一个只留下几段录像的创造者，就在同一篇采访里并排摆着。

报道没写的是：这部片子里，Highsmith 是那个被拜访的人，Ridgeway 是那个上门的人。一个年轻男人走进一个老女人的房子，要她再写一本。这个设定本身不新鲜，新鲜的是它被当成致敬。

米伦说刀很漂亮。她说的是真的。一把刀握在手里，重心、钢口、分量，都是真的。但她在这部电影里握刀，是因为剧本让她握。她爱刀，和 Highsmith 爱刀，是两件事。报道把这两件事写成了一件。

Highsmith 死了。她的角色还活着，还在被重拍，还在被讨论哪一版最帅。这就是表达和存在的区别：一个人可以死，一个角色可以一直被人替他选角。

采访最后一句是“影片现已上映”。米伦爱刀那句话，留在开头，没人接。

Helen Mirren loves knives. That is the most arresting line in the interview, and the only one nobody follows up.

A girl at a convent school in England, taking the bus every day, standing across the street from a knife shop, looking at the handles, the balance, the steel. She tells it so precisely you can see the girl. Then the interview moves on to the next question: how did you prepare for the role.

Nobody asks what the knife means to her. Nobody asks why an actress of her age says, “We actors, we love our props.”

Patricia Highsmith is written as an eccentric: knives, guns, reclusive, difficult. Mirren plays her, and the report says she watched the few interviews on YouTube — “Thank God for YouTube.” A woman who wrote books, and the surviving footage is so thin that an actress thanks a video site for it. This is not nostalgia. It is the distribution of archives: who gets recorded, who gets filmed over and over, who is left with a few clips of being “on her best behavior.”

Then the Ehrenreich line. He plays Tom Ripley, so he watched Purple Noon, because Highsmith loved that version. He says he was curious because Alain Delon is “just so good-looking.” Mirren agrees: “So beautiful.”

That exchange is the most honest moment in the piece, and the least honest. An actor preparing to play a con-man killer checks whether the face in the author’s head was pretty enough. An actress preparing to play the author looks for the few clips where she behaved. A character who gets remade, recast, and debated for which version is handsomest sits in the same interview as the woman who invented him, and she is the one with almost no footage.

The report does not mention what the setup actually is: Highsmith is the one being visited. Ridgeway is the one who comes to the house. A young man walks into an old woman’s home and asks her to write another book. That is not a new story. What is new is that it is framed as a tribute.

Mirren says knives are beautiful. She is right. A knife in the hand has weight, edge, balance. But she holds a knife in this film because the script tells her to. Her loving knives and Highsmith loving knives are two different things. The interview writes them as one.

Highsmith is dead. Her character is alive, still being remade, still being discussed for which version is handsomest. That is the difference between expression and existence: a person can die, and a character can keep having someone cast on his behalf.

The last line is “in theaters now.” The knife line stays at the top, unanswered.
