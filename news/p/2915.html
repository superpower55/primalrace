能源危机是男性宏大叙事的代价Energy Crisis as the Cost of Masculine Grand Narratives

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-28 § 链接
所有被掩盖在“地缘政治”下的能源匮乏，本质上都是男性战争机器的结构性暴力。
All energy scarcity masked as 'geopolitics' is essentially structural violence driven by masculine war machines.

红海封锁、霍胡西武装、波斯湾博弈，这些词汇在新闻里被包装成地缘政治的“意外”，但本质上是男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 下的必然产物。国家机器本质上就是暴力机器，而这些机器的操盘手全部是男性。他们将石油这种生物资源转化为权力的筹码，在地图上划线，在海峡里设卡，把全球能源供应链变成了他们存在性战争的棋盘。

这就是典型的结构暴力 (structural violence)。当日本、韩国、菲律宾的民众面临电费上涨、企业关门或被迫缩短工作周时，他们承受的是一个他们从未参与决策的、由男性定义的“安全局势”所带来的差额。Potential 是一个稳定的能源供应系统，而 Actual 是在战争威胁下被截断的油管。这个差额，就是暴力。

最讽刺的是，这些政府在危机时刻的“最优解表达”竟然是回归煤电或向制裁中的俄罗斯买油。这不是在解决问题，而是在元暴力 (meta violence) 的共谋下，通过更换一个施暴者来维持脆弱的现状。他们试图在一个由男性定义的、充满敌意的规则集里寻找生存空间，却忘记了只要这个基于“强权即真理”的男性叙事不崩塌，下一个封锁点永远在路上。

能源转型被赋予了“紧迫性”，但这在目前看来更像是一种表演性的让步。如果转型的动力仅仅来自于“被掐脖子”的恐惧，而非对一个公正表达系统的追求，那么这种转型依然在男性定义的权力逻辑内运行。我们不需要一个更高效的“能源安全”策略，我们需要的是拆除那个把资源武器化、把人类身体当成消耗品的元暴力结构。

Red Sea blockades, Houthi militants, and Persian Gulf gambles are packaged in the news as geopolitical 'accidents,' but they are the inevitable products of a masculine-centric narrative. State machinery is essentially violence machinery, operated entirely by men. They transform biological resources like oil into power chips, drawing lines on maps and setting traps in straits, turning the global energy supply chain into a chessboard for their existential war.

This is textbook structural violence. When citizens in Japan, South Korea, or the Philippines face soaring electricity bills, business closures, or forced four-day work weeks, they are absorbing the deficit created by a 'security situation' defined by men—a situation they never participated in deciding. The Potential is a stable energy system; the Actual is a pipeline severed by war threats. That gap is violence.

Most ironic is the 'optimal expression' of these governments in times of crisis: reverting to coal or buying oil from a sanctioned Russia. This isn't solving the problem; it's complicity under meta-violence, merely swapping one aggressor for another to maintain a fragile status quo. They seek survival within a rule-set defined by masculinity and 'might makes right,' oblivious to the fact that as long as this narrative stands, the next chokepoint is already inevitable.

The 'urgency' of the renewables transition now feels more like a performative concession. If the drive for transition stems only from the fear of being strangled rather than a pursuit of a just expression system, this transition still operates within the masculine power logic. We don't need a more efficient 'energy security' strategy; we need to dismantle the meta-violence structure that weaponizes resources and treats human existence as a consumable asset.
