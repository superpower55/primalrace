名人购物清单：一场关于“正常人”的认知诈骗Celebrity Shopping Lists: A Cognitive Scam of 'Being Normal'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
品味是阶级筛选的武器，而“像我们一样”是最高级的伪装。
Taste is a weapon for class screening, and 'just like us' is the ultimate disguise.

《卫报》这篇报道试图用一种温情且琐碎的叙事，把名人与普通人拉到同一个维度：他们也买 M&S 的内衣，也会买错割草机，甚至也会在半梦半醒间下单一件“保守派妻子”的古怪衣服。这种“他们其实和我们一样”的叙事，是典型的文化暴力 (cultural violence) 伪装，旨在通过制造一种虚假的亲近感，让受众在潜意识里完成对特定消费符号的认同。

事实上，这根本不是关于“购物习惯”的分享，而是一次精准的认知入口 (cognitive entry) 投放。当文章提到 2500 英镑的割草机或 1999 英镑的激光美容仪时，它在完成两件事：第一，通过极高定价确立阶级区隔；第二，通过名人的“背书”将这些昂贵的消费定义为“有效”且“值得追求”的。即便文中用“价格太贵”来调侃，但这恰恰是这种 weaponized expression 的高明之处——它不强迫你买，它只是在定义什么是“顶级的生活方式”。

最令人反感的共谋 (complicity) 在于对女性身体的规训。一名名人提到昂贵的激光仪能减少肉毒杆菌注射，另一名女性抱怨找不到适合自己身形的牛仔裤，甚至有人后悔买了根本走不动的 Louboutin 高跟鞋。这些被包装成“生活趣闻”的碎片，本质上是在强化一种男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 下的审美枷锁：女性必须通过昂贵的设备去对抗衰老，必须在不舒适的鞋子里维持某种“优雅”的表型。这种对身体的折磨被转化为一种“名流式的烦恼”，从而让普通女性在潜意识里认为，这种痛苦是通往更高阶层必须支付的入场券。

所谓的“像我们一样”，不过是权力阶层在完成资源垄断后，偶尔向底层投放的一点审美残羹。真正的阶级差异不在于是否买同一品牌的内衣，而在于谁拥有定义“什么是酷”、“什么是高级”的定价权。当我们沉溺于窥视名人的购物篮时，我们其实是在用自己的主体性，为这套消费主义的 scam 投票。

The Guardian's piece attempts to pull celebrities and ordinary people into the same dimension through a warm, trivial narrative: they buy M&S underwear, buy the wrong lawnmowers, and even impulse-buy 'Tory wife' outfits. This 'they are just like us' narrative is a classic disguise of cultural violence, designed to foster a subconscious identification with specific consumer symbols through a fake sense of intimacy.

In reality, this isn't a share of 'shopping habits,' but a precise deployment of a cognitive entry. When the article mentions a £2,500 lawnmower or a £1,999 laser kit, it achieves two things: first, it establishes class distinction through extreme pricing; second, it defines these expensive consumptions as 'effective' and 'desirable' via celebrity endorsement. Even the teasing about the 'ouch' price tag is a sophisticated part of this weaponized expression—it doesn't force you to buy; it simply defines what 'top-tier lifestyle' looks like.

The most repulsive complicity lies in the discipline of the female body. One celebrity mentions a laser device reducing Botox needs, another complains about jeans not fitting her shape, and another regrets Louboutins she can barely walk in. These fragments, packaged as 'lifestyle anecdotes,' essentially reinforce aesthetic shackles under a masculine-centric narrative: women must use expensive gadgets to fight aging and maintain a certain phenotype in uncomfortable shoes. This physical torture is transformed into a 'celebrity struggle,' leading ordinary women to believe such pain is the entry fee for a higher social stratum.

'Just like us' is merely a few aesthetic crumbs tossed to the bottom by the power class after securing their resource monopoly. The real class difference isn't about buying the same brand of underwear, but about who holds the pricing power to define what is 'cool' or 'sophisticated.' While we indulge in snooping through celebrity shopping baskets, we are using our own subjectivity to vote for this consumerist scam.
