淘汰的是最会看的人The One Who Saw It All Got Voted Off

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《与星共舞》第35季迪士尼之夜，八对选手分成两队各跳一支团体舞，团体分计入总分。评委Cynthia Erivo坐镇，Harry Shum Jr.与Jenna Dewan两对拿到满分40，Julia Stiles与Maura Higgins进入最后淘汰环节，最终Stiles被淘汰。
The newsDancing With the Stars season 35's Disney Night split the eight remaining couples into two teams, with group-dance scores added to each couple's total. Guest judge Cynthia Erivo watched Harry Shum Jr. and Jenna Dewan each score a perfect 40; Julia Stiles and Maura Higgins landed in the bottom, and Stiles was eliminated.
她被淘汰，因为她把判断力攥在自己手里
She was eliminated for keeping her own judgment

Julia Stiles 被淘汰了。她是这档节目自认的粉丝，跳完还说自己「从身体里掏出了以前不知道存在的东西」。一个从小看这节目长大的人，上场跳了，然后被请下台。

这话听起来像煽情，其实是这档节目最诚实的一句台词：它卖的不是舞蹈，是「一个普通人被这套体系改造」的过程。Stiles 是演员，不是舞者，肋骨受伤，排练时倒下，评委 Carrie Ann Inaba 盯着她的眼睛说「我看见你了，我看见你在往深处挖」。挖出来的东西很动人，但打动评委不等于拿到票。

真正决定她去留的，是那支团体舞。团体分直接加进每对选手的总分，Ballas 在排练里说得很清楚：一支团体舞可以彻底翻掉排行榜。于是这个晚上最要紧的能力不是跳舞，是选队友。Wood 和 Ali 因为总分垫底，反而拿到了先选人的权利，Wood 一把抓走 Shum、Dewan、Frech——当晚最强的三对，然后他们拿了满分 40。Ali 那边呢，Higgins 在排练里崩溃，反复说自己「总是在搞砸」。

这不是比赛，这是微型社会。规则写得像体育，运作起来是结盟。谁和谁站在一起，比谁跳得好更决定结果。这一点我在别处写过很多次，今天它穿着亮片出现在迪士尼之夜。

还有一句值得单独拎出来。Inaba 夸 Ali 时说的是「你身上辐射出一种 divine feminine energy」。这句话在 2026 年的黄金档综艺里被当作最高级的赞美说出来，而它赞美的东西——滋养的、发散的、母性的能量——恰好是把女性固定在一个位置上的那套词。Ali 那支舞跳得确实好，但好在哪里，评委给不出比「神圣阴性能量」更精确的语言。解释权的贫瘠，比任何一次低分都更能说明问题。

顺带一提，Inaba 还问了句「这是 DWTS 历史上第一次有女人领舞男人吗？如果是，bravo」。这问题本身就是答案。

Stiles 下台时，Sosa 对着她说「我很抱歉」。他抱歉什么？抱歉这套规则把她筛掉了，还是抱歉他自己也在这套规则里？镜头拉远，主持人收工，没人追问。

节目最后一行字是下一集周二晚八点播出。Stiles 的成长被剪成了一段告别感言，然后是广告位。她掏出来的那些东西，节目组不负责保管。

Julia Stiles is gone. She called herself a DWTS fangirl, said she pulled things out of herself she didn't know were there. Someone who grew up watching this show, finally danced on it, and got sent home.

That sounds like sentimentality. It's actually the most honest line of the night: what this show sells isn't dancing, it's the spectacle of an ordinary person being remade by the machine. Stiles is an actress, not a dancer. Rib injury, collapsed in rehearsals. Carrie Ann Inaba locked eyes with her and said, "I see you." Seeing her was not the same as voting for her.

What actually decided her fate was the group dance. Those scores go straight into each couple's total, and Ballas said it plainly in rehearsals: a team dance can flip the leaderboard. So the skill that mattered most on Disney Night wasn't dancing. It was picking teammates. Wood and Ali, sitting at the bottom, got to choose first. Wood grabbed Shum, Dewan, and Frech — the three strongest dancers in the competition — and walked off with a perfect 40. On Ali's side, Higgins was falling apart in rehearsals, repeating that she's "always f---ing up."

This isn't a competition. It's a miniature society. The rules are written like sport and operate like coalition-building. Who stands next to whom matters more than who dances well. I've written this elsewhere; tonight it showed up in sequins.

One more line deserves its own paragraph. Inaba praised Ali for the "divine feminine energy that just radiates from you." Said in 2026, on prime-time television, as the highest compliment available. And what it praises — nurturing, radiating, maternal energy — is exactly the vocabulary that pins women to a position. Ali danced beautifully. The judges had no more precise language for why. That poverty of interpretation explains more than any low score.

Inaba also asked, "Is that the first time we've had a woman leading a man in the history of DWTS?" The question is the answer.

As Stiles left, Sosa could be seen telling her, "I'm so sorry." Sorry for what? Sorry the rules filtered her out, or sorry he's inside them too? The cameras pulled back, the hosts signed off, nobody asked.

The last line of the recap is that new episodes air Tuesdays at 8. Stiles's growth got cut into a farewell segment, then a commercial break. Whatever she pulled out of herself, the show doesn't keep it for her.
