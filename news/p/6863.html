国家安全挡不住排气管National Security Can't Cap a Smokestack

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 直接层 Truthout ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要10月30日，联邦地区法院首席法官 Debra M. Brown 驳回了 xAI 要求驳回 NAACP 诉讼的动议。NAACP 今年4月起诉 xAI，指其在密西西比州建设 Colossus 数据中心时未取得许可、违反《清洁空气法》。此前10月19日，Brown 也驳回了司法部以国家安全为由要求驳回诉讼的动议。
The newsOn October 30, Chief U.S. District Judge Debra M. Brown denied xAI's motion to dismiss the NAACP's lawsuit. The NAACP sued in April, alleging the company built its Mississippi data center, Colossus, without permits and in violation of the Clean Air Act. On October 19, Brown also denied a DOJ motion to dismiss that cited national security.
换尺子换不掉涡轮机，法官没上当
Changing the ruler doesn't change the turbine; the judge didn't buy it.

xAI 的辩护策略很眼熟：现实不配合，就重新定义现实。涡轮机明明是钉在地上的固定排放源，xAI 偏说它是「移动源」——仿佛发电机长了腿，可以随时开走，所以不用申请许可。同一套逻辑我们在别处见过：做不出小芯片，就宣布一条新定律；改不动污染，就改分类。换尺子不换能力，换名词不换涡轮机。

这次法官 Debra M. Brown 没有配合演出。10月30日，她驳回了 xAI 要求撤诉的动议；更早的10月19日，她也驳回了司法部那份动议。司法部说这个数据中心关乎国家安全，还承认美军在伊朗的军事行动里用了 xAI 的 Grok Gov Model，五角大楼的 Maven Smart Systems 拿它做「瞄准」和「情报」。Brown 的回应只有一句：本院不认为美国的国家安全关切与本案相关。

这句话值得单独拎出来。因为司法部那份动议真正做的，是把一条《清洁空气法》的许可问题，翻译成一场国家存亡的叙事——一旦翻译成功，密西西比河边谁在喘什么空气就不再是问题。这是标准的武器化入口：夺取解释权，把「违法排放」重写成「支持战争努力」，然后让质疑者背上不爱国。加尔通的暴力三角在这里是联动的：文化层的叙事（国家安全）试图合法化结构层的豁免（不用许可），最终落到直接层——住在数据中心旁边的居民吸进去的东西。Earthjustice 的 Laura Thoms 说得直白：这届政府「特意 shielding 马斯克的公司」。

但要注意，这不是一场已经赢了的仗。法官只是让案子继续走下去，没有判定污染存在，也没有判罚一分钱。NAACP 的 Abre' Conner 说这是「重要的胜利」，用词准确——是程序上的门没被关上，不是空气变干净了。structural 层的暴力要靠执行来削，而执行还没发生。

真正刺眼的是谁在这场博弈里始终没有名字。整篇报道里，密西西比那个社区的人一次也没有开口，只有 NAACP、Earthjustice、法官、司法部、xAI 轮番发言。污染的实际承受者被代表、被统计、被「为正义而诉」，却不在叙事里占一个位置。案子可以继续打，但解释权的席位上，他们还是空着的。

xAI's defense is familiar: when reality won't cooperate, redefine reality. The turbines are bolted to the ground, but xAI argued they were 「mobile sources」—as if a gas plant could grow legs and drive away, exempting it from permits. We've seen this move before: can't build the small chip, announce a new law; can't clean the emissions, change the category. Swap the ruler, keep the capability. Swap the noun, keep the turbine.

Judge Debra M. Brown didn't play along. On October 30 she denied xAI's motion to dismiss; on October 19 she denied the DOJ's. The DOJ said the data center was vital to national security and admitted the U.S. military used xAI's Grok Gov Model in operations in Iran, with the Pentagon's Maven Smart Systems deploying it for 「targeting」 and 「intelligence.」 Brown's answer was one line: the Court does not consider the United States' national security concerns relevant to this case.

That line deserves its own paragraph. The DOJ's motion was doing translation work: turning a Clean Air Act permitting question into a story about national survival—and once the translation lands, who breathes what along the Mississippi stops being a question. This is the standard weaponization entry point: seize the interpretive slot, rewrite 「illegal emissions」 as 「supporting the war effort,」 and let the skeptics carry the unpatriotic label. Galtung's triangle runs in sequence here—a cultural-layer narrative (national security) trying to legalize a structural-layer exemption (no permits), landing finally on the direct layer: what the people near the data center inhale. Laura Thoms of Earthjustice put it plainly: the administration 「has gone out of its way to shield」 Musk's company.

But this is not a won war. The judge let the case proceed; she did not find pollution, did not levy a dollar. Abre' Conner of the NAACP called it an 「important victory,」 and the wording is precise—a procedural door left open, not cleaner air. Structural violence gets reduced by enforcement, and enforcement hasn't happened.

What's sharpest is who never gets a name. In the entire report, the people of that Mississippi community don't speak once—only the NAACP, Earthjustice, the judge, the DOJ, xAI, taking turns. The people actually breathing the pollution are represented, counted, sued-for, and still absent from the narrative. The case can go on. Their seat at the interpretive table is still empty.
