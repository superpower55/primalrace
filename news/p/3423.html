名字的错位与权力的幽灵：一场关于“被认错”的男性游戏The Misplaced Name and the Ghost of Power: A Masculine Game of Mistaken Identity

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
当错位被转化为幽默，是因为博弈者拥有足够的结构性权力来消解暴力。
Mistake becomes humor only when the players possess enough structural power to neutralize the violence.

两个名字相差一个字母的男人，在同一个体育领域工作，其中一个因为评论老虎伍兹而被愤怒的粉丝围攻，而另一个则在社交媒体上替他承受了十五年的口水。在大多数人的叙事里，这是一个关于“误会”和“幽默”的温馨故事，但如果用 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式来看，这其实是一次典型的权力消解过程。

对于 Ewan Murray 来说，被认错意味着他成了某种程度上的“肉盾”或“替身”。但有趣的是，他并没有感到被侵犯，反而将其变成了一种“扮演”的快感——他用一个更傲慢、更刻薄的 alter ego 去回击那些愤怒的观众。这种“以火攻火”的博弈之所以能变成笑话，是因为他处于一个安全的结构位置：他不需要依赖那些愤怒的粉丝生存，他拥有足够的文化资本（cultural capital）将这种攻击转化为一种社交货币，甚至在同行和名将之间分享这种“趣闻”。

真正的暴力发生在那些发送邮件的愤怒者身上，以及被误认为目标的 Ewan 身上。但在这场博弈中，男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）提供了一个完美的掩体：他们将这种误认、辱骂甚至潜在的身体威胁（如在酒吧被寻衅）定义为“体育文化”的一部分，定义为“幽默”。当一个男性可以对着被误认的辱骂大笑时，是因为他知道这种暴力在结构层面上不足以撼动他的主体性。

这种“幽默”是特权者的奢侈品。如果被认错的是一个结构性弱势者，这种 mistaken identity 将会迅速从“趣闻”转化为直接暴力（direct violence）或严重的精神折磨。在这里，两个 Murray 的相遇不是什么命运的巧合，而是一次关于权力如何将暴力转化为娱乐的演示。他们大笑着谈论彼此，因为他们共谋在同一个由男性主导的、宽容且自洽的体育话语体系之中。

Two men, names differing by a single letter, working in the same sporting domain. One is attacked by fans for criticizing Tiger Woods; the other spends fifteen years absorbing the fallout on social media. To most, this is a heartwarming tale of 'misunderstanding' and 'wit,' but through the lens of Violence = Potential − Actual, it is a textbook case of power neutralization.

For Ewan Murray, being mistaken meant becoming a 'shield' or a 'proxy.' Yet, he felt no violation. Instead, he transformed this into a pleasure of performance, using a more arrogant, biting alter ego to fight back. This 'fire with fire' strategy only works as a joke because he occupies a secure structural position: he does not depend on the approval of those angry fans. He possesses sufficient cultural capital to convert aggression into social currency, sharing the 'anecdote' with peers and champions.

The real violence resides in the rage of the senders and the target of the mistake. However, the masculine-centric narrative provides a perfect bunker: they define this misidentification, the abuse, and even the latent threat of physical violence (like being hunted in a pub) as part of 'sports culture'—as 'humor.' When a man can laugh at the insults directed at his mistaken identity, it is because he knows this violence is structurally insufficient to destabilize his subjectivity.

Such 'humor' is a luxury of the privileged. If the mistaken party were a structurally marginalized individual, this mistaken identity would rapidly shift from 'curiosity' to direct violence or severe psychological trauma. The meeting of the two Murrays is not a coincidence of fate, but a demonstration of how power converts violence into entertainment. They laugh together because they are complicit in the same male-dominated, indulgent, and self-consistent discourse of sports.
