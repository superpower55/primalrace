谁在为谁背书Who Is Endorsing Whom

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要密歇根州共和党参议员候选人迈克·罗杰斯的竞选团队中有两名登记为以色列外国代理人的工作人员，其中发言人阿比盖尔·朗森促成并主持了民主党众议员黑莉·史蒂文斯背书罗杰斯的记者会。史蒂文斯的未婚夫杰森·特基此前公开表示将投票给罗杰斯，多名友人及前同僚称特基对她施加了影响。
The newsTwo staffers on Republican Mike Rogers's Michigan Senate campaign are registered foreign agents for Israel, including spokesperson Abigail Ronson, who facilitated the press conference where Democratic Representative Haley Stevens endorsed Rogers. Stevens's fiancé Jason Turkish had said he would vote for Rogers, and friends and former colleagues say he influenced her decision.
背书的人换了阵营，登记代理人没换雇主。
The endorser switched sides; the registered agents kept their employer.

先看清这场记者会的结构：一个登记为以色列外国代理人的发言人，站在一个民主党众议员和一个共和党候选人中间，宣布前者背书后者。这不是丑闻的边角料，这是丑闻的全部构图。

朗森五月就登记为外国代理人，注册文件写明她的工作是「为以色列国开发并执行一场全美范围的公关行动，以对抗反犹主义」。她通过Convergence Media进入罗杰斯团队，分包在Havas Media Group之下——Havas是亲以色列广告的主要管道之一。另一位登记代理人杰森·斯托克斯十月登记，替罗杰斯写了不少新闻稿。一个竞选团队，两个外国代理人，一个负责对外发声，一个负责对内写稿。

FARA的存在本身就承认了一件事：政治表达可以被外国利益购买，只是要求你申报。申报了，就合法了。合法了，就没人再问一句：密歇根州选民的参议员，为什么由替别国做公关的人来包装？

再看史蒂文斯这一侧。她的未婚夫特基是民主党人，选前公开说自己要投罗杰斯，理由是民主党候选人埃尔-赛义德批评以色列对密歇根犹太人构成威胁。前州长布兰查德转述特基如何「磨」她——「所有人都对你失望」。她的朋友、密歇根大学校董阿克尔说，史蒂文斯失联了，「她的未婚夫拿走了她的手机」。前发言人莱加基说，两人秘密订婚，特基开始对竞选施加过大影响，霸凌年轻员工，逼她更用力地为以色列辩护。

这些是身边人的指控，特基否认了拿走手机的说法。但报道里没有出现的，是史蒂文斯本人的解释。她背书了一个MAGA共和党人，然后没有给出任何理由。一个人从「我会支持本党提名人」到「我背书对手」，中间发生了什么，报道只给了旁人的转述和一个沉默。

这里要问的不是她是不是被谁控制了——那是把女性当成没有主体性的容器，正是元暴力的老套路。要问的是：谁在这笔交易里拿到了什么。罗杰斯拿到了一个民主党人的背书，用来证明自己「跨党派」；以色列政策拿到了一个公开的、由登记代理人导演的背书场面；特基拿到了他想要的政治表态。史蒂文斯拿到了什么，报道没写。

布兰查德反复说「这不是我认识的黑莉」，「她在二十四小时里变了个人」。这句话本身值得停下来看：一个政治人物改变立场，周围人的第一反应是她的伴侣在操控她。这个解释很方便——它让背叛变成一个私人情感问题，而不是一个政治交易问题。可报道里同时摆着另一条线：朗森、斯托克斯、Havas、Convergence，一整套职业化的、有注册文件的、按合同运作的机器。私人情感和外国代理人的公关合同，哪个更能解释这场背书？

报道把这两条线并排放着，没有说哪条是真的。我的判断是：它们不冲突。一个人可以在被亲密关系施压的同时，被一台专业机器接住、包装、推上台。前者是私人空间的博弈，后者是公共空间的武器化，两者在同一个人身上合流。

苏珊·怀尔德那句话是整篇报道里最诚实也最偷懒的一句：「任何女人，一有新男人出现就抛弃朋友，就不是朋友。」她把一场涉及外国代理人、政党背书和选举资金的政治事件，压缩成一句关于女人和男人的道德评语。这句话骂的是史蒂文斯，放过的却是那台机器——那台机器不需要她「变了个人」，它只需要一个愿意站上台的人。

埃尔-赛义德的竞选在事后收到了迟来的捐款激增。这是这场闹剧里唯一一个具体的、可计量的后果：一部分民主党选民的钱，流向了被背叛的那个人。至于密歇根的选民最终拿到什么，要等票数出来才知道。

Look at the composition of that press conference first: a spokesperson registered as a foreign agent for Israel, standing between a Democratic representative and a Republican candidate, announcing that the former endorses the latter. This is not the edge of the scandal. This is the whole picture.

Ronson registered as a foreign agent in May. Her filing states she will provide strategic communications and media services in support of Havas's engagement by the State of Israel to run a nationwide campaign in the United States to combat anti-semitism. She reached Rogers's campaign through Convergence Media, subcontracted under Havas Media Group, one of the main vessels for pro-Israel advertising. Jason Stokes, the second registered agent, signed on in October and has written many of Rogers's press releases. One campaign, two foreign agents: one speaks outward, one writes inward.

FARA itself admits the premise: political expression can be bought by foreign interests, as long as you file the paperwork. Filed means legal. Legal means nobody asks why a Michigan Senate seat is being packaged by people whose day job is public relations for another state.

Now Stevens's side. Her fiancé Jason Turkish, a Jewish Democrat, said publicly before the election that he would vote for Rogers, convinced that Abdul El-Sayed's criticism of Israel threatened Jews in Michigan. Former governor Jim Blanchard recounted how Turkish worked her over: everyone is disappointed in you. Her friend Jordan Acker wrote that her fiancé took her phone away; Turkish denies it, saying she simply turned it off. Former spokesperson Caitlin Legacki says the couple got engaged in secret and that Turkish bullied junior staffers and pushed Stevens to defend Israel more loudly.

These are accusations from people around her. What the report does not contain is any explanation from Stevens herself. She endorsed a MAGA Republican and offered nothing. Between I will support my party's nominee and I endorse the other guy, the report gives us bystanders' accounts and a silence.

The question is not whether some man controlled her. That framing turns a woman into a vessel without agency, which is the oldest move of meta-violence. The question is who got paid. Rogers got a Democrat's endorsement to prove he is bipartisan. The Israel policy got a public endorsement scene directed by a registered agent. Turkish got the political statement he wanted. What Stevens got, the report does not say.

Blanchard says several times: this is not the Haley I know, something snapped in her, and it snapped in twenty-four hours. Pause on that. When a politician changes position, the first reflex is that her partner is steering her. That explanation is convenient: it turns betrayal into a private romance problem instead of a political transaction. But the report places a second line right beside it: Ronson, Stokes, Havas, Convergence, a professional machine with filings and contracts. Which better explains the endorsement, private pressure or a foreign agent's PR contract?

The report sets the two lines side by side without adjudicating. My judgment is that they do not conflict. A person can be pressured inside an intimate relationship and simultaneously caught, packaged, and walked onstage by a professional apparatus. The first is a game in the private sphere; the second is weaponized expression in the public one. They converge in one body.

Susan Wild's line is the most honest and laziest sentence in the piece: any woman who abandons her friends when a new guy comes along is no friend at all. She compresses a political event involving foreign agents, party endorsements, and campaign money into a moral verdict about women and men. It punishes Stevens and lets the machine go. The machine never needed her to become a different person. It needed someone willing to stand at the podium.

El-Sayed's campaign saw a late funding surge. That is the one concrete, countable consequence here: some Democratic money moved toward the person who was betrayed. What Michigan voters end up with will not be known until the count.
