在剧院禁掉智能眼镜：一场关于身体主权的微小胜利Banning Smart Glasses: A Tiny Victory for Bodily Sovereignty

好消息 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-15 § 链接
真正的政治不是在舞台上表演崩溃，而是夺回身体不被凝视的权力。
True politics is not performing collapse on stage, but reclaiming the right not to be gazed upon.

Dominic Cooke 在 Almeida 剧院大谈特谈世界的“崩溃”和英国的衰落，这种叙事在文化层面上几乎成了某种中产阶级艺术家的标准配置：通过在舞台上模拟危机来缓解现实中的无力感。这种“政治”是表演性的，它在讨论帝国衰落的同时，依然处于一个安全的、被允许的认知入口之内。

但这条新闻里真正具有政治意义的，反而是那个被随口提及的细节：剧院决定禁止 Meta 的智能眼镜。这才是真正的 existence war。智能眼镜的本质是把“凝视”武器化，它将采集、记录和传播的权力隐蔽化，让被拍摄者在毫无察觉的情况下被客体化。Cooke 提到这对女性尤其具有侵入性，这触及了生物墙之外的身体主权问题：当一个人的身体在不自愿的情况下被数字化并转化为他人的数据资源时，这就是一种 structural violence。

这种禁令是 Actual 朝着 Potential 走近了一步。它不是在舞台上讨论“苦难”，而是在物理空间里通过制度（structural layer）切断一种具体的、具有侵入性的暴力入口。当 Cate Blanchett 等演员开始反击那些在演出中拍摄的观众时，她们在对抗的不仅是分心，而是一种根深蒂固的、认为“我的凝视高于你的主体性”的 masculine-centric narrative。

当然，我们不需要 naive 地庆祝。禁掉一副眼镜无法瓦解整个 surveillance capitalism 的共谋体系。但至少在剧院这个特定的场域里，人们意识到：一个公正的表达空间，首先得保证参与者不需要在恐惧被偷拍的压力下才能生存。真正的政治不在于剧本写得多么 searing，而在于你是否拥有决定自己何时被看见的权力。

Dominic Cooke talks grandly about the world "falling apart" and Britain's decline at the Almeida. This narrative has become a standard configuration for middle-class artists: alleviating real-world impotence by simulating crisis on stage. This kind of "politics" is performative; it discusses the fall of empires while remaining safely within a sanctioned cognitive entrance.

The only part of this news with actual political weight is the casual mention of banning Meta's smart glasses. This is a real existence war. Smart glasses weaponize the gaze, making the power to capture and broadcast invisible, turning the subject into an object without their knowledge. As Cooke noted, this is especially invasive for women. It strikes at the heart of bodily sovereignty beyond the biological wall: when a body is digitized and converted into data for others without consent, it is structural violence.

This ban is a move where Actual approaches Potential. It isn't about discussing "suffering" in a script; it's about using a structural layer to cut off a specific, invasive entrance of violence. When actors like Cate Blanchett fight back against filming audiences, they aren't just fighting distraction, but a deep-seated masculine-centric narrative that believes "my gaze supersedes your subjectivity."

We shouldn't be naive. Banning a pair of glasses won't dismantle the complicity of surveillance capitalism. But in the specific field of the theatre, we recognize that a space for Just Expressions must first ensure that participants can exist without the pressure of being covertly recorded. True politics isn't in how "searing" the script is, but in whether you possess the power to decide when you are seen.
