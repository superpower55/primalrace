蝙蝠侠的反派靠模具泄露Batman's Villains Leaked Through a Prosthetic Hand

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要《蝙蝠侠2》今夏确认塞巴斯蒂安·斯坦、斯嘉丽·约翰逊、布莱恩·泰里·亨利、查尔斯·丹斯加盟，角色未公布。万圣节海蒂·克鲁姆的断手道具由曾参与2022年《蝙蝠侠》的特效公司制作，网传工作照背景出现写有「Zsasz」「Owl 3」的模具。影片在里夫斯因家事退出后由索德伯格接手，档期改为2028年2月18日。
The newsSebastian Stan, Scarlett Johansson, Brian Tyree Henry and Charles Dance joined The Batman Part 2's cast this summer, roles unconfirmed. A photo circulating from the prosthetics team behind Heidi Klum's Halloween severed hand appears to show molds labeled "Zsasz" and "Owl 3." Steven Soderbergh took over directing after Matt Reeves stepped down for a family matter; the film is now set for Feb. 18, 2028.
连反派都要靠玩具模具来官宣，这就是IP的定价权
Even the villain gets announced by a toy mold. That's IP pricing power.

一条选角新闻，最后是被一副万圣节假手坐实的。海蒂·克鲁姆扮断手，做道具的公司做过2022年的《蝙蝠侠》，于是背景里几个模具上的字——Zsasz、Owl 3——就成了比片方公告更可信的官宣。这不是八卦的胜利，这是IP把信息本身也变成商品的又一步：连「谁演谁」都不由剧组说，由周边供应链漏。

值得看的是漏出来的是什么。猫头鹰法庭，一群躲在暗处操控哥谭的富有家族。这是蝙蝠侠故事里最接近结构性暴力的设定：没有具体的坏人，只有一张网，网里的人互相认识、互相掩护、共享同一套体面。它被选中做续集的反派，里夫斯自己说这个反派「电影里从来没真正拍过」。一个关于隐形权力网的题材，在2026年上映，观众会怎么读它，不用我多说。

但别急着把这部片算成批判。猫头鹰法庭之所以好用，正因为它可以被安全地消费：观众看完走出影院，骂的是虚构的哥谭精英，而不是任何一张真实的网。文化暴力最舒服的形态就是这样——它让你以为自己看穿了权力，同时把看穿这件事本身卖成一张票。武器化的入口从来不在反派是谁，在于谁有权力决定这个反派被拍成什么形状。

真正被定价的是演员。斯坦、约翰逊、亨利、丹斯，四个名字被确认，四个角色被猜测，猜错猜对都不重要，重要的是这几个人在传闻里已经完成了一轮曝光。斯坦自己接受采访时说了角色，那篇稿子后来被改掉、把那句话删了——删掉这个动作比那句话更说明问题：不是他没说，是有人决定这句话不该存在。谁在共谋，回报是什么，报道没写，但模具照片流出来的时机，巧得像排过。

还有一层没人提：里夫斯因家事退出，索德伯格接手，档期从2025年10月推到2028年2月。一个导演的家庭事务，在新闻里只是一句括号，然后整部电影的时间表跟着挪了两年多。这不是丑闻，这只是提醒——在这个工业里，一个人的私事可以直接改写几亿美元项目的日程，而绝大多数在片场搬东西的人，连请一天假都要打报告。

所以这条新闻里真正被泄露的不是反派。是一个行业把「秘密」本身做成了营销物料：先放风，再否认，再让一副假手的背景替你确认。观众以为自己在破解谜题，其实谜题的答案早就被安排在该被看到的位置。猫头鹰法庭在故事里靠控制信息统治哥谭，而这部片子的宣发，正在用同样的手法统治它的观众——只不过这一次，没有蝙蝠侠来查。

A casting story that got confirmed by a Halloween prop. Heidi Klum dressed as a severed hand; the company that made it worked on 2022's The Batman; so the words on the molds in the background—Zsasz, Owl 3—became more credible than any studio announcement. This isn't gossip winning. It's IP turning information itself into merchandise: even who-plays-who doesn't come from the production, it leaks out of the supply chain.

What leaked matters. The Court of Owls: wealthy Gothamites running the city from the shadows. It's the closest thing Batman has to structural violence—no single villain, just a web whose members know each other, cover for each other, share the same respectability. It was picked as the sequel's antagonist, and Reeves has said this villain has never really been done in a movie before. A story about an invisible power network, arriving in 2028. Audiences will read it however they read it. I don't need to spell that out.

But don't file this as critique. The Court of Owls is useful precisely because it can be consumed safely: you leave the theater angry at fictional Gotham elites, not at any real web. That's cultural violence at its most comfortable—it lets you believe you've seen through power while selling the seeing-through as a ticket. The weaponized entry point was never which villain; it's who gets to decide what shape that villain takes.

What's actually being priced is the cast. Stan, Johansson, Henry, Dance—four names confirmed, four roles guessed, and whether the guesses are right doesn't matter, because the rumor already did a round of exposure for them. Stan said his role in an interview; the article was later updated, the mention removed. The removal says more than the quote: it isn't that he didn't say it, it's that someone decided the sentence shouldn't exist. Who conspired, and for what return, the report doesn't say—but the timing of that mold photo is suspiciously tidy.

One layer nobody mentions: Reeves stepped down for a family matter, Soderbergh took over, and the release slid from October 2025 to February 2028. A director's family business gets one clause in the story, and a multi-hundred-million-dollar schedule moves two-plus years. Not a scandal. Just a reminder—in this industry one person's private life rewrites a project's calendar, while most people carrying things on set need paperwork to take a single day off.

So what really leaked here isn't the villain. It's an industry that has made secrecy itself into marketing material: tease, deny, then let the background of a fake hand confirm it for you. The audience thinks it's solving a puzzle; the answer was placed where it was meant to be seen. The Court of Owls rule Gotham by controlling information. This film's rollout is running the same play on its audience—except this time no Batman is investigating.
