骗局背后是另一场奴役Behind the Scam, Another Slavery

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要一位作家收到大量假冒粉丝邮件，实为利用生成式AI写成的诈骗信息，以推广作品为名索要数百美元。作者与自称柏林读书会创办人者周旋，对方最终提供一个位于菲律宾的邮寄地址。作者未汇款，而是将信息提供给菲律宾网络犯罪机构。
The newsAn author received a flood of fake fan emails, AI-written scams seeking hundreds of dollars to promote his work. He engaged one sender claiming to run a Berlin book club, who gave a postal address in the Philippines. He sent no money and reported the details to Philippine cybercrime authorities.
假粉丝信背后，写的人也是被关着的人。
The fake fan letter is written by someone who is also locked up.

一个作家收到假粉丝信，一路追到菲律宾，最后发现对面可能是个被关在隔音亭里、桌上摊着十几部手机、身后主管腰上别着电击枪的人。这条新闻里真正的暴力不在"我被骗了"这一层，而在更下面：整个骗局产业的燃料，是一批被拐卖、被扣押证件、被暴力逼着上班的人。

加尔通的三角在这里叠得很整齐。直接层是诈骗园区里的酷刑室、电击枪、性虐待；结构层是缅甸、老挝、柬埔寨、菲律宾这条跨国产业链，老板在隔壁泳池游泳，工人睡上下铺的小宿舍；文化层是那套被训练出来的叙事——"你创造了一个梦"，前园区主管自己说的，和小说家干的是同一件事。三层咬合，最上面那层才轮到收到信的作家。

报道里最锋利的一句是那位前主管的坦白：全靠操纵情绪，你造一个梦。这和第三章讲的表达武器化是同一个机制，只是这次连武器都不必是浪漫爱或宗教，直接是"有人真心读过你所有的书"。AI 让这个梦更便宜、更像真的。作家们收到的信是用自己被侵权的作品训练出来的模型写的——Anthropic 赔了 15 亿美元，出版商起诉 Google，而这两笔账和菲律宾那栋白墙建筑之间，隔着一条没人打算去连的线。

所以这篇报道里最该被点名的缺席者是 AI 公司。作者自己写到了：指望它们主动堵住技术的犯罪用途，和指望菲律宾的地方官一样不现实，理由也一样——钱赚得太狠。Alice Guo，那个园区所在的班班市市长，就是这条逻辑的本地版本。元暴力在这里不是隐喻：解释权、定价权、"什么是事实"的制造权，最后都落在能出钱腐化任何公职的人手里。

作者最后没寄那 500 美元，把地址交给了网络犯罪机构。这是这条新闻里唯一一处差额被具体缩小的地方，缩得很小。他自己也承认，揭穿反派的幻想就只是幻想。

真正没被算进去的是那些工人的名字。报道写了几百人被解救、来自中国、越南、马来西亚、台湾、印尼、卢旺达，证件被扣。他们既是这套暴力的执行者，也是它的原料。作家们心疼自己被 AI 偷走的书，这没问题；但那句"屏幕另一边有人惊恐、远离家乡"，是全文唯一一处把镜头转过去的地方，转过去就没了。

A writer gets fake fan mail, follows it to the Philippines, and finds that the person on the other end is probably someone locked in a sound-insulated booth with a dozen phones on the desk and a manager walking past with a taser on his hip. The real violence in this story isn't "I got scammed." It's underneath: the fuel of the whole scam industry is a workforce that was trafficked, had its documents withheld, and is held in place by force.

Galtung's triangle stacks neatly here. Direct: the torture room, the taser, the sexual abuse inside the compounds. Structural: a multi-billion dollar cross-border industry running through Myanmar, Laos, Cambodia, the Philippines, bosses swimming in the pool next door while workers sleep in bunk dorms. Cultural: the trained narrative — "you create a dream," as one former compound manager put it, which is exactly what a novelist does. Three layers interlocking, and only the top one reaches the writer's inbox.

The sharpest line in the piece is that manager's confession: it's all about exploiting emotion, you create a dream. Same mechanism as the weaponisation of expression in chapter three, except this time the weapon doesn't even need romance or religion — it's just someone who has genuinely read all your books. AI makes the dream cheaper and more convincing. The letters are written by models trained on the writers' own stolen work: Anthropic paid $1.5bn, publishers sued Google, and between those settlements and the white-walled building in the Philippines there is a line nobody intends to draw.

The most conspicuous absentee is the AI companies. The author makes the point himself: expecting them to stop the criminal use of their technology is as unrealistic as expecting the local mayor to, and for the same reason — it's making them fantastically rich. Alice Guo, the mayor of Bamban, is the local edition of that logic. Meta-violence isn't a metaphor here: the power to define, to price, to manufacture what counts as fact, ends up with whoever can afford to corrupt anyone in public office.

The author didn't send the $500. He handed the address to the cybercrime agency. That's the one place in this story where the gap between potential and actual got concretely narrowed, and it got narrowed a little. He admits the fantasy of unmasking the villain was just a fantasy.

What never gets counted is the workers. Hundreds freed, from China, Vietnam, Malaysia, Taiwan, Indonesia, Rwanda, documents withheld. They are both the executors of this violence and its raw material. Writers mourning their stolen books is fine. But that one sentence — someone frightened and far from home on the other side of the screen — is the only place the camera turns, and it turns away immediately.
