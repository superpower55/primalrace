集体惩罚写在账本上Collective Punishment, Itemized

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
围城不是反恐，是把一城人当人质记账
A siege is not counterterrorism; it is a city held hostage and billed daily.

希伯伦围到第十天，市长报出的数字是每天损失超过1200万谢克尔，折合380万美元。占领当局管这个叫安全措施。安全措施不会按天计价，围城会。

10月23日两名以色列士兵被杀，此后三千三百多名巴勒斯坦人流离失所或受影响，15处房屋被拆，327家商铺被关，8万学生十天没进教室。这是把一城人的日常当成可扣押的筹码：你们中间有人做了那件事，所以整座城替他还债。市长用了"集体惩罚"这个词，他说得客气了，这是把报复做成了行政流程。

加尔通的公式在这里算得极清楚：希伯伦本来可以达到的样子，和它现在的样子，差额就是暴力。这个差额不需要谁流血才成立，它写在关掉的卷帘门上，写在十天没开门的教室里，写在基础设施190万美元的损坏清单上。直接暴力是那15处被推倒的房子，结构暴力是327家店被强制关停、8万人的教育被中断，而让这一切听起来像"回应"的，是文化层那句永远有效的话：我们是在保护自己。

保护。这个词每次出现，都值得先问一句：谁被保护了，谁被定价了。

报道里还有一行更沉的东西：10月有记录的定居者袭击397起，是2006年有监测以来最高的单月数字。49个巴勒斯坦社区在收橄榄的季节被袭击，57人受伤，其中9个是孩子。橄榄收获是巴勒斯坦人一年里最要紧的活计，袭击专挑这个时候，不是巧合，是让人知道自己连收成都不配有。自2015年以来，以色列当局对巴勒斯坦房产发出9518份拆除或停工通知，今年前九个月就有344份。这是一条流水线，不是一连串意外。

而这条流水线上，谁在共谋、回报是什么，报道只写了一半：定居者动手，军队抓人——10名巴勒斯坦教师在南希伯伦的Masafer Yatta被捕，车也被扣了；联合国近东救济工程处在Shufat难民营办的学校被突袭。教师和学校被列为目标，说明这套秩序很清楚自己要掐断的是什么：不是武器，是下一代还能不能正常长大。

市长说希伯伦占巴勒斯坦经济约四成。围住它，等于掐住一整条命脉。所谓"扩大攻势"的威胁，说给的不只是希伯伦人听。

账算到这里还剩一笔没算：那397起袭击里，有哪一起被追究过？报道没写。没有写，就是答案。

Hebron has been under siege for ten days. The mayor's figure is more than 12 million shekels a day in losses, about $3.8m. The occupation calls this a security measure. Security measures don't come with a daily invoice. Sieges do.

Two Israeli soldiers were killed on October 23. Since then more than 3,300 Palestinians have been displaced or affected, 15 homes and structures demolished, 327 businesses shut, 80,000 students out of school for ten days. One city pays for what one person did. The mayor called it "collective punishment." That was polite. This is retaliation run as an administrative procedure.

Galtung's formula is almost embarrassingly clean here: the gap between what Hebron could be and what it is, that gap is the violence. It doesn't need blood to exist. It sits on the pulled-down shutters, in the classrooms dark for ten days, on the $1.9m infrastructure damage list. Direct violence is the 15 demolished homes. Structural violence is 327 businesses forced closed and 80,000 educations interrupted. And the cultural layer is the sentence that makes all of it sound like an answer: we are protecting ourselves.

Protection. Every time that word appears, ask first: who is protected, and who gets priced.

There is a heavier line in the report: 397 settler attacks across the occupied West Bank in October, the highest monthly figure since monitoring began in 2006. Forty-nine Palestinian communities were targeted between October 20 and 26, mostly while people harvested olives on their own land. Fifty-seven Palestinians injured, nine of them children. Olive harvest is the most important work of the Palestinian year. Attacking precisely then is not coincidence; it is a reminder that even the harvest is not yours. Since the start of 2015, Israeli authorities have issued 9,518 demolition or stop-work notices against Palestinian property, 344 of them in the first nine months of this year. This is an assembly line, not a run of accidents.

The report gives us only half of who is complicit and what they get: settlers attack, the army arrests. Ten Palestinian teachers were detained in Masafer Yatta and their vehicle seized. A UN-run school in Shufat refugee camp was raided. Teachers and schools as targets tells you exactly what this order knows it must cut: not weapons, but whether the next generation grows up normally.

The mayor says Hebron accounts for about 40 percent of the Palestinian economy. Surround it and you have your hand around an entire lifeline. The threat of a "wider offensive" is not addressed only to Hebron.

One entry is still missing from the ledger: of those 397 attacks, how many were ever prosecuted? The report doesn't say. That silence is the answer.
