棒球解说员的‘正确打法’与暴力共谋The Broadcaster's 'Right Way' and the Complicity of Violence

哲学 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Athletic ↗ 2026-08-31 § 链接
所谓的‘体育精神’常被用作掩盖直接暴力的文化遮羞布。
所谓的 'Sportsmanship' is often a cultural veil used to legitimize direct violence.

一个广播解说员在直播中公开呼吁投手去‘drill’（击中）对方球员并挑起斗殴，这在大多数语境下是煽动暴力，但在体育叙事里，这被包装成一种‘激情’或‘对比赛的热爱’。Will Flemming 愤怒的原因极其荒诞：对方球员在领先六分的情况下偷垒，这在他眼中是‘plays the game the wrong way’（打法不对）。

这里的‘正确打法’是什么？是一套由男性中心叙事定义的、关于‘尊重’和‘潜规则’的权力契约。当 Jazz Chisholm Jr. 追求个人最优解（通过偷垒增加个人数据或单纯地玩弄对手）而破坏了这套契约时，他被标记为‘bush league’（业余/低级）。这种愤怒本质上是对‘不服从’的厌恶。解说员通过定义什么是‘正确’，试图将对方球员客体化为‘违规者’，从而使‘投球击中对方头部’这种直接暴力（direct violence）在文化层面上变得合理且‘解气’。

这正是典型的文化暴力（cultural violence）在起作用：它利用一种名为‘体育精神’的伪装，将身体伤害合法化。解说员、评论员乃至部分球迷在此刻成为了共谋者（complicity），他们共同维护一套‘强者必须遵守潜规则’的元暴力逻辑。在这种逻辑中，暴力不是禁忌，而是一种用来修正‘错误表达’的工具。

最讽刺的是，这种‘正确打法’的定义权永远在那些掌握话语权的男性权威手中。他们定义什么是‘勇敢’，什么是‘自私’，以及什么时候‘打人’才是合理的。当暴力被冠以‘维护比赛纯洁性’之名时，它就成了一场关于解释权的权力游戏。

A radio broadcaster openly calling for a pitcher to 'drill' an opponent and incite a fight would be seen as inciting violence in any other context. In sports narratives, however, this is packaged as 'passion' or 'love for the game.' Will Flemming's rage stems from something absurd: an opponent stealing bases while up by six runs, which he labels as 'playing the game the wrong way.'

What is this 'right way'? It is a power contract of 'respect' and 'unwritten rules' defined by a masculine-centric narrative. When Jazz Chisholm Jr. pursues his own optimal expression—increasing personal stats or simply mocking the opponent—and breaks this contract, he is branded as 'bush league.' This anger is essentially a hatred of non-conformity. By defining the 'correct' behavior, the announcer objectifies the player as a 'transgressor,' making the direct violence of throwing a ball at someone's head seem culturally justifiable and 'satisfying.'

This is cultural violence in action: using the guise of 'sportsmanship' to legitimize physical harm. The broadcaster, analysts, and certain fans become complicit, collectively upholding a meta-violence logic where the 'strong' must obey unwritten rules. In this framework, violence is not a taboo, but a tool to correct 'incorrect expressions.'

The irony is that the power to define the 'right way' always rests with masculine authorities. They decide what is 'brave,' what is 'selfish,' and when 'fighting' is acceptable. When violence is branded as 'preserving the purity of the game,' it becomes a power game over the right to interpret reality.
