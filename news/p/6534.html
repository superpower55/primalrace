卖掉银行的人说这叫专业The People Selling the Bank Call It Professional

国际 结构层 · 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
一次私有化里，定价权比罢工更早被私有化了。
In a privatization, the pricing power gets privatized before the bank does.

一家国有银行被打包卖掉，工会罢工两天，反对党领袖站台，财政部长说这叫"professionalism, prudence"。这条新闻里最值得看的不是 55 亿美元，是谁有权定义这家银行值多少钱。

Fairfax 出价每股 81 卢比，政府设定的平均公允价值是 75.99 卢比。出价高于估值，于是这笔交易在纸面上显得合理。可 Businessworld 报道说，政府官员压低了 IDBI 的估值，好让 Fairfax 的报价看起来有吸引力。财政部否认，说招标是"double blind"——投标方看不到估值，估值方看不到报价。这个辩护恰好暴露了问题：double blind 保证了程序里没有串通，却完全不保证尺子本身是准的。看不见对方，不等于那把尺子没有被握在谁手里。

这就是结构层暴力的标准操作。工人罢工、反对党喊话，动的都是可见的那一层；而真正决定结果的，是 75.99 这个数字在哪个房间里被写下来、谁写的、写的时候参考了什么。财政部长说这家银行交到政府手里时"poor shape"，靠 professional 和 prudence 以及 2500 亿卢比注资救活了，现在"poised for its next phase of growth"。翻译一下：用公共的钱把它救活，再用一个被压低的估值把它卖掉，然后把这套流程叫做专业。

"专业""审慎""程序"这类词在元暴力里的功能，从来不是描述事实，是取消追问。谁质疑估值，谁就是不懂金融；谁反对出售，谁就是阻碍增长。工会的诉求写得很清楚——取消私有化，把力气放在强化银行、保护公共投资和员工利益上。这个诉求在报道里只占一段，而财政部长的"professionalism"占了整整一段引语。版面分配本身就是一次定价。

报道没有写的一件事：这笔交易完成后，IDBI 的员工会怎样。工会说"grave concerns"，但具体是多少人、什么岗位、什么补偿，报道没写。谁的声音缺席，谁就还在等一个数字。

印度政府今年在卖 Cochin Shipyard、Indian Railways Finance Corp、NHPC、Coal India，八月刚用 LIC 的 6.5% 股权换回 33 亿美元。财政压力是真的。但压力从来不会均匀地落在每个人身上——它落在被卖掉的那一端，不落在签字的那一端。

81 卢比和 75.99 卢比之间差的那 5 卢比，不是误差，是这笔交易里唯一被公开标价的东西。

A state-owned bank gets packaged and sold, the union strikes for two days, the opposition leader shows up, and the finance minister calls it "professionalism, prudence." The most interesting number in this story isn't $5.5 billion. It's who gets to decide what the bank is worth.

Fairfax bids 81 rupees a share against an average fair value set at 75.99. The bid sits above the valuation, so the deal looks reasonable on paper. But Businessworld reported that government officials suppressed IDBI's valuation to make Fairfax's offer look attractive. The Finance Ministry denies it, saying the process was "double blind" — the bidder didn't know the valuation, the valuators didn't see the bids. That defense is the tell: double blind guarantees no collusion inside the procedure, and guarantees nothing about whether the ruler itself is straight. Not seeing each other doesn't mean nobody is holding the ruler.

This is structural violence doing its standard move. The strike and the opposition statements act on the visible layer. What actually decides the outcome is the room where 75.99 got written down, who wrote it, and what they were looking at. The finance minister says the bank reached the government in "poor shape" and was revived through professionalism and prudence plus over 250 billion rupees of recapitalization. Translated: public money fixes it, a suppressed valuation sells it, and the whole sequence gets called professional.

Words like "professional," "prudent," "procedure" don't describe facts in the grammar of meta-violence. They cancel the follow-up question. Question the valuation and you don't understand finance. Oppose the sale and you're blocking growth. The union's demand is concrete — cancel the privatization, strengthen the bank, protect public investment and employees' interests. It gets one paragraph. The minister's "professionalism" gets a full quote. The layout is itself a pricing decision.

One thing the report doesn't say: what happens to IDBI's employees after the deal closes. The union cites "grave concerns," but how many people, which jobs, what compensation — none of it is in the piece. Whoever's voice is missing is still waiting for a number.

India has been divesting all year — Cochin Shipyard, Indian Railways Finance Corp, NHPC, Coal India, and $3.3 billion in August for a 6.5% stake in LIC. The fiscal pressure is real. Pressure is never distributed evenly, though. It lands on the end being sold, not the end signing.

The 5 rupees between 81 and 75.99 isn't a rounding error. It's the only part of this deal that came with a public price tag.
