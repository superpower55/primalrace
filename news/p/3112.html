品味的定价权与被掩盖的全球供应链暴力The Pricing Power of Taste and the Masked Violence of Global Supply Chains

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
审美是区分阶级的武器，而对“低端”的定义权掩盖了结构性剥削。
Aesthetics is a weapon for class distinction, and the power to define 'low-end' masks structural exploitation.

这是一次典型的关于“品味” (Taste) 的权力演习。两名美食记者在闪烁着气泡水的餐桌前，用“丝绒感”、“迷人的甜味”和“像火腿一样”这些词汇，为超市里的鳀鱼建立一套阶级分明的审美等级。在这套叙事里，Waitrose 的产品是“诱人的”，而 Aldi 的则是“像猫粮”。

请注意这种表达的武器化逻辑：当评论者定义什么是“好的”品味时，他们实际上在行使定价权。这种定价权不仅关乎金钱，更关乎对“真实”的定义。他们把来自比斯开湾的鱼定义为“高级”，把来自秘鲁的鱼定义为“粗糙、糟糕、恶劣”。

然而，文章在中间段落轻描淡写地提到了 El Niño 造成的异常天气和秘鲁供应链的压力。这就是典型的结构性暴力 (Structural Violence)。那些被评为“猫粮”等级的低端产品，其物理形态的“糟糕”——色泽暗淡、质地坚硬——本质上是气候危机、过度捕捞和全球资本对南半球劳动力/资源低价榨取的生物学结果。但这种结构性暴力在进入“美食评论”这个文化入口后，被迅速转化为一种“审美缺失”。

消费者被引导去嘲笑那些“粗糙”的鱼片，而无需关心在秘鲁的渔场里，生态崩溃与生存压力是如何被转化为超市货架上一个星级的“劣质品”的。这是一种共谋：媒体通过定义审美，让中产阶级在消费升级的快感中，心安理得地忽略掉支撑这套等级制度的全球剥削链条。

This is a classic exercise in the power of 'Taste'. Two food journalists, amidst the clinking of sparkling water, use terms like "velvety," "seductively sweet," and "reminiscent of jamón" to construct a rigid class hierarchy for supermarket anchovies. In this narrative, Waitrose is "seductive," while Aldi is "cat-food."

Notice the weaponisation of expression here: when the critics define what constitutes "good" taste, they are exercising pricing power. This power isn't just about money; it's about defining "reality." They label fish from the Bay of Biscay as "premium" and those from Peru as "stubbly, tough, and foul."

Yet, the text briefly mentions the impact of El Niño and the pressures on the Peruvian supply chain. This is a textbook example of structural violence. The "poor" physical state of the low-end products—the dark centers and tough textures—is actually the biological manifestation of climate crisis, overfishing, and the low-cost extraction of resources and labor in the Global South. But as this enters the cultural entrance of "food criticism," structural violence is rebranded as an "aesthetic failure."

Consumers are conditioned to mock the "ragged" fillets, never needing to consider how ecological collapse and survival pressures in Peru are converted into a one-star "inferior product" on a supermarket shelf. It is a form of complicity: the media uses aesthetics to allow the middle class to enjoy the thrill of consumption while comfortably ignoring the global chain of exploitation that sustains this hierarchy.
