解放叙事是掩体，暴力循环是真经Liberation as a Shield, Violence as the Only Currency

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
当革命叙事成为生存最优解，暴力便成了唯一的货币。
When revolutionary narrative becomes the optimal expression for survival, violence becomes the only currency.

哥伦比亚的这场战争是典型的 structural violence 演变为 direct violence 的闭环。ELN 所谓的“政治原则”和“解放叙事”，在本质上是第三章提到的 weaponized expression。他们用马克思主义和解放神学构建认知入口，将对土地和资源的掠夺包装成“胚胎国家”的税收。这种叙事不仅是为了招募底层，更是为了在存在性战争中为自己的暴力合法化提供文化掩体。

最讽刺的是，无论是左翼的 Petro 还是右翼的 De la Espriella，他们提供的都只是不同版本的 scam。前者试图用微小的制度修补（pension reform）来 pacify 民众，后者则直接用 Bukele 式的巨型监狱来实施物理清除。在这种结构性绝望中，加入游击队成了底层贫民的一种“最优解表达”——因为在没有公共空间、没有阶级跃升可能性的丛林里，拿起 AK-47 是唯一能获得即时权力感和生存保障的手段。

注意到报道中那个细节：两名女性在指挥官 Yerson 面前战战兢兢，不敢开口。这揭示了 ELN 内部依然运行着一套极其严苛的 masculine-centric narrative。所谓的“男女平分”只是表型上的分布，在实际的权力结构中，解释权依然被像 Yerson 这样的男性指挥官垄断。这不是革命，这只是在丛林里重建了一套微缩的、更暴力的父权结构。

战争的燃料从来不是意识形态，而是金矿和可卡因。当所有的参与者都把对方定义为“敌人”并以此获利时，和平协议就成了博弈中的表演性让步。这场战争没有所谓的“正确的一方”，只有在元暴力驱动下，不断通过制造新的受害者来维持自身存在性的共谋者。

The conflict in Colombia is a textbook loop where structural violence crystallizes into direct violence. The ELN's so-called "political principles" and "liberation narratives" are precisely the weaponized expressions I've discussed. By using Marxism and Liberation Theology as cognitive entries, they package the plunder of land and resources as "taxes" for an "embryonic state." This narrative isn't just for recruitment; it's a cultural shield to legitimize their violence in an existential war.

The irony is that both the leftist Petro and the right-wing De la Espriella offer different versions of a scam. One attempts to pacify the masses with superficial institutional patches, while the other proposes physical erasure via Bukele-style mega-prisons. In this structural despair, joining a guerrilla group becomes a fake optimal expression for the poor—because in a jungle devoid of public space or social mobility, gripping an AK-47 is the only way to secure immediate power and survival.

Notice the detail: two women struggled to speak in the presence of Commander Yerson. This reveals that the ELN still operates under a rigid masculine-centric narrative. The "equal split" of men and women is merely a phenotypic distribution; the actual power structure and the right of interpretation remain monopolized by male commanders. This isn't a revolution; it's the reconstruction of a miniature, more brutal patriarchal structure in the wild.

The fuel of this war is never ideology; it is gold mines and cocaine. When all players profit from defining the "Other" as an enemy, peace deals become mere performative concessions. There is no "right side of history" here—only co-conspirators driven by meta-violence, sustaining their own existence by manufacturing new victims.
