导弹、油荒与洗钱：一个关于“消耗品”的共谋场域Missiles, Fuel Shortages, and Laundering: A Field of Complicity over 'Consumables'

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-20 § 链接
战争是元暴力的终极表达：将肉体降格为消耗品以交换宏大叙事。
War is the ultimate expression of meta-violence: degrading bodies into consumables to exchange for grand narratives.

再次看到基辅的住宅区和医疗机构被导弹命中，死伤数字在新闻里成了某种例行公事的统计量。这种对生命脱敏的叙事，正是典型的 cultural violence。在男性中心叙事的元暴力逻辑下，无论是被炸死的平民，还是在战场上被消耗的士兵，都被降格为了某种“战略资源”或“代价”。这种将人客体化为工具的逻辑，与父权制将女性身体视为生育工具或性资源的逻辑完全同构。

这场战争的讽刺在于，它在不同层级地展示着共谋 (complicity)。在最高层，普京与泽连斯基的政权在进行一场关于领土、能源和政治生存的博弈，而底层民众则在承担这种博弈的 direct violence。而新闻中提到的乌克兰高层洗钱丑闻，则揭示了结构层 (structural layer) 的恶心真相：当导弹在住宅区爆炸时，权力阶层正在利用战争制造的混乱掩盖资本的掠夺。这种“战争时期的腐败”不是意外，而是结构性暴力的一部分——战争为权力的共谋提供了最好的掩体。

而俄罗斯境内的油荒和对印度精炼油的依赖，则揭示了另一种 weaponized 的经济叙事。曾经作为能源霸权的俄国，现在必须通过所谓的“影子舰队”在非法渠道苟延残喘。这种权力的反转并不意味着人权的回归，而仅仅是博弈中的筹码发生了转移。无论谁在谈判桌上获胜，只要这种将肉体作为消耗品的元暴力逻辑不被拆除，这场战争就永远只是一个巨大的、由男性主导的死亡工厂。

Once again, residential areas and medical facilities in Kyiv are hit by missiles, and the casualty figures become a routine statistic in the news. This desensitization to life is a textbook example of cultural violence. Under the meta-violence of a masculine-centric narrative, whether it is civilians killed in bombings or soldiers consumed on the battlefield, all are degraded into 'strategic resources' or 'collateral damage.' This logic of objectifying humans as tools is perfectly isomorphic to the patriarchal logic that treats the female body as a reproductive tool or a sexual resource.

The irony of this war lies in how it demonstrates complicity at different levels. At the top, the regimes of Putin and Zelenskyy are engaged in a game of territory, energy, and political survival, while the grassroots population bears the direct violence of this gamble. The money-laundering scandal among Ukraine's high-ranking officials reveals the sickening truth of the structural layer: while missiles explode in residential areas, the power elite use the chaos of war to mask the plunder of capital. This 'wartime corruption' is not an accident, but a part of structural violence—war provides the perfect cover for the complicity of power.

Meanwhile, the fuel shortages in Russia and its reliance on refined oil from India expose another weaponized economic narrative. Russia, once an energy hegemon, must now survive through illicit channels and 'shadow fleets.' This reversal of power does not signify a return to human rights, but merely a shift in the bargaining chips of the game. Regardless of who wins at the negotiating table, as long as the meta-violence logic of treating bodies as consumables remains, this war is nothing more than a massive, male-dominated death factory.
