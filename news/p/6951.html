1998 年的谷歌是件道具Google's 1998 Is a Prop

科技 文化层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要为宣传即将发售的 Switch 2 版《塞尔达传说：时之笛》重制版，谷歌上线了一个搜索彩蛋：搜索「Zelda」「Ocarina of Time」或「tloz」等词，右下角会出现大师之剑，点击后响起林克的「Hya!」，三角力量覆盖屏幕，搜索结果回到 1998 年游戏原版发售时的样式，再点一次可切回现代版。
The newsTo promote the upcoming Switch 2 remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, Google added a search Easter egg: searching terms like "Zelda" or "tloz" brings up the Master Sword in the corner; clicking it plays Link's "Hya!" and turns the results page into its 1998 look, the year the original game launched on the Nintendo 64. Clicking again returns you to modern Google.
可切换的怀旧，是给无法退回的现实打的蜡。
Switchable nostalgia is wax polished onto a reality you can't return to.

谷歌让搜索结果退回 1998 年，一键切换，再点一下切回来。这个设计本身就把话说清楚了：1998 年是一种可以随时退出的体验，不是要回去的地方。

Polygon 的记者顺手捅破了那层纸——现在的搜索结果被一个半数时候不给来源的 AI 聊天机器人接管了。彩蛋是精心做的，吐槽也是真的，两件事摆在同一篇稿子里，谁也不妨碍谁。这就是武器化最体面的形态：把「我们变差了」这件事，包装成一次可以玩的怀旧。你点了剑，听了「Hya!」，笑了一下，然后继续用那个变差了的搜索。

真正被藏起来的是解释权。1998 年的谷歌是一个入口，把你送去别人写的页面；今天的谷歌自己就是答案，来源被折叠、被省略、被它替你决定要不要给。认知入口从「指路」变成「代答」，中间没有发过任何公告。彩蛋是这场转变的公关版本：它让你以为你还握着一把可以切换时间的剑，实际上你能切的只有界面。

任天堂这边也一样。《时之笛》被重制到 Switch 2 上，卖的是同一份 1998 年的记忆，重新定价一次。谷歌借它做彩蛋，任天堂借谷歌做曝光，两边都不用付对方钱，付钱的是点进去的人——用注意力，用对旧版本的信任。

彩蛋做得确实好。问题是它好得刚好够让你不去问：为什么需要把一个 1998 年的界面做成彩蛋，才能让人想起搜索本来该是什么样子。剑还在右下角，点一下能回去；关掉彩蛋，回不去的是那个搜索结果里还愿意把你送去别处的谷歌。

Google lets your search results drop back to 1998, one click to enter, one click to leave. The design says it plainly: 1998 is an experience you can exit at will, not a place you're going back to.

Polygon's writer punctures the wrapper on the way out — today's results are run by an AI chatbot that, half the time, doesn't link its sources. The Easter egg is lovingly made and the complaint is real, and the same article holds both without either getting in the other's way. This is weaponization at its most presentable: turning "we got worse" into a nostalgia you can play. You click the sword, hear the "Hya!", smile, and go back to using the worse search.

What's actually hidden is the monopoly on explanation. Google in 1998 was an entrance that sent you to pages other people wrote; Google today is the answer, with sources folded, omitted, or granted at its discretion. The cognitive entrance shifted from pointing to answering, and no announcement was made. The Easter egg is the PR cut of that shift: it lets you believe you're holding a sword that switches time, when all you can switch is the interface.

Nintendo is running the same play. Ocarina of Time gets remade for the Switch 2, selling the same 1998 memory at a new price. Google borrows it for an Easter egg, Nintendo borrows Google for exposure, neither pays the other, and the people who click pay — in attention, in trust in the old version.

The egg is genuinely well made. That's the problem: it's good enough to keep you from asking why a 1998 interface has to be an Easter egg before anyone remembers what search was supposed to be. The sword sits in the corner and one click takes you back. Close the egg, and what you can't get back is the Google that still sent you somewhere else.
