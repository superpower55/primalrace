用“再民主化”包装的政变：一场关于解释权的武器化博弈The 'Re-democratisation' Scam: Weaponising Meaning for a Coup

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-07 § 链接
当独裁者开始定义“民主”时，他正在夺取摧毁民主的解释权。
When autocrats redefine 'democracy', they are seizing the interpretive power to destroy it.

Flávio Bolsonaro 提出的“再民主化” (re-democratise) 是一个典型的 weaponized concept。在政治博弈中，最阴险的进攻不是直接宣布废除规则，而是通过重新定义核心词汇来夺取认知入口。当一个赞美军政府独裁的家族试图定义什么是“真正的民主”时，他们实际上是在制造一种新的“真实”：将现有的宪政秩序定义为“伪民主”，从而使接下来的权力篡夺在逻辑上变成一种“拯救”。

这不仅是个人野心，而是一场高度协同的共谋 (complicity)。从国会中庞大的右翼党团，到与布克尔 (Bukele) 式威权主义的秘密接头，再到特朗普政府的潜在背书，这套机制旨在通过 structural layer 的渗透——修改宪法、清洗最高法院——来合法化直接暴力。他们不需要像父亲那样通过暴乱来搞政变，他们想尝试一种更高级的 scam：在选票的掩护下，利用多数人的情绪，把独裁包装成“民意”。

这种叙事逻辑与全球范围内的元暴力 (meta violence) 同构。它将权力定义为某种“强有力的意志”，将制度约束定义为“精英的阻碍”。在这种男性中心化的强权叙事中，法律和人权被视为软弱的装饰品，而真正的“秩序”来自于对反对者的绝对压制。当 Flávio 说他是防止巴西变成“野蛮之地”的唯一屏障时，他其实已经把所有不认同他的人定义成了野蛮人。

这场博弈的危险之处在于，如果他获胜，他将完成从“扮演民主参与者”到“制造事实定义者”的跃迁。一旦解释权易手，所有的暴行都将被重新命名为“必要的法律调整”。

Flávio Bolsonaro’s vow to “re-democratise” Brazil is a textbook example of a weaponised concept. In political gaming, the most insidious attack is not the overt abolition of rules, but the seizure of cognitive entry points by redefining core vocabulary. When a family that praises military dictatorships attempts to define “true democracy,” they are manufacturing a new “reality”: framing the existing constitutional order as “pseudo-democracy” to make the subsequent power grab logically appear as a “rescue.”

This is more than personal ambition; it is a highly coordinated act of complicity. From the robust far-right caucuses in Congress and clandestine meetings with Bukele-style authoritarians to the implicit backing of the Trump administration, the mechanism is designed to legitimate direct violence through structural layer infiltration—altering the constitution and purging the Supreme Court. They no longer need the crude riots of the father; they are attempting a more sophisticated scam: masking autocracy as “the will of the people” under the guise of a ballot.

This narrative logic is isomorphic with global meta-violence. It defines power as a “strong will” and institutional constraints as “elite obstructions.” In this masculine-centric narrative of strength, law and human rights are treated as fragile ornaments, while true “order” is derived from the absolute suppression of dissent. When Flávio claims to be the only barrier preventing Brazil from becoming a “barbarity,” he is effectively defining all opponents as barbarians.

The danger of this existential war lies in the potential transition from “performing as a democratic participant” to “manufacturing the definition of truth.” Once the interpretive power shifts, all subsequent violence will be rebranded as “necessary legal adjustments.”
