反犹标签是新的猎巫The Antisemitism Label Is the New Witch Hunt

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国中期选举投票前一天，密歇根州民主党众议员哈利·史蒂文斯公开支持共和党参议员候选人迈克·罗杰斯，而非本党提名人阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德。民主党高层批评此举，参议院民主党领袖舒默称其为「可鄙的背叛」，并表态支持埃尔-赛义德。截至周日，约5450万登记选民已提前投票。
The newsOne day before the US midterms, Democratic Congresswoman Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Senate candidate Mike Rogers in Michigan over her own party's nominee, Abdul El-Sayed. Democratic leaders criticized the move, with Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer calling it a "despicable betrayal" and backing El-Sayed. About 54.5 million registered voters had cast early ballots as of Sunday.
用反犹指控封杀反战者，是元暴力的标准操作
Branding an anti-war candidate antisemitic is meta-violence's standard move.

Stevens 输掉初选，然后跨党背书对手，这不是什么政治分歧，是一次共谋的公开结算。她被 El-Sayed 击败，现在把票送给共和党的 Rogers，Trump 在爱荷华集会上夸她“勇敢”——回报当场兑现，毫不遮掩。

但真正值得拆的是她给 El-Sayed 扣的那顶帽子：反犹。El-Sayed 反对美国对以色列的军事支持，说内塔尼亚胡把美国拖进伊朗战争，于是就成了反犹。这套逻辑的功能是把“反对战争”翻译成“仇恨犹太人”，一旦翻译完成，辩论就结束了，不需要看加沙被指控种族灭绝的事实，不需要看谁在付代价。

这是第三章里最典型的武器化入口：占领一个词，然后垄断它的解释权。“反犹”本该指向真实的仇恨，现在被用来保护一个国家的军事行动不被质疑。谁在共谋？Stevens 在共谋，她拿这个标签换跨党背书的政治资本；Trump 在共谋，他第一时间收割；那些把批评以色列等同于反犹的叙事机器也在共谋，它们让一个穆斯林候选人必须反复自证不是仇恨者，而他的对手从来不需要自证。

报道里 El-Sayed 遭到伊斯兰恐惧症攻击，这句话一笔带过，没有细节，没有谁说了什么。而 Stevens 的“反犹”指控被完整引用，还带上了她输掉初选的背景。这就是元暴力在新闻写作里的样子：一方的指控是新闻，另一方的受害是脚注。

Schumer 说这是“卑鄙的背叛”，Michigan 的民主党主席说她“不代表更大的社区”，Michigan Jews for Democracy 说她不代表密歇根的犹太社区。这些反击有用，但都停在“她背叛了党”这一层，没有人去拆那顶帽子本身。

投票日前一天，54.5 百万人已经投了票（来源：Al Jazeera），德州 770 万，南卡早期投票比 2022 年同期涨了 83%。数字很热闹，但真正决定这场选举叙事的，是像 Stevens 这样的背书能不能把“反战”永久地钉在“反犹”的柱子上。如果钉住了，下一个反对战争的人会先算一遍自己付不付得起这个标签的代价。

报道没写的是：Stevens 的背书到底说服了谁。民调说 El-Sayed 基本整合了民主党支持（来源：路透社），那这出戏的观众是谁？是那些本来就不会投他的人，还是那些需要一个理由不投票的人。

Stevens lost the primary, then endorsed the other party's candidate. This is not a political disagreement; it is a public settling of accounts among co-conspirators. El-Sayed beat her in August, and now she hands her support to Republican Rogers. Trump praises her "bravery" at an Iowa rally — the payoff is immediate and undisguised.

But the real move to dismantle is the label she pinned on El-Sayed: antisemitism. He opposed US military support for Israel and said Netanyahu dragged America into the Iran war. That becomes antisemitism. The function of this logic is to translate "opposing war" into "hating Jews." Once the translation is done, the debate is over. No need to look at Gaza, where Israel has been accused of genocide. No need to ask who pays the cost.

This is the most classic weaponization entry from Chapter 3: seize a word, then monopolize its interpretation. "Antisemitism" should point to real hatred. Now it protects a state's military operations from being questioned. Who is conspiring? Stevens conspires — she trades the label for cross-party political capital. Trump conspires — he harvests it immediately. And the narrative machine that equates criticism of Israel with antisemitism conspires too: it forces a Muslim candidate to repeatedly prove he is not a hater, while his opponent never has to prove anything.

The report mentions in passing that El-Sayed faced Islamophobic attacks. One clause, no detail, no quotes. Stevens's antisemitism charge gets full quotation, plus the context of her primary loss. This is what meta-violence looks like in news writing: one side's accusation is the story, the other side's injury is a footnote.

Schumer calls it a "despicable betrayal." Michigan's Democratic chair says she "does not speak for that larger community." Michigan Jews for Democracy says she does not speak for Michigan's Jewish community. These counterattacks matter, but they all stop at "she betrayed the party." Nobody dismantles the label itself.

One day before the vote, 54.5 million people have already cast ballots (source: Al Jazeera). Texas has 7.7 million early votes. South Carolina's early voting is up 83 percent over the same point in 2022. The numbers are loud. But what really decides the narrative of this election is whether an endorsement like Stevens's can permanently nail "anti-war" to the "antisemitic" pillar. If it sticks, the next person who opposes a war will first calculate whether they can afford the cost of that label.

What the report does not say: whom Stevens's endorsement actually persuaded. Polling suggests El-Sayed has largely consolidated Democratic support (source: Reuters). So who is the audience for this drama? The people who were never going to vote for him, or the people who need a reason not to vote at all.
