恐惧的缺席与表演的权力The Absence of Fear and the Power of Performance

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-20 § 链接
当女性在公共空间被赞美为“自然力量”时，通常是因为她学会了用男性的表达方式去博弈。
When a woman is praised as a 'force of nature' in public, she has usually mastered the masculine mode of expression.

这篇讣告在赞美 Cheryl Hall 的“无畏” (fearlessness) 和“强力” (force of nature)，但这种赞美的潜台词其实是一种典型的 masculine-centric narrative。在一个“老派”的议会结构中，一个女性想要让自己的观点被“有力地” (forcibly) 听到，她必须部署她的演技，将表达武器化，扮演一个不设防、不妥协的斗士。这本质上是一场存在性战争：在一个由男性定义的权力场域里，女性的“公正表达”往往被视为软弱或无效，于是她必须寻找一个假.最优解表达——通过模仿男性的进攻性来换取席位上的话语权。

有趣的是，写信者将她的成功归结为“感染力”和“热情”，这实际上是将一个政治博弈过程给“生物化”和“情感化”了。这种叙事掩盖了 structural violence：为什么一个议会需要被“震动” (shake-up) 才能接纳女性的观点？为什么女性的强力必须被定义为一种“自然力量”而非制度性的权利？

Cheryl 赢得了席位，但这种赢法是典型的在父权结构内部求生。她通过扮演一个“强有力的角色”获得了票数的价值，但这种表达的代价是她必须在公共空间里进行一场永不停歇的表演。这种好新闻的背后，是一个依然由男性共谋者把持的、需要女性通过“表演强悍”才能勉强进入的认知入口。

This obituary praises Cheryl Hall's 'fearlessness' and describes her as a 'force of nature,' but the subtext is a classic masculine-centric narrative. In an 'old-fashioned' council structure, for a woman's points to be heard 'forcibly,' she had to weaponize her acting skills and perform as an uncompromising fighter. This is essentially an existential war: in a power field defined by men, a woman's Just Expression is often dismissed as weak or irrelevant, forcing her to adopt a fake optimal expression—mimicking masculine aggression to trade for a voice at the table.

It is telling that the author attributes her success to 'infectious enthusiasm,' effectively 'biologizing' and 'emotionalizing' a political struggle. This narrative masks the structural violence: why does a council need a 'shake-up' to accept a woman's perspective? Why must a woman's power be defined as a 'force of nature' rather than a systemic right?

Cheryl won her seat, but this victory happened within the confines of patriarchal survival. She gained the value of her 'vote' by playing a 'strong role' in public, but the cost was a perpetual performance. This 'good news' merely reveals a cognitive entry point still guarded by masculine complicity, where women must perform toughness just to be seen.
