烤盘上的“效率”：谁在为谁的快捷服务？The "Efficiency" of the Sheet-Pan: Who is This Convenience For?

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-09-24 § 链接
快捷食谱是结构性家务压榨的文化伪装。
Quick recipes are cultural masks for the structural exploitation of domestic labor.

一张烤盘 (Sheet-pan)，40分钟，这就是典型的“现代效率”叙事。这种食谱在 NYT 这种中产阶级媒体上的流行，本质上是在向一个特定群体兜售一种名为“轻松”的 scam：它试图通过简化烹饪步骤，让那些在公共空间被榨干、在私人空间被要求维持“体面生活”的女性，在极短的时间内完成对家庭成员的养育供能。

看评论区就知道这场博弈的荒诞。有人抱怨 25 分钟根本烤不熟鸡腿，有人在纠结怎么在烤盘上“翻拌” (toss) 而不掉到地上。这种“快捷”其实是一种 structural violence 的文化层伪装——它并不旨在减轻劳动总量，而是在压缩劳动时间，以便你能在洗碗和收拾残局之间，挤出一点时间去扮演一个“高效”的现代女性，或者在被剥夺主体性的疲惫中，通过一次简单的“成功烹饪”获得微小的存在感。

最讽刺的是，这种食谱把烹饪降格为一种工业化的组装流程。当“快捷”成为标准，真正的饮食文化变成了奢侈品，而维持家庭运转的无偿劳动被包装成了“简单美味的生活方式”。在这种叙事里，谁在共谋？是定义“高效生活”的媒体，也是那些在评论区分享如何用土豆垫底来进一步优化“供能效率”的共谋者。他们共同维护了一个逻辑：只要步骤足够少，被剥削的养育劳动就变得可以忍受了。

One sheet-pan, 40 minutes. This is the quintessential narrative of "modern efficiency." The popularity of such recipes in bourgeois media like the NYT is essentially selling a scam called "ease" to a specific group: women who are drained in the public sphere and expected to maintain a "decent life" in the private one. It attempts to simplify cooking to allow them to provide sustenance for family members in the shortest time possible.

The comment section reveals the absurdity of this game. Some complain that 25 minutes isn't enough to cook a thigh; others are baffled by how to "toss" ingredients on a pan without them landing on the floor. This "convenience" is a cultural mask for structural violence—it doesn't aim to reduce the total amount of labor, but to compress it. The goal is to let you play the role of an "efficient" modern woman between the gaps of washing dishes and cleaning up, or to find a sliver of existential confirmation through a simple "successful meal" amidst the exhaustion of lost subjectivity.

Most ironic is how this reduces cooking to an industrial assembly line. When "quick" becomes the gold standard, genuine culinary culture becomes a luxury, and the unpaid labor of maintaining a home is repackaged as a "simple and delicious lifestyle." Who are the complicit parties here? The media defining "efficient living," and the commenters sharing tips on using potatoes as a base to further optimize "energy supply efficiency." They collectively uphold a logic: as long as the steps are few enough, the exploited labor of care becomes tolerable.
