一个男孩的死，换不来一句负责A Boy Died, and No One Is Responsible

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
制度把责任推给了法律，法律把责任推给了事实。
The institution outsourced blame to the law; the law outsourced it to the facts.

一个十七岁的男孩被学校开除，几小时后自杀。法院说，这件事不构成任何可以追责的法律理论。

先把机制讲清楚。Devon Shelton 被 Rummel 高中开除，理由是和他所说的校园霸凌者发生推搡。他母亲 Sara Brannon 起诉新奥尔良总教区，主张学校对学生的纪律处分负有相应、非任意行事的义务，学校没做到，导致儿子死在前继父家中。州地区法院法官 Nancy Miller 七月驳回了教区的撤诉请求。教区上诉。第五巡回法院的三位法官 Susan Chehardy、Jude Gravois、Marc Johnson 推翻了她。

上诉法院的判决词里反复出现“tragic”这个词。然后说，这件事在法律上“无法归入任何救济理论”。一个孩子的死被承认是悲剧，同时被宣布为不可追责。这不是冷酷，这是结构暴力的标准语法：先承认痛苦，再证明没有任何人需要对痛苦负责。

教区的抗辩理由是路易斯安那州法律不允许原告就“学校纪律决定之后校外的学生自伤行为”索赔。注意这句话的结构：学校做了一个决定，决定发生在校内，后果发生在校外，于是责任在校门那道线上被切断了。这道线不是自然存在的，是法律画出来的。画线的权力，就是解释权。

再看这所学校是什么。Rummel 属于新奥尔良罗马天主教总教区。这个教区 2020 年因神职人员性侵丑闻的财务后果申请破产保护。去年十二月，教区和保险公司同意向约七百名神职人员性侵幸存者支付约三亿零五百万美元。到十月结束时，这些幸存者还没有拿到钱。

也就是说，同一个机构，一边在处理自己长达数十年的性侵赔付，一边在法庭上主张自己对一个学生的死没有任何法律义务。它请的律师在打这场官司，它请的另一批律师在打那场官司。机构的责任可以被破产程序重新排列、分期、延后；一个母亲的责任追究，被上诉法院一句话挡在门外。

Brannon 的律师之一是 Richard Trahant，他代理过许多新奥尔良教区神职人员性侵的索赔人。这个细节不是巧合，是同一条产业链上的两个出口：一个机构长期以“我们是道德权威”的姿态管理孩子，当孩子受到伤害时，它用法律技术把自己从责任里摘出去。

报道里没有写那场推搡的另一方后来怎样，没有写学校在开除前做过什么调查，没有写 Devon 在死前那几个小时里和谁说过话。这些空白本身就是材料。一个被霸凌的孩子还手，被开除；霸凌者呢？报道没说。谁的名字没出现，谁就被制度默认无责。

Devon 的讣告写他会多种乐器，是 Rummel 行进乐队的成员，也是校保龄球队的队员，每年在为本市多发性硬化症患者筹款的骑行活动上做志愿者。一个十七岁的人，生活里已经装下了这么多东西。

教区发言人说，他们记得 Devon，为他和他家人祈祷。祈祷是免费的。诉讼是被推翻的。

上诉法院说这些事实“无法归入任何救济理论”。那就换一个问法：当一个机构有权开除一个孩子，却没有任何机制追问这个决定是否合理，这个权力本身是什么？它不是悲剧的背景，它就是悲剧。

Brannon 说她要继续上诉。她还能上诉的那部分，是法律允许她上诉的那部分。

A seventeen-year-old boy was expelled from school. Hours later he was dead. The court says this does not fit any theory of legal recovery.

Here is the mechanism. Devon Shelton was expelled from Rummel High School over what he described as a shoving match with a campus bully. His mother, Sara Brannon, sued the Archdiocese of New Orleans, arguing the school owed her son a duty to discipline him in a commensurate, non-arbitrary way, failed that duty, and that the failure led to his suicide at his former stepfather's house. District judge Nancy Miller refused the archdiocese's motion to dismiss in July. The archdiocese appealed. Three judges of the fifth circuit — Susan Chehardy, Jude Gravois, Marc Johnson — reversed her.

The appellate opinion uses the word "tragic" repeatedly. Then it declares the facts legally unfit for any theory of recovery. A child's death is acknowledged as tragedy and simultaneously declared unaccountable. This is not coldness. This is the standard grammar of structural violence: first admit the pain, then prove that no one is responsible for it.

The archdiocese argued that Louisiana law does not let plaintiffs recover for "off-campus acts of student self-harm following school disciplinary decisions." Look at the structure of that sentence. The school makes a decision on campus. The consequence happens off campus. Responsibility is severed at the school gate. That line is not natural. The law drew it. The power to draw it is the power of interpretation.

And what is this school? Rummel belongs to the Roman Catholic Archdiocese of New Orleans, which filed for bankruptcy protection in 2020 amid the financial fallout of the clergy molestation scandal. Last December the archdiocese and its insurers agreed to pay about $305m to roughly 700 clergy abuse survivors. As October ended, the survivors had not been paid.

So the same institution, while still working through decades of its own sexual abuse liabilities, stood in court to argue it owed a student nothing. One set of lawyers handles the abuse claims. Another set handles the mother. Institutional responsibility can be reorganized, stretched, deferred through bankruptcy. A mother's claim is stopped by one appellate sentence.

One of Brannon's attorneys is Richard Trahant, who has represented numerous clergy abuse claimants against the same archdiocese. That detail is not a coincidence. It is the same pipeline with two exits: an institution that manages children while presenting itself as a moral authority, and when a child is harmed, uses legal technique to extract itself from responsibility.

The report does not say what happened to the other student in the shoving match. It does not say what investigation the school conducted before expelling Devon. It does not say who he spoke to in those final hours. The blanks are the material. A bullied kid pushes back and is expelled. The bully? Not mentioned. Whose name is absent is who the system defaults to blameless.

Devon's obituary says he played multiple instruments in Rummel's marching band, was on the varsity bowling team, and volunteered every year at a charity bike ride for people with multiple sclerosis. Seventeen years old, and his life already held that much.

The archdiocese spokesperson says they remember Devon and lift him and his family in prayer. Prayer is free. The lawsuit was overturned.

The appellate court says these facts fit no theory of recovery. Then ask a different question: when an institution has the power to expel a child, and no mechanism exists to ask whether that decision was reasonable, what is that power? It is not the background of the tragedy. It is the tragedy.

Brannon says she will keep appealing. What she can still appeal is the part the law permits her to appeal.
