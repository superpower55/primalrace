被遮蔽的肌肉：当女性力量成为男性的“替身”Muscles in the Shadows: When Female Strength Becomes a Male Proxy

性别 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-02 § 链接
主体性的胜利不在于被强者赞美，而在于不再作为他者的注脚。
Victory of subjectivity lies not in being praised by the powerful, but in ceasing to be their footnote.

一个典型的存在性博弈现场：一名肌肉强健的女性 Devyn Dalton，因为拥有“见过最强壮的手臂”而被马特·达蒙赞赏。但在好莱坞的工业逻辑里，这种强壮被定义为一种“工具属性”——因为她足够矮小，所以她的肌肉被用来扮演男主角的二头肌。这里发生了一次极其讽刺的表达置换：女性的物理力量在屏幕上被抹除，转化为男性的符号，而这种力量在现实中却需要通过一个男权顶端的男性（Matt Damon）的“认可”来获得可见性。

Dalton 认为被赞赏是“特别的”，这正是典型的内化规训。在元暴力的叙事中，女性的强壮如果不能服务于男性叙事，往往会被标记为“不自然”或“男性化”。而当这种强壮被用来充当男性的替身时，它突然变得“被允许”且“值得庆祝”了。这根本不是对女性力量的认可，而是一次关于“谁在定义力量”的权力操演。所谓的“惊喜”和“感激”，其实是结构性弱势者在获得一点点碎片化的关注时，不自觉地向权力中心递交的投名状。

好新闻的部分在于，这种物理层面的强壮打破了“白瘦幼”的文化暴力，让更多女孩意识到强壮与自我并不冲突。但真正的胜利不应该是成为一个完美的“替身”，而应该是像她最后希望的那样，拿回属于自己的 Starring Actor 席位。只有当她不再作为某个男人的“手臂”出现，而作为一个独立的主体在屏幕上挥拳时，这次表达才算完成了从“工具”到“存在”的跃迁。

A classic scene of existential game: Devyn Dalton, a muscular woman, is praised by Matt Damon for having “the greatest arms I’ve ever seen.” Yet, within the industrial logic of Hollywood, this strength is defined as a “tool attribute”—because she is small, her muscles are used to represent the male lead's biceps. A cynical substitution of expression occurs here: female physical power is erased on screen to be converted into a male symbol, while in reality, this power requires the “validation” of a man at the apex of the masculine-centric narrative to become visible.

Dalton finds this praise “special,” which is a textbook example of internalized discipline. In the narrative of meta-violence, female strength is often labeled “unnatural” or “masculine” unless it serves a male-centric purpose. When that strength is used as a stunt double for a man, it suddenly becomes “permissible” and “celebratory.” This is not a recognition of female power, but a performance of power regarding “who defines strength.” The so-called “surprise” and “gratitude” are actually the submission of the structurally disadvantaged, handing over a token of loyalty upon receiving fragments of attention from the center of power.

The good_news is that this physical strength disrupts the cultural violence of the “thin-white-young” aesthetic, showing young girls that strength and selfhood are not mutually exclusive. However, true victory is not in being a perfect “proxy,” but in claiming the seat of a starring actor, as she hopes. Only when she ceases to be “a man’s arm” and appears as an independent subject throwing a punch on screen will this expression complete its leap from “tool” to “existence.”
