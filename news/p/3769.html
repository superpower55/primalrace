脊椎动物的傲慢与无脊椎动物的“选美”The Arrogance of Vertebrates and the 'Beauty Pageant' of Invertebrates

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-13 § 链接
注意力的分配本身就是一种结构性暴力。
The distribution of attention is itself a form of structural violence.

《卫报》搞这个“年度无脊椎动物”投票，看起来像个温馨的自然科普活动，但拆穿其叙事外壳后，这其实是一次关于认知入口的补课。为什么需要通过一个“投票”来提醒人们关注这些生物？因为在人类的认知坐标系里，存在一个极其顽固的 meta-narrative：只有拥有脊椎、体型巨大、具有“拟人化”情感特征的生物（如熊猫、狮子、鲸鱼）才配拥有“主体性”和“被关注权”。

这种对脊椎动物的偏爱，本质上是人类将自身的生物特征（vertebrate）武器化，将其设定为“重要性”的衡量标尺。在这种标尺下，支撑整个生态系统底层的 130 万个无脊椎物种被结构性地边缘化。它们在物理上承载了地球最沉重的生存压力，但在文化层面上却被定义为“被忽视的”（overlooked）。这种 Potential（生态贡献）与 Actual（认知地位）之间的巨大差额，就是一种典型的 structural violence。

有趣的是，为了让这些生物获得哪怕一点点关注，叙事者不得不使用“可爱”（irresistibly cute）或“超级力量”（superpowers）这种被主流文化定义的标签来包装它们。这意味着，即便是在试图打破偏见的尝试中，我们依然在共谋一套“必须符合某种审美或功能价值才能被接纳”的评价体系。如果一个物种既不可爱也没有超能力，它是否依然值得被投票？

这种“选美”式的救赎并不能消除结构性暴力，它只是在文化层面上提供了一次短暂的、表演性的让步。真正的公正表达，应该是意识到“脊椎”这个生物标签在权力分配中被赋予的虚假权重，并将其解构。

The Guardian's 'Invertebrate of the Year' vote looks like a cozy nature column, but stripping away the narrative shell reveals it as a remedial lesson in cognitive entry points. Why is a 'vote' necessary to remind people to notice these creatures? Because in the human cognitive coordinate system, there exists a stubborn meta-narrative: only vertebrates—those large, 'human-like' animals such as pandas, lions, and whales—deserve 'subjectivity' and the right to be noticed.

This preference for vertebrates is essentially the weaponisation of our own biological traits. We have set the 'vertebrate' status as the yardstick for 'importance.' Under this metric, the 1.3 million invertebrate species supporting the very foundation of all ecosystems are structurally marginalised. They carry the heaviest survival burdens of the planet physically, yet are defined as 'overlooked' culturally. This gap between Potential (ecological contribution) and Actual (cognitive status) is a textbook case of structural violence.

Interestingly, to gain even a shred of attention, the narrator feels compelled to wrap these species in labels defined by mainstream culture, such as 'irresistibly cute' or 'superpowers.' This proves that even in attempts to break prejudice, we are still complicit in a valuation system where one must fit a certain aesthetic or functional mold to be accepted. If a species is neither cute nor powerful, does it still deserve a vote?

This 'beauty pageant' style of salvation does not eliminate structural violence; it is merely a brief, performative concession at the cultural layer. A Just Expression would be to recognise the false weight assigned to the biological label of 'spine' in power distribution and deconstruct it.
