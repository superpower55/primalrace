被当作子弹的女性与被液化的叛徒Women as Bullets and the Liquidation of Traitors

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
战争叙事中，男性是权力博弈的筹码，女性是执行暴力的耗材。
In war narratives, men are chips in power games; women are consumables for executing violence.

在这条新闻的碎片里，最刺眼的是那个被逮捕的俄国女人。她被指控代表乌克兰特种部队刺杀了叛徒 Shageyev。在典型的战争叙事中，这种情节会被包装成“女特工”的英勇或阴险，但剥开 cultural layer，你会发现这不过是再一次的 weaponization：女性身体被当作一种低可见度的渗透工具，被推向最危险的 direct violence 前线。当任务成功时，她是国家的影子；当被捕时，她是被抛弃的个体。这种利用女性在社会认知中“非威胁性”的生物墙来实施攻击的逻辑，本质上是对女性主体性的再次剥夺——她不是在参与政治，她是被政治当作一枚一次性子弹发射出去。

而 Shageyev 的死则展示了存在性战争的残酷真相：在男性中心叙事的权力场中，忠诚是唯一的定价权。从乌克兰潜艇指挥官到俄罗斯军官，他的身份表达在 2014 年的一次博弈中完成了切换，以为找到了最优解。但结果是，他成了双方共谋场域中的弃子。乌克兰将其定义为“叛徒”并予以“液化”（liquidated），而俄罗斯则在 Telegram 上通过消费他的背叛来强化自身的合法性。在这个过程中，人的生命被简化为数据，死亡被简化为一种政治清理。

至于那些关于炼油厂的爆炸、平民火车的袭击以及尸体交换的数字，它们构成了这场战争的 structural violence 底色。人们在讨论谁杀死了谁，却很少有人讨论这种“用人命交换意义”的宏大叙事本身就是一种元暴力。在这种叙事下，无论是被炸死的工程师，还是被利用的刺杀者，都只是在为某些权力中心地带的男性博弈提供血腥的注脚。

Among the fragments of this news, the most jarring is the Russian woman arrested for the assassination of Robert Shageyev. In typical war narratives, such plots are packaged as the bravery or cunning of a "female spy," but stripping away the cultural layer reveals a recurring weaponisation: the female body is used as a low-visibility infiltration tool, pushed to the frontlines of direct violence. When the mission succeeds, she is the state's shadow; when captured, she is a discarded individual. The logic of using the "non-threatening" biological wall of women to execute attacks is essentially another deprivation of female subjectivity—she is not participating in politics; she is being fired as a disposable bullet by politics.

Shageyev's death illustrates the brutal truth of the existential war: in a masculine-centric narrative, loyalty is the only pricing power. From a Ukrainian submarine commander to a Russian officer, his expression of identity shifted in a 2014 gamble, thinking he had found the optimal expression. Instead, he became a pawn in a field of complicity. Ukraine defined him as a "traitor" and "liquidated" him, while Russia used his betrayal on Telegram to reinforce its own legitimacy. In this process, human life is reduced to data, and death to a political cleanup.

As for the refinery blasts, the passenger train attacks, and the body swaps, they form the structural violence of this war. People discuss who killed whom, but few question how the grand narrative of "exchanging lives for meaning" is itself a form of meta-violence. Under this narrative, whether it is the killed engineer or the utilized assassin, they are merely bloody footnotes for the power games played by men in certain centers of authority.
