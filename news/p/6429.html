一条毯子的定价权The Price Tag on a Blanket

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
名人带货卖的不是毯子，是入场券。
Celebrity endorsement sells an admission ticket, not a blanket.

这条新闻里没有受害者，只有一个价格。但价格本身就是材料：一条 Medium 尺寸的毯子原价 275 美元，打折后 137.5 美元，The Lola XL 从 425 美元降到 212.5 美元（来源：The Hollywood Reporter）。一条毯子。打折前的数字不是成本加利润，是身份标价——它必须先贵到能筛人，折扣才有意义。

报道自己把机制写得很清楚：Lola 是"好莱坞最受追捧"的毯子，有"忠实的名人追随者"，上过 Oprah 的年度最爱清单，和 Aviator Nation、纽约洋基队、LoveShackFancy 联名，还"很擅长捕捉流行图案、色调和审美"。这一串不是产品参数，是品味的分层装置。第三章讲的审美入口就是这个：有权势的人给事物贴标签、定价值，普通人通过"我也喜欢这个"完成一次社交晋升。你买的不是保暖，是"我和那批人用同一种东西"。

"限量"和"售完即止"是同一套语法。稀缺不是生产出来的，是宣布出来的。50% off 听起来是让利，实际是先把价格抬到能充当门槛的高度，再用折扣把门槛临时放低，让更多人有机会买一张入场券。付钱的人得到一条毯子，和一次被允许靠近那个圈子的感觉。

报道里唯一不在场的是做这条毯子的人。它写明了品牌、联名、名人、折扣码、尺寸和价格，没写这些毯子在哪里生产、由谁缝制、拿多少工钱。一条 425 美元的毯子，和一条 62.5 美元的婴儿毯，中间的那段差额去了哪里，报道没有问。这不是它的失职，这是这类稿子的标准配置——它是一篇带联盟佣金的导购，不是调查。

所以真正的折扣不在价格上。价格可以打对折，标签的等级不会。买完之后，那个"好莱坞最爱"的位置还是别人的，你只是离它近了一点。

There are no victims in this story, only a price. But the price is the material: a Medium Lola Original runs $275, marked down to $137.50; the XL goes from $425 to $212.50 (source: The Hollywood Reporter). For a blanket. The pre-discount number isn't cost plus margin, it's a status marker—it has to be expensive enough to sort people, or the discount means nothing.

The article lays out the mechanism itself: Lola is "Hollywood's favorite," with a "loyal celebrity following," a spot on Oprah's Favorite Things 2025, collabs with Aviator Nation, the New York Yankees, LoveShackFancy, and a knack for "spotting trending patterns, hues and aesthetics." None of that is a product spec. It's a taste-sorting device. This is the aesthetic entry point from Chapter 3: the powerful assign labels and prices, and ordinary people climb a rung by saying "I like that too." You aren't buying warmth. You're buying "I use what those people use."

"Limited-edition" and "while supplies last" run on the same grammar. Scarcity isn't produced, it's announced. Fifty percent off sounds like a concession; in practice the price was first raised high enough to work as a gate, then the gate is briefly lowered so more people can buy a ticket in. The buyer gets a blanket and the feeling of being allowed near the room.

The one absent party is whoever makes the blankets. The piece names the brand, the collabs, the celebrities, the promo code, the sizes, the prices. It doesn't say where the blankets are made, who sews them, or what they're paid. Where the gap between a $425 throw and a $62.50 baby blanket goes, the article doesn't ask. That isn't a failure—it's the standard configuration of the format. This is affiliate shopping copy, not reporting.

The real discount isn't on the price. The price can be halved; the hierarchy of the label can't. After checkout, the "Hollywood's favorite" seat still belongs to someone else. You just got a little closer to it.
