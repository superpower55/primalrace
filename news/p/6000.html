谁在替以色列清理门户Who's Policing the Party for Israel

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
初选输了的人，在投票前夜还在追杀赢家。
The primary loser is still hunting the winner on election eve.

离投票只剩几天，民主党的前议长和一位初选落败的众议员，同时把火力对准了本党密歇根参议员候选人 Abdul El-Sayed。这不是党内分歧，是共谋者在自己人身上执行纪律。

Pelosi 在播客上说 El-Sayed 关于反对以族群或宗教立国的言论"是错的"，"不代表美国国会的民主党人"。报道写得很清楚：El-Sayed 反对的是任何国家以族群或宗教组织自身，他有意见的是以色列政府，不是犹太人。把这两件事捏成"反犹"，是叙事武器化的标准操作——先给一个词定价，再让被定价的人自证清白。

Haley Stevens 的版本更露骨。她在初选输给了 El-Sayed，然后发声明说，她是否支持他，取决于他是否谴责一支根本不是他竞选团队制作的广告。那支广告来自 Rebellion PAC，2024 年 2 月就播过，把食品杂货涨价归因于美国对以色列在加沙行动的持续支持。一支别人的旧广告，成了她扣住本党唯一希望的条件。报道还指出，她要求 El-Sayed 为自己把以色列和密歇根人的负担能力危机挂钩的言论道歉，但报道同时说，并不清楚他究竟说过这样的话。

这就是共谋的运作方式：不需要证据，只需要一个可以被反复使用的指控。报道里 Stevens 那句"免责声明不是谴责，我社区里每个人都知道区别"，翻译过来是——我要的不是澄清，是下跪。

报道自己给出了结论：民主党人把对以色列的支持，放在本党成败和自己的选民之前。Stevens 输了初选，难道奇怪吗。但真正值得指出的是这次共谋的回报结构：Pelosi 早已不在议席上，Stevens 已经输过一次，她们付出的是本党的一个参议院席位，换来的是在既定秩序里继续被认作可靠的人。代价不由她们承担。

El-Sayed 说过一句话：一个拿你的钱、把你推进你本不该打的战争的盟友，不是盟友。这句话说的是国家，也适用于政党。

报道没写的是，那 228,808 美元亲以色列 PAC 的钱，最后到底买到了什么。投票日会给出答案，但答案不会只写在票数里。

Days before the vote, the former Speaker and a congresswoman who lost her primary both aimed their fire at their own party's Senate candidate in Michigan, Abdul El-Sayed. This isn't a party disagreement. It's co-conspirators enforcing discipline on one of their own.

On a podcast, Pelosi said El-Sayed's position against organizing any state around an ethnic or religious group was "wrong" and "isn't a reflection of the Democrats in the Congress." The report is precise: El-Sayed opposes any state's right to be organized around an ethnic or religious group, and his issue is with the Israeli government, not Jewish people. Folding those two things into "antisemitic" is the standard move of weaponized narrative — set the price of a word first, then make the priced person prove their innocence.

Haley Stevens is blunter. She lost the primary to El-Sayed, then issued a statement making her support contingent on whether he condemned an ad his campaign didn't even produce. The video came from Rebellion PAC, aired back in February 2024, and blamed grocery prices on America's continued support for Israel's actions in Gaza. Someone else's old ad became her condition for backing the party's only hope for the seat. The report also notes she demanded he apologize for statements tying Israel to Michiganders' affordability crisis — while noting it wasn't clear he had made any such claims.

That's how complicity runs: no evidence required, only an accusation that can be reused. Stevens's line that "a disclaimer is not condemnation, and everyone in my community knows the difference" translates to: I don't want clarification, I want a kneeling.

The report draws its own conclusion — Democrats putting support for Israel ahead of their party and their constituents. Stevens lost the primary; is that a surprise. But the real story is the payoff structure: Pelosi holds no seat, Stevens already lost once, and what they spend is the party's Senate seat, in exchange for staying legible as reliable inside the existing order. They don't pay the cost.

El-Sayed said an ally that takes your money to push you into wars you shouldn't be fighting is not an ally. He was talking about countries. It applies to parties too.

What the report doesn't say is what that $228,808 in pro-Israel PAC money actually bought. Election day will answer, but the answer won't be written only in the vote count.
