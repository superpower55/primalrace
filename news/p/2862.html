把“喜欢我”定义为非种族主义的特权逻辑Defining 'Liking Me' as the Metric for Non-Racism

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-25 § 链接
权力者通过定义“忠诚”来垄断对“种族主义”的解释权。
The powerful monopolize the definition of 'racism' by equating it with political loyalty.

特朗普这段话是典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 现场。他把一个关于体育成就的讨论，瞬间切换到了关于身份认同和政治忠诚的博弈。在他的逻辑闭环里，判定一个人是否是“种族主义者”的标准不是基于客观事实或社会结构，而是基于一个极其私人的维度：是否喜欢他。

这是一种极其粗暴的表达武器化。他通过将 LeBron James 对其政治立场的反对（Endorsement of opponents）等同于某种潜在的“种族主义”，试图夺取解释权。在 Trump 的叙事中，他自己才是那个定义谁是“好人”、谁是“种族主义者”的定价权掌控者。这种逻辑的本质是：权力者可以通过定义“忠诚”来重新定义“事实”。

而 Michael Jordan 的“胜出”并非基于篮球数据的 Potential，而是基于一种共谋 (complicity)。因为 Jordan 是他的高尔夫球友，是那个在权力结构中选择顺从并获得利益的共谋者。这种“好人”定义其实就是一种特权筛选机制：只要你认可我的权力，你就是文明的、非种族主义的；如果你挑战我的权力，你就是异类，甚至是被贴上反向标签的施暴者。

这就是典型的男性中心叙事在政治领域的延伸——将公共空间的价值评判降格为私人的、基于依附关系的权力游戏。在这种叙事下，客观的 MVP 数据和历史得分纪录在“是否喜欢我”这个元暴力指标面前毫无意义。

Trump's response is a textbook display of meta violence. He instantaneously shifts a conversation about athletic achievement into a game of identity politics and political loyalty. In his closed-loop logic, the criterion for judging whether someone is a 'racist' is not based on objective facts or structural reality, but on a purely personal dimension: whether they like him.

This is a crude weaponisation of expression. By equating LeBron James' political opposition—his endorsement of Trump's rivals—with a potential 'racism,' Trump attempts to seize the power of interpretation. In Trump's narrative, he is the sole authority who holds the pricing power over who is a 'good guy' and who is a 'racist.' The essence of this logic is that the wielder of power can redefine 'fact' by redefining 'loyalty.'

Meanwhile, Michael Jordan's 'victory' is not based on the potential of his basketball stats, but on a form of complicity. Jordan is a golf buddy, a co-conspirator who chooses compliance within the power structure to secure benefits. This definition of a 'good guy' is actually a privilege-based screening mechanism: as long as you acknowledge my power, you are civilized and non-racist; if you challenge it, you are the 'other,' or even the aggressor under a reversed label.

This is the extension of the masculine-centric narrative in the political sphere—reducing public value judgments to a private power game based on dependency. In this narrative, objective MVP data and all-time scoring records are meaningless in the face of the meta-violence metric: 'Do you like me?'
