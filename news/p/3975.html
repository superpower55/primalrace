运动不能救你的性生活，因为你的身体不是故障的机器Exercise Won't Save Your Sex Life Because Your Body Isn't a Glitching Machine

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
将性功能视为可升级的硬件性能，是典型的男性中心叙事。
Treating sexual function as a hardware performance to be upgraded is a textbook masculine-centric narrative.

《卫报》这篇典型的 wellness 报道，试图把性生活简化为一个可以通过“规律运动”来升级的硬件性能问题。血液循环、激素分泌、盆底肌强度——这些词汇把人类的欲望和快感拆解成了生物化学指标。这种逻辑极其危险，因为它在潜意识里传达了一个信息：如果你在性生活中不满意，那是因为你的“性能”不够，你需要通过某种训练来“升级” (upgrade)。

这种“升级”叙事是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。在男本位叙事中，性被简化为一种 performance（表现），而表现的好坏取决于身体的输出功率。这种逻辑在男性身上表现为对勃起能力的执念，而在女性身上则被伪装成“提升快感”的健康建议。报道中提到女性通过运动可以获得更高的得分，但这依然是在一个被量化的、由研究者定义的“功能性”框架内打分。

真正的暴力在于，它把性生活从一个关于主体性、权力博弈和情感连接的 complex 领域，降格为了一个关于“血流”和“心率”的生理问题。当一个人被告知可以通过 yoga 或 cardio 来改善性生活时，他被引导去关注自己的身体是否“合格”，而不是去追问：我的欲望是被谁定义的？我在关系中是否拥有主体性？我的快感是否在为他人的最优解而服务？

运动可以改善健康，但它不能解决 structural violence 带来的欲望缺失。如果一个女性在生活中被繁重的家务和情绪劳动榨干，如果她在关系中处于被支配的弱势，那么无论她的盆底肌多么强壮，她的欲望依然会被结构性地抹除。把这种缺失归结为“缺乏运动”，本质上是在为结构性的压迫寻找一个生物学上的 scapegoat，从而让人们在跑步机上寻找答案，而不是在权力结构中寻找出口。

This typical wellness piece from The Guardian attempts to reduce sex life to a hardware performance issue that can be solved by 'regular exercise.' Blood flow, hormone release, pelvic floor strength—these terms dismantle human desire and pleasure into biochemical metrics. The danger here is the implicit message: if you're unsatisfied in bed, it's because your 'performance' is lacking, and you need an 'upgrade.'

This 'upgrade' narrative is a textbook masculine-centric narrative. In this framework, sex is simplified into a performance, and the quality of that performance depends on the body's output power. For men, this manifests as an obsession with erectile function; for women, it's disguised as health advice to 'enhance pleasure.' The article mentions women scoring higher through exercise, but this scoring still happens within a quantified, 'functional' framework defined by researchers.

The real meta-violence lies in degrading sex from a complex realm of subjectivity, power games, and emotional connection into a physiological issue of 'blood flow' and 'heart rate.' When someone is told they can improve their sex life via yoga or cardio, they are conditioned to ask if their body is 'qualified,' rather than questioning: Who defined my desire? Do I possess subjectivity in this relationship? Is my pleasure serving someone else's optimal expression?

Exercise improves health, but it cannot fix the loss of desire caused by structural violence. If a woman is drained by domestic labor and emotional work, or if she is structurally dominated in her relationship, no amount of pelvic floor strength will bring back a desire that has been structurally erased. Attributing this void to a 'lack of exercise' is simply finding a biological scapegoat for structural oppression, leading people to seek answers on a treadmill instead of within the power structure.
