用冷战轰炸机追逐阴影：科学的特权与叙事的浪漫Chasing Shadows with Cold War Bombers: The Privilege of Science and the Romance of Narrative

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
真正的认知突破往往依赖于对暴力机器的挪用与特权空间的占领。
True cognitive breakthroughs often rely on the appropriation of violence machinery and the occupation of privileged spaces.

这则新闻在表面上是一个典型的 science-themed 浪漫叙事：NASA 的飞行员在 50,000 英尺的高空追逐日食的阴影，试图捕捉 nanoflares 的瞬间。这种叙事试图将读者拉入一种“人类在宇宙面前显得渺小”的哲学沉思中，用“mind-blowing”和“connected to the Earth”来包裹整个行动。

但如果我们剥离这种 cultural layer 的浪漫化包装，会发现一个极具讽刺意味的 structural 事实：这架用来观测太阳的 WB-57 飞机，其原型是 English Electric Canberra——一架冷战时期的战术核打击轰炸机。这就是典型的暴力机器挪用：曾经被设计用来投掷核弹、实施 direct violence 的战争工具，现在成为了科学家获取认知入口的特权载体。科学的进步在这里不是某种纯粹的理性攀升，而是建立在对军事遗产的继承与占领之上。

最有趣的是这种“特权表达”的量化：地面的观察者只能忍受 2 分钟 18 秒的黑暗，而拥有高科技飞机和飞行速度的精英们，可以将这个时间延长到 3 分钟。这多出来的 38 秒，就是由资本、技术和军事工业复合体共同支付的“认知溢价”。

我们不必 naive 地庆祝这种科学探索，而应意识到：所谓的“人类”对宇宙的认知，其实是由极少数掌握了这些“核打击级”资源的群体代为完成的。当科学家感叹人类是宇宙中的“little mote”时，他们正坐在一个曾经能毁灭数个城市的战争机器里，享受着最高级别的认知特权。

On the surface, this is a classic science-themed romantic narrative: NASA aviators chasing the shadow of an eclipse at 50,000 feet to capture nanoflares. The narrative attempts to pull the reader into a philosophical meditation on human insignificance, wrapping the mission in terms like "mind-blowing" and "connected to the Earth."

But if we strip away this romanticized cultural layer, we find a deeply ironic structural fact: the WB-57 aircraft used for this observation is based on the English Electric Canberra—a Cold War tactical nuclear strike bomber. This is a textbook case of appropriating the machinery of violence: a tool designed for delivering nuclear bombs and exercising direct violence has now become a privileged vehicle for scientists to access a cognitive entry point. Scientific progress here is not a pure ascent of reason, but is built upon the inheritance and occupation of military legacy.

The most intriguing part is the quantification of this "privileged expression": observers on the ground must settle for 2 minutes and 18 seconds of darkness, while the elite with high-tech aircraft and flight speed can extend this to 3 minutes. Those extra 38 seconds are the "cognitive premium" paid for by capital, technology, and the military-industrial complex.

We should not naively celebrate this exploration. Instead, we must recognize that the so-called "human" understanding of the universe is actually performed by a tiny minority who control these "nuclear-strike-grade" resources. While the scientist laments that humans are but a "little mote" in the universe, they are sitting inside a war machine that could once have annihilated entire cities, enjoying the highest form of cognitive privilege.
