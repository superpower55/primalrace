饮食审美是阶级筛选的武器，而健康是特权者的PRCulinary Taste as a Weapon of Class Filtering, Health as Elite PR

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
品味是对异己的筛选，而对UPF的厌恶是结构性暴力的文化掩体。
Taste is a filter for the other; hatred for UPF is a cultural shield for structural violence.

这篇关于《The Full English》的评论揭示了一个极其典型的认知入口：食物不仅是能量，更是政治性的表达。在英国，57%的饮食是超加工食品 (UPF)，而法意两国仅在14%左右。这在表面上被讨论为“健康”或“饮食习惯”的问题，但本质上是 structural violence 的结果——低收入群体被剥夺了获取新鲜食材的时间与经济能力，被迫在UPF中生存。

但最阴险的部分在于 cultural layer 的运作。当“精致饮食”被定义为 pretentious（做作）时，这其实是一场关于定价权的博弈。权势者通过不断移动“posh”的定义（比如把鱼and chips也变成高端料理），将审美武器化。他们先定义什么是“低俗”的食物（如 Turkey Twizzler），通过对他人的饮食习惯进行道德审判，完成阶级筛选和身份确立。这是一种典型的通过品味区分异己的武器化表达。

最讽刺的是，当精英阶层开始公开“手腕地”担忧UPF时，这种叙事往往成了另一种掩体。他们通过定义“健康”来获得道德优越感，而忽略了这种健康是建立在对底层生存空间的挤压之上的。所谓的“饮食阶级战争”，其实是元暴力在餐盘上的延伸：解释权在谁手里，谁就决定了什么样的身体是“文明”的，什么样的饮食是“低端”的。在这种共谋下，底层人不仅失去了健康的身体，还被植入了“我的品味很低俗”的自我规训。

This commentary on *The Full English* exposes a classic cognitive entry point: food is not just energy, it is a political expression. In the UK, 57% of the diet consists of ultra-processed foods (UPF), compared to around 14% in France and Italy. While framed as a matter of "health" or "habit," it is fundamentally a result of structural violence—low-income groups are stripped of the time and capital needed for fresh produce, forced into a UPF survival mode.

Yet, the most insidious part lies in the cultural layer. When "posh food" is labeled as pretentious, it is a game of pricing power. The powerful weaponize aesthetics by constantly shifting the definition of "posh" (even turning fish and chips into high-end cuisine) to filter out the other. By defining certain foods (like Turkey Twizzlers) as "low-class," they use taste to establish identity and exclude others. This is a textbook case of weaponized expression used for class stratification.

Ironically, when the elite begin their "hand-wringing" over UPF, this narrative becomes another shield. They use the definition of "health" to secure moral superiority, ignoring that this health is built upon the erosion of the lower class's survival space. The so-called "food class war" is merely an extension of meta-violence on a dinner plate: whoever holds the power of interpretation decides which body is "civilized" and which diet is "low-end." Through this complicity, the marginalized not only lose their health but internalize a self-discipline that whispers, "my taste is vulgar."
