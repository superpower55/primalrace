用利息给战争买单：一场关于成本转嫁的共谋Paying for War with Interest: A Conspiracy of Cost Transfer

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
宏大叙事的战争成本，最终通过货币政策转化为底层个体的生存暴力。
The costs of grand narratives in war are eventually converted into survival violence for individuals via monetary policy.

典型的 masculine-centric narrative：特朗普对伊朗的战争被轻描淡写为“战争开始了”，而其结果则是油价飙升。这里有一个极其冷酷的逻辑链条：男性政治家在公共空间决定发动战争 $\rightarrow$ 能源价格上涨 $\rightarrow$ 通胀压力 $\rightarrow$ 央行加息 $\rightarrow$ 普通民众房贷压力增加、生活成本上升。

这就是一个完美的 structural violence 闭环。战争的决策权在极少数男性手中，但战争的代价却通过一个名为“货币政策”的精密机器，被均匀地分摊到了每一个借贷者、房奴和底层消费者的头上。所谓的“经济预测”和“利率走势”，本质上是这场成本转嫁的 accounting 过程。经济学家们在讨论 $90 还是 $100 一桶油时，其实是在计算这个社会的底层需要支付多少额外的“战争税”来维持这个系统的稳定性。

最讽刺的是，央行在考虑加息以“遏制通胀”，这在 cultural layer 看来是理性的、专业的，但实际上这是一种元暴力的伪装。它将由于政治决策（战争）导致的成本上升，转化为一种金融管理问题，从而掩盖了最初的施暴者。底层民众在面对房贷上涨时，感受到的是一种无形的压力，而这种压力正是由那个在地图前决定开战的人制造的。

这场博弈中没有公正的表达，只有共谋。金融市场、央行、经济学家共同构建了一套专业话语体系，将一场政治性的掠夺包装成一次技术性的利率调整。当人们在抱怨利息太高时，他们其实是在为一场他们从未投票支持的战争买单。

A textbook masculine-centric narrative: Trump's war on Iran is glossed over as "war began," while the fallout is merely "rising oil prices." There is a chilling logic chain here: male politicians decide on war in public spaces $\rightarrow$ energy prices spike $\rightarrow$ inflation rises $\rightarrow$ central banks hike rates $\rightarrow$ ordinary people face higher mortgages and living costs.

This is a perfect loop of structural violence. The power to initiate war rests with a tiny elite of men, yet the cost is evenly distributed across every borrower and low-income consumer through a sophisticated machine called "monetary policy." The "economic forecasts" and "interest rate outlooks" are essentially the accounting process for this cost transfer. When economists debate whether oil hits $90 or $100, they are calculating how much additional "war tax" the bottom of society must pay to maintain system stability.

Most ironic is the Bank of England considering rate hikes to "curb inflation." In the cultural layer, this is framed as rational and professional, but it is actually a camouflage for meta-violence. It transforms costs resulting from political decisions (war) into a technical financial management problem, thereby erasing the original perpetrator. As the public feels the squeeze of rising interest, they are experiencing a violence manufactured by the man who decided to strike the first blow on a map.

There is no Just Expression in this game, only complicity. Financial markets, central banks, and economists jointly construct a professional discourse that packages a political plunder as a technical rate adjustment. While people complain about high interest, they are effectively paying for a war they never voted for.
