学生公寓崩盘：被当成金融衍生品的生存权Student Housing Collapse: Survival Rights as Financial Derivatives

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-19 § 链接
当居住被定义为资产而非权利，学生就成了金融博弈的耗材。
When housing is defined as an asset rather than a right, students become disposable fodder for financial gambling.

这是一场典型的 structural violence。在英国的教育叙事里，学生公寓被包装成“Perfect Home”，但其底层的真实逻辑是 Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV) 驱动的金融套利。资本通过杠杆在邓迪、考文垂、利兹这些城市疯狂堆砌塔楼，本质上是在赌国际学生的流量和租金回报。当伊朗战争、生活成本危机导致流量下跌，这些被金融化的房产迅速从“资产”变回“负债”。

最令人作呕的是这种 weaponized expression 的欺骗性：公司在 6 月底还在 Facebook 上用“Book now before it’s gone!”这种制造焦虑的营销话术诱导学生签约，而实际上在 7 月中旬就已经申请破产。这种认知入口的操纵，让学生在完全不知情的情况下，将自己的生存基准（住宿）抵押给了一个已经死亡的金融壳公司。

这里的共谋链条清晰可见：房地产管理公司 90 North、科威特投资公司 Rasameel 以及那些提供贷款的金融机构，他们共同构建了一个将学生居住权“证券化”的系统。在这个系统里，学生的焦虑是营销的燃料，而当风险爆发时，所谓的“深表同情” (deeply sympathise) 只是 PR 版本的文化掩体。对于那些远在海外、必须支付第三方存储费的国际学生来说，这种暴力是直接且具体的——他们的生活被物理性地驱逐，而资本在通过资产剥离 (disposing of properties) 快速止损。

所谓的“通勤学生”增加，不是什么生活方式的演变，而是结构性剥削导致的最优解表达：当 purpose-built housing 的租金被推高到只有富裕海外学生能负担时，本地学生只能通过“回家住”来对抗被驱逐的风险。生存空间的定价权被资本垄断，学生在这个博弈中毫无筹码。

This is a textbook case of structural violence. In the UK's educational narrative, student accommodation is packaged as a "Perfect Home," but the underlying logic is financial arbitrage driven by Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs). Capital aggressively piled up tower blocks in cities like Dundee, Coventry, and Leeds, essentially betting on the flow of international students and rental yields. When that flow dropped due to the Iran war and the cost-of-living crisis, these financialized properties rapidly flipped from "assets" to "liabilities."

The most repulsive part is the weaponized expression of this deception: the company used anxiety-driven marketing on Facebook in late June, shouting "Book now before it’s gone!", while they were already filing for administration by mid-July. This manipulation of the cognitive entry point forced students to pledge their basic survival—housing—to a dead corporate shell without any knowledge of the risk.

The chain of complicity is crystal clear: the real estate manager 90 North, the Kuwaiti investment firm Rasameel, and the lending institutions. Together, they constructed a system that "securitized" the right to live. In this system, student anxiety is the fuel for marketing, and when the risk explodes, phrases like "deeply sympathise" serve as mere cultural shields for PR. For international students who must now pay third-party storage fees from abroad, the violence is direct and concrete—their lives are physically evicted while capital performs a quick exit through the disposal of properties.

The rise of the "commuter student" is not a lifestyle evolution, but an optimal expression of survival against structural exploitation. When rents for purpose-built housing are pushed to levels only affordable for wealthy overseas students, domestic students are forced to live at home to avoid the risk of eviction. The pricing power of living space has been monopolized by capital, leaving students with zero leverage in this existential game.
