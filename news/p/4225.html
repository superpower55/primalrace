用纳税人的钱为犯罪者的利润买单Taxpayers Paying for Criminal Profits

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
结构性暴力就是让受害者通过缴税来清理施暴者的垃圾
Structural violence is making victims pay taxes to clean up the perpetrator's waste.

这根本不是什么“带来希望”的好新闻，而是一场典型的 structural violence 闭环。逻辑极其荒诞：犯罪团伙通过非法倾倒垃圾（illegal dumping）将处理成本外部化，直接转化为纯利润；而政府在失效多年后，决定用纳税人的钱来清理。这意味着，那些生活在毒废料旁、健康受损的底层社区，在遭受了直接暴力后，还要通过缴纳税款，在财务上为这些犯罪分子的利润买单。

Andy Burnham 试图用“政治终于在起作用”这种叙事来包装这次行动，但这只是典型的 cultural violence。他把一个长期监管失职的结构性溃败，包装成一次慷慨的政府恩赐。所谓的 AI 无人机和提高罚金，不过是在 Actual 已经造成巨大损害后，试图通过增加技术噱头来掩盖监管能力的缺失。如果监管在第一天就有效，就不需要用纳税人的钱去清理 22,000 吨垃圾。

最讽刺的是，Burnham 在自己的选区 Makerfield 面对那座烂了 20 个月的垃圾山，直到补选需要选票时才将其作为政治筹码。这证明了在当前的权力博弈中，底层社区的生存环境只是政治人物换取“票”的价值的货币。这种共谋机制非常清晰：监管机构维持低效，犯罪者攫取高利，政治家在危机爆发后通过“清理”来收割政治资本。在这个三角关系中，唯一被物化且被剥削的是那些被定义为“被忽视社区”的居民。

This is not 'good news' bringing hope; it is a textbook loop of structural violence. The logic is absurd: criminal gangs externalize their disposal costs through illegal dumping, converting those costs directly into pure profit. Now, the government decides to use taxpayer money for the cleanup. This means the marginalized communities, whose health was already compromised by toxic waste, are now financially subsidizing the profits of the criminals through their taxes.

Andy Burnham attempts to wrap this in a narrative of 'politics finally working,' which is pure cultural violence. He is rebranding a long-term systemic failure of regulation as a generous act of government grace. The promised AI-powered drones and increased fines are merely technical gimmicks designed to mask the collapse of regulatory capacity. If the system had worked on day one, there would be no need to spend public funds on 22,000 tonnes of waste.

Most cynically, Burnham's handling of the waste mountain in his own constituency, Makerfield—rotting for 20 months—shows that the living conditions of the poor are merely currency used to buy 'votes' during by-elections. The complicity is clear: regulators remain inefficient, criminals reap high profits, and politicians harvest political capital by 'cleaning up' after the crisis has peaked. In this triangle, the only ones objectified and exploited are the residents of these so-called 'ignored communities.'
