六十三亿美元的银行，和没有存款保险的银行A $6.3 Billion Bank With No Deposit Insurance

科技 结构层 NPR ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要本周，全球最大加密货币交易所币安遭黑客攻击，约20分钟内被盗走逾63亿美元加密货币，成为史上最大网络盗窃案。专家普遍认为黑客是朝鲜军方一支专业网络部队。币安已冻结约30亿美元被盗资产，并承诺赔偿受害客户。
The newsBinance, the world's largest crypto exchange, was hacked this week, with over $6.3 billion in cryptocurrencies stolen in about 20 minutes—the biggest cyber heist in history. Experts widely believe a North Korean military hacking unit was responsible. Binance has frozen about $3 billion of the stolen funds and promised to repay affected customers.
托管和交易合在一家，风险就合法地转给了用户。
Custody and trading in one firm: the risk gets legally handed to users.

六十三亿美元，二十分钟。NPR 请来的那位 TRM Labs 政策主管说这是「人类历史上最大的银行抢劫案」。数字是真的，比喻是假的：银行抢劫案里，被抢的是银行的钱，储户的账户第二天照常；这次被拿走的是投资者自己的币，Binance 说会赔，但报道里写得很清楚，按现行监管它并不一定非赔不可。

真正的问题不在朝鲜黑客有多快，而在这门生意被设计成了什么样。Better Markets 的 Amanda Fischer 把对照摆得很干净：你在纽交所交易，钱存在券商那里，券商受 SEC 审计监管，还得买保险，出事由保险把客户补上。加密交易所把交易和托管塞进同一家公司，既当券商又当金库，却没有对应的审计、监管和保险。这不是安全漏洞，这是结构本身。

所以「Wild West」这个说法只说对了一半。西部片里的荒野蛮横，至少还承认有枪战；这里的野蛮是写在条款里的：钱放在我这儿，风险归你，收益归我。加尔通的算式在这里很好用——本可以达到的状态是「被偷了也能拿回来」，实际达到的状态是「看交易所愿不愿意赔」。这个差额不是黑客抢走的，是制度提前让渡出去的。

报道里最值得记下的一笔是 FTX。2022 年那家交易所挪用客户资金自救，最后 Sam Bankman-Fried 因欺诈等罪名被判 25 年。Fischer 说，交易一旦亏了，动用客户资金「几乎不可抗拒」。黑客是从外面进来抢，创始人是从里面拿——同一把锁的两面。

至于朝鲜，报道说这是军方的一支专业黑客队伍，这笔钱将用于武器扩散和其他破坏稳定的活动。这个判断我信，但它同时也是一块很好用的挡箭牌：把六十三亿讲成一个敌国军队的故事，Binance 的结构问题就变成了地缘政治问题，需要被追问的监管缺口就变成了需要被同情的受害者。报道自己写了，目前还无法判断 Binance 的安全系统有没有重大缺陷——也就是说，连「漏洞在哪」都还不知道，唯一确定的是损失落在了谁的账上。

被冻结的三十亿是好事，但那是追回，不是保障。真正没被回答的问题是：那些在更小的交易所里、没有三十亿可冻结、也没有一句「我们会赔」的普通投资者，他们的差额由谁来补。报道替他们问了一句，然后停在了那里。

$6.3 billion in twenty minutes. The TRM Labs policy chief NPR quotes calls it "the largest bank robbery in human history." The number is real; the metaphor is not. In a bank robbery, the bank's money is taken and depositors' accounts open the next morning. Here, investors' own coins walked out the door, and Binance says it will pay them back — though, as the piece notes, under current rules it isn't actually obliged to.

The real story isn't how fast the North Koreans moved. It's how the business was built. Amanda Fischer of Better Markets draws the line cleanly: you trade on the NYSE, but your funds sit with your broker, who is audited and regulated by the SEC and carries insurance that makes customers whole after a cyberattack. Crypto exchanges fold trading and custody into one company — broker and vault at once — with none of the audit, regulation, or insurance. That isn't a security hole. That's the structure.

So "Wild West" is only half right. The Wild West at least admitted there were gunfights. This wildness is written into the terms: your money sits with us, the risk is yours, the upside is ours. Galtung's arithmetic fits — the potential state is "robbed and still made whole," the actual state is "whatever the exchange feels like paying." That gap wasn't stolen by hackers. It was signed away in advance.

The detail worth keeping is FTX. In 2022 that exchange dipped into customer funds to save itself; Sam Bankman-Fried got 25 years for fraud and other charges. Fischer says that once a trade goes bad, reaching into client money becomes "almost irresistible." Hackers break in from outside; founders reach in from inside. Same lock, two faces.

As for North Korea: a military hacking unit, the money bound for weapons proliferation and other destabilizing activity. I believe it. It also makes a convenient shield. Frame $6.3 billion as an enemy army's story and Binance's structural problem becomes geopolitics, the regulatory gap becomes a victim to sympathize with. The piece itself says it's too early to know whether Binance's security had major flaws — meaning we don't even know where the hole was, only whose account the loss landed in.

The $3 billion frozen is good news, but that's recovery, not protection. The unanswered question is the ordinary traders on smaller exchanges with no $3 billion to freeze and no promise to make them whole. The piece asks it once, then stops.
