选票被摆布时，没人问选民想要什么
When ballots get rigged, nobody asks what voters wanted.

阿拉斯加这场选举里真正值得看的不是谁赢，而是所有人都在谈"程序"，没有一个人在谈选民。

一个退休教师被招募来当 spoiler，同名同姓，把票从现任共和党参议员身上分走——这件事《纽约时报》报了，佩尔托拉否认，州长下令刑事调查，州最高法院让他留在选票上。ballot-stacking 通常不违法，两党都用过。所以这不是法律问题，是表达问题：选票上出现一个名字，这个人的表型、职业、姓氏，全都在替另一场博弈干活。他本人是谁，没人问。

苏利文在 CNN 上说对方"知道自己没法拿政绩竞选，所以只能作弊"。这话本身没错，但说这话的人所在的党，正在让司法部起诉阿拉斯加，要求如果现任输了就不许认证结果。同一个星期，一边要求对手接受程序，一边准备不认程序。共谋不分党派，只看回报。

佩尔托拉说"这是我们阿拉斯加，我们不玩这种把戏"。这句话很动人，也很空。她的竞选团队有没有参与招募，报道里她否认，纽约时报说她参与了，州长在查。在查清楚之前，"我们不玩把戏"和"她不适合当官"是同一种东西：都是叙事，不是事实。

真正被挤掉的是选民。这场选举的赌注是参议院多数，民主党需要翻四席，阿拉斯加是其中一席，Cook 政治报告评为 toss-up，FiftyPlusOne 的民调均值佩尔托拉略占优。在这个量级上，一个同名候选人能挪动的票数足以决定结果——而决定结果的方式，和任何一个阿拉斯加人走进投票站时脑子里想的事，没有关系。

选举是表达最集中的场合。你投的票是你对"这个社会该是什么样"的一次表态，而现在这张票被设计成了别人博弈里的一个变量。设计它的人不违法，质疑它的人也不违法，只有投票的人被当成了可以被搬运的筹码。

司法部那起诉讼的理由是"选民会混淆"。这个理由成立的前提，是选民足够蠢。一个为了保住席位而假设选民蠢的诉讼，和一场为了翻盘而假设选民蠢的招募，是同一枚硬币。硬币两面都印着同一个字：没人在乎你投的是什么，只在乎你投给了谁。

The thing worth watching in Alaska isn't who wins. It's that everyone is talking about procedure, and no one is talking about voters.

A retired schoolteacher gets recruited as a spoiler. Same name. Votes siphoned off the incumbent Republican. The New York Times reported it. Peltola denies it. The governor ordered a criminal investigation. The state Supreme Court kept him on the ballot. Ballot-stacking is typically not illegal, and both parties have used it. So this isn't a legal question. It's an expression question: a name appears on a ballot, and that person's phenotype, profession, surname all go to work for someone else's game. Who he actually is, nobody asks.

Sullivan told CNN the other side "recognizing they couldn't run on their record, so they had to cheat." Fine. But the same party is now letting Trump's DOJ sue Alaska to block certification if the incumbent loses. Same week. One side demands the other accept the process while preparing not to accept it. Complicity has no party. It only has a payoff.

Peltola says, "This is Alaska, we're not in the business of tricking each other up here." Moving, and empty. Whether her campaign was involved is disputed. The Times says yes. She says no. The governor is investigating. Until that's settled, "we don't play tricks" and "she's not fit for office" are the same substance: narrative, not fact.

What gets squeezed out is the voter. The stakes are Senate control. Democrats need four flips. Alaska is one. Cook rates it a toss-up. FiftyPlusOne has Peltola slightly ahead. At that margin, one same-name candidate can move enough votes to decide the outcome — and the way it decides has nothing to do with what any Alaskan is thinking when they walk into the booth.

Election is the most concentrated form of expression there is. Your vote is a statement about what this society should be. Now that statement has been designed into a variable in someone else's game. The designers broke no law. The challengers broke no law. Only the voter got turned into freight.

The DOJ lawsuit's rationale is "voter confusion." That rationale only works if voters are dumb. A lawsuit that assumes voters are dumb to save a seat, and a recruitment that assumes voters are dumb to flip one, are the same coin. Both faces read the same: nobody cares what you're voting for, only who you voted for.
