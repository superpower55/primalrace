换党不换暴力A New Party, the Same Machine

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要美国中期选举临近，亚太地区因美国与以色列对伊朗开战及特朗普的关税政策而承压。分析人士表示，即便民主党赢得众议院，也难以改变战争与关税政策，国会两党唯一共识是对中国技术实施更严限制。
The newsAsia-Pacific countries hit by the US-Israel war on Iran and Trump's tariffs are watching the US midterms. Analysts say a Democratic Congress would not change the war or tariff policy; the one bipartisan consensus is tougher restrictions on Chinese technology.
选举换的是人，不是那台机器
Elections change the people, not the machine.

亚太地区在等的不是一场选举结果，是一次撤军和一次降税。两样都等不到。这篇报道说得很清楚：伊朗战争打了八个月，菲律宾九成以上的原油进口来自海湾，三月起进入国家能源紧急状态；柬埔寨、老挝、缅甸、东帝汶的外汇储备被油价掏空。这些国家的民众反对这场战争，反对了八个月，没用。

暴力三角里，这里动的是结构层，而且是最硬的那种：一条海峡被关上，一个地区的穷人先倒下。决策发生在华盛顿和特拉维夫，代价落在金边和帝力。中间没有投票箱相连。加尔通说结构暴力的特点是「缓慢、弥散、被当成现状」——不，这次它很快，很具体，只是不在施暴者的选区里。

报道说民主党可能拿下众议院，也可能拿下参议院。然后呢？国会可以调查，可以开听证会，Okun 说甚至可能弹劾国防部长——「但这不改变战争的整体影响」。宣战权写在宪法里，特朗普说这是自卫，国会通过的撤军决议他直接不理；就算新国会立法切断军费，他否决，两院三分之二推翻否决，民主党几乎肯定凑不齐。这套设计从一开始就不是为了约束战争，是为了让战争看起来经过了同意。

关税同理。九十来个国家被加征 10% 到 50%，柬埔寨 49%，老挝 48%，越南 46%。最高法院二月推翻了，政府七月换个名目又来一遍：六十个贸易伙伴，10% 和 12.5%，理由是「强迫劳动」。换的是法律依据，不是那双手。Okun 那句话可以裱起来：「关税不变。战争不变。」

唯一两党一致、新国会无论谁赢都会通过的东西，是对中国的技术封锁：Remote Access Security Act、Chip Security Act、MATCH Act。也就是说，选举能改变的只有一件事——围堵中国的手会握得更紧。这不是例外，这是这台机器的正常输出。至于台湾那笔 140 亿美元的军售被特朗普压着不批，是为了保住和习近平的贸易休战。台湾在这套账本里是筹码，不是盟友。

报道里有一句话值得单独拎出来：亚太国家希望美国在南海、在台海有「更清晰、更明显的存在」。这就是共谋的位置——被关税打、被战争拖垮的地区，转头请求那个打它的国家多派点军舰来。安全焦虑被转化成对保护者的依赖，依赖再转化成下一轮被定价的资格。谁在这场共谋里得利，报道没写，但军售清单会写。

这篇报道里没有一个亚太普通人的名字。菲律宾的国家能源紧急状态是一个状态，不是一个人。谁在加油站前算钱、谁家的电价账单翻倍、哪个柬埔寨工人因为 49% 的关税被裁掉——这些都不在稿子里。新闻业的结构暴力就在这儿：战争和关税被写成「政策」，代价被写成「影响」，而承受代价的人连一个引语都分不到。

最后说一句报道自己承认的事：Olson 认为真正的转变要等到 2028 年总统选举。四年。这四年里海峡还关着，关税还挂着，那些外汇储备已经见底的国家不会等来一次投票。选举是给投票的人设计的，代价是给不投票的人付的。

What the Asia-Pacific is waiting for is not an election result. It is a withdrawal and a tariff cut. It will get neither. The report is clear: eight months of war on Iran, the Philippines drawing more than 90 percent of its crude imports from the Gulf and under a national energy emergency since March, the foreign exchange reserves of Cambodia, Laos, Myanmar and East Timor drained by fuel costs. Public opinion in the region has opposed this war for eight months. It changed nothing.

In the Violence Triangle, this is the structural layer at its hardest: a strait is closed, and the region's poorest fall first. The decision is made in Washington and Tel Aviv; the cost lands in Phnom Penh and Dili. No ballot box connects the two. Structural violence is supposed to be slow and diffuse, mistaken for the status quo. This one is fast, specific, and simply located outside the perpetrator's constituency.

The Democrats may take the House, maybe the Senate. Then what? Hearings, investigations, perhaps even impeachment of the defence secretary — which, as Okun says, does not change the overall impact of the war. Congress holds the power to declare war; Trump calls the strikes self-defence and ignores its resolutions. If a new Congress cuts funding, he vetoes; overriding takes two-thirds in both chambers, which the Democrats almost certainly won't have. The design was never meant to restrain war. It was meant to make war look consented to.

Tariffs follow the same logic. Ninety countries hit with 10 to 50 percent, Cambodia at 49, Laos at 48, Vietnam at 46. The Supreme Court struck them down in February; in July the administration returned with a new label — 60 trading partners, 10 and 12.5 percent, this time citing forced labour. The legal basis changed. The hand did not. Okun's line deserves framing: tariffs don't change, the war doesn't change.

The one thing both parties agree on, and will pass whatever the midterms bring, is tighter technology controls on China: the Remote Access Security Act, the Chip Security Act, the MATCH Act. The election can change exactly one thing — the grip on China gets firmer. That is not an exception. That is the machine working normally. And the $14bn arms package to Taiwan sits unapproved because Trump wants to keep his trade truce with Xi. In this ledger, Taiwan is a chip, not an ally.

One line deserves to be pulled out: Asia-Pacific countries want a clearer, more obvious US presence in the South China Sea and the Taiwan Strait. Here is the co-conspiracy — a region tariffed and bankrupted by a war turns around and asks the country that did it to send more warships. Security anxiety becomes dependence on a protector; dependence becomes the next round of being priced. Who profits is not in the report. The arms sales list will say.

Not one ordinary person in the region is named. The Philippines' energy emergency is a status, not a person. Who is counting money at the pump, whose electricity bill doubled, which Cambodian worker lost a job to a 49 percent tariff — none of it is here. That is journalism's own structural violence: war and tariffs written as policy, costs written as impact, and the people paying them not granted a single quote.

And the report admits the timeline itself: real change waits for 2028. Four years. The strait stays closed, the tariffs stay on, and the countries whose reserves are already gone will not be saved by a vote. Elections are designed for those who vote. The bill is sent to those who don't.
