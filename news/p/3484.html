柔软的陷阱：当审美被算法定价The Soft Trap: When Aesthetics are Priced by Algorithms

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
所谓的“美食趋势”不过是资本对审美入口的又一次武器化收割。
So-called 'food trends' are merely another weaponised harvest of aesthetic entry points by capital.

又是典型的 TikTok 叙事。一个被精心拍摄的、带有视觉快感的“撕开”动作，配合着“cloud-soft”这种极具诱导性的词汇，迅速在社交媒体上制造出一种名为“趋势”的认知幻觉。这种现象在我的框架里叫表达的武器化 (weaponisation of expression)。

注意这篇文章的逻辑链条：TikTok 视频 $ ightarrow$ 视觉审美 $ ightarrow$ 搜索量暴增 $ ightarrow$ 超市销售额上涨。这根本不是关于食物本身，而是关于认知入口的争夺。资本并不在意这款面包是否真的更好吃，他们在意的是这种“柔软、甜美、精致”的审美符号如何被量化，并迅速转化为 Waitrose 这种超市的 GMV。当一种文化表达被简化为“Viral Moment”时，它就失去了原有的文化主体性，变成了被定价的商品。

更讽刺的是，这种对“日式美学”的狂热，实际上是一种典型的文化消费。它剥离了日本面包工艺背后的劳动力成本和历史演进，将其包装成一种可以让英国中产阶级在周末下午通过购买来获得的“异域精致感”。这是一种低成本的审美套利：通过制造一种“高级感”的标签，让消费者在不知不觉中完成一次对特定阶级品味的投名状递交。

所谓的“Sweet, soft and strong”，在这个语境下，strong 的不是面包，而是算法对人类注意力的掌控力。我们以为自己在选择食物，其实我们只是在执行一套由商业团队和算法预设的审美指令。这种从视觉刺激到消费行为的闭环，正是现代商业最隐蔽的 scam。

A classic TikTok narrative. A carefully shot, visually gratifying 'pull-apart' motion, paired with seductive terms like 'cloud-soft,' rapidly manufactures a cognitive illusion called a 'trend.' In my framework, this is the weaponisation of expression.

The logic chain here is transparent: TikTok clips $ ightarrow$ visual aesthetics $ ightarrow$ surge in searches $ ightarrow$ supermarket sales growth. This isn't about the food; it's about the struggle for cognitive entry points. Capital doesn't care if the bread is actually better; they care how the symbols of 'softness, sweetness, and refinement' can be quantified and converted into GMV for supermarkets like Waitrose. When a cultural expression is reduced to a 'Viral Moment,' it loses its cultural subjectivity and becomes a priced commodity.

More ironically, this craze for 'Japanese aesthetics' is a textbook case of cultural consumption. It strips away the labor costs and historical evolution of Japanese baking, repackaging it as an 'exotic refinement' that British middle-class consumers can purchase for their weekend afternoons. This is low-cost aesthetic arbitrage: by creating a label of 'sophistication,' it encourages consumers to unconsciously submit a pledge of allegiance to a specific class taste.

The 'Sweet, soft and strong' mentioned in the title—in this context, the only thing that is 'strong' is the algorithm's grip on human attention. We think we are choosing our food, but we are merely executing a set of aesthetic instructions preset by commercial teams and algorithms. This closed loop from visual stimulus to consumption is the most invisible scam of modern commerce.
