偶像崇拜：一场关于欲望的廉价重启Idol Worship: A Cheap Reboot of Desire

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
将主体性的觉醒寄托于对他者的崇拜，是另一种形式的自我规训。
Anchoring the awakening of subjectivity in the worship of another is just another form of self-discipline.

这篇文章试图讲述一个关于“觉醒”和“重启”的温情故事，但剥开那层 iridescent golden light 的滤镜，这其实是一次典型的存在性战争溃败。作者在经历母亲身份和伴侣身份的剥离后，获得了短暂的 autonomy，但她无法在没有“客体”的情况下自我驱动欲望。她所谓的 spiritual reboot，本质上是将自己的 libido 再次锚定在一个男性偶像身上。

注意到那个细节：她回忆起 14 岁时追逐 Sting，认为那是进入异性世界的 gateway drug。而 58 岁时，她竟然通过重复这个 14 岁的模式来寻找性冲动。这不是“第二次青春期”，而是一种认知的退行。她没有通过挖掘自身的生命本体来找回欲望，而是再次掉入了第三章所说的“偶像化”陷阱——通过无限制美化一个遥远的男性主体，来激活自己被冻结的性欲。

最讽刺的是，这种欲望的回归被描述为“精神上的”且“来自灵魂深处”。事实上，这正是元暴力的潜移默化：女性被训练成必须通过“仰望”一个男性主体，才能确认自己的性感和存在感。她的 libido 不是被 Richard Ashcroft 唤醒的，而是被那种“被定义为崇拜者”的旧有叙事激活的。这是一种假.最优解表达，她通过扮演一个狂热粉丝，换取了短期内情绪上的亢奋，但她的主体性依然是缺失的——她的快乐依赖于一个在舞台上被包装出来的产品。

当她把这种冲动转化为在 dating apps 上寻找新伴侣时，这场博弈再次回到了原点：她需要一个男性来作为她生命能量的触发开关。这种将欲望的定价权让渡给他者的逻辑，正是文化暴力的最高级形态：让你以为你在追求自由，其实你只是在更换锁链。

This piece attempts to tell a heartwarming story of "awakening" and "rebooting," but once you strip away the filter of iridescent golden light, it is a classic defeat in an existential war. After stripping away the identities of motherhood and partnership, the author gained a brief autonomy, yet she found herself unable to drive her own desire without an "object."

Her so-called spiritual reboot is, in essence, re-anchoring her libido to a male idol. Note the detail: she recalls chasing Sting at 14 as a "gateway drug" to the opposite sex. At 58, she believes she is repeating this level of the game. This isn't a "second puberty"; it is a cognitive regression. She didn't recover her desire by excavating her own biological essence, but by falling back into the trap of idolization—activating her libido through the unlimited idealization of a distant male subject.

The irony is that this revival is described as "spiritual" and "from deep in the soul." In reality, this is meta-violence at work: women are trained to believe they must "look up" to a male subject to validate their own sexiness and existence. Her libido wasn't awakened by Richard Ashcroft, but by the old narrative of being a "worshiper." This is a fake optimal expression; by playing the role of a fan, she trades her subjectivity for a short-term emotional high.

When she translates this spark into scrolling through dating apps, the game returns to zero: she still requires a male to serve as the trigger switch for her life energy. This logic of surrendering the pricing power of desire to another is the most sophisticated form of cultural violence—making you believe you are pursuing freedom while you are merely swapping chains.
