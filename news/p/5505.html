十字架成了大棒，而信仰成了外套The Cross as a Club, Faith as a Coat

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-11 § 链接
当宗教被剥离实践，它就不再是信仰，而是武器化的身份标签。
When religion is stripped of practice, it ceases to be faith and becomes a weaponized identity marker.

这篇报告拆穿了一个典型的 scam：所谓的“基督教民族主义”根本不是基督教的复兴，而是一次精准的表达武器化。一个群体在口头上极度认同基督教，但在实际生活中却几乎不进教堂、不读圣经。这意味着，基督教在他们这里不是一种 embodied practice（具体实践），而是一件被套上的 identity coat（身份外套）。

这种现象是典型的文化层暴力（cultural violence）。这些人并不在乎耶稣教了什么，他们在乎的是“基督教”这个标签所代表的权力结构——一个白人中心、男性中心且排外的旧秩序。通过将宗教符号化，他们成功地将“捍卫基督教遗产”等同于“排斥移民和伊斯兰教”。十字架在游行队伍中被举起，不再是为了救赎，而是作为一种划分敌我的生物墙，用来标记谁才是“真正的英国人”。

有趣的是，真正实践信仰的人反而更包容、更多元。这证明了实践（Practice）具有消解元暴力（meta violence）的可能性：当你被迫与不选择的邻座共处时，你面对的是具体的人，而不是一个被妖魔化的标签。而民族主义者追求的是一种纯净的、垄断的解释权。他们不需要神，他们只需要一个能让他们在存在性战争中获得优越感的图腾。

最讽刺的共谋在于，这种叙事试图通过定义“文明”来掩盖其底层的恐惧与暴力。当他们高喊“基督为王”时，他们实际上是在呼唤一个能够合法化排外行为的旧时代。这再次证明，任何被武器化的叙事，其入口永远是制造焦虑，而终点则是夺取定义“事实”的权力。

This report exposes a classic scam: the so-called "Christian nationalism" is not a revival of Christianity, but a precise weaponisation of expression. A group claims extreme alignment with Christianity while rarely attending church or reading the Bible. This means Christianity, for them, is not an embodied practice, but an identity coat worn for strategic purposes.

This is a textbook case of cultural violence. These individuals do not care about the teachings of Jesus; they care about the power structure that the "Christian" label represents—a white-centric, masculine-centric, and exclusionary old order. By symbolising religion, they successfully equate "defending Christian heritage" with "hostility toward immigrants and Islam." The cross, carried in rallies, is no longer for redemption but serves as a biological wall to mark who the "true British" are.

Interestingly, those who actually practice their faith are more inclusive and diverse. This proves that practice has the potential to dissolve meta violence: when you are forced to coexist with people you didn't choose in a pew, you encounter actual humans, not demonised labels. Nationalists, however, seek a pure, monopolised right of interpretation. They don't need a God; they need a totem that grants them superiority in the existential war.

The most cynical complicity lies in how this narrative uses "civilisation" as a cover for underlying fear and violence. When they chant "Christ is King," they are actually summoning an old era that legitimises xenophobia. This confirms once again that any weaponised narrative always enters through the door of manufactured anxiety and ends with the seizure of the power to define "fact."
