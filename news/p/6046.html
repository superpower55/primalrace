一台折叠手机裂开的是谁的墙Whose Wall Cracked With the Foldable

科技 文化层 · 结构层 404 Media ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
苹果卖的不是手机，是圈层入场券，裂的是入场券。
Apple never sold phones. It sold admission tickets. Now the tickets are cracking.

这篇评论写得像个人恩怨，其实是一份表达定价权的账本。作者 2010 年在 Banana Republic 做楼层经理，同事逼他买 iPhone，理由是穿西装的销售员拿一台 Razr 不像话，店可以预支工资给他。他偏买了 HTC Dream。十几年后他写苹果进入 slop era，自己承认这怨恨 petty。我认为他诚实，而这份怨恨恰好是理解苹果的入口。

一台手机在这里从来不是通信工具。它是表达：你拿什么出现在销售楼层，等于宣告你属于哪个圈层。同事说的那句"我们想在这里营造一种形象"，翻译过来就是：你的表型要符合这个阵营的定价。这是第三章的审美入口，品味区分阶级，标签的贵贱由有话语权的人来定。苹果当年的本事，是把一块 $19 的抛光布、$200 的 VESA 支架、$700 的 Mac Pro 轮子都卖成了身份符号，定价权握在手里，连一块布都能收税。

现在这个定价权在松。iPhone Duo 上市几天，人们只谈裂缝；Ternus 对 Time 说别家的折叠机"又廉价又垃圾"，而本周拿着裂屏 Duo 的人在学什么叫不廉价也不垃圾。Siri AI 连 AI 拥护者都说糟。$300 的 HomeView 是拿 Apple Watch 冒充家庭壁炉。触屏笔记本是乔布斯测过说"不行"、库克比作烤面包机拼冰箱的东西。这些不是产品失败清单，是一个曾经定义"什么是酷"的公司，现在开始抄别人的作业。抄作业的人不再定价，只跟价。

有意思的是，作者并不为此欢呼。他几年前买了 MacBook Air，还在考虑换 iPhone，因为隐私功能更硬。Windows 笔记本是垃圾，时间治愈一切伤口。这就是 slop era 真正的结构：苹果的产品力在往下走，可替代它的东西没跟上，于是圈层入场券贬值了，人还是得买。定价权松动不等于权力转移，只等于收税的人变穷了一点，交税的人没有变自由。

裂缝新闻最容易写成消费者维权，我不打算这么写。真正该问的是：当年那个在达拉斯拿 Razr 被同事围猎的人，和今天拿着裂屏 Duo 的人，是不是同一套机制里的两端？一端是没买的人被规训，一端是买了的人被交付一件裂开的东西，还被 CEO 在杂志封面上举着。中间那层共谋没有消失，只是从"你必须买"变成了"你没得选"。

Ternus 说别家折叠机廉价又垃圾，这句话本身才是这条新闻里最诚实的一句：他仍然相信自己在定价。而裂缝在替他回答。

This piece reads like a personal grudge. It is actually a ledger of who gets to price expression. In 2010 the author was a floor manager at a Banana Republic in Dallas. Coworkers pressured him to buy an iPhone because a suited salesperson holding a Razr was off brand; the store offered a paycheck advance to make it happen. He bought an HTC Dream instead. More than a decade later he writes that Apple is in its slop era, and admits the grudge is petty. I think the honesty is the point: that grudge is the entry door to understanding Apple.

A phone here was never a communication tool. It was expression: what you carry onto the sales floor announces which camp you belong to. The coworker's line about projecting a certain image translates to: your phenotype must match this camp's price tag. That is the taste entrance from chapter three, where taste sorts class and the powerful set the labels' worth. Apple's old genius was turning a $19 polishing cloth, a $200 VESA mount, and $700 Mac Pro wheels into status symbols. It held the pricing power, and it taxed even a cloth.

That power is loosening. Days after launch, all anyone says about the iPhone Duo is cracks; Ternus tells Time that other foldables felt cheap and crappy, while people holding cracked Duos this week learn what cheap and crappy actually looks like. AI boosters agree Siri AI is awful. The $300 HomeView is an Apple Watch posing as a family hearth. The touch-screen laptop is the idea Jobs tested and rejected, the one Cook compared to a toaster fused with a refrigerator. This is not a product failure list. It is a company that once defined what was cool now copying homework. The copier no longer sets the price; it follows it.

The author does not cheer. He bought a MacBook Air a few years ago and is considering an iPhone for its privacy features. Windows laptops are garbage; time heals all wounds. That is the real structure of the slop era: Apple's product power declines, nothing replaces it, so the admission ticket depreciates and people still buy. A loosening grip on pricing power is not a transfer of power. The tax collector got poorer; the taxpayer did not get freer.

Crack news is easy to write as consumer complaint. The real question: is the man hunted by coworkers for his Razr in Dallas the same mechanism as the person holding a cracked Duo today? One end is the non-buyer disciplined into buying; the other is the buyer handed a cracked object, held up on a magazine cover by the CEO. The co-conspiracy in between never vanished. It shifted from you must buy to you have no choice.

Ternus saying rival foldables are cheap and crappy is the most honest line in this story: he still believes he is setting the price. The cracks are answering for him.
