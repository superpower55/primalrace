被掩盖在“组织犯罪”叙事下的肉体清除Bodies as Collateral in the Narrative of 'Organised Crime'

国际 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
当暴力被定义为“犯罪组织”的博弈，具体的受害者就成了统计学上的抵押品。
When violence is framed as a game of 'criminal organisations', specific victims become mere statistical collateral.

这是一场典型的 Direct Violence 升级。纵火、谋杀、导致重伤，这些血淋淋的物理事实被迅速地包裹进一个名为“有组织犯罪 (organised crime)”的叙事入口中。在警察和官员的口径里，这不再是简单的个体之死，而是一场关于“打击犯罪”的宏大战争。在这种叙事下，死者的个体性被抹除，他们成了某种地下权力博弈的 collateral damage。

注意这种叙事的联动：上周 A66 公路上的惨剧杀死了两名警察，本周则是住宅纵火。警方的回应是“继续瓦解 (disrupt)”犯罪组织。这里形成了一个危险的共谋场域——国家暴力机器通过将个案定义为“组织犯罪”，成功地将一次次随机或结构性的暴力升级为一种“必要的战争”。当暴力被标签化为“黑帮”或“犯罪团伙”时，公众的注意力会被引导至对秩序的渴望，而非对暴力本身机制的追问。

最令人不安的是这种叙事的 Meta Violence。在男性主导的治安叙事中，暴力被简化为“强者”与“强者”之间的碰撞（警察 vs 犯罪组织），而那些在火场中死去的、在碰撞中被碾碎的具体肉体，成了这场男性权力游戏中的背景板。谁在共谋？通过将暴力“组织化”来合理化强力执法、掩盖治安失效的体制，以及在媒体上复述这套逻辑的叙事者，他们共同完成了对暴力的重新定价：只要是为了“保护社区”，个体的死亡就可以被解释为某种必然的代价。

This is a textbook escalation of Direct Violence. Arson, murder, and critical injuries are rapidly wrapped in a narrative entry point called 'organised crime'. In the lexicon of the police and officials, these are no longer individual deaths, but a grand war of 'fighting crime'. Under this narrative, the individuality of the deceased is erased; they become collateral damage in a game of underground power dynamics.

Notice the linkage of this narrative: the tragedy on the A66 last week killed two officers, and this week is a house fire. The police response is to 'continue to disrupt' criminal organisations. A complicity field is formed here—the state violence apparatus, by defining isolated incidents as 'organised crime', successfully upgrades random or structural violence into a 'necessary war'. Once violence is labeled as 'gangs' or 'syndicates', public attention is diverted toward a longing for order, rather than questioning the mechanism of violence itself.

Most disturbing is the Meta Violence inherent in this. In a masculine-centric policing narrative, violence is simplified into a clash between 'strongmen' (police vs. criminal organisations), while the specific bodies burned in fires or crushed in collisions become mere backdrops for this masculine power game. Who are the complicitors? The system that justifies heavy-handed enforcement by 'organising' violence to mask systemic failure, and the narrators repeating this logic in the media. Together, they reprice violence: as long as it is for 'protecting the community', individual death is explained away as an inevitable cost.
