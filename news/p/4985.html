用 DEI 抹黑自行车道：一场关于“阳刚之气”的认知战争Labeling Bike Lanes as 'DEI': A Cognitive War Over Masculinity

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-13 § 链接
将基础设施定义为 DEI，是试图通过操纵叙事将“生存权”降格为“特权”的武器化表达。
Defining infrastructure as DEI is a weaponized expression designed to downgrade 'right to survive' into 'special privilege'.

特朗普政府把自行车道称为“DEI”（多样性、公平与包容），这简直是一次教科书级的认知入口劫持。在逻辑上，自行车道与 DEI 毫无关系，但在文化层面上，这是一次极其精准的 weaponization。通过给公共基础设施贴上“进步派”的标签，他们成功地将一个关于城市规划、碳排放和生命安全的问题，转化成了一场关于文化认同的阵营战争。

这背后的元暴力 (meta violence) 极其清晰：在男性中心叙事中，汽车不仅仅是交通工具，它是 machismo（阳刚之气）的物理延伸，是速度、力量与掌控权的象征。骑自行车被定义为“非男性的”、“弱势的”或“精英阶层的表演”，因此，为自行车提供专用道，在 Trumpists 的逻辑里，就是对“真男人”掌控权的掠夺，是对汽车霸权的一种 structural violence。

这种操作最阴险的地方在于，它试图通过重新定义现实来制造一种假象：将那些为了生存而骑车的人（因为买不起车或为了健康）定义为在享受某种“不公正的特权”。当基础设施被定义为 DEI，那么拆除它们就不再是剥夺公民的出行权，而变成了“拨乱反正”的正义之举。这正是典型的通过夺取解释权来夺取事实制造权。

谁在共谋？那些在空调车厢里抱怨自行车道造成拥堵的司机，在潜意识中参与了这场共谋。他们通过维护汽车的绝对优先权，获得了某种虚假的阶级/性别优越感，而代价则是将那些暴露在汽车威胁下的骑行者推向更直接的 direct violence。这场博弈的本质不是交通规划，而是一场关于谁才拥有街道定义权的存在性战争。

The Trump administration calling bike lanes 'DEI' is a textbook hijacking of the cognitive entry point. Logically, bike lanes have nothing to do with Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion; however, on a cultural level, this is a precise weaponization. By slapping a 'progressive' label on public infrastructure, they successfully transform an issue of urban planning, carbon emissions, and safety into a tribal war of cultural identity.

The meta violence here is glaring: in a masculine-centric narrative, the car is not just a vehicle—it is a physical extension of machismo, symbolizing speed, power, and control. Cycling is framed as 'non-masculine,' 'weak,' or 'elite performance.' Therefore, providing dedicated lanes is seen by Trumpists as a plunder of the 'real man's' dominance and a form of structural violence against car hegemony.

The most insidious part of this operation is the attempt to manufacture a new reality: defining those who bike for survival (due to poverty or health) as beneficiaries of an 'unjust privilege.' Once infrastructure is defined as DEI, dismantling it is no longer the deprivation of a citizen's right to mobility, but an act of 'restoring order.' This is exactly how seizing the power of interpretation leads to the power of manufacturing facts.

Who are the complicit ones? The drivers complaining about bike-lane congestion from their air-conditioned cabins are participating in this complicity subconsciously. By upholding the absolute priority of the car, they gain a illusory sense of class or gender superiority, while the cost is pushing cyclists into more direct violence. This game is not about traffic; it is an existential war over who defines the street.
