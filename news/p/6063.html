防御条约保护的是谁Who Does the Defence Pact Protect?

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
枪口一致对外，代价永远落在不在场的人身上
Guns point outward; the cost always lands on those not in the room.

叙利亚外长在麦纳麦说，大马士革正在"评估"加入麦加联合防御协议。翻译一下：沙特、土耳其、巴基斯坦三个国家签了条约，一国被打等于三国被打，现在叙利亚想挤进这张保护网。这个网的名字叫"防御"，但它真正分配的东西不是安全，是站队。

沙特在也门被胡塞武装打得很难受，巴基斯坦和土耳其承诺提供军事支持。但土耳其官员已经提前打了补丁：未来对利雅得的支持不包括派土耳其士兵去也门作战，也不包括攻击胡塞武装。这就是防御条约的真相——签的时候是三国一体，执行的时候各自算账。承诺是集体的，代价是分摊的，但分摊的规则由出枪的人来定。

阿萨德2024年12月倒台后上台的沙拉政府，靠沙特这个关键金主撑着。现在要加入一个由金主主导的防御协议，这不是选择，是交保护费。外长说叙利亚是地区安全的"组成部分"，会支持任何保护地区稳定的框架——"组成部分"这个词很妙，它把叙利亚从一个需要被保护的对象，变成了一个可以被调用的资源。

同一场讲话里，这位外长说以色列不到两年对叙利亚发动了一千多次袭击，杀死数十人。然后他问：叙利亚主权每天都在被侵犯，边界控制和武器收归国家所有的呼吁凭什么成立？这个问题问得对。但紧接着他表态支持也门沙特支持的政府恢复"全境主权与稳定"。主权这个词，用在自己身上是控诉，用在别人身上是站队。

也门人在这条新闻里没有名字。胡塞武装有名字，沙特有名字，土耳其巴基斯坦叙利亚以色列都有名字，被轰炸的、被封锁的、被"恢复主权"的那群人，只以"全境"的形式出现。防御条约的语法里，永远有施暴者和签约者，没有承受者。

加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual。麦加协议声称要缩小这个差额，方式是让三个国家的军队互相背书。但它缩小的不是任何一个人实际承受的暴力，它缩小的是签约国政府的风险敞口。差额被转移了，从利雅得的账本转移到也门的村庄，从大马士革的谈判桌转移到戈兰高地附近每天被炸的那片土地。

叙利亚加入这个协议，最可能的结果是：它获得了对抗以色列的纸面威慑，同时把自己绑进了沙特对胡塞的战争逻辑里。而土耳其已经说了，它的士兵不会去也门。那么谁去？答案不在报道里，报道只写了谁在谈。

Syria's foreign minister told the Manama Dialogue that Damascus is "evaluating" entry into the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement. Translation: Saudi Arabia, Turkiye and Pakistan signed a pact in August saying an attack on one is an attack on all three, and now Syria wants in on the umbrella. The umbrella is called defence, but what it actually distributes isn't security. It's alignment.

Saudi Arabia is taking heavy fire from the Houthis, and Pakistan and Turkiye have pledged military support to Riyadh. But Turkish officials have already attached the caveat: future support won't include deploying Turkish troops to fight in Yemen or attack the Houthis. That's the truth of a defence pact — signed as one body of three, executed as three separate account books. The commitment is collective, the cost is shared, but the people who send the guns decide how it's shared.

Syria's government, led by Ahmed al-Sharaa after Assad fell in December 2024, is backed heavily by Saudi Arabia. Now it wants to join a defence agreement led by its own patron. That isn't a choice. It's protection money. The foreign minister said Damascus is an "integral" part of regional security and would support any framework aimed at protecting regional stability. "Integral" is doing a lot of work there — it turns Syria from something that needs protecting into a resource that can be called upon.

In the same speech, he said Israel has carried out more than 1,000 attacks on Syria in under two years, killing dozens. Then he asked how calls for border control and placing weapons exclusively under state control can be justified while Syrian sovereignty is violated daily. Fair question. But he also voiced support for Yemen's Saudi-backed government restoring "sovereignty and stability across its entire territory." Sovereignty, pointed at yourself, is an accusation. Pointed at someone else, it's a side.

The Yemenis don't have names in this story. The Houthis have a name. Saudi Arabia, Turkiye, Pakistan, Syria, Israel all have names. The bombed, the blockaded, the ones whose "entire territory" is being restored — they appear only as a territory. The grammar of a defence pact always has aggressors and signatories. It never has the people on the receiving end.

Galtung's formula: Violence = Potential − Actual. The Mecca pact claims to shrink that gap by having three armies vouch for each other. But it doesn't shrink the violence any actual person endures. It shrinks the risk exposure of the governments that sign. The gap gets transferred — from Riyadh's ledger to Yemen's villages, from Damascus's negotiating table to the ground near the Golan that gets hit every day.

The likeliest outcome for Syria: paper deterrence against Israel, and a binding tie to Saudi Arabia's war logic against the Houthis. Turkiye has already said its soldiers won't go to Yemen. So who goes? The report doesn't say. It only says who's talking.
