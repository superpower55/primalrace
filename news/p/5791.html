把厕所变成外交辞令Toilets as Diplomatic Language

国际 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
用马桶回应侮辱，是公关，不是权力翻转
Answering an insult with toilets is PR, not a transfer of power.

特朗普把挪威叫作“shithouse country”，Visit Norway 的回应是把国家的厕所照片摆出来，问“这是不是世界上最好的厕所”。这场交锋里，谁都没有损失什么，谁都没有让出什么。

特朗普的动机写得很清楚：没拿到诺贝尔和平奖，于是“我不会忘记，挪威”。奖是挪威诺贝尔委员会发的，委员会独立于挪威政府和议会——挪威外长 Espen Barth Eide 自己出来强调过这一点，还说“我们知道他很清楚这一点”。也就是说，特朗普骂的是一个没有颁奖权的国家，为的是一个他没拿到的奖。这不是外交，是一个人在台上发脾气，而发脾气本身是他最熟练的表达。

Visit Norway 的回应被挪威学者 Jens Kjeldsen 概括为“幼儿园里的霸凌者，你太当真，霸凌者就赢了”。这句话说得准，但它同时暴露了这套应对的全部边界：它的目标不是让霸凌者停止，而是让围观者觉得被霸凌的一方体面。体面是文化层的胜利，也只停在文化层。特朗普那句“shithouse country”之前用在海地、利比亚、索马里、也门上，那些国家没有设计奖厕所可以拿出来拍照。

这里值得看清楚的是，这套“用幽默接住侮辱”的操作，成本极低，回报也极低。Visit Norway 的高级顾问 Evgenia Egorova 说“世界需要一点笑声”，还说社交媒体的反响“巨大”。反响巨大是真的——但反响的对象是挪威的厕所，不是特朗普的言论。一个美国总统在集会上按心情给国家排等级，这条规则本身没有被触碰一下。

真正被这次回应绕过去的东西，是诺贝尔奖归属的错位。特朗普把怒火对准挪威，挪威用厕所接住，两边默契地不谈那个真正的结构：一个自认为该得和平奖的人，把奖项当成可以被“记住”和“报复”的个人财产。奖是发给 Navi Pillay 的，特朗普说“我从没听过这个人”，还说和平奖“已被永久玷污”。这句话才是这次事件里唯一有实质内容的表达——它宣告的是，解释谁配得上和平，应该由他说了算。

Visit Norway 最后说还有更多厕所要上线，包括一个用六十多棵树干木材做的森林风格厕所，结尾是“Toodeloo!”。一个国家的旅游机构被骂了之后，能拿出的最好的东西是一座座得过奖的公共厕所，这件事本身挺好笑的。好笑的地方在于，它确实就是挪威能拿出来的最好的东西之一——而对方要的从来不是厕所。

特朗普说“我们不会忘记，挪威”。Visit Norway 说“准备好你一生中最棒的一次中途停留”。两句话都很响，两句话都没有指向任何会被改变的东西。下一次他再骂一个国家，那个国家如果拿不出设计奖厕所，就只剩下沉默这一个选项——而沉默不在 Visit Norway 的文案里。

Trump called Norway a “shithouse country.” Visit Norway answered by posting photographs of the nation’s lavatories and asking whether these are the world’s best shit houses. Nobody lost anything in this exchange. Nobody conceded anything either.

The motive is right there in the report: he did not get the Nobel peace prize, so “we will not forget, Norway.” The prize is awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee, which operates independently of the Norwegian state — the foreign minister, Espen Barth Eide, made a point of saying so, adding that Trump “knows that very well.” So Trump insulted a country with no say over the award, over a prize he did not receive. This is not diplomacy. It is a man throwing a tantrum on stage, and the tantrum is his most practiced form of expression.

Visit Norway’s response was summed up by the University of Bergen rhetoric professor Jens Kjeldsen: it is like the bully in kindergarten, and if you get too serious, the bully wins. That is accurate, and it also marks the exact limit of the move. The goal was never to make the bully stop. The goal was to make the onlookers think the bullied party behaved well. Dignity is a cultural-layer win, and it stops at the cultural layer. Trump had used “shithouse country” before for Haiti, Libya, Somalia and Yemen. None of them had award-winning toilets to photograph.

What deserves attention is how cheap this maneuver is, and how cheap the payoff. Evgenia Egorova, a senior consultant at Visit Norway, said the world needs a little laugh, and that the response on social media had been immense. Immense it was — but the object of the response was Norwegian toilets, not Trump’s statement. A US president ranking countries by mood at a rally: that rule was not touched.

What the response quietly stepped around is the actual displacement at the center of this. Trump aimed his fury at Norway; Norway caught it with toilets; both sides agreed not to discuss the structure underneath: a man who believes a peace prize is personal property that can be “remembered” and avenged. The prize went to Navi Pillay. Trump said he had never heard of her and that the peace prize “has been forever discredited.” That sentence is the only substantive expression in the whole affair. It announces that the power to decide who deserves peace should belong to him.

Visit Norway closed by promising more toilets, including a forest-inspired latrine built from the dried wood of more than 60 trees, and signed off with “Toodeloo!” A national tourist board, having been insulted, reached for its best available asset: a series of prize-winning public restrooms. That is funny. It is funny because those restrooms really are among the best things Norway has to offer — and restrooms were never what the other side wanted.

“We will not forget, Norway,” said Trump. “Get ready for the pit stop of your life,” said Visit Norway. Both lines land hard. Neither points at anything that will change. The next time he insults a country, that country, if it has no design-award toilets to show, will be left with silence — and silence is not in Visit Norway’s copy.
