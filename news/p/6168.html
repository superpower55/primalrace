用强奸受害者的母亲打强奸受害者A Rapist's Defender, Hired to Call the Victim a Liar

性别 文化层 · 直接层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
保护儿童是口号，消费儿童是生意
Protecting children is the slogan; consuming one is the business.

俄亥俄州州长竞选里，Ramaswamy 的团队把 Amy Acton 十二岁时被继父性侵的旧案翻出来，让她的母亲 Donna Arthur 在广告里说“她一辈子都在撒谎”。Acton 说她 1978 年之后就没见过这个母亲，而那一年俄亥俄州大陪审团正好以“与不满十三岁的 Acton 发生性接触”起诉了她的继父 Cledith Arthur；他带着 Acton 的母亲逃出俄亥俄，一直躲到 2004 年在宾夕法尼亚去世。Acton 的哥哥 Philip Stearns 的声明里写得很清楚：母亲和那个被控强奸她女儿的男人一直保持婚姻，直到他死。

这条广告的机制不复杂：它不需要证明 Acton 撒谎，只需要让“她可能撒谎”这句话和她被强奸的事实并排放着。母亲的身份就是那个并置装置——一个母亲说女儿撒谎，观众自动相信母亲。至于这个母亲当年站在谁那边，广告一个字不提。这是第三章的老手艺：夺取认知入口，不需要制造事实，只需要制造一个足够响的怀疑，剩下的交给观众自己完成。

记者问 Ramaswamy“你觉得 Acton 医生在强奸这件事上撒谎了吗”，他答“我们的广告说的是她对我撒谎”。这不是回避，这是精确的躲闪。他不能承认自己在指控一个强奸受害者编造强奸，也不能否认广告在干这件事，于是他把问题换成了另一个问题。竞选团队把他拉走之前，他一个字都没有回答。

更值得看的是同一场活动上二十分钟前的事。州总检察长候选人 Keith Faber 在台上说共和党通过了全国最严的法律，“谁猥亵我们的孩子，就终身监禁不得假释”，Ramaswamy 在台下鼓掌。同一场活动，同一批人，一边宣布自己是保护儿童免受性侵的政党，一边把儿童性侵的案卷做成竞选物料。这不矛盾，这是同一套东西的两面：儿童的身体在台上是政绩，在台下是弹药，取决于哪一样更好用。

报道里唯一完整说出代价的人叫 Susan，来自 Xenia，以前投共和党，这次投不下去了。她对着记者哭，说她家里人在执法部门工作，“把那个女人请上台——太恶心了”。一个普通选民比候选人更清楚这条广告在做什么。而 Acton 本人在这篇报道里没有声音，她的创伤由母亲代言、由哥哥辩护、由对手定价。

Faber 那句“终身监禁不得假释”说得越响，这条广告就越安全——因为保护儿童的口号喊得足够大声，就没人会去看是谁在拿被侵害的儿童换选票。差额在这里：一个孩子本可以不被打扰地长大，现在她的案卷成了州长竞选的一个素材。这个差额不会因为选举结束而补回来。

In Ohio's governor race, Ramaswamy's campaign dug up the case of Amy Acton being raped at twelve by her stepfather and put her mother, Donna Arthur, in an ad saying Acton has lied her entire life. Acton says she hasn't seen her mother since 1978 — the same year an Ohio grand jury indicted her stepfather, Cledith Arthur, for sexual contact with Acton when she was less than thirteen. He fled Ohio with Acton's mother and evaded warrants until he died in Pennsylvania in 2004. Acton's brother, Philip Stearns, said it plainly in his statement: their birth mother stayed married to the man accused of raping her daughter until he died.

The mechanism isn't complicated. The ad doesn't need to prove Acton lied; it only needs to place the sentence she might have lied next to the fact that she was raped. The mother is the device — a mother saying her daughter lies, and the audience believes the mother automatically. What the mother was standing next to back then goes unmentioned. This is the old craft: seize the cognitive entry point, don't manufacture a fact, manufacture a loud enough doubt, and let the audience finish the job.

Asked whether he thinks Dr. Acton lied about being raped, Ramaswamy answered that the ad was about her lying about him. That's not evasion, that's precision. He can't admit he's accusing a rape victim of inventing a rape, and he can't deny the ad is doing exactly that, so he swaps in a different question. Campaign staff pulled him away before he answered a single word of the one he was asked.

Look at what happened twenty minutes earlier in the same room. State auditor Keith Faber, the GOP nominee for attorney general, told the crowd Republicans passed the toughest laws in the country, that anyone who molests a child gets life without parole. Ramaswamy applauded. Same event, same people: one minute announcing themselves as the party that protects children from sexual abuse, the next turning a child's sexual abuse case file into campaign material. Not a contradiction — two faces of one thing. A child's body is an achievement on stage and ammunition off it, whichever is more useful that day.

The only person in this story who names the cost is Susan from Xenia, a former Republican voter who now can't support Ramaswamy. She cried telling the reporter her family works in law enforcement, and that giving that woman a platform is disgusting. An ordinary voter understands the ad better than the candidate does. Acton herself has no voice in this report — her trauma is spoken for by her mother, defended by her brother, priced by her opponent.

The louder Faber's life-without-parole line, the safer the ad gets. Shout protection loud enough and nobody looks at who's trading a molested child for votes. Here's the gap: a child could have been left alone to grow up, and instead her case file became a prop in a governor's race. That gap doesn't close when the election does.
