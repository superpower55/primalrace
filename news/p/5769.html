妻子出庭作证，成了最完美的辩护A Wife's Testimony Is the Perfect Defense

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
她证明的不是他无罪，是他擅长经营关系。
She proved he's good at relationships, not that he's innocent.

劳拉·布兰德在法庭上说他是个「了不起的、体贴的丈夫」。这句话是真的。问题在于，它和六名女性提出的七项指控之间，本来就不构成任何关系——一个男人可以在婚姻里极其细心，同时对婚姻外的女人施暴。辩方把这句话搬上证人席，靠的就是让陪审团相信这两件事不能同时成立。

这套操作在第四章里有个名字：共谋。共谋不需要撒谎，只需要选择性地提供真实。劳拉说的是她经历过的布兰德：2006 年在爱丁堡，他夸她朋友漂亮，转头问她「告诉我你没有男朋友」；他们交往后他在性上「完全」尊重她的节奏，她说自己「被完全照顾着」。这些都是她真实的经验。但同一段叙述里她自己交代了另一面：2007 年 2 月他们因为他出轨而分手，她说自己「心碎了」。她认识他的时候就知道他「喜欢女人」，那年下半年就知道他在和别的女人来往。一个在关系里被善待的女人，和一个在关系外被侵犯的女人，可以同时存在，而辩方要的正是让前者的证词盖住后者。

法官和检察官都拦了一次。辩方律师问劳拉，她怎么看那些刑事指控「是不是真的」，法官直接说「她不是作为专家证人出庭的」。这句拦截很关键：法庭知道她的证词只能证明他的为人，不能证明事实。但「为人」恰恰是这类案件的战场。七项指控横跨 1999 年起的十年，涉及六名女性，全部否认——当直接证据难以还原二十多年前的现场，陪审团对「这个人是什么样的人」的印象就成了隐性证据。检方追问她旁听了大部分庭审、还做了笔记，问的也是同一件事：她的证词是在什么位置上生产出来的。

还有一层。两名前女友的陈述被读进法庭，说他为人不错，同时说他出轨、她们还资助过他。辩方把这些当品格证据用，可这些陈述自己就带着裂缝：一个被出轨、被经济上占便宜的女人，仍然愿意说他好——这不是他品格的证明，这是他被训练出来的表达方式在起作用。他能让身边的女人在受损之后依然替他说话，这项能力在法庭上比任何辩词都值钱。

劳拉说的「我们的关系一直很诚实」和检方拿出的 2017 年采访是同一件事的两面。那年他们刚有第一个孩子，他在美国脱口秀上说，他们交往时「我以为世界是一个由高潮做成的游乐园」。她回答：「我不否认他说过，我大概没看。」她没看，但她坐在证人席上替他作证。

庭审还在继续。这里被削掉的不是任何一层暴力，是一个男人在婚姻里的体贴被拿来当作他不可能强奸的证据——而这项证据，是一个女人用自己的心碎换来的。

Laura Brand told the court her husband is an "amazingly attentive husband." That may well be true. The problem is that it has nothing to do with the seven offences six women have alleged against him over ten years. A man can be exquisitely attentive inside a marriage and violent outside it. The defence put that sentence in the witness box precisely to make the jury believe the two cannot coexist.

Chapter Four has a name for this: complicity. It doesn't require lying, only selectively offering the truth. What Laura described is the Brand she lived with: at Edinburgh in 2006 he complimented her friend, then asked her to tell him she had no boyfriend; once they were together he "absolutely" respected her sexual pace, and she felt "entirely cared for." That is her real experience. But the same account carries the other side: they split in February 2007 over his infidelity, and she was "heartbroken." She knew when she met him that he "liked women," and by late that year knew he was seeing others. A woman treated well inside a relationship and a woman violated outside it can exist at once — and the defence needs the first to cover the second.

The judge and the prosecutor each blocked a question. When the defence asked Laura what she thought about "the truth" of the criminal allegations, the judge cut in: "She's not here as an expert witness." That interruption matters. The court knows her testimony can speak to character, not to fact. But character is exactly the battlefield here. Seven charges spanning a decade from 1999, six women, all denied — when direct evidence cannot reconstruct a night twenty years ago, the jury's impression of what kind of man he is becomes implicit evidence. The prosecutor's questions about Laura sitting through most of the trial and taking notes were about the same thing: from what position was this testimony produced.

There is another layer. Statements from two ex-girlfriends were read into court: they spoke well of him, and also said he cheated on them and that they supported him financially. The defence offers these as character evidence, but they arrive cracked. A woman who was cheated on and financially drained, still willing to say he's decent — that is not proof of his character, it is his trained mode of expression at work. His ability to keep women speaking for him after they have paid for it is worth more in a courtroom than any argument.

Laura's claim that their relationship "has always been honest" sits beside the 2017 interview the prosecutor produced. Shortly after their first child, he said on a US talkshow that when they dated, "I was in a phase where I thought the world was an amusement park made out of orgasms." She replied: "I'm not denying he said that. I probably didn't watch it." She didn't watch, and she is on the stand for him.

The trial continues. What is being chipped away here is not violence at any layer — it is the use of a man's tenderness inside a marriage as proof he could not have raped, a proof a woman paid for with her own heartbreak.
