涨价的账单，永远往下传The Bill Always Travels Downhill

其他 结构层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
成本从不消失，只换一个人扛。
Costs never vanish. They just find someone else to carry them.

O'Leary 把话说得很清楚：油价翻倍，票价涨 10%，也许 20%。但同一篇报道里还有一句更值得看的——瑞安不指望涨价能完全覆盖那 20 亿欧元的燃料涨幅，所以启动成本审查：削减机组配给、可能临时冻结工资、用 AI 提效。

翻译一下：涨价是给乘客的，降本是给机组和地勤的。两头都动，中间那 20 亿的缺口才算勉强填上。填不上的部分，落在谁身上，报道没写。

这就是结构暴力的标准运作方式。它不是某个人作恶，是成本在账本上找一个最没有还手之力的位置停下。乘客有选择，可以不飞；股东有选择，可以卖股票；机组没有——工资冻结就是冻结，配给削减就是配给削减，发生在三万英尺的高空，发生在凌晨四点的排班表上。

O'Leary 说“我们都在面对一个巨大的成本悬崖”。这个“我们”是修辞。悬崖边上站的人不一样高，掉下去的顺序也不一样。

真正被定价的从来不是机票，是扛成本的能力。谁扛得住，谁就被留在原地；谁扛不住，谁就变成那条被削减的“配给”。

至于 AI 提效——在成本审查的语境里，它只有一个意思：又一部分人的劳动被重新估值了。技术不是中立的，它总是站在账本那一边。

下一次你看到 20% 的涨幅，别只问“为什么这么贵”。问一句：这 20 亿的缺口，最后是谁在填。

O'Leary is clear: fuel doubled, fares up 10%, maybe 20%. But the same report has a line worth more attention — Ryanair doesn't expect the increase to fully cover the 2-billion-euro fuel hit, so it's launching a cost review: lower crew rations, a possible temporary pay freeze, AI for efficiency.

Translation: the fare hike goes to passengers, the cost cut goes to crew and ground staff. Both ends move so the 2-billion gap can be barely closed. What can't be closed, the report doesn't say who carries it.

This is structural violence operating exactly as designed. Not a villain, just a cost finding the spot on the ledger with the least capacity to push back. Passengers can choose not to fly. Shareholders can sell. Crew cannot — a pay freeze is a pay freeze, a ration cut is a ration cut, at 30,000 feet, on a 4 a.m. roster.

O'Leary says "we're all facing a cost cliff." That "we" is rhetoric. People stand at different heights on the cliff edge, and they fall in a different order.

What's being priced was never the ticket. It's the capacity to absorb cost. Whoever can absorb it stays; whoever can't becomes the "ration" that gets cut.

And AI for efficiency — in a cost review, that means one thing: another chunk of labor gets revalued. Technology isn't neutral. It stands on the ledger's side.

Next time you see a 20% increase, don't just ask why it's so expensive. Ask who ends up filling the 2-billion gap.
