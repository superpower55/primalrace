用罐头掩盖的结构性弃权Canned Food as a Mask for Structural Abandonment

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
让公民囤粮是政府将结构性失能转化为个体生存焦虑的PR手段。
Telling citizens to stock up is a PR move to shift structural failure into individual anxiety.

英国政府这次的“韧性”计划（resilience campaign）本质上是一场典型的表达武器化：它通过制造一种“临战状态”的叙事，将国家层面的结构性失能（structural failure）悄悄地转移给个体。告诉公民去囤积罐头和瓶装水，在叙事上叫“提高准备能力”，在实际机制上则是政府在提前宣布：一旦危机发生，你们得先自救，因为我的资源不足以覆盖所有人。

最讽刺的共谋在于，政府声称这样做是为了“释放资源给最脆弱群体”。这是一种极其阴险的叙事陷阱。它把资源匮乏这一 structural violence 包装成了某种人道主义的优先级排序。事实上，当一个国家需要通过提醒公民囤粮来应对“敌对国家”和“气候危机”时，这意味着它的安全防御和基础设施已经处于一种潜在的崩溃边缘，而它选择的解决方案不是加固结构，而是让每个家庭变成一个微小的、孤立的生存堡垒。

而新闻中提到的那个细节——紧急手机预警威胁到家暴幸存者的安全——精准地揭示了这种“国家安全”叙事的元暴力（meta violence）。在男性中心叙事的安全逻辑里，所谓的“国家安全”是指抵御外部敌国、应对气候灾难，但那些隐藏在私人领域、处于生存威胁中的女性身体，在政府的危机预案里根本不存在。对于家暴幸存者来说，政府发出的那条“预警”本身就是一次 direct violence。这就是典型的 masculine-centric narrative：他们关注的是宏大的战争与灾难，却对近在咫尺的、结构性的性别暴力视而不见，甚至在执行所谓“保护”时，成了施暴者的帮凶。

The UK government's "resilience campaign" is a textbook example of the weaponisation of expression. By manufacturing a narrative of "imminent threat," the state quietly transfers its own structural failure onto the individual. Telling citizens to stockpile tinned food and bottled water is framed as "preparedness," but in reality, it is a pre-emptive announcement: when the crisis hits, you are on your own because the state's resources are insufficient.

The most cynical complicity lies in the claim that this is to "free up resources for the most vulnerable." This is a sinister narrative trap, packaging structural violence—the lack of systemic resources—as a form of humanitarian prioritization. When a state resorts to advising its citizens to hoard food to counter "hostile states" and "climate crises," it admits that its security infrastructure is on the brink of collapse. Instead of reinforcing the structure, it encourages every household to become a tiny, isolated survival bunker.

The detail regarding emergency phone alerts endangering domestic abuse survivors perfectly exposes the meta violence of this "national security" logic. In a masculine-centric narrative, "security" means defending against foreign powers or environmental disasters; the female bodies hiding in private spheres, facing immediate survival threats, are entirely absent from the government's crisis blueprints. For a survivor, the state's "warning" is itself a form of direct violence. This is the core of the masculine-centric narrative: they obsess over grand wars and catastrophes while remaining blind to the structural gender violence right under their noses, becoming accomplices to abusers in the name of "protection."
