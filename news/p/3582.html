在公共空间接吻：一场关于“亲密”的叙事scamPublic Kissing: A Narrative Scam of 'Intimacy'

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
将亲密关系的质量量化为PDA频率，是典型的用行为表演掩盖结构性匮乏。
Quantifying relationship quality via PDA frequency is a performance designed to mask structural void.

这篇报道试图用一项样本量仅461人的研究来证明 PDA (Public Displays of Affection) 与关系质量的正相关。这种典型的“生活方式”报道，本质上是在向大众兜售一种关于亲密的 weaponized 叙事：只要你在公共空间通过接吻、拥抱来“表达”爱，你的关系就是高质的。这不仅是 naive，简直是个 scam。

从存在性战争的角度看，公共空间的表达从来不是为了沟通，而是为了确证身份。当名人们在镜头前亲吻，或者普通人在巴士上接吻时，他们是在进行一次关于“我们很幸福”的身份投票。这种表达是表演性的，它将亲密关系从私人的 emotional connection 抽离，转化为一种可被观察、可被量化的社会货币。当研究者说 PDA 是沟通亲密的方式时，她忽略了最关键的一点：在父权结构的共谋下，女性在公共空间中的“亲密表达”往往带有某种被动属性——她们通过扮演“热恋中的女性”来换取某种被认同的身份标签。

最讽刺的是，文中提到的“皮肤饥渴” (skin hunger) 被用来恐吓读者，诱导人们增加身体接触。这实际上是在用生物学事实来掩盖 structural violence。一个人的焦虑和抑郁，往往源于她在社会结构中的失权，而非仅仅因为少接了几个吻。把心理健康简化为“缺乏接触”，是将复杂的社会性痛苦还原为简单的生物学缺失，从而让人们在低成本的身体抚慰中忘记追问：为什么我们的生活如此匮乏，以至于只能靠在超市货架前接吻来获得短暂的 anxiolytic 效果？

真正的最优解表达，应该是基于主体性的、不被外界凝视的公正表达。如果一段关系的质量需要通过在公共空间“展示”来获得确认，那么这段关系本身可能正处于主体性死亡的边缘。

This piece attempts to use a study of only 461 people to link PDAs with relationship quality. This typical 'lifestyle' reporting is essentially peddling a weaponized narrative: that 'expressing' love through public kissing equals a high-quality bond. This is not just naive; it is a scam.

From the perspective of existential war, expression in public spaces is never about communication; it is about the establishment of identity. When celebrities kiss for the cameras, or couples do so on a bus, they are casting a vote for the identity of 'being happy.' This expression is performative, extracting intimacy from private emotional connection and converting it into a observable, quantifiable social currency. When researchers claim PDA is a way to communicate closeness, they ignore a crucial point: under the complicity of patriarchal structures, a woman's 'intimate expression' in public often carries a passive attribute—she performs the role of the 'woman in love' to secure a recognized identity label.

Most ironic is the use of 'skin hunger' to scare readers into increasing physical contact. This is using biological facts to mask structural violence. A person's anxiety and depression often stem from their disempowerment within the social structure, not merely from a lack of kisses. Reducing mental health to a 'lack of touch' reduces complex social suffering to simple biological deficiency, encouraging people to forget and stop questioning why their lives are so void that they must rely on kissing in a supermarket aisle for a brief anxiolytic effect.

True optimal expression should be a just expression based on subjectivity, free from the external gaze. If the quality of a relationship requires 'display' in public spaces to be validated, then that relationship may already be on the verge of the death of subjectivity.
