公主的门票是身体The Ticket to Princesshood Is the Body

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Vulture ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要演员 Mckenna Grace 在接受 Cosmopolitan 采访时表示，自己仍希望有一天能成为迪士尼公主，因此不接裸戏和过度性化的角色，但并非只演纯良角色——她在《惊声尖叫》中的角色就被鬼脸杀手开膛。她已发行两张原创 EP，多首歌曲进入电影原声。
The newsActor Mckenna Grace told Cosmopolitan she still dreams of becoming a Disney princess, so she takes no nudity or hypersexual roles—though not only wholesome ones: her character in Scream gets gutted by Ghostface. She has released two EPs of original songs, several of them on film soundtracks.
她守住的不是纯洁，是定价权。
She isn't protecting purity. She's protecting her price.

一个二十出头的女演员说她不接裸戏、不接过度性化的角色，因为她的梦想是当迪士尼公主。这句话被当成可爱的少女心愿转述出来。它其实是一份报价单。

迪士尼公主是一个明确的产品规格：脸要干净，身体要被遮住，性是缺席的，可婚配的。要拿到这个规格，演员得先证明自己身上没有别的东西可供出售。Grace 说的「terrifying」不是怕镜头，是怕一旦身体被看过，就再也回不到那个货架上。

这不新鲜。每个少女明星都走过这条路：先被包装成无害的，等商业价值兑现完，再被推去演成年角色，那时裸露从「禁忌」变成「突破」，同一具身体，前后两次被定价，定价的人没变。

第三章讲浪漫爱叙事的时候说过，最有效的控制不是鞭子，是自己相信。Grace 的边界是她自己划的，这一点我尊重。但值得看清楚的是：她划这条线，是因为这条线在市场上有价格。她不是在拒绝被物化，她是在选择一个更贵的物化方式。

真正的问题不是她要不要脱。是为什么一个女演员的职业天花板，长在她的衣服上。男演员没有这道题。迪士尼王子的选角从来不需要候选人先声明自己不拍什么。

她说她想演一个「alternative、rocker 公主」，给那些 emo 小孩一个公主。这个愿望是真的，也是聪明的——她在试着把规格往自己这边挪一寸。但规格的所有权不在她手里。

Cosmopolitan 的采访里她没说的一件事是：等她三十五岁，公主的门关了，那时候她要不要接裸戏？如果接，那不是她的选择变了，是市场对她的报价变了。她今天守住的边界，保质期由别人决定。

A twenty-something actress says she won't do nudity or hypersexual roles, because her dream is to be a Disney princess. This gets reported as a charming girlish wish. It is a price sheet.

A Disney princess is a product spec: clean face, covered body, sex absent, marriageable. To qualify, the actress must first prove there is nothing else about her for sale. Grace's word is "terrifying"—not of the camera, but of the fact that once the body has been seen, the shelf is gone.

This is not new. Every teen star walks it: packaged as harmless, cashed out, then pushed into adult roles where nudity flips from taboo to breakthrough. Same body, priced twice, by the same people.

Chapter Three said it about romantic love: the most effective control needs no whip, only belief. Grace's line is her own. I respect that. But notice why she draws it—because the line has a market price. She is not refusing objectification. She is choosing the more expensive kind.

The real question isn't whether she takes her clothes off. It's why a woman's career ceiling is sewn into her wardrobe. Male actors don't get this question. Nobody asks a Disney prince candidate to declare what he won't shoot.

She wants an alternative, rocker princess for the emo kids. That wish is real, and smart—she is trying to move the spec an inch her way. She doesn't own the spec.

What she didn't say in that interview: when she's thirty-five and the princess door has shut, will she take the nude scene? If she does, her choice didn't change. Her quote did. The shelf life of the boundary she holds today is set by someone else.
