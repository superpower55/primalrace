两万人的名单，四成查无此人Twenty Thousand Names, Forty Percent Ghosts

其他 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要特朗普政府试图阻止加州约2万名「疑似非公民」选民参加中期选举投票，但加州大学洛杉矶分校投票权项目分析发现，名单中超过40%的人根本不在州选民名册上，近7500名入籍公民可能被错误标记。联邦政府称名单由商业购买的部分选民数据与移民系统名单比对生成，多个法院已驳回其相关请求。
The newsThe Trump administration seeks to stop 20,000 "likely noncitizen" voters in California from casting midterm ballots, but UCLA Voting Rights Project analysts found over 40 percent were not on state voter rolls and nearly 7,500 naturalized citizens may have been wrongly flagged. The Justice Department said the list came from comparing purchased partial voter data with immigration records; multiple judges have rejected its demands.
名单的准确性从来不是目的，制造可被清除的选民才是。
Accuracy was never the point. Manufacturing a purgeable electorate was.

司法部那份两万人的「疑似非公民」名单，四成以上的人根本不在加州选民册上。UCLA 投票权项目的两个数据分析员查完，这个数字是四十多个百分点；另有近 7,500 名已入籍的公民「极有可能」被误标为不合格（来源：Mother Jones）。名单是十月十三日交给加州的，加州不肯配合逐人核验，司法部就起诉了加州，理由是「防止美国公民的选票被非法稀释」。

一份四成人查无此人的名单，怎么稀释选票？它稀释不了任何东西，它只能做另一件事：把「谁有资格投票」的定义权，从选民册挪到一份商业买来的数据和移民系统编号的交叉比对里。周二听证会上司法部自己说了，整个名单就是这么拼出来的——买来的部分选民册，对上一份联邦移民系统的编号名单。这不是核验，这是用一个系统里「出现过」的痕迹，去否定另一个系统里的资格。

这套机制在第三章里有个名字：制造事实。不是发现两万个非法投票者，是先宣布存在两万个非法投票者，再让名单去追认这个宣布。名单错了不要紧，因为名单的功能不是准确，是让「清理」这个动作获得合法性——已经有 28 人被起诉，至少 11 人被捕，指控他们非法登记投票，报道说这些指控没有证据支撑。人已经被抓了。木已成舟，后面名单被证伪，也改不了这个舟。

真正被瞄准的不是这两万人。报道里写得很清楚：司法部还威胁要起诉那些处理名单上选票的州和地方选举官员。这才是入口——不是抓住两万个假选民，是让每一个经手选票的人知道，经手可能带来起诉。选举管理的成本被抬高，处理选票的动作被吓住，剩下的选票自然就少了。这是结构层的暴力，用直接层的逮捕（11 人）做示范，用文化层的叙事（「每一张不合格的选票都在抵消一个美国公民的选票」）做背书。三层一起动，缺一层这套戏就演不成。

司法部发言人那句话值得单独看：「每一张由不合格选民投出的选票，都在抵消一个美国公民的选票。」这句话的修辞结构是零和的：你的票之所以不值钱，是因为有人的票混进来了。这是最老的一招——把结构性的失权，转写成一场关于「谁配」的争夺。谁配当公民，谁配被计入，谁来判定。判定的权力一旦被拿走，名单准不准就不重要了，重要的是名单存在过，且有人为它被捕。

多个法官已经驳回了联邦政府的要求，认定违宪、威胁选举。但周一它又去问华盛顿州要 400 多人的选票。驳回一次，换个州再来一次。这不是在打一场会输的官司，是在用官司本身消耗对方的行政能力和时间——每驳回一次，就多一个州知道联邦政府会来查。

洛杉矶的联邦检察官 Bill Essayli 在 X 上把这份分析称作「激进非法移民权利团体 CHIRLA 的假报告」。注意他攻击的是发布者的身份，不是数据。四成这个数字，他没有反驳。

More than 40 percent of the people on the Justice Department's list of 20,000 "likely noncitizens" were not on California's voter rolls at all. Two data analysts at UCLA's Voting Rights Project ran the numbers; nearly 7,500 naturalized citizens were "highly likely" to have been wrongly flagged as ineligible (source: Mother Jones). The list went to California on October 13. The state declined to validate it person by person, so the DOJ sued, to "prevent unlawful dilution of American citizens' votes."

How does a list that is four-tenths ghosts dilute anyone's vote? It doesn't. It does something else: it moves the definition of who is eligible to vote out of the voter rolls and into a cross-match between a commercially bought dataset and a set of federal immigration numbers. That is what the DOJ told a federal judge on Tuesday it had done — bought partial rolls, matched them against immigration-system identifiers. That is not verification. That is using a trace in one system to void eligibility in another.

The mechanism has a name in Chapter 3: manufacturing facts. Not finding 20,000 illegal voters, but announcing that 20,000 illegal voters exist and letting the list ratify the announcement. The list does not need to be accurate, because its function is not accuracy — it is to give the act of purging a legal footing. Twenty-eight people have already been charged, at least eleven arrested, on allegations the report says lack evidence. The arrests happened. The list can be debunked afterward; the boat has already sailed.

The real target is not the 20,000. The report is explicit: the DOJ has threatened to prosecute state and local election officials who process ballots from the list. That is the entry point — not catching 20,000 fake voters, but making every person who touches a ballot know that touching it may bring a prosecution. The cost of running an election goes up, the act of processing ballots gets scared down, and the ballots that remain are fewer. This is structural violence, with direct violence (eleven arrests) as the demonstration and cultural violence ("every ballot cast by an ineligible voter cancels out the vote of an American citizen") as the justification. All three layers move at once. Remove one and the trick collapses.

That DOJ spokesperson's line deserves its own paragraph: every ineligible ballot cancels an American citizen's. The rhetoric is zero-sum by construction — your vote is worth less because someone else's got in. It is the oldest move there is: rewrite structural disenfranchisement as a contest over who deserves to count, and who gets to decide. Once the deciding is taken, accuracy is irrelevant. What matters is that the list existed, and that people were arrested for it.

Multiple judges have already rejected the administration's demands as unconstitutional and a threat to elections. On Monday it asked Washington state for the ballots of 400-plus people anyway. Lose once, move to the next state. This is not a case being litigated to win. It is a case being litigated to burn the other side's administrative capacity — every rejection teaches one more state that the federal government will come looking.

Bill Essayli, the US Attorney in Los Angeles, called the analysis "a bogus report by the activist illegal immigrant rights group CHIRLA." Note what he attacked: the messenger's identity, not the data. He did not dispute the 40 percent.
