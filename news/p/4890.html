拒绝被定义为女权，才是最高级的女权Refusing the Label is the Ultimate Feminism

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-09 § 链接
真正的最优解表达，是直接地掠夺资源而非乞求身份认同。
The true optimal expression is to seize resources directly rather than begging for identity recognition.

Diana Rigg 拒绝称自己为 feminist，因为她不需要通过一个被男性定义的标签来获得合法性。在很多人的认知里，女权是一场关于“身份”的博弈，但对 Rigg 来说，这是一场关于“资源”的战争。当她发现摄像师的薪水都比她高时，她没有去讨论某种意识形态，而是直接要求 raise。这种对 equal pay 的追求，是典型的真.最优解表达：100% 有利于自己，且在逻辑上对他人公正。而当时媒体将她贴上 grasping diva 的标签，本质上就是一种 cultural violence，试图通过污名化女性的欲望来掩盖结构性的剥削。

Rigg 对婚姻的看法——不渴望 security——精准地拆穿了婚姻作为一种经济合作的 scam。在父权结构中，security 是男性抛给女性的诱饵，用来交换生育力和无偿家务。Rigg 意识到这种 security 其实是主体性的死亡，所以她选择在公共空间保持 fierce 和 complicated。她扮演的 Emma Peel 不是一个 subordinate sidekick，而是一个在存在性战争中赢回了定义权的个体。她不需要烧掉胸罩来证明自由，因为她本身就掌握着对自己身体和职业的定价权。

有趣的是，Rigg 的女儿在书中描述的母女关系，呈现出一种极强的共生性。这种 intensity 某种程度上是女性在男性中心叙事之外，尝试建立的一种深层连接。尽管 Rigg 在职业上对女儿严苛，但这种基于血缘和共同表达的纽带，成为了女儿在演艺圈这个共谋场域中的 superpower。这场胜利不是靠扮演某个角色赢来的，而是靠一个真实、复杂、拒绝被简化为“女性”或“女权”的生命样本赢来的。

Diana Rigg refused to call herself a feminist because she didn't need a label defined by men to achieve legitimacy. For many, feminism is a game of identity; for Rigg, it was a war over resources. When she discovered the camera operator earned more than her, she didn't debate ideology—she demanded a raise. This pursuit of equal pay is a textbook example of a true optimal expression: 100% beneficial to herself and logically just to others. The press labeling her a "grasping diva" was pure cultural violence, an attempt to stigmatize female desire to mask structural exploitation.

Her view on marriage—the refusal to yearn for "security"—precisely exposes the scam of marriage as an economic arrangement. In a patriarchal structure, security is the bait men offer women in exchange for reproductive labor and unpaid domestic work. Rigg recognized that this security is the death of subjectivity. By remaining fierce and complicated in the public eye, and by refusing to be a subordinate sidekick as Emma Peel, she won back the right to define her own existence. She didn't need to burn her bra to prove her freedom; she already held the pricing power over her own body and career.

Interestingly, the co-dependency described by her daughter reveals a profound attempt to build a connection outside the masculine-centric narrative. While Rigg was fiercely critical of her daughter professionally, this bond based on shared expression became a superpower in the complicity of the acting world. This victory wasn't won by playing a role, but by being a real, complex human sample who refused to be simplified into a "woman" or a "feminist."
