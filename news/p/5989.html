恐怖片的成本表，和谁的身体在付账The Horror Slate's Budget Sheet, and Who Pays It

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Deadline ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
低成本高概念，省下的是钱，抵押的是女人的身体
Cheap, high-concept horror saves money by mortgaging women's bodies.

这条新闻里唯一值得看的一句话，是 Hashmi 自己说的：用更少的场景、更少的资源，讲一个足够强的概念。这是制片逻辑，不是道德指控。问题在于，当这套逻辑落到题材上，"资源"从哪省、"概念"从哪来，是有性别的。

三部片里，Cinder 脱胎于他 2020 年的短片 Amber，故事是一个偏远公路小镇的理发师对抗绑架了小女孩的凶徒。Latching 被他定义为 feminist possession-revenge horror，一个发生在卡拉奇、横跨一个恐怖夜晚的附身复仇故事。绑架小女孩，附身，复仇——恐怖类型最稳定的燃料，历来是被侵害的女性的身体。这不是 Hashmi 一个人的账，是这一类型的默认设置：恐惧要具体，就得有一个可以被撕开的人，而那个人通常是女的。

他说这是 feminist。feminist 这个词在恐怖片宣传里已经流通得足够久，久到它几乎成了一个类型标签，而不是一个立场。判断它是不是真的，不看标签，看镜头停在哪里：是停在施害者的动机、受害者的恐惧，还是停在复仇本身带来的快感。报道没写。它只写了片名、地点、夜晚。谁的身体被拿去换了那九十分钟的紧张感，报道没写。

真正被这套模式定价的，是制作端的结构。预算压在一百万美元以下，靠模块化拍摄、共享剧组、独立排期来摊薄成本——Off Plan 的六个故事可以拆成六支短片分别拍。这是很聪明的工程学。但摊薄的成本不会消失，它转移到更便宜的地点、更短的周期、更少的议价权上。迪拜正在长出一个新兴的独立电影市场，而新兴市场里最先被压价的，从来不是导演。

报道里出现的人名、公司名、城市名，几乎全是男性导演、男性监制、投资方和发行方。女性在这个故事里的位置，是剧本里的被绑架者、被附身者、复仇者。这不是说 Hashmi 有什么问题，是说一个行业的启动方式，往往在第一批项目里就把位置排好了：谁写，谁投，谁被写。

把恐怖片拍得便宜，本身是好事。真正的差额在于，这套模式如果跑通，跑通的是谁的可持续——是导演的下一部片，还是那些被反复借用的身体，也能拿到一份能持续的分成。

The one line worth reading in this announcement is Hashmi's own: fewer locations, fewer resources, a concept strong enough that it doesn't feel cheap. That's a production logic, not a moral charge. The question is where the savings come from and where the concept comes from — and both have a gender.

Of the three features, Cinder grows out of his 2020 short Amber, in which a barber in a remote highway town faces a kidnapper who has abducted a young girl. Latching he calls a feminist possession-revenge horror, one harrowing night in Karachi. Abducted girls, possession, revenge: the most reliable fuel in the genre has always been the violated female body. This isn't Hashmi's personal ledger; it's the genre's default setting. Fear has to be concrete, so someone has to be torn open, and that someone is usually a woman.

He calls it feminist. The word has circulated in horror marketing long enough to become a genre tag rather than a position. Whether it's real isn't settled by the label but by where the camera rests: on the perpetrator's motive, on the victim's terror, or on the pleasure of the revenge itself. The report doesn't say. It gives titles, locations, a night. Whose body was traded for those ninety minutes of tension, the report doesn't say.

What this model actually prices is the production side. Budgets under $1 million, modular shoots, shared crews, independently scheduled segments — Off Plan's six stories can be filmed as six separate shorts. That's smart engineering. But a cost that gets thinned out doesn't vanish; it moves to cheaper locations, shorter schedules, weaker bargaining power. Dubai is growing an emerging indie market, and in emerging markets the first thing squeezed is never the director.

The names in this report are almost all male directors, producers, investors, distributors. Women appear in the story as the abducted, the possessed, the avenger. This isn't a claim that Hashmi did something wrong. It's that an industry's launch tends to fix the seating in its first slate: who writes, who funds, who gets written.

Making horror cheap is fine. The real gap is whose sustainability this model secures — the director's next feature, or also the bodies borrowed again and again, with a share that lasts.
