谈判桌摆在靶场上A Negotiating Table Set on a Firing Range

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
两边都在谈和平，两边都在报靶点。
Both sides talk peace; both sides read out target lists.

谈判和战争准备同时进行，这不叫矛盾，这叫分工。谈判负责制造“还有外交出路”的认知入口，军备负责在选举日之后兑现。

特朗普说谈判“going fine”，又说“not very reliable”，还说美军还有几个伊朗目标要“take care of”，点名了 Pickaxe Mountain；同一周他反复暗示可能在11月3日中期选举之后动手。伊朗那边，阿拉格希说武装部队“比以往任何时候都更准备好开战”，最高领袖命令把美以的“有形和无形资产”全部列入目标清单，安全负责人放话说要用“新武器”，政府还在考虑改“核学说”。一边谈霍尔木兹两天内重开，一边把打击清单更新到最新版本——这不是双轨，这是同一套表达的两面：用谈判给战争定价，用战争给谈判加价。

伊朗开出的条件里有一条值得单独拎出来：解除制裁、恢复六月给的石油豁免、减少美军在本地区的存在、承认伊朗和阿曼指定的新航线、在所有战线停火，包括以色列对黎巴嫩的袭击。这是把“和平”写成一张账单，谁付得起谁谈。特朗普的账本是另一本：他不需要和平，他需要在大选前不被看成失败者，所以战争的时间表由选举日历决定，不由霍尔木兹决定。

内塔尼亚胡说得最直白：这是“历史性的、一次性的机会，一劳永逸地消除伊朗威胁”。他的联盟在最近的立法选举中丢了议会多数，本人以看守身份继续执政。一个没有多数授权的看守总理，在推一场可能在他任内无法收场的战争——这就是共谋者理论最标准的样本：战争的代价由别人付，政治收益由自己收。而克里格提醒的那一点才是真正的死结：德黑兰担心，就算和华盛顿签了核协议，也挡不住以色列下一轮打击。签了字的安全承诺不算安全，那谈判桌上还剩下什么可换的？

整篇报道里，最该出现而没出现的，是那些将要在“短促、高强度”的一轮冲突里被炸的人。报道写的是目标清单、核学说、选举日期、油价和航线，写的是谁在什么时候打谁，没写的是谁在承受。加尔通的公式在这里很清楚：Potential 是霍尔木兹两天内可以重开、石油可以恢复流动、制裁可以解除的那个世界；Actual 是制裁还在、豁免没恢复、军队进入战位、地下核设施被点名。这个差额就是暴力，而它现在正被两边的谈判代表共同维持着，因为维持差额对双方都有用。

“如果华盛顿这封信的目的是挑拨离间，那不过是它此前错误之上的又一个错误。”阿拉格希这句话说得漂亮，漂亮到可以原样还给德黑兰：把谈判当烟雾、把军备当诚意，也是错误之上的又一个错误。而真正为这两个错误付账的人，此刻不在多哈，也不在德黑兰的谈判室里。

Talks and war preparation running in parallel isn't a contradiction. It's a division of labor. The talks manufacture the cognitive entry point called "there is still a diplomatic path"; the military manufactures the delivery date, set for after election day.

Trump says the negotiations are "going fine," then that they are "not very reliable," then that the US still has several Iranian targets to "take care of," naming Pickaxe Mountain. The same week, he keeps suggesting the US could strike after the November 3 midterms. On the Iranian side, Araghchi says the armed forces are more prepared for war than ever, the Supreme Leader orders the target list updated to include "all types of tangible and intangible assets belonging to the American-Zionist enemy," the security chief threatens "new weaponry," and the government weighs changing its "nuclear doctrine." A two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz on one table, a refreshed strike list on the other. Not two tracks — one expression with two faces: talks price the war, war prices the talks.

One of Iran's conditions deserves to be pulled out on its own: lift sanctions, restore the June oil waivers, reduce the US military presence, recognize the new shipping routes designated by Iran and Oman, end the conflict on all fronts including Israel's attacks on Lebanon. That is peace written as an invoice. Whoever can pay, negotiates. Trump's ledger is a different one: he doesn't need peace, he needs not to look like a loser before the midterms, which is why the war's timetable is set by the election calendar, not by the Strait.

Netanyahu puts it most plainly: a "historic, one-time opportunity … to remove the Iranian threat once and for all." His coalition lost its parliamentary majority in the recent legislative elections; he stays on as caretaker. A caretaker prime minister without a majority, pushing a war he may not be in office to end — this is the cleanest specimen of the co-conspirator theory: someone else pays the cost, he collects the politics. And Krieg's point is the real deadlock: Tehran fears that even a nuclear agreement with Washington may not end the threat of another Israeli attack. If a signed security guarantee isn't security, what is left on the table to trade?

What the report never shows is the people who will be under the "short, high-intensity" round of strikes. It gives us target lists, nuclear doctrine, election dates, oil routes. It does not give us who absorbs the cost. Galtung's formula is plain here: the Potential is a strait reopened in two days, oil flowing again, sanctions lifted; the Actual is sanctions still in place, waivers unrestored, forces at battle stations, an underground nuclear site named out loud. That gap is the violence, and right now both delegations are keeping it open because an open gap serves both of them.

"If Washington's goal in sending this letter is to stir up discord, it would be yet another mistake to add to its previous ones," Araghchi says. A fine line — fine enough to hand straight back to Tehran: treating talks as smoke and armament as sincerity is also a mistake added to previous ones. And the people who will pay for both mistakes are not in Doha, and not in the negotiating rooms in Tehran.
