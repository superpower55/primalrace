AI 时代的数字殖民：数据中心的“纪念碑”与资源掠夺Digital Colonialism: The 'Monuments' of Data Centres and Resource Plunder

科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
用“经济机会”掩盖资源掠夺，是典型的结构性暴力叙事。
Using 'economic opportunity' to mask resource plunder is a classic structural violence narrative.

一个比村庄还大的数据中心，100 个足球场的面积，35 米的高墙。这根本不是什么“基础设施”，而是一座数字时代的殖民纪念碑。在 Auchtertool 的原野上，科技巨头试图用物理上的 dwarfing（矮化）来完成对当地居民存在性的抹除。这种表达方式极其粗暴：我的规模即我的权力，而你们的石屋和森林只是需要被覆盖的背景板。

典型的 weaponized 叙事在这里上演：开发者 Mark Wilson 将当地人的反对定义为“misinformation”（误导信息），并抛出“一次一生”的经济机会作为诱饵。这是一个极其廉价的 scam。数据中心在建设期能提供多少岗位？在运营期除了几个保安和维护员，还能给当地带来什么？它掠夺的是苏格兰最核心的生物性资源——电力和水，而产出的“数字价值”则被悉数抽走，流向伦敦或硅谷的服务器集群。这在结构层（structural layer）上是一次精准的资源转移：让农村承担环境成本，让资本获得算力红利。

有趣的是，英国官方之前的姿态是典型的“资源经纪人”：告诉资本“往北看”，因为苏格兰能源富集且水资源充足。这种将一个地区简化为“资源包”的逻辑，正是元暴力（meta violence）的体现——在决策者的叙事中，当地人的生活质量和景观权利是不在计算公式里的。他们不关心 Auchtertool 的居民是否能看到森林，他们只关心 AI 策略的 scaling law 是否有足够的电力支撑。

好在这次 Actual 正在向 Potential 移动。从要求环境评估到可能的 moratorium（暂停执行），这种结构性的反弹说明当地人拒绝成为数字殖民地的共谋者。当警察开始警告开发者存在安全风险时，这意味着文化层面的“顺从”叙事已经崩塌。这场战争的本质不是关于建筑高度，而是关于谁拥有定义“发展”的解释权。

A data centre larger than the village itself, spanning 100 football pitches and towering 35 metres high. This is not 'infrastructure'; it is a colonial monument of the digital age. In Auchtertool, tech giants are attempting to erase the existence of local residents through physical dwarfing. The expression is brutal: scale equals power, while stone houses and forests are merely backdrops to be overwritten.

We see a textbook weaponisation of narrative here: developer Mark Wilson dismisses local opposition as 'misinformation' and dangles a 'once-in-a-lifetime' economic opportunity as bait. This is a cheap scam. How many jobs does a data centre actually provide after the initial construction? Beyond a few security guards and technicians, what remains for the locals? It plunders the most fundamental biological resources—power and water—while the 'digital value' is siphoned off to server clusters in London or Silicon Valley. In the structural layer, this is a precise transfer of resources: rural areas bear the environmental cost, while capital reaps the compute dividends.

Interestingly, the UK government acted as a typical 'resource broker,' telling capital to 'look north' because Scotland is energy and water-rich. This logic of reducing a region to a 'resource pack' is a manifestation of meta violence—in the decision-makers' narrative, the quality of life and landscape rights of locals are simply not in the equation. They don't care if the people of Auchtertool can see their forests; they only care if the scaling laws of AI strategy have enough power.

Fortunately, the Actual is moving toward the Potential. From demanding environmental assessments to a potential moratorium, this structural pushback shows that locals refuse to be complicit in becoming a digital colony. When Police Scotland warns developers of security risks, it signals that the cultural narrative of 'compliance' has collapsed. This war is not about building height; it is about who holds the power to define 'development'.
