所谓的“进步”不过是换了种方式地共谋Progressivism as a New Form of Complicity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-04 § 链接
福利削减的共识，是结构性暴力在文化层面的再次合法化。
The consensus on welfare cuts is the re-legitimation of structural violence at the cultural level.

看到这个 poll 结果，不需要惊讶。所谓的“工党成员”，在面对 welfare spending 时展现出的这种“tougher view”，本质上是一次典型的共谋。47% 的成员认为政府在福利上花钱太多，而 68% 的人支持增加国防开支。这在逻辑上极其荒谬：他们一方面标榜进步，另一方面却在潜意识里认同将资源从底层生存保障转移到暴力机器（defence）上的逻辑。

这就是典型的结构性暴力 (structural violence) 转化为文化共识的过程。福利被定义为“过多”，意味着那些依赖福利生存的底层——其中绝大多数是处于原初种族地位的女性、少数族裔和残障人士——他们的生存权在这些“进步成员”眼中已经变成了可以被审计的成本。当人们讨论“如何通过提高高收入者税收来筹资”时，他们实际上是在进行一场精英内部的资源重新分配博弈，而真正被福利削减伤害的底层，根本不在这个博弈的议程之内。

最讽刺的是，ThinkLabour 这种组织通过一个 poll 告诉首相，成员们其实很“强硬”。这不仅仅是数据的分享，而是一次认知入口的抢夺。它在制造一种“真实”：即削减福利、加强国防才是赢得连任的“最优解表达”。当这种叙事被内化为党内共识，它就成了元暴力的一部分——定义谁的生存是必要的，谁的生存是浪费的。

这场博弈里没有赢家，只有被牺牲的 Actual。Potential 的社会保障被 Defense 的宏大叙事吞噬，而共谋者们在讨论税率时，心安理得地在文化层面上完成了对底层生存权的剥夺。

The results of this poll are hardly surprising. The so-called 'Labour members' and their 'tougher view' on welfare spending are engaging in a textbook case of complicity. When 47% believe the government spends too much on welfare while 68% back increased defence spending, they are subconsciously endorsing the transfer of resources from basic survival to the machinery of violence.

This is the process of structural violence being converted into cultural consensus. Defining welfare as 'too much' means that the survival of the most vulnerable—predominantly women of the Primal Race, ethnic minorities, and the disabled—is now viewed as a cost to be audited. While they debate funding this through taxes on high earners, they are merely playing a game of resource redistribution among elites. The marginalized, who actually suffer from these cuts, are entirely absent from this agenda.

It is peak irony that ThinkLabour uses this poll to tell the Prime Minister that members are 'tough'. This isn't just data; it's a weaponized attempt to seize the cognitive entrance. They are manufacturing a 'reality' where cutting welfare and boosting defence is the 'optimal expression' for electoral success. Once this narrative is internalized, it becomes part of the meta-violence—deciding whose existence is necessary and whose is a waste.

In this game, there are no winners, only a shrinking of the Actual. The potential for social security is devoured by the grand narrative of Defence, while the co-conspirators comfortably strip away the right to survive under the guise of fiscal responsibility.
