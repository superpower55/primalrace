用“实习”修补结构性暴力，是最高明的PRUsing 'Internships' to Patch Structural Violence: The Ultimate PR Move

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
将系统性的排斥包装成个体能力的缺失，是结构暴力的标准操盘。
Packaging systemic exclusion as individual deficit is the standard operating procedure of structural violence.

这篇报道读起来像个 a good_news：一个前政府官员在呼吁为特殊教育需求（Send）青年提供更多实习机会，把就业率从 5% 提升到 70%。叙事很完整：从“被低估的潜力”到“成功的个案”，最后落脚在“道德危机”。

但如果我们套用 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式，这件事的本质是 structural violence 的一次温情修补。系统在承认这些青年无法进入职场的同时，并没有反思为什么职场（如 Amazon 或 Asda 的仓库）的结构是如此僵化，以至于一个自闭症青年需要一整年的“特制支持”才能勉强适应。这里的“支持”实际上是一种翻译机制，将一个不符合男性中心主义、标准化生产效率的个体，强行翻译成系统可接受的“劳动力”零件。

最阴险的部分在结尾：Milburn 提到系统过于依赖福利金而非就业支持。这在逻辑上完成了一次危险的转移——将“社会未能提供包容性环境”的责任，转移到了“福利制度让年轻人失去了动力”的叙事上。这种逻辑是典型的共谋者游戏：政府和企业通过提供少量、碎片化的实习岗位（目前全英仅 4500 个），在文化层面上塑造一种“只要你努力实习就能成功”的假象，从而为未来削减 disability benefits 提供道德合法性。

当一个 20 岁的自闭症青年在仓库里被夸奖为“自信的小伙子”时，我们庆祝的不是他的主体性觉醒，而是他成功地内化了系统的规训，成为了一个合格的、可定价的工具。真正的 Potential 应该是建立一个不再需要“特制实习”也能容纳多样性神经类型的社会，而不是在原有的剥削结构里，为弱势群体申请一张临时的入场券。

This report reads like a good_news story: a former official calling for more supported internships for youth with special educational needs (Send) to boost employment from 5% to 70%. The narrative is seamless, moving from 'undervalued potential' to 'success stories' and ending on a 'moral crisis.'

But applying the formula Violence = Potential − Actual reveals this as a sentimental patch for structural violence. While the system admits these youths struggle to enter the workforce, it refuses to question why the workplace structures—like Amazon or Asda warehouses—are so rigid that an autistic youth requires a year of 'specialized support' just to fit in. This 'support' is actually a translation mechanism, forcibly translating an individual who doesn't fit the masculine-centric, standardized productivity model into a 'labor' part the system can accept.

The most sinister part is the ending: Milburn suggests the system relies too much on benefits rather than job support. This completes a dangerous shift in logic—moving the responsibility from 'society's failure to provide an inclusive environment' to the narrative that 'benefit systems kill motivation.' This is a classic game of complicity: the government and corporations use a handful of fragmented internship slots (only 4,500 nationwide) to create a cultural illusion that 'effort in internships leads to success,' thereby providing moral legitimacy for future cuts to disability benefits.

When a 20-year-old autistic youth is praised as a 'confident young fella' in a warehouse, we aren't celebrating the awakening of his subjectivity; we are celebrating his successful internalization of systemic discipline, becoming a qualified, priced tool. True Potential would be building a society that accommodates neurodiversity without needing 'special internships,' rather than fighting for a temporary ticket into an existing exploitative structure.
