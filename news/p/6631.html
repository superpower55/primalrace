AI搜索不是更准，是替你把解释权收走了
AI search doesn't find truth better. It takes the interpreting away from you.

WIRED这篇使用指南写得挺实用，但它漏了最关键的一层：AI搜索改变的首先是"谁在决定什么是答案"，而不只是"搜索快不快"。

文章自己说得很清楚：AI会先猜你的问题是什么意思，再去找来源，再把来源里的东西抽出来，最后包装成一段流畅的回答。三步里每一步都是一次解释。传统搜索把这一步留给你：你看见一排蓝色链接，自己判断哪个可信。AI把这一步收进黑箱，还给你一个结论。文章管这叫"更不透明"（more opaque），说得太客气了。这是把判断权交出去，换一点方便。

最有意思的是加州大学圣地亚哥分校那个发现：AI有时会选"和查询更相关"的来源，而不是"更权威"的来源，于是个人博客可能压过专业医疗网站。这不能算AI的bug，它只是忠实执行了"匹配"这个指标。问题在于，相关性是机器可以算的，权威性不是。权威性是人建立的，是一整套关于谁有资格说话的社会共识。AI把这个共识跳过去了，然后给你一个看起来同样自信的答案。

这就是第三章那套"制造可能性的艺术"的日常版本。权力争夺的从来不是事实本身，而是事实如何被描绘。传统搜索至少把描绘权摊在桌面上，AI搜索把它收进模型权重里。你以为自己在查帝国大厦多高，其实你在训练自己习惯一个没有来源感的答案。

文章还建议，遇到健康、法律、财务这类高风险查询，最好回到传统搜索。这条建议是对的，但它暴露了整件事的真相：AI搜索只在答案错了也无所谓的地方好用。一个只在无关紧要时才可靠的工具，被包装成"搜索的重大升级"，这个落差本身就是一种叙事操作。

真正该问的不是"什么时候用AI搜索"，而是"我为什么越来越不想自己看链接了"。答案很简单：看链接累，判断累，承担判断错误的后果更累。AI卖的就是这份累的豁免权。代价是，你慢慢不再知道自己信的是什么，只知道AI说得挺顺。

帝国大厦的高度错了，没人受伤。可这套"把解释权外包出去换省事"的习惯一旦养成，等到真正要紧的问题出现时，你已经没有力气自己找答案了。

WIRED's guide is useful, but it misses the layer that matters most: AI search doesn't mainly change how fast you find things. It changes who decides what counts as an answer.

The article lays it out plainly. The AI guesses what your question means, finds sources, pulls information out of them, and packages the result in polished prose. Every one of those steps is an act of interpretation. Traditional search leaves that step to you: you see a list of blue links and judge which one to trust. AI swallows the step into a black box and hands you a conclusion. The article calls this "more opaque." That's too kind. It's trading judgment for convenience.

The best part is the UC San Diego finding: AI sometimes picks sources because they're more relevant to the query, not because they're more authoritative, so a personal blog can outrank a professional medical site. This isn't a bug. The model is faithfully optimizing for match. The trouble is that relevance is computable and authority is not. Authority is a social consensus about who has the right to speak. AI skips the consensus and gives you an answer that sounds just as confident either way.

This is the everyday version of the art of manufacturing possibilities. Power never fights over what the facts are. It fights over how the facts get described. Traditional search at least puts the describing on the table. AI search files it away inside model weights. You think you're checking how tall the Empire State Building is. You're actually training yourself to accept answers that have no felt source.

The article also suggests going back to old-school search for high-stakes queries about health, law, or money. That advice is right, and it gives the game away: AI search works best exactly where being wrong doesn't cost much. A tool that's only reliable when the stakes are low, sold as a major upgrade to searching, is a gap that is itself a narrative move.

The real question isn't when to use AI search. It's why you're less and less willing to read the links yourself. The answer is simple: reading is tiring, judging is tiring, and living with a wrong judgment is worse. AI sells you an exemption from that tiredness. The price is that you slowly stop knowing what you believe, and only know that the AI sounded smooth.

A wrong height for a building hurts no one. But once the habit of outsourcing interpretation for convenience sets in, by the time a question that actually matters arrives, you'll have no strength left to look for the answer yourself.
