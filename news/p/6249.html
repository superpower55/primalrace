换个牌照，暴力照旧New Licence, Same Violence

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要英国财政大臣John Healey本周首份预算将老虎机税率从20%上调至25%，并取消英格兰成人游戏中心（AGC）的商业税率优惠。但《卫报》分析发现，Merkur和Admiral等公司为数百家以老虎机为主的场所申请了宾果厅牌照，可能借此规避新规。工党议员Alex Ballinger呼吁政府警惕这一漏洞。

财政大臣换了尺子，赌场换了招牌，输的还是同一个人。
The chancellor changed the ruler; the slot farms changed the sign. The loser is the same person.

英国财政大臣把老虎机的税率从20%提到25%，顺手取消了成人游戏中心（AGC）的商业地产税减免，口号是"向赌博征税，支持每条高街的良好增长"。听起来像是一次结构层面的纠正。但《卫报》的调查说，Merkur 和 Admiral 这两家最大的老虎机连锁，早就给门店申请了宾果牌照——因为宾果厅不在这次加税的靶子上。换一块招牌，税率就绕过去了。

这就是加尔通那个公式最赤裸的样子：Violence = Potential − Actual。政策本可以把盘剥的入口堵上，实际堵上的是一条缝，旁边那扇门原封不动。差额不是抽象数字，是 Stockport 那家 Merkur Slots 里，Wendy Hughes 在两个16小时的时段里输掉的2000英镑。她当时已是癌症晚期。Merkur 因此被罚10万英镑。罚款和她的损失，不在一个量级上。

《卫报》走访了 Merkur 的四家店和 Admiral 的一家，橱窗上都写着宾果。店里几乎全是老虎机，2.5秒转一次，一次2英镑，头奖500英镑。宾果只存在于角落里堆着的几台平板电脑上。至少有一家店，员工在暗访镜头里说，从来没人玩那些平板，而且它们看起来根本不能用。这就是牌照和现实之间的落差：牌照是给监管看的，机器是给客人玩的。

更精确地说，被绕过去的不是税，是"谁在付代价"这件事的定义权。财政大臣说他要向赌博收税，但收税的前提是先认出哪家店是赌场。而当一家店可以合法地把自己登记成宾果厅，同时店里摆满 B3 类机器——研究显示这类机器与更高程度的赌博伤害相关——那么"什么是赌场"这个判断，就交到了运营方手里。这是第三章的认知入口，只不过这次武器不是广告，是一张营业执照。

注意谁在共谋，以及回报是什么。Merkur 和 Admiral 对《卫报》的置评请求没有回应。行业公会 Bacta 的主席 Joseph Cullis 说，这项税收变化会重创行业，他家的家族生意从1962年做起，做了六十多年，现在不得不重新考虑投资。这段话里没有一句提到那些在2.5秒一次的转盘前输掉积蓄的人。家族生意是真实的，代价也是真实的，只是这两件事被放在了不同的秤上，而制定秤的人站在前者那边。

议员 Alex Ballinger 说，必须确保这些牌照不能被用来规避新的商业地产税规则，他还催文化、媒体和体育部把正在征求意见的牌照收紧方案推下去。这话是对的，但它自己就说明了问题：政府已经知道漏洞在哪，正在咨询，而运营方在这段时间里可以把剩下的 AGC 牌照一张张转成宾果牌照。立法和执法之间的时差，从来不是中立的，它是一段被明码标价的窗口期。地产税专家 Paul Turner-Mitchell 说得更直白：光有一张宾果牌照，并不决定它的商业地产税怎么算，最终要看财政部怎么定义 AGC，以及地方议会怎么执行。定义权和执行权都还悬着，悬着的部分就是钱。

所以这不是一条"加税被钻空子"的新闻，这是一条关于尺子的新闻。财政大臣换了一把尺子，运营方换了一块招牌，两边都在调整"什么算赌场"的表述，而唯一没有参与定义的人，是那个坐在机器前的人。她死前输掉的钱，成了这场定义之争里唯一一个不需要被定义的数。

The chancellor raised duty on slot machine takings from 20% to 25% and stripped adult gaming centres (AGCs) of their business rates discount, billing it as "raising taxes on gambling to support good growth on every high street." It reads like a structural correction. The Guardian's investigation says Merkur and Admiral, the two largest slot farm operators, had already secured bingo licences for venues that are not bingo halls. Swap the sign, and the tax misses.

This is Galtung's formula at its most naked: Violence = Potential − Actual. The policy could have closed the extraction point. What it closed was one seam, while the door beside it stayed open. The gap is not an abstraction. It is the £2,000 Wendy Hughes lost over two spells of 16 hours at the Merkur Slots in Stockport, while she had terminal cancer. Merkur was fined £100,000 after the Guardian revealed the details. The fine and her loss are not on the same scale.

The Guardian visited four Merkur venues and one Admiral. All advertised bingo in their windows. Inside, they were almost entirely slot machines: a £2 spin every 2.5 seconds, a £500 jackpot. Bingo existed only on tablets stacked in one small corner. In at least one case, caught on covert film, a staff member said nobody ever used the tablets and they appeared not to be working. That is the distance between the licence and the room: the licence is for the regulator, the machines are for the customers.

What gets bypassed here is not merely tax. It is the power to define who pays. The chancellor says he is taxing gambling, but taxing gambling first requires recognising which shop is a casino. When a venue can legally register as a bingo hall while its floor is packed with B3 machines, which studies associate with higher levels of gambling-related harm, the question of what counts as a casino has been handed to the operator. This is the cognitive entrance from Chapter 3, except the weapon is not an advertisement. It is a licence.

Watch who is complicit, and what they get. Merkur and Admiral did not respond to requests for comment. Joseph Cullis, president of the trade body Bacta, says the tax change will hit the industry hard: his great-grandfather started the family business in 1962, they have spent more than sixty years building it, and now they must reconsider investment. Not one sentence in that statement mentions the people losing their savings at 2.5-second intervals. The family business is real. The cost is real. They are simply placed on different scales, and the people who built the scales stand on the side of the former.

MP Alex Ballinger says licences must not be used to sidestep the new business rates rules, and urges the culture department to push through its licensing consultation. He is right, and his own statement shows why it matters: the government already knows where the loophole is, it is consulting, and in the meantime operators can convert their remaining AGC licences one by one. The lag between legislation and enforcement is never neutral. It is a window with a price on it. The property tax expert Paul Turner-Mitchell puts it plainly: a bingo licence alone does not settle the business rates treatment. It depends on how the Treasury defines an AGC and how councils enforce it. The definition and the enforcement are both still hanging. The hanging part is the money.

So this is not a story about a tax rise being dodged. It is a story about rulers. The chancellor changed one ruler, the operators changed one sign, and both are busy adjusting the phrase "what counts as a casino." The only person who took no part in the definition is the one sitting in front of the machine. The money she lost before she died is the one number in this whole contest that needs no defining at all.
