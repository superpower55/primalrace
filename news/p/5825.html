肥胖不是意志力问题Obesity Is Not a Willpower Problem

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把结构暴力说成个人懒惰，是元暴力的标准动作
Calling structural violence personal laziness is meta-violence's standard move

英国议会这份报告说得很客气：政府的肥胖计划“注定失败”。真正值得看的是它给出的失败原因——没有首相亲自挂帅、没有跨部门方案、没有衡量指标。一个把三分之二成年人装进去的“流行病”，需要首相亲自督战才能推动，这句话本身就承认了：肥胖不是一个健康问题，是一个权力分配问题。

报告里最有分量的一句，是议员们指出历届政府把“预防未来肥胖”和“支持已经肥胖的人”混为一谈。这两件事的受众根本不是同一批人。前者要动的是食品工业——广告、促销、标签、学校门口的炸鸡店；后者要动的是医疗资源的分配。Layla Moran把话说得很白：有效的计划“意味着在诸多问题上顶住食品工业”，限制那些“贴满我们生活”的不健康食品广告。也就是说，这件事的对手从来不是脂肪，是卖脂肪的人。

加尔通的暴力三角在这里是现成的。结构层：不健康食品的定价、货架位置、广告时段，这些不是消费者投票投出来的，是利润算出来的。文化层：肥胖被叙述成个人自律的失败，于是解决方案永远是“少吃多动”，而不是“为什么便宜的、好吃的、随手可得的食物恰好是最差的”。两层一联动，责任就从生产端滑到了身体上。政府说已经禁了晚九点前的垃圾食品广告、禁了多件优惠、给了地方议会挡新外卖店的权力——这些是动作，但报告说得很清楚：一年多了，连“怎么衡量进展”都还没答上来。没有衡量指标的政策，等于没有政策。

这里还有一个更隐蔽的共谋。报告要求首相亲自领导，理由是“这个议题超越部门”。但“超越部门”的另一面是：没有一个部门真正为它负责，也就没有一个部门真正被问责。跨部门委员会是英国政治里最常见的表演性让步——它让问题看起来被接管了，实际上只是把责任摊薄到没人认领。Moran说要“首相发号施令”，听起来很强硬，但首相发号施令的对象是谁？是食品工业。而食品工业恰好是最擅长把“顶住”拖成“磋商”、把“磋商”拖成“自愿承诺”的行业。

真正被这份报告定价的，是那些已经在肥胖中生活的人。报告提到要扩大体重管理支持和减肥药的可及性——这是治疗端，是好的，是具体的。但它同时说明了一件事：这个系统已经默认，预防来不及了，先给已经受损的身体发药。三分之一的儿童超重或肥胖，这个数字不是十年计划能“逆转”的，它是一个社会决定把成本记在谁账上的问题。

报告的措辞是“注定失败”，但失败从来不是中性的。计划失败，代价不会落在唐宁街，也不会落在食品公司的财报上，它落在那些被广告包围、被货架引导、然后被告知“你要自律”的人身上。这份报告唯一没问的问题是：如果这个“流行病”本来就不该由个人来终结，那为什么每一次失败的账单，都还是寄给同一批人？

The UK parliament's report says, politely, that the government's obesity plan is "doomed to fail." What matters is the reason it gives: no prime minister in charge, no cross-departmental plan, no way to measure progress. An "epidemic" that contains two-thirds of adults needs the prime minister personally at the helm — that sentence alone admits obesity is not a health problem but a question of who holds power.

The report's sharpest line is that successive governments have conflated preventing future obesity with supporting people who already have it. These are not the same constituency. The first has to move the food industry — advertising, promotions, labelling, the fried chicken shops outside schools. The second has to move the allocation of medical resources. Layla Moran puts it plainly: an effective plan "will mean standing up to the food industry," regulating the ads and promotions for unhealthy food and drink "that wallpaper our lives." The opponent was never fat. It is the people selling it.

Galtung's violence triangle fits without adjustment. Structural layer: the price, shelf position and airtime of unhealthy food are not voted on by consumers; they are calculated from profit. Cultural layer: obesity is narrated as a failure of personal discipline, so the solution is always "eat less, move more," never "why is the cheap, tasty, readily available food precisely the worst." The two layers lock together and responsibility slides off the production end onto the body. The government lists what it has done — pre-9pm junk food ad restrictions, a ban on volume-price promotions, stronger council powers to block new takeaways near schools. These are moves. But the report is clear: more than a year on, even how progress will be measured remains unanswered. A policy without a metric is not a policy.

There is a quieter complicity too. The report wants the prime minister to lead because the issue "transcends government departments." But transcending departments also means no department truly owns it, and therefore no department is truly accountable. A cross-departmental cabinet committee is Westminster's most familiar performative concession: it makes the problem look taken over while thinning responsibility until nobody claims it. Moran says the prime minister must call the shots. Call them at whom? The food industry — an industry that has perfected the art of turning "standing up to" into "consultation," and "consultation" into "voluntary commitments."

What this report actually prices is the people already living with obesity. It calls for expanded weight management support and access to NHS weight-loss drugs. That is the treatment end, and it is concrete and good. But it also concedes something: the system has already decided prevention came too late, so hand drugs to the bodies already damaged. Close to a third of children overweight or obese is not a number a ten-year plan reverses. It is a number that records where a society has chosen to book the cost.

The report's word is "doomed." Failure is never neutral. When the plan fails, the bill does not land on Downing Street or in a food company's earnings report. It lands on the people surrounded by advertising, steered by shelf placement, and then told to be disciplined. The one question the report does not ask: if this epidemic was never going to be ended by individuals, why does every failed bill keep getting mailed to the same address?
