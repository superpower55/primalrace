一个强奸犯替法拉奇挡人群A Rapist Walked Beside Farage

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
安保外包是共谋的防火墙，没人负责就是所有人负责。
Outsourced vetting is the firewall of complicity: nobody responsible means everybody is.

一个被控强奸的男人，站在人群和一位政客之间，代表这个政党和公众直接互动。报道说，瑞安·迪纳奇在四月和五月至少四次出现在法拉奇的竞选走访现场（来源：The Guardian）。六月他被定罪，七月被判八年半。他在2022年5月16日凌晨进入一辆房车，强奸了里面睡觉的女人。

改革党说，人不是我们找的，是第三方承包商提供的，对方保证过已经审查。这句话是共谋的标准句式：把责任交给一个抽象的中介，然后自己站在干净的立场上说“震惊和恶心”。报道写得很清楚，改革党没有回答是否还在用这家承包商，也没有回答对承包商做了什么处理。谁签的合同、谁核的名单、谁决定一个2025年就被控强奸的人可以靠近政客和公众——这些名字一个都没出现。

这不是安保漏洞，这是结构暴力在正常运转。审查机制存在的意义，本来就是把危险挡在人群之外；当它被外包、被口头保证替代、被“我们不知情”兜住，它就从保护变成了免责。一个被控严重性暴力的人被放在有公众的场合，这件事的代价不是由改革党承担的，是由那天在现场的每一个女人承担的——她们不知道站在几步之外的人是谁。

法拉奇本人拒绝政府提供的安保，理由是“不够”。他收了加密货币亿万富翁五百万英镑的礼金，用来支付“余生”的安保。钱越多，越显得专业；越专业，越不需要问那个人是谁。这里真正被买卖的不是安全，是“看起来安全”。

工党要求法拉奇解释“改革党在什么时候知道”。这个问题问对了，但问得太窄。要问的是：一个政党把“保护公众”这件事外包出去之后，还剩下什么责任？报道里改革党的全部回应是“我们一发现就停用了他”。发现之前呢？一个被控强奸的人在竞选现场保护政客，这不是“发现”的问题，这是设计的问题。

报道末尾还写了另一件事：保守党雇过一个前候选人杰克·罗斯，他被暗访记者拍到似乎在讨论雇凶杀人的价格。改革党拿这件事骂保守党。两个党在互相指责审查失败，用的却是同一套外包逻辑。

那辆房车里的女人报案的当天，警察第二天就抓了人。三年后，他站在政客身边。系统的审查没能拦住他，系统的判决拦住了他——但那是另一套系统，来得太晚，而且从没被用来问一句：这三年里，谁让他一直在人群里走。

A man facing a rape charge stood between the public and a politician, representing the party in direct contact with crowds. Ryan Dinnage appeared beside Nigel Farage on at least four walkabouts during the local election campaign in April and May, according to The Guardian. In June he was convicted; in July he was sentenced to eight and a half years. In the early hours of 16 May 2022, he entered a caravan where a woman was sleeping and raped her.

Reform says it did not hire him: a third-party contractor supplied him and assured the party he had been vetted. That is the standard sentence of complicity—hand responsibility to an abstract intermediary, then stand clean and say you were shocked and disgusted. The report is precise: Reform did not answer whether it still works with the contractor, or what action it took against them. Who signed the contract, who checked the list, who decided a man charged with rape in 2025 could stand near a politician and the public—none of these names appear.

This is not a security lapse. It is structural violence running normally. Vetting exists to keep danger away from people; when it is outsourced, replaced by verbal assurance, and covered by “we didn't know,” it turns from protection into exemption. A man accused of serious sexual violence was placed in public spaces. The cost is not carried by Reform. It is carried by every woman who was there that day and did not know who was standing a few steps away.

Farage turned down government security as inadequate. He accepted a £5m gift from a crypto billionaire to pay for his security for the rest of his life. The more money, the more professional it looks; the more professional, the less anyone asks who the man is. What is being bought here is not safety. It is the appearance of safety.

Labour demands Farage explain when Reform knew. The question is right but too narrow. The real question: after a party outsources the protection of the public, what responsibility remains? Reform's entire response in the report is that he ceased to be used as soon as they found out. Before they found out? A man charged with rape protecting a politician on the campaign trail is not a discovery problem. It is a design problem.

The report ends with another case: the Conservatives employed a former election candidate, Jack Ross, who was filmed by an undercover reporter apparently discussing the cost of hiring contract killers. Reform attacks the Tories over it. Two parties accusing each other of vetting failures, using the same outsourcing logic.

The woman in the caravan reported the crime that day; police arrested him the next. Three years later he was standing beside a politician. The vetting system did not stop him. The court system did—but that is another system, it arrived late, and it was never used to ask who let him walk among crowds for those three years.
