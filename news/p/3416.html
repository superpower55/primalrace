Burnham 的“务实”是元暴力的标准剧本Burnham's 'Pragmatism' is the Standard Script of Meta-Violence

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
所谓的“务实”通常是权力在向既得利益共谋时用来掩盖背叛的遮羞布。
'Pragmatism' is usually a shroud used by power to mask complicity with vested interests.

Andy Burnham 的表演路径极其经典：在担任市长时，他通过支持《化石燃料条约》等激进表达来获取进步派的“票”；而一旦进入 No 10 的权力中心，他立刻切换到“务实” (pragmatic) 模式。这种切换不是策略的优化，而是主体性的让渡。在政治博弈中，“务实”这个词是一个危险的认知入口，它试图将“对资本的顺从”重新定义为“对现实的理性”，从而让背叛承诺的行为显得公正且必要。

这本质上是一场典型的共谋。Burnham 面对的是一个由能源巨头、右翼政客和工业资本组成的共谋场域。在这个场域里，北海石油的钻探权就是定价权。当他用“油气来自哪里”这种话术来转移“是否继续依赖油气”的核心矛盾时，他实际上是在为结构性暴力 (structural violence) 寻找合法性。他不再是那个定义未来的领导者，而成了维护既定秩序的共谋者。

Sadiq Khan 的呼吁在文化层面上是对的，但在结构层面上显得 naive。在元暴力 (meta violence) 的逻辑里，男性中心叙事习惯于将“资源掌控”和“地缘安全”置于“生态生存”之上。Burnham 的沉默和模糊，正是这种叙事在权力顶端的一次成功复刻。他不需要通过否认气候危机来获胜，他只需要通过“延迟决策”和“重新定义务实”来让危机在官僚程序的磨损中被消解。

最讽刺的是，这种“务实”的代价由谁承担？由那些在极端天气中生存的底层民众，以及那些被剥夺了未来表达空间的下一代。这种 Potential（一个绿色的、可持续的英国）与 Actual（继续被油气资本绑架的现状）之间的差额，就是由 Burnham 这种政客亲手制造的暴力。

Andy Burnham's performance follows a classic path: as Mayor, he secured progressive votes through radical expressions like supporting the Fossil Fuel Treaty; once in No 10, he immediately switched to 'pragmatic' mode. This is not an optimization of strategy, but a surrender of subjectivity. In political gaming, 'pragmatism' is a dangerous cognitive entry point; it attempts to redefine 'submission to capital' as 'rationality toward reality,' making the betrayal of promises seem just and necessary.

This is essentially a case of complicity. Burnham is facing a field of co-conspirators consisting of energy giants, right-wing politicians, and industrial capital. In this field, the right to drill in the North Sea is the power to set the price. When he uses the rhetoric of 'where the oil comes from' to divert the core conflict of 'whether to continue reliance,' he is effectively seeking legitimacy for structural violence.

Sadiq Khan's call is correct on a cultural level, but naive on a structural one. In the logic of meta-violence, the masculine-centric narrative habitually prioritizes 'resource control' and 'geopolitical security' over 'ecological survival.' Burnham's silence and ambiguity are a successful replication of this narrative at the peak of power. He doesn't need to deny the climate crisis to win; he only needs to dissolve the crisis through 'delayed decisions' and 'redefining pragmatism' within bureaucratic attrition.

The irony is: who pays for this 'pragmatism'? The grassroots enduring extreme weather and the next generation stripped of their future expression. The gap between the Potential (a green, sustainable UK) and the Actual (a state still hijacked by oil and gas capital) is the violence personally manufactured by politicians like Burnham.
