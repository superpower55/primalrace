朱利安尼的“冒犯感”与元暴力的继承权Giuliani's 'Offense' and the Inheritance of Meta-Violence

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-07 § 链接
将宗教身份等同于恐怖主义，是典型的武器化叙事入口。
Equating religious identity with terrorism is a classic weaponised narrative entry point.

朱利安尼在 Newsmax 上的表演是一次标准的 cultural violence 投放。他试图通过将现任市长 Mamdani 的穆斯林身份与 9/11 恐怖分子强行挂钩，来夺取对“纪念日”这一认知入口的解释权。在他看来，这个仪式的 entry ticket 不是对死者的哀悼，而是必须符合他定义的“纯洁”身份。这种逻辑极其卑劣：他把一个人的信仰直接等同于某种潜在的敌意，从而将 Mamdani 标记为 the other。

这背后是深层的 meta violence 在运作。朱利安尼代表的是那种典型的 masculine-centric narrative——他不仅垄断了权力，还试图垄断“谁有资格悲伤”的定义权。他口中的“offends me”其实是一种权力宣示：在这个叙事里，白人男性的冒犯感高于一个穆斯林市长的合法存在权。这种叙事通过将特定群体“非人化”或“敌对化”，为结构性的歧视提供合法性背书。

而 Mamdani 的应对则是一次关于存在性的博弈。他没有陷入对方的情绪陷阱，而是通过发布行政令将 9/11 定义为“纪念与服务之日”，试图将这个认知入口从“仇恨与排外”转移到“公共服务与共同哀悼”上。这是在尝试用 Just Expressions 替换掉被武器化的仇恨叙事。

但这场博弈的残酷之处在于，10 万人的签名请愿证明了共谋者 (complicit) 的规模之大。很多人在潜意识中认同了朱利安尼的逻辑，他们认为一个穆斯林的出席本身就是一种“亵渎”。这种共谋将个体对恐怖主义的恐惧，转化为对一个特定族群的结构性暴力。只要这种“身份即罪名”的元暴力不被拆除，无论市长是谁，他都永远在面对一道生物墙和文化墙的双重围剿。

Giuliani's performance on Newsmax is a textbook deployment of cultural violence. By forcibly linking Mayor Mamdani's Muslim faith to the 9/11 hijackers, he attempts to seize the interpretative power over the 'memorial' as a cognitive entry point. In his worldview, the entry ticket to this ceremony is not the mourning of the dead, but a 'purity' of identity defined by him. This logic is vile: it equates a person's faith with inherent hostility, marking Mamdani as the other.

This is meta-violence in action. Giuliani represents a typical masculine-centric narrative—he doesn't just monopolise power; he seeks to monopolise the definition of 'who is allowed to grieve.' His claim that it 'offends me' is a declaration of power: in this narrative, the offense felt by a white male outweighs the legitimate existence of a Muslim mayor. Such narratives justify structural discrimination by dehumanising or antagonising specific groups.

Mamdani's response is an existential game. He avoids the emotional trap, instead using executive orders to redefine 9/11 as a 'Day of Remembrance and Service.' He is attempting to shift the cognitive entry point from 'hate and exclusion' toward 'public service and shared mourning,' replacing weaponised hatred with Just Expressions.

Yet, the cruelty of this game lies in the scale of the complicity, evidenced by the 100,000 signatures. Many subconsciously accept Giuliani's logic, believing a Muslim's presence is a 'desecration.' This complicity transforms individual fear of terrorism into structural violence against a race. As long as this meta-violence—where 'identity equals guilt'—remains, any mayor from that background will forever face the double siege of the biological wall and the cultural wall.
