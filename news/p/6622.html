炸伊朗前，先数钱Count the Profits Before the Bombs

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 NPR ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
战争叙事换选票，油价换利润，代价记在别人账上。
War narrative buys votes, oil prices buy profits, and someone else pays.

中期选举前一天，特朗普把轰炸伊朗重新摆上桌。NPR 的原话是：选举结束后，他可能再次轰炸伊朗，这个选项已经和以色列讨论过。海湾国家在紧张地等，卡塔尔过去 72 小时一直在伊朗和美国之间穿梭谈判。

时间点就是全部内容。选票还没投完，炸弹先不落——这不是克制，是排期。战争在这里不是政策，是选举日程表上的一个格子，投完票再填。第三章讲战争叙事如何被武器化：先有结论，再找理由；理由可以晚两天给。

而这场还没打的战争，已经有人在收钱了。同一份简报里，埃克森美孚、壳牌、雪佛龙上一季度合计赚了超过 360 亿美元，NPR 写得明白：伊朗战争推高了油价。炸弹还没落下，利润已经落袋。这就是暴力三角最露骨的样子——potential 是和平，actual 是战争边缘，中间那道差额，被换算成了季度财报上的一行数字。付代价的是海湾地区要躲空袭的普通人，是加油站前多掏钱的司机，不是做决定的人和收钱的人。

简报里还有一条：最高法院明天审天主教教会和科罗拉多州政府资助的学前班项目。争的是宗教学校能不能拒绝接收同性恋和跨性别家长的孩子，而这会违反州反歧视法。科罗拉多为了让几乎所有四岁孩子都能上学前班，把宗教学校也纳了进来，条件是遵守州法。

把这两条放在一起看，机制是同一个：谁有资格定义“我们照顾的是谁”。战争叙事里，被照顾的是“需要被解放的人”；教会案里，被照顾的是“符合教义的儿童”。每一次划线，都有一批人被划到线外，而且划线的人不用付账。

Stevens 倒戈那条也一样。她背书罗杰斯，理由是 El-Sayed 和 Hasan Piker 的关系没处理干净，没能安抚黑人和犹太选民。她的幕僚长和多名员工辞职，密歇根民主党宣布她不再是党员。这是党内路线的一次公开换边，不是什么道德觉醒——她算的是自己这一票在初选之后的残值。

三件事，一条线：决定谁被保护、谁被牺牲的权力，始终握在不用承担后果的人手里。海湾国家在等一个他们无权决定的答复，科罗拉多的四岁孩子在等九个大法官划线，密歇根的选民在等一个已经换边的议员。

选举夜会有结果。石油公司的下一份财报也会有。

The day before the midterms, Trump puts bombing Iran back on the table. NPR's wording: after tomorrow's elections, he may choose to bomb Iran again — an option he has discussed with Israel. Gulf states are bracing. Qatar has spent the past 72 hours shuttling between Iran and the U.S. trying to prevent more fighting.

The timing is the whole story. Ballots aren't counted yet, so the bombs wait — not restraint, scheduling. War here isn't policy, it's a slot on the election calendar, filled in after the vote. Chapter 3's lesson on weaponized war narrative: conclusion first, justification later, and the justification can arrive two days late.

And the war that hasn't happened is already paying out. In the same newsletter: ExxonMobil, Shell and Chevron earned more than $36 billion combined last quarter, and NPR says it plainly — the war in Iran is driving up gas prices. Bombs not yet dropped, profits already banked. This is the Violence Triangle at its most naked: the potential is peace, the actual is the edge of war, and the gap between them gets converted into a line on a quarterly report. The people paying are the ones who might have to dodge airstrikes and the drivers paying more at the pump — not the ones deciding, not the ones collecting.

One more item: the Supreme Court hears arguments tomorrow in the case between the Catholic Church and Colorado's taxpayer-funded pre-K program. The question is whether religious schools can refuse admission to children of gay and trans parents, which would violate the state's anti-discrimination law. To get nearly every four-year-old into pre-K, Colorado let religious schools in — on condition they follow state law.

Put the two stories side by side and the mechanism is identical: who gets to define who counts as cared for. In the war narrative, the cared-for are "people who need liberating." In the church case, the cared-for are "children who fit the doctrine." Every line drawn leaves someone outside it, and the people drawing the line don't pay.

The Stevens defection runs on the same logic. She backs Rogers because El-Sayed didn't do enough to distance himself from Hasan Piker and reassure the Black and Jewish voters who backed her in the primary. Her chief of staff and other staff quit; the Michigan Democratic Party chair says she's no longer a member. This is a public changing of sides inside a party, not a moral awakening — she's pricing the residual value of her own vote after the primary.

Three stories, one line: the power to decide who is protected and who is sacrificed stays with people who bear none of the cost. The Gulf states wait for an answer they have no say in. Colorado's four-year-olds wait for nine justices to draw a line. Michigan voters wait for a representative who has already switched sides.

Election night will produce results. So will the oil companies' next earnings report.
