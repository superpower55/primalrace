街头和选票，都是谁的表达The Street and the Ballot Are Both Someone's Expression

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
人民力量被欢呼时，先问谁被算进人民
When people power is cheered, first ask who counts as the people

这条新闻只有一张图和一句说明：塞尔维亚、巴西、美国中期选举，从街头到投票箱，人们在证明改变是可能的。整篇没有一个人名，没有一件具体的事，没有一个数字。它不报道什么，它只提供一种情绪——希望。

加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual。差额是暴力。这张图做的正好是反过来的事：它把差额抹平，宣布差额正在被缩小，却不告诉我们缩小的方式、缩小的幅度、谁在付代价。这不是报道，是叙事的封装。

把三件完全不同的事并排放在一个句子里，本身就是一次表达的操作。塞尔维亚的街头、巴西的选票、美国的中期选举，被压成同一种「people power」。可「人民」是一个需要被追问的词：谁被算进人民，谁在街头被算在外面，选票箱里投出来的多数，和街头站出来的多数，常常不是同一批人。把两者并称，等于替它们签了一份并不存在的共识。

第三章讲得很清楚，武器化的本质是夺取话语权、夺取解释权、夺取「什么是事实」的制造权。这张图没有制造假事实，它制造的是没有事实——一个只剩方向、没有内容的「希望」。读者读完会觉得自己已经知道发生了什么，其实什么都不知道。这是最省力的一种认知入口：不用撒谎，只要省略。

更值得看的是它把什么算作胜利。街头抗议被算作胜利，因为它被看见了；选票被算作胜利，因为它被数了。可结构层的差额——法律有没有落地、资源有没有重新分配、盘剥的入口有没有被堵上——一样都没提。被看见和被数清，是表达的胜利，不是暴力的缩小。这两件事经常被混为一谈，混的人往往不是抗议者，是画这张图的人。

我不否认有人真的在街上站了很久，有人真的投了票。他们的代价是真的：时间、身体、被逮捕的风险、被邻居举报的风险。这张图把他们的代价收编成一句「change is possible」，然后卖回给他们自己。他们赢了什么，报道没写；他们失去什么，报道也没写。谁的名字没出现，谁的声音缺席——这张图里一个人都没有，这本身就是材料。

真正的人民力量不需要一张图来宣布它存在。需要被宣布的，往往是还没发生或者已经过去的东西。

This piece is one illustration and one line: Serbia, Brazil, the US midterms — from the streets to the ballot box, people are showing that change is possible. No names. No events. No numbers. It doesn't report anything. It supplies a mood called hope.

Galtung's formula is Violence = Potential − Actual. The gap is the violence. This image does the opposite: it smooths the gap over, announces the gap is closing, and tells us nothing about how, how much, or who is paying. That is not reporting. That is narrative packaging.

Putting three entirely different situations into one sentence is itself an act of expression. Street protests in Serbia, ballots in Brazil, a midterm election in the US, compressed into a single "people power." But "the people" is a word that has to be interrogated: who is counted in, who is left outside the street, and whether the majority inside the ballot box is the same majority standing on the street. Calling them one thing signs a consensus on their behalf that does not exist.

Chapter Three is blunt about this: weaponised expression seizes the power to speak, the power to interpret, the power to manufacture what counts as fact. This image doesn't manufacture a false fact. It manufactures the absence of fact — a "hope" with a direction and no content. Readers finish it believing they know what happened when they know nothing. It is the cheapest kind of cognitive entry: no lying required, only omission.

What gets counted as victory is the real tell. A protest counts because it was seen. A ballot counts because it was counted. The structural gap — whether laws are enforced, whether resources are redistributed, whether the extraction points are actually closed — goes unmentioned. Being seen and being counted are victories of expression, not reductions in violence. These two things get conflated constantly, and the people doing the conflating are usually not the ones in the street. They are the ones drawing the picture.

I don't doubt that people stood outside for a long time, that people voted. Their costs were real: hours, bodies, the risk of arrest, the risk of a neighbour's report. The image absorbs those costs into one line — change is possible — and sells it back to them. What they won is unwritten. What they lost is unwritten. Nobody appears in this picture at all, and that is the material.

Real people power does not need an illustration to announce that it exists. What needs announcing is usually what has not happened yet, or what has already passed.
