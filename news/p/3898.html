用“文明”的垃圾袋，打包掉贫穷的人Packaging the Poor in the Trash Bags of 'Civility'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
结构性暴力最擅长的就是将“清除弱者”包装成“维护秩序”。
Structural violence excels at framing the 'cleansing of the weak' as 'maintaining order'.

Westminster council 的这套操作是典型的 structural violence。他们没有直接用暴力驱赶流浪者，而是使用了更高级的 weaponized 表达：噪音、垃圾、安全。当一个权力机构开始谈论“环境卫生”和“公众安全”时，它实际上在定义谁是这个空间里“合法”的存在，而谁是需要被清理的“垃圾”。

最讽刺的是 council 的逻辑：他们声称 soup kitchen 不安全，因为会吸引毒贩和反社会行为。这是一种极其卑劣的共谋叙事——将受害者的聚集定义为危险的根源，从而通过打击救济者来完成对弱势群体的物理隔离。在他们的叙事里，提供热餐的志愿者成了“制造混乱”的共谋者，而那些因为饥饿而聚集的人则成了破坏伦敦心脏地带“文明”的污点。

从罚单到垃圾处理许可证，这些 administrative obstacles 是精准的武器。他们不禁止你救人，但他们让救人的 cost 高到让小型志愿者组织崩溃。这本质上是一场存在性战争：council 想要的是一个只有消费、没有贫穷可见的 Soho 和 West End。在这种 masculine-centric 的统治逻辑下，弱势者的生存权必须让位于商业地段的“美学”和中产阶级的“舒适”。

所谓的“尊严”被 council 挂在嘴边，但真正的尊严不是在被驱逐时被温柔地告知，而是拥有不被饥饿威胁、不被视为城市垃圾的权利。当权力者决定用“管理”来掩盖“清洗”时，这种文明的伪装本身就是最深的元暴力。

The operation by Westminster council is a textbook example of structural violence. They aren't using direct force to purge the homeless; instead, they employ weaponized expressions: noise, litter, and safety. When a power structure begins discussing 'environmental hygiene' and 'public safety,' it is actually defining who is 'legally' permitted in a space and who is the 'trash' that needs to be cleared.

The council's logic is particularly cynical: they claim soup kitchens are unsafe because they attract drug dealers and antisocial behavior. This is a vile narrative of complicity—defining the gathering of victims as the source of danger to justify the removal of relief efforts. In their narrative, volunteers providing hot meals are transformed into 'disruptors,' while the hungry are framed as stains on the 'civility' of the heart of London.

From parking fines to costly waste disposal licenses, these administrative obstacles are precision weapons. They don't ban the act of helping; they simply raise the cost of survival until small volunteer organizations collapse. This is an existential war: the council desires a Soho and West End where consumption is visible, but poverty is not. Under this masculine-centric logic of governance, the survival rights of the vulnerable must yield to the 'aesthetics' of commercial districts and the 'comfort' of the middle class.

'Dignity' is tossed around by the council, but true dignity isn't being told gently that you're being evicted; it's the right to not be threatened by hunger or viewed as urban refuse. When power uses 'management' to mask 'cleansing,' the veneer of civility becomes the most profound form of meta-violence.
