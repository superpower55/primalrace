朋克史把黑人删成了注脚Punk History Erased the Black Band That Made It

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要Bad Brains 1979年在 Inner Ear 录音室录制的六首歌小样被 Trust Records 重新母带处理，以《Are You Ready: The First Studio Recording》为名发行。报道回顾了乐队从马里兰 Central High 出发、被华盛顿多家俱乐部以「煽动骚乱」为由禁演、以及主唱 HR 因大麻案入狱期间用电话录唱的经历。吉他手 Miller 正从中风和癌症中恢复，HR 在接受治疗，乐队不太可能再演出。
The newsBad Brains' six-song 1979 demo, recorded at Inner Ear Studio, has been remastered and released by Trust Records as Are You Ready: The First Studio Recording. The Guardian's profile traces the band from a Maryland high school to being banned from Washington clubs for allegedly inciting riots, and HR recording vocals by phone from jail. Guitarist Miller is recovering from a stroke and cancer; HR is in treatment.
他们改写了朋克的肤色，历史却把肤色改了回去
They changed punk's face; the record put the face back in the footnotes.

「谁是第一个、谁最快」——老朋克为这些吵不完，但看过 Bad Brains 现场的人在这件事上不吵。报道说他们是这个星球上最好的现场乐队，YouTube 上的影像作证：HR 翻着跟头，乐队在后面烧。这不是怀旧，这是记录。

真正值得说的是他们出现的位置。七十年代末的朋克场景里，年轻、黑人、信拉斯塔法里、读拿破仑·希尔，他们是 outlier。报道里 Jenifer 的堂姐妹说得很准：「Darryl 听的是你跳不了舞的白人男孩音乐。」而 Miller 最后那句话才是刀：他们该去翻摇滚史，那里面全是 Muddy Waters 那些人，不是 Elvis Presley。一个黑人乐队做出最快最狠的朋克，最后要提醒采访者摇滚乐的账本记在谁名下。

这就是认知入口的运作方式。Bad Brains 影响了 Rage Against the Machine、Deftones、Lil Jon、the Roots、Beastie Boys，把美国朋克的脸改了，然后那半张脸在主流叙事里被慢慢擦掉，剩下「hardcore 的奠基者之一」这种安全头衔。书写历史的人不否认你，只是不把你放在你该在的位置上。

报道也没替他们遮丑。和 Big Boys 的冲突、Bob Mould 回忆的恐同遭遇、1989 年那首后来被乐队自己否认的歌——这些不是花边，是同一支乐队的一部分。Jenifer 现在的说法是年轻时的狂热，他说自己被教导要爱所有人。这话可以信，但不必替他打折：被伤害的人不需要因为施暴者后来成长了就撤回记忆。

另一个不该滑过去的细节是 HR 入狱那段。他因为大麻案服刑，Ron Saint Germain 提议他在电话里唱《Sacred Love》的人声。他平时拿拖把擦地，周日争取到时间，说「行，我试试」。一个乐队最锋利时期的录音，是从监狱的电话线里出来的。这不是浪漫，这是国家机器在具体的人身上收的税——收税的人不会出现在任何一张专辑的致谢里。

所以这张再版真正做了什么？它把一段被稀释的记录重新放回原位：1979年，Don Zientara 的录音室，六首歌，来得快去得也快，Zientara 自己都说「我录过这些人吗」。这是 cultural 层的一点修补，不是胜利。Miller 在中风和癌症之后恢复，HR 在治疗 Sunct 综合征和分裂情感性障碍，乐队大概率不会再演。

差额还在。他们被华盛顿的俱乐部以「煽动骚乱」为由赶出去的时候，俱乐部老板说的是「你们把孩子弄疯了」——孩子疯了是真的，但疯的不是被煽动的，是终于被允许疯的那部分。那部分至今没有对应的场地，只有一张再版小样。

Old punks argue about who was first and who was fastest. Nobody who saw Bad Brains argues about this. The Guardian calls them the best live band on the planet, and the YouTube footage backs it up: HR backflipping, the band burning behind him. That isn't nostalgia. That's a record.

What matters is where they stood. Young, Black, Rastafari, reading Napoleon Hill in a late-70s punk scene that had no slot for them. Jenifer's cousins nailed it: Darryl listens to white boy music you can't dance to. And Miller's closing line is the knife: go back to the history of rock'n'roll, it's all Muddy Waters and them guys, not Elvis Presley. A Black band makes the fastest, hardest punk there is, then has to remind the interviewer whose ledger rock'n'roll is written in.

That's how the cognitive entrance works. Bad Brains inspired Rage Against the Machine, Deftones, Lil Jon, the Roots, Beastie Boys. They changed the face of American punk, and then half that face got sanded down to a safe title: one of the founders of hardcore. The people who write history don't deny you. They just don't put you where you belong.

The piece doesn't airbrush them either. The clashes with Big Boys, Bob Mould's account of homophobia, the 1989 song the band has since disowned. Jenifer says it was youthful misguided zealotry, that he was taught to love all people. Believe him if you want. But nobody who got hurt has to withdraw the memory because the man grew.

Then there's HR in jail on a marijuana bust, singing Sacred Love down a phone line because Ron Saint Germain suggested it. He mopped floors, got a Sunday off, said OK, I'll give it a try. The sharpest recordings of the band's peak came through a prison phone. That isn't romance. That's the state collecting tax from a specific body, and the collectors never appear in the liner notes.

So what does this reissue actually do? It puts a diluted record back where it was: 1979, Don Zientara's studio, six songs, in and out so fast Zientara asks whether he even recorded them. That's a small repair at the cultural layer. Not a victory. Miller is recovering from a stroke and cancer. HR is in treatment for Sunct syndrome and schizoaffective disorder. The band probably won't play again.

The gap stays open. When the Washington clubs threw them out for inciting riots, the owners said the kids were going crazy. The kids were. Not because they were incited, but because they were finally allowed to be. That part still has no venue. Only a remastered demo.
