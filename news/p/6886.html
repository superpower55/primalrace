关掉法院，暴力就合法了Shut the Courts, and Violence Becomes Legal

国际 直接层 · 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要人权观察组织11月2日发布报告，要求赞比亚政府停止大选后的镇压。报告称，8月13日大选后，警方和军方强迫失踪、任意拘押、殴打反对派成员、律师和记者；反对派领袖布赖恩·蒙杜比莱被控叛国并关押，前内阁成员穆托特韦·卡夫瓦亚在警方突袭中被枪杀，法院在大选两周后突然关闭。
The newsHuman Rights Watch on Sunday called on Zambia to halt its post-election crackdown. It said police and military forcibly disappeared, arbitrarily detained and beat opposition members, lawyers and journalists after the August 13 vote; opposition leader Brian Mundubile is held on treason charges, former minister Mutotwe Kafwaya was shot dead in a police raid, and courts were shut two weeks after the election.
关闭法院不是维稳，是给暴力发许可证。
Closing courts is not stability. It is a license for violence.

赞比亚政府在大选两周后突然关闭全国法院，警察厅说这是「情报主导的预防性措施」，不提供细节。预防什么？人权观察替它说清楚了：预防反对派把选举舞弊告上法庭。

暴力三角里，直接层的拳头需要结构层的合法性才能持续。关闭法院这个动作，是把结构层和直接层焊死：先掐断申诉的通道，再让警察和军队在街头动手。前者是后者的许可证。

于是我们看到这样一条链条：8月13日投票，次日警察突袭蒙杜比莱的家，前内阁成员卡夫瓦亚在突袭中被枪杀；蒙杜比莱本人被控叛国，在总统就职典礼当天还关在里面；另有约十名质疑选举结果的反对派人士被捕。人权观察说，这些人在投票日前后被强迫失踪、任意拘押、殴打、威胁。

「叛国」这个罪名值得停一下。一个人说「我们赢了选举」，然后被控叛国——这不是法律判断，这是把「质疑结果」本身定义为对国家犯罪。定义权在谁手里，谁就赢了一半。另一半靠枪。

人权观察的报告要求释放所有被任意拘押者、允许会见律师、提供医疗、追究安全部队的责任。这些都是对的，但注意它是什么：一份报告，一份呼吁。它没有强制力。呼吁的对象正是被呼吁者本身，这就是人权话语在结构暴力面前的经典困境——它能命名暴力，但命名不等于阻止。

这里没有出现任何女性的名字。报道里被枪杀的、被控叛国的、被突袭的，全是男性政治人物；被逮捕的记者和律师，报道没有交代性别。这不是说赞比亚的镇压只针对男性——而是说，当「政治迫害」被写成一份名单时，名单上通常只有那些被默认代表「政治」的人。谁的身体算政治身体，谁的不算，这个筛选本身就是元暴力在运作。

最后一句：法院关闭了，但总统在体育场宣誓就职，仪式照常。秩序没有崩塌，秩序正在被使用。

Two weeks after the election, Zambia's government suddenly closed the country's courts. The police called it an "intelligence-led and preventive measure" and gave no details. Preventive of what? Human Rights Watch supplies the answer the police withheld: preventive of the opposition taking vote-rigging claims to a judge.

In the Violence Triangle, the fist at the direct layer needs legitimacy at the structural layer to keep going. Shutting the courts welds the two together: cut the appeals channel first, then let police and soldiers work the streets. The first is the permit for the second.

So here is the chain: polls on August 13, police raid Mundubile's home the next day, former cabinet member Mutotwe Kafwaya shot dead during the operation; Mundubile himself charged with treason and still detained on inauguration day; about a dozen other opposition figures who questioned the results arrested. HRW says they were forcibly disappeared, arbitrarily detained, beaten and threatened around the vote.

Stop on the word treason. A man says "we won the election" and is charged with treason. That is not a legal judgment; it is defining "questioning the result" as a crime against the state. Whoever holds the definition wins half the game. Guns win the other half.

HRW asks for the release of all arbitrarily detained, access to lawyers, medical care, accountability for security forces. All correct, and all of it is a report, an appeal. It has no enforcement. The addressee of the appeal is the same authority being appealed against. That is the classic bind of human rights language before structural violence: it can name the violence, but naming is not stopping.

No woman's name appears in this story. The shot, the treason charge, the raid: all male political figures. The arrested journalists and lawyers are not identified by sex. This is not to say the crackdown targets only men, but that when political persecution is written as a list, the list usually contains only those who are assumed to represent "politics." Whose body counts as a political body is itself a filter, and that filter is meta-violence at work.

The courts are shut, but the president was sworn in at a stadium and the ceremony went ahead. Order did not collapse. Order is being used.
