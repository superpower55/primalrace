空调缺失不是意外，而是结构性暴力的定价权Air Conditioning Gap: The Pricing of Structural Violence

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
所谓的“环境敌对”其实是资源分配的暴力：Potential − Actual = 暴力。
What is called a 'hostile environment' is actually the violence of resource allocation: Potential − Actual = Violence.

当医院病房温度飙升至 42°C，手术被迫取消，护士晕厥，媒体在讨论“气候危机”或“基础设施老化”时，其实是在掩盖一个简单的公理：Violence = Potential − Actual。一个现代医疗系统本应达到的安全标准（Potential）与现实中破败的设施（Actual）之间的差额，就是最纯粹的 structural violence。

这不是简单的“没钱”或“运气不好”，而是一场关于定价权的博弈。在 NHS 的资源分配模型里，谁的生存权被定价最高？谁的舒适度被视为“奢侈品”？当冷却单元在屋顶失效，当救护车无法维持药物温度，这说明在决策者的认知入口中，底层医护和弱势患者的身体耐受力被预设成了“可牺牲的成本”。

最讽刺的是那些试图通过“放宽着装规定”让员工穿短裤来解决问题的叙事。这是一种典型的 cultural violence：用微小的、表演性的让步（permission）来掩盖结构性的剥夺（deprivation）。穿短裤不能让病房降温，也不能让手术室恢复运转。这种“人性化”的补丁，本质上是在诱导受害者接受一个低于生存底线的现状，将其转化为一种“共同面对困难”的共谋叙事。

如果一个社会能为金融中心地标楼的恒温系统投入巨资，却让救命的医院变成“敌对环境”，那么这种“敌对”就不是来自自然界的热浪，而是来自权力结构对特定人群的冷漠。这种差额，就是暴力。

When hospital wards hit 42°C and surgeries are scrapped, the media frames it as a 'climate crisis' or 'aging infrastructure.' In reality, this is a textbook case of structural violence. According to Galtung's formula, Violence = Potential − Actual. The gap between the safety standards a modern healthcare system should provide (Potential) and the dilapidated reality (Actual) is where the violence resides.

This isn't a mere lack of funding; it's a game of pricing power. In the NHS resource allocation model, whose survival is priced highest? When cooling units fail on the roof, it reveals that the physical endurance of frontline staff and vulnerable patients has been factored in as an 'acceptable cost' in the cognitive entrance of decision-makers.

Most cynical is the narrative of 'relaxing dress codes' to let staff wear shorts. This is a classic move of cultural violence: using a tiny, performative concession (permission) to mask a structural deprivation. Shorts don't cool a ward or fix an operating theatre. This 'humanized' patch is designed to trick the victims into accepting a sub-survival baseline, framing systemic failure as a 'shared struggle.'

If a society can afford precision climate control for financial district skyscrapers but allows hospitals to become 'dangerously hostile,' the hostility isn't coming from the heatwave—it's coming from the power structure. That gap is the violence.
