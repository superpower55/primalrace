「选择国家而非仇恨」的定价权The Pricing Power of "Country Over Hate"

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 直接层 Vanity Fair ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要密歇根州民主党众议员 Haley Stevens 公开背书共和党人 Mike Rogers，称这是「选择国家而非仇恨」，并否认此举反穆斯林。Rogers 则把对手 Abdul El-Sayed 描绘成穆斯林兄弟会的工具。报道回顾了特朗普、Newsmax、RFK Jr. 等人围绕 El-Sayed 的穆斯林身份制造的攻击，以及这类叙事如何为针对穆斯林和阿拉伯世界的战争铺路。
The newsMichigan Democratic congresswoman Haley Stevens endorsed Republican Mike Rogers, framing it as choosing "country over hate" and denying it was anti-Muslim. Rogers has cast his opponent Abdul El-Sayed as a tool of the Muslim Brotherhood. The report traces how Trump, Newsmax and RFK Jr. have weaponized El-Sayed's Muslim identity, and how that narrative underwrites war on the Muslim and Arab world.
先把人定义成威胁，再谈宽容。
First define a people as a threat, then talk about tolerance.

「我选择国家而非仇恨」，这句话的定价权在谁手里，决定了它是宽容还是武器。Stevens 背书 Rogers 时说了这句话，还补一句「这完全不反穆斯林」。谁需要补这一句，谁心里清楚这句话在替什么打掩护。

Rogers 的竞选主线只有一条：把 El-Sayed 说成穆斯林兄弟会的工具，说成东方阴影势力伸进美国国会的手。他讲「我用多年追捕恐怖分子、把本·拉登绳之以法」，然后说「从没想过会和一个认为美国活该经历 9/11 的人竞选」——那句话是 Hasan Piker 说的，El-Sayed 已经否认过。事实不是重点，重点是「穆斯林」这个词被当成侮辱性标签来用。特朗普把对手的名字念成「Abduladadalalalala」，或者干脆叫「Mohamed」，还说「我是总统，我想怎么叫就怎么叫」。

这就是第三章说的认知入口：先夺取「谁是威胁」的定义权，再夺取「什么是事实」的制造权。RFK Jr. 给这套粗鄙话术披上「知识」外衣，说五亿阿拉伯人「不产出科学，不拿诺贝尔奖」。一个脑子被虫子感染过的人讲这种话，不值得辩论；值得看的是它服务什么。

报道写得很清楚：这场仇恨竞选的目标，是让穆斯林和阿拉伯人变得更可杀。1953 年 CIA 支持政变推翻伊朗的民主政府；1965 年支持印尼政变，杀了五十万到一百万人。冷战时的理由是共产主义威胁，9/11 之后的理由是种族主义。理由换了，死的还是同一批人。

McCain 2008 年对支持者说奥巴马「不是阿拉伯人，他是一个体面的顾家男人」，这句话被当成爱国举动反复引用。但「体面的人在一处、阿拉伯人在另一处」这个前提，本身就是暴力三段论的大前提：婚礼被无人机炸、政府被推翻、女学生被炸死、碍事的人被清除。

Stevens 不是被党内斗争逼走的。报道说，她自称在梦里看见的那个国家，才是她投向 Rogers 的原因。她醒来，选择了暴力。

共谋者理论在这里不需要点名：Stevens 用一个黑人教堂做舞台，借社会正义的光环，替一套让特定族群更可杀的叙事背书。她得到的回报是中间选民的票，付代价的人不在她的选区里。

报道从头到尾没有出现一个巴勒斯坦人或加沙人的名字，只有「集中营」和「每一颗从美国制造的飞机上投下的炸弹」。谁的名字不出现，谁的声音缺席，本身就是这篇报道最该被追问的部分。

"I'm choosing country over hate." Who holds the pricing power over that sentence decides whether it is tolerance or a weapon. Stevens said it while endorsing Rogers, then added, "This is not anti-Muslim at all." Whoever needs to add that clause knows exactly what the sentence is covering for.

Rogers's campaign has one line: El-Sayed is a tool of the Muslim Brotherhood, a hand from shadowy Eastern forces reaching into the US Congress. He says he spent years hunting terrorists and bringing bin Laden to justice, then says he never imagined running against someone who believes America deserved 9/11. Hasan Piker said that, not El-Sayed, who has disavowed it. Facts are not the point. "Muslim" is the slur.

Trump mangles the name into "Abduladadalalalala," or just "Mohamed," and says, "I'm the president, I can call him whatever the hell I want." This is the cognitive entry point from Chapter 3: seize the power to define who is a threat, then seize the power to manufacture what counts as fact. RFK Jr. gives the crude version intellectual cover, saying 500 million Arabs "don't produce science, they don't win the Nobel Prize." A man whose brain was infected by a worm is not worth debating. What his line serves is worth watching.

The report is explicit: the goal of this hate campaign is to make Muslims and Arabs more killable. In 1953 the CIA backed a coup against Iran's democracy. In 1965 it backed a coup in Indonesia that killed between 500,000 and a million people. During the Cold War the justification was communism; after 9/11 it is racism. The reason changes. The dead are the same.

McCain in 2008 told a supporter Obama was not an Arab, he was "a decent family man." That line gets quoted as patriotism. But the premise that decent people live in one place and Arabs in another is the major premise of a violent syllogism: wedding parties droned, governments overthrown, schoolgirls bombed, inconvenient people eliminated.

Stevens was not pushed out by party intrigue. The report says the state she claims to have seen in her dreams is what drove her into Rogers's arms. She woke up and chose violence.

The co-conspirator theory needs no naming here. Stevens staged the endorsement in a Black church, borrowing the aura of social justice to endorse a narrative that makes a specific people more killable. Her reward is centrist votes. The people who pay are not in her district.

Not one Palestinian or Gazan name appears in the report, only "the concentration camp of Gaza" and bombs dropped from American-made planes. Whose names are missing, whose voices are absent—that absence is the part of this story still owed an answer.
