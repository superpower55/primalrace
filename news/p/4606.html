泥石流掩埋的不是房屋，是结构性的遗忘Mudslides Bury More Than Homes: The Quantified Silence of Structural Violence

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-28 § 链接
自然灾害的死亡率是结构暴力的量化指标。
Disaster mortality rates are simply the quantified metrics of structural violence.

很多人把尼泊尔的闪洪看作是 unpredictable 的自然灾害，但这正是文化暴力最成功的掩体：用“天灾”这个词，把 Potential 与 Actual 之间的差额抹除。当泥石流冲走 Bidur 的房屋时，被冲走的不仅是砖瓦，更是这个地区长期被剥夺的基建权与生存资源。这种死亡不是随机的，而是 structural violence 的必然结果。

在加尔通的暴力三角里，直接层是汹涌的泥石流，但支撑它的结构层是全球资源分配的极度不均，以及一个被长期殖民、边缘化的地缘政治背景。为什么这里的房屋会被“完全冲走”？因为在资本和权力的定价权里，这些人的生命被定义为“可承受的损失”。这种定义权就是元暴力——它决定了谁生活在易碎的河谷，谁拥有坚固的堤坝。

那些在树上挣扎求生的幸存者，他们面对的是一场关于存在性的绝望博弈。在这样一个系统里，底层民众唯一的“最优解表达”竟然是祈祷和攀爬。这根本不是什么生存韧性，而是一场被结构性贫困逼入死角的生存战争。如果一个社会的生存底线如此之低，那么任何关于“人道主义救援”的叙事，都不过是掩盖结构性掠夺的 PR 行为。

Most view the Nepal flash floods as an unpredictable natural disaster. This is exactly how cultural violence operates as a shield: using the term "natural disaster" to erase the gap between Potential and Actual. When mudslides wiped out Bidur, it wasn't just bricks and mortar that vanished, but the long-denied right to infrastructure and survival resources. These deaths are not random; they are the inevitable output of structural violence.

In Galtung's Violence Triangle, the direct layer is the rushing mud, but the structural layer is the extreme inequality of global resource distribution and a geo-political background of long-term marginalization. Why were the homes "completely washed away"? Because in the pricing power of capital and power, these lives are defined as "acceptable losses." This power of definition is meta-violence—it decides who lives in a fragile valley and who owns the reinforced dam.

Those survivors clinging to trees are trapped in a desperate existential game. In such a system, the only "optimal expression" available to the marginalized is prayer and climbing. This isn't "resilience"; it's a survival war forced into a dead end by structural poverty. When the baseline of survival is this low, any narrative of "humanitarian aid" is merely a PR exercise to mask systemic plunder.
