披风飞了，领舞没飞The Cape Flew, the Lead Didn't

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要在《与星共舞》迪士尼之夜，奥运花样滑冰选手 Amber Glenn 穿金色胸甲配白色披风，扮演《大力士》中的 Hercules 跳快步舞，披风在关键动作时飞到脸上，她坚持跳完，评委给出 40 分中的 34 分。节目中她领舞男搭档 Pasha Pashkov，评委称这是该节目历史上首次由女性领舞男性。
The newsOn Dancing With the Stars' Disney Night, Olympic figure skater Amber Glenn danced a Hercules-themed quickstep in a gold chest plate and white cape; the cape flew into her face mid-routine but she finished and scored 34 out of 40. She led her male partner Pasha Pashkov, which judge Carrie Ann Inaba said was the first time a woman has led a man on the show.
她抢的不是领舞位，是表达的定价权
What she seized wasn't the lead, it was the right to price her own expression.

这条新闻里真正值得看的不是披风飞到脸上，是评委那句「节目历史上第一次由女性领舞男性」。三十多季的节目，第一次。披风只是意外，领舞是差额。

Glenn 在赛前说，她以前害怕自己的肌肉，觉得自己不够优雅，现在不怕了，要让 Pashkov 看看这段搭档里谁才是真正的 Hercules。这句话是整条新闻里最有分量的部分。害怕肌肉，是女性被规训得最标准的一种表达：力量被读成不优雅，不优雅就被读成不合格。她花了很多年才把这条内化的评分标准从自己脑子里拆掉。

Pashkov 在赛前片段里说「我把缰绳交给你」，这句台词本身也值得看。权力是被「交」出来的，交的人还是那个一直握着的人。节目把女性领舞处理成一个奇观，一个可以放进预告片的卖点，而不是一个早就该是常态的事。奇观的意思是：它稀有，所以它安全。

服装也在这条线上。金色胸甲、白色披风、扮演 Hercules——她选择的表达是力量型的、英雄式的，而不是公主裙。她自己说从来不是穿公主裙的那种人。在一个把女性舞者默认塞进蓬蓬裙的节目里，这个选择本身就是一次小小的夺权。但披风不听话，飞到她脸上，这个细节反而说明：英雄装扮也是被设计出来的道具，道具会出错，出错的时候撑住场面的是她的身体，不是那件披风。

34 分，评委给了 8 和 9。分数不低，但也没有因为「历史首次」而破格。这大概是对的：真正的公正不是给女性领舞发一个同情分，是有一天女性领舞不再需要被评委当作历史事件宣布出来。

披风飞回背上，她继续跳完了。这条新闻里还没有被缩小的是那个「第一次」——第一次之后，第二次在哪里，第三十次在哪里，什么时候它不再是一条新闻。

The real story here isn't the cape hitting her face. It's the judge's line that this was the first time in the show's history a woman has led a man. Thirty-plus seasons. First time. The cape was an accident; the lead was the gap.

Glenn said before the dance that she used to be scared of her muscles, thought she wasn't graceful enough, and that she isn't scared anymore. That sentence carries the most weight in the whole piece. Being afraid of your own muscles is one of the most standard forms of feminine training: strength gets read as graceless, graceless gets read as failing. It took her years to pull that internalized scorecard out of her own head.

Pashkov's pre-dance line — I'll give you the reins of power — is worth a second look too. Power is handed over, and the one handing it over is still the one who held it. The show treats a woman leading as a spectacle, a selling point for the promo, not as something that should have been normal all along. Spectacle means: it's rare, so it's safe.

The costume sits on the same line. Gold chest plate, white cape, playing Hercules — she chose a strength-coded, heroic expression instead of a princess dress. She said she was never the princess-dress type. In a show that defaults its female dancers into tulle, that choice is a small seizure of power. But the cape misbehaved and flew into her face, which is its own reminder: the hero outfit is also a designed prop, props fail, and what held the routine together was her body, not the cape.

A 34, with 8s and 9s. Not low, but not inflated for the historic first either. That's probably right: justice isn't a sympathy score for a woman leading. It's the day a woman leading stops being announced by a judge as a historical event.

The cape found its way back. She kept dancing. What hasn't been narrowed here is that first — where's the second, the thirtieth, and when does it stop being news.
