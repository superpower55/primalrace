乐园的糖霜，工人的账单Sugar Coating, Workers' Bills

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
利润在台上，代价在台下，这就是共谋。
Profits on stage, costs off stage: that is complicity.

一个做餐饮的工人，在台上的人讲利润和利润率的时候，冲上去说自己在车里、在园区里过夜。这件事最刺眼的地方不是打断，是打断之前的那套空间安排：水晶吊灯下面谈娱乐法的未来，吊灯照不到的后厨里，做饭的人算着今天能不能吃上饭。

这不是个案，是结构暴力在按小时结算。Chris Lillian 时薪 24 美元，去年平均每周 40 小时，今年约 32 小时，今年已经瘦了 20 磅。Heather Saxxon 时薪 23 美元，2025 年的“年度团队成员”，同时开 Lyft 和 HopSkipDrive 补贴生活，有时停掉自己的药。Cat 时薪 28 美元，一周不到 30 小时，今年差点因为交不上房租被赶走。工会调查里，828 个受访者中六成多在食品、药、住房之间做选择，近 53% 饿着也不吃，超过四分之一在领食品援助。两年前 UCLA 劳工中心的数据是 67%、53%、四分之一——数字几乎没动。2024 年那份合同给了 23% 的加薪，Miguel Jauregui 说得对：起点接近最低工资，三年摊下来 23% 不叫 23%，何况工时在缩。

工时为什么缩，报道里管理方没有解释，只说还在招新人、新人要记培训工时。一边砍老人的钟点，一边进新人，这不是经营困难的自然结果，是选择。选择让谁的时间值钱、让谁的时间随时可以归零。

再看上面的数字。2025 年 Comcast 联席 CEO Mark Cavanagh 薪酬 7180 万美元，是 2024 年的 150% 以上；Brian Roberts 3510 万美元，涨 4%。管乐园的 Mark Woodbury 的薪酬不向股东披露。第三季度主题乐园部门收入降到 26 亿美元，降 4.4%，公司归因于宏观环境和奥兰多新园的能力问题。利润薄了，先薄在做饭的人身上。

Donna Langley 说她不管主题乐园，这话技术上准确。但她在台上代表公司讲利润，台下的人代表公司做饭、卖黄油啤酒冰淇淋，两者是同一张资产负债表的两端。她说会转告对的人——这就是共谋的日常语法：责任被切块，切到每一块都不足以让任何一个人负责。Rodriguez 问她的话其实很简单：我流了血汗，你代表公司，那这算什么。

工会 9 月投票，98% 以上授权罢工。这是结构性弱势者少数还能用的工具：把分散的、被切成 32 小时一周的个体，重新拼成一个能谈判的单位。但谈判还在进行，合同已经过期近九个月，罢工授权攥在手里还没打出去。

Rodriguez 说他喜欢这份工作，喜欢看游客脸上的笑。这句话不是温情，是账单：一个人的喜欢，被定价成了可以睡在车里、冬天回墨西哥住的成本。乐园卖的是快乐，快乐的原料是有人算着下一顿在哪。糖霜越厚，账单越清楚。

A food-service worker walked up to the stage while the keynote was still talking margins and said he sleeps in his car, sometimes in the park. The jarring part isn't the interruption. It's the seating chart that made the interruption necessary: crystal chandeliers over a conversation about the future of entertainment law, and behind the kitchens, the people cooking the food counting whether they can eat today.

This is not an individual case. It is structural violence billed by the hour. Chris Lillian makes $24 an hour; last year he averaged around 40 hours a week, this year roughly 32, and he has lost 20 pounds this year. Heather Saxxon makes $23 an hour, was named 2025 Team Member of the Year, drives for Lyft and HopSkipDrive to stay afloat, and sometimes skips her medications. Cat makes $28 an hour, works a bit under 30 hours a week, and nearly got evicted earlier this year. In the union's survey, of 828 respondents, a little over 60 percent had to choose between food, medications or housing; nearly 53 percent went hungry rather than spend the money; more than a quarter received food assistance. Two years ago the UCLA Labor Center found 67 percent, 53 percent, a quarter — the numbers barely moved. The 2024 contract won a 23 percent raise over three years, and Miguel Jauregui is right: starting near minimum wage, 23 percent spread over a contract isn't 23 percent, especially when hours are being cut.

Why the hours are cut, management has not explained, per the union — while still hiring new workers who must log training hours. Cutting veterans' shifts while bringing in rookies is not the natural result of a bad quarter. It is a choice about whose time is worth what, and whose time can be zeroed out.

Now the numbers above. In 2025, Comcast co-CEO Mark Cavanagh received $71.8 million, more than 150 percent of his 2024 pay; co-CEO Brian Roberts received $35.1 million, a 4 percent raise. Compensation for Mark Woodbury, who manages the parks, is not disclosed to shareholders. Theme parks division revenue fell 4.4 percent to $2.6 billion in the third quarter, which the company attributed to the macroeconomic environment and capacity issues at the Orlando park. When the margin thins, it thins first on the people cooking.

Donna Langley said the parks aren't under her purview. Technically accurate. But she was on stage representing the company's profits, and the man in the back was representing the company's kitchens, selling Butterbeer Ice Cream — two ends of the same balance sheet. She said she would make sure the right people were aware. That is the everyday grammar of complicity: responsibility sliced until no single slice is large enough for anyone to be responsible. Rodriguez's question was simple. I gave blood and sweat to this company. You represent the company. So what is this?

In September, more than 98 percent of voting bargaining unit members authorized a strike. It is one of the few tools the structurally weak still have: reassembling individuals chopped into 32-hour weeks into a unit that can negotiate. But talks continue, the contract expired nearly nine months ago, and the authorization sits unused in their hands.

Rodriguez says he loves the job, loves seeing the smiles on people's faces. That isn't warmth. It's an invoice: one man's love, priced at sleeping in a car and wintering in Mexico. The park sells joy. The raw material is someone calculating where the next meal comes from. The thicker the sugar coating, the clearer the bill.
