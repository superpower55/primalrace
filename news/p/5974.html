记忆不是反抗，谁在替谁记忆Memory Is Not Resistance. Who Remembers for Whom?

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 IndieWire ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
专制先删记忆，再卖你一部关于记忆的纪录片
Authoritarians erase memory, then sell you a documentary about memory.

阿杭古拉尼说得对：伊朗的专制者不只审查人，还试图抹掉记忆，用他们自己的版本替换历史。这句话本身没有错。错的是它被说出来、被录下来、被包装成一部由迪士尼和國家地理联合体发行的纪录片，然后在一个洛杉矶的活动上被当作反抗宣言播放。

记忆是一种表达，而表达从来不是中立的。谁来决定记住什么、怎么记住、由谁来记住——这是第三章的问题：认知入口。国家机器抢的是书写历史的权力，而一部关于“记忆即革命”的纪录片，抢的是同一块地皮，只是换了个招牌。阿杭古拉尼被流放到伦敦，她的记忆因此获得了发行渠道；伊朗国内那些没有被流放、没有摄像机、没有英语采访的人，他们的记忆没有进入这部片子。这不是她的错，但这是这部片子的结构。

“记住受害者，哀悼他们，然后才能开始疗愈”——这套叙事听着很干净。但疗愈是谁的疗愈？观众坐在洛杉矶的放映厅里，看完一部关于伊朗记忆的片子，获得一种道德上的舒展感，然后散场。而伊朗国内那些正在被抹掉记忆的人，没有因此多出一分钟的发言权。文化暴力最擅长的就是把结构问题转化成情感体验，让你觉得你参与了反抗，其实你只是消费了反抗的叙事。

国家地理纪录片部门是迪士尼和国家地理学会的合资企业，它的片子覆盖180个国家、33种语言、3亿人。这个体量本身就是一种解释权。当一部反抗专制的纪录片由这个体量的机构发行，它就不再只是反抗，它同时也在定义什么叫反抗、谁有资格被看见、什么样的记忆值得被记住。这不是阴谋，这是结构。

片子开头那个词“Yad”——记忆与纪念。记住是少数我们还能做的事。但记住什么、由谁记住、记住之后谁来付账，这些问题没有被问出来。阿杭古拉尼说专制者试图改写历史，她说得对。但改写历史的权力从来不只在一个国家手里，它也在发行渠道、在电影节、在流媒体平台的推荐算法里。那些没有被拍进片子的伊朗人，他们的记忆还在等一个入口。

Ahangurani is right: Iran's authoritarians don't just censor people, they try to erase memory and replace it with their own version of history. That sentence is correct. What's wrong is that it gets said, recorded, packaged into a documentary co-released by Disney and National Geographic, and played at a Los Angeles event as a statement of resistance.

Memory is an expression, and expression is never neutral. Who decides what gets remembered, how, and by whom — that's the question of the cognitive entry point. The state machine fights for the power to write history; a documentary about "memory as revolution" fights for the same territory under a different banner. Ahangurani was exiled to London, and her memory therefore acquired a distribution channel. The people inside Iran who were not exiled, who have no camera, no English-language interview — their memories did not make it into this film. That's not her fault, but it is the film's structure.

"Remember the victims, mourn them, and only then can healing begin" — this narrative sounds clean. But whose healing? The audience sits in a Los Angeles screening room, watches a film about Iranian memory, feels a moral unclenching, and goes home. The people inside Iran whose memories are being erased right now did not gain a single extra minute of voice from this. Cultural violence's favorite trick is converting structural problems into emotional experiences, making you feel you participated in resistance when you only consumed its narrative.

National Geographic Documentary Films is a joint venture between Disney and the National Geographic Society. Its films reach 300 million people in 180 countries and 33 languages. That scale is itself a form of interpretive power. When a documentary about resisting tyranny is distributed at that scale, it is no longer only resistance — it is also defining what resistance looks like, who gets to be seen, which memories are worth remembering. This isn't a conspiracy. It's a structure.

The film opens with "Yad" — memory and remembrance. Remembering is one of the few things we have left. But what gets remembered, by whom, and who pays for it afterward — these questions don't get asked. Ahangurani says authoritarians try to rewrite history. She's right. But the power to rewrite history has never belonged to one country alone. It also lives in distribution channels, in film festivals, in streaming recommendation algorithms. The Iranians who didn't make it into the film are still waiting for an entry point.
