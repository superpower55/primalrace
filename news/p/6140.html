丑萌不是美学，是定价权Ugly-Cute Isn't an Aesthetic, It's Pricing Power

文娱 文化层 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
谁定义丑，谁就定义了谁该被看
Whoever defines ugly also defines who deserves to be seen.

一个怪物短片被写成"ugly-cute"，这条新闻里唯一值得拆的东西就在这个词上。

丑和可爱本来是两套评价体系，把它们焊在一起需要一个前提：先有人拥有给事物贴标签的权力。谁有权说一个东西丑？谁有权说它可爱？谁有权宣布两者可以叠加、并且这种叠加是一种值得消费的品味？报道没有回答，因为报道本身就在执行这个动作——它替读者完成了"这是丑萌"的判定，读者只需要点头。

这就是第三章说的审美入口。品味从来不是中性的个人偏好，它是筛选机制：定义"什么是高级的、什么是土的、什么是丑得可爱的"，等于在人群里划线，把能接住这个梗的人划进圈内，把接不住的人划在外面。时尚媒体把"松弛感""老钱风"写成新的高级审美，靠的是同一套操作——先由有话语权的一方给出标签，再让普通人争着认领，认领的过程就是自我筛选的过程。

报道里说这个短片"从标题角色出现在屏幕上的第二秒起"就证明了喜剧和恐怖的交叉点。这句话很轻，但它把一件作品的成败压缩成一个视觉瞬间的判断，而这个判断的作者是写稿的人，不是观众。观众被邀请去"填饱"自己的丑萌需求，需求是谁制造的，没人问。

真正的问题不是这个短片好不好笑。是这个"丑萌"标签被投放出来之后，谁在付代价。报道没写。没写谁被这个审美标准排除在外，没写什么样的脸、什么样的身体在这个标准下变成"丑"的那一半，也没写这个标签最终为谁服务。名字缺席的地方，往往就是评论最该停下来的地方。

一个怪物被叫做可爱，前提是它不会真的伤到人。现实里被叫做丑的那些人，没有这个豁免。

A monster short gets written up as "ugly-cute," and that phrase is the only thing in this news worth taking apart.

Ugly and cute are two separate systems of evaluation. Welding them together requires a precondition: someone already holds the power to stick labels on things. Who gets to call a thing ugly? Who gets to call it cute? Who gets to declare the two can stack, and that the stack is a taste worth consuming? The report doesn't answer, because the report is performing the act itself—it hands the reader the verdict "this is ugly-cute," and the reader only has to nod.

This is the aesthetic entry point from Chapter 3. Taste is never neutral personal preference; it's a screening mechanism. Defining what counts as high-class, what counts as tacky, what counts as ugly-cute, is drawing a line through a population—sorting in the people who catch the joke and out the people who don't. Fashion media writing "relaxedness" or "old money" into the new premium aesthetic runs the exact same operation: the side with discursive power issues the label, ordinary people race to claim it, and the claiming is the self-sorting.

The report says the short proves the intersection of comedy and horror "from about the second its title character shows up on screen." That line is light, but it compresses a work's success into a single visual instant—and the author of that judgment is the writer, not the audience. The audience is invited to "get your fill" of ugly-cute. Who manufactured the appetite is not asked.

The real question isn't whether the short is funny. It's who pays once the ugly-cute label is in circulation. The report doesn't say. It doesn't say who gets excluded by this standard, which faces and which bodies land on the "ugly" half of it, or who the label ultimately serves. Where names are absent is usually where the commentary should stop.

A monster can be called cute only on the condition that it can't really hurt anyone. The people called ugly in real life don't get that exemption.
