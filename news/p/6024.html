把恐怖排成榜单，把女人排成布景Ranking Horror, Ranking Women as Set Dressing

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
十三季里女人永远围着男人转，这才是真恐怖
Thirteen seasons of women circling men. That's the real horror.

一篇把十三季《美国恐怖故事》从最差排到最好的稿子，读完我记住的不是哪季好哪季烂，是它替这部剧说出的那句自白：每季都"披着社会评论和坎普的外衣"。外衣这个词用得诚实。

先看它排第一的《谋杀屋》。一对夫妻买下打折的维多利亚式房子来挽救婚姻，十几岁的女儿很快和屋里的Tate发展出一段古怪的关系，隔壁的Constance有自己的算盘。三个女人，一个为婚姻焦虑，一个为男孩神魂颠倒，一个在算计。这就是十五年前让观众决定"一直追下去"的开局。

排第二的《疯人院》更直白。记者Lana Winters因为同性恋被送进精神病院，这是全剧写得最狠的一条线，一个女人的身体被制度收走。同一季里还有自残式堕胎和厌恶疗法，稿子自己都写了。这些恐怖不是编的，是二十世纪精神病院真实干过的事。可它被放进一个"必看榜单"的第二名，和"外星人结局"并列成一段趣闻。

再往下看，模式清楚得不用我拆。《女巫集会》里一群女人争一个"至尊"的位置，真正的恐怖被稿子点明是美国南方的奴隶制，但伏都教的套路"没经受住时间考验"——一句轻飘飘的评语，把被殖民者的宗教打成过时的布景。《旅馆》里Lady Gaga是复仇的吸血鬼，拿了金球奖，稿子夸她。《1984》是青春夏令营加连环杀手，"令人愉快地坎普"。

最该被拿出来说的是第十三季。稿子骂它骂得最狠：为了重聚熟脸和口头禅而生，每几分钟让观众"嘿我记得这个"，每集被砍到二十到三十分钟，Jessica Lange的戏份还没起飞就被剪了翅膀。骂得对。但它骂的是执行，不是结构。一个把历届女主演召回、让她们重复各自招牌角色的季，本质上就是把这群女人做成了可回收的符号，观众认脸，平台收流量。稿子把这叫"自我实现的预言"，我看这是把女人当库存。

然后是第十二季。一个好莱坞女演员在做试管婴儿，坚信有黑暗力量盯上她未出生的孩子。这是《罗斯玛丽的婴儿》的翻版，稿子自己说了。一个关于生育、关于女人的身体被谁支配的故事，被拍成节奏拖沓、情节散乱，观众在Reddit上编理论比看剧还投入。生育这件事在剧里是恐怖的来源，在剧外是收视率的来源，两头都有人赚。

整篇稿子唯一一次让我觉得作者摸到了边，是它说第十一季《纽约》"最悲伤也最恐怖"，因为那一季讲的是八十年代艾滋病的蔓延，病毒盯上的是这座城市的边缘社群。这是唯一一季，恐怖落在了真实死掉的人身上，而不是落在布景上。它排第七。

一份榜单的功能是定价：哪季值钱，哪季不值。这部剧十五年，卖的是恐惧，付账的是屏幕里的女人——被送进精神病院的、被邪灵盯上子宫的、被写成蛇蝎美人的、被召回重聚当彩蛋的。稿子把这一切排得井井有条，读者点开，滑动，记住名次，关掉。

真正没被排进去的那一季，是这些女人不用再围着谁转的那一季。它还没拍出来。

A piece ranking all thirteen seasons of American Horror Story from worst to best. What I remember isn't which season was good — it's the confession the show makes through the piece: every installment comes "cloaked in social commentary and camp." Cloaked is the honest word.

Start with its number one, Murder House. A couple buys a discounted Victorian to salvage their marriage, their teenage daughter quickly develops an odd relationship with Tate, and next-door neighbor Constance has an agenda. Three women: one anxious about a marriage, one swooning over a boy, one scheming. This is the pilot that made viewers decide to keep coming back for fifteen years.

Number two, Asylum, is blunter. Journalist Lana Winters is committed for her homosexuality — the sharpest thread the show ever wrote, a woman's body taken by an institution. The same season has a self-induced abortion and aversion therapy; the piece says so itself. These horrors weren't invented; asylums really did this into the twentieth century. And it gets filed as the second-best entry, its alien finale a fun anecdote.

The pattern below that is clear enough that I don't need to dismantle it. Coven has a pack of women fighting over who gets to be Supreme; the piece names slavery as the season's true horror, then waves off the voodoo tropes as not having aged well — one breezy line turning a colonized religion into period decor. Hotel gives Lady Gaga a Golden Globe as a vengeful vampiress. 1984 is summer camp plus slasher, "delightfully campy."

Season 13 deserves the most attention. The piece savages it: reverse-engineered to reunite familiar faces and catchphrases, making the audience go "hey, I remember that" every few minutes, episodes chopped to twenty or thirty minutes so Jessica Lange's moments have their wings clipped. Fair. But it's attacking execution, not structure. A season that recalls the show's leading women and has them replay their signature roles is, at bottom, turning those women into recyclable symbols — audiences recognize faces, the platform collects traffic. The piece calls it a self-fulfilling prophecy. I call it inventory.

Then season 12. A Hollywood actress on an IVF journey is convinced dark forces are after her unborn child — a Rosemary's Baby dupe, the piece admits. A story about reproduction, about who controls a woman's body, gets made with a glacial pace and incoherent plotting, viewers generating more Reddit theories than scares. Reproduction is the source of horror on screen and the source of ratings off it. Somebody profits at both ends.

The one moment the writer brushes against something real is calling season 11, NYC, "the most sad and horrific entry yet," because it's about AIDS spreading through the city's marginalized communities. The only season where the horror lands on people who actually died rather than on set dressing. It ranks seventh.

A ranking list exists to price things: which season is worth what. Fifteen years of this show sells fear, and the ones paying are the women on screen — committed to asylums, hunted for their wombs, written as femme fatales, recalled as reunion easter eggs. The piece arranges all of it neatly. Readers tap, scroll, memorize the order, close the tab.

The season that never got ranked is the one where none of these women have to circle anyone. It hasn't been made yet.
