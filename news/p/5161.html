在中东的‘大动作’里，只有男人在博弈Only Men are Gaming in the Middle East Crisis

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-20 § 链接
战争叙事是最高级的元暴力：将人体降格为筹码，将毁灭包装成选项。
War narratives are the ultimate meta-violence: degrading human bodies into chips and packaging destruction as 'options'.

特朗普在 Fox News 上的那句“非常大的事情即将发生”，是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。在他构建的认知入口里，中东不是由具体的人、家庭和生存权组成的，而是一个由“抹除”、“烂掉”或“达成交易”构成的博弈棋盘。这种表达方式将数百万人的生命简化为三个选项，这就是元暴力的本质：掌握解释权的人，通过定义“可能性”来剥夺他者的主体性。

这场所谓的“局势升级”其实是一场规模宏大的共谋。从伊朗的革命卫队到胡塞武装，再到沙特的王室，双方都在进行激烈的 sabre rattling。他们通过袭击油田、封锁海峡、发出威胁来增加自己在存在性战争中的“票价”。而美国则在其中扮演一个傲慢的定价者，通过暗示军事打击或突然的冷漠（如拒绝沙特求援）来操纵地区焦虑。这种共谋的回报是权力的再分配，而代价则是被结构性地推向直接暴力的底层平民。

最讽刺的是，在这种宏大叙事中，人们习惯于讨论“战略港口”和“能源供应”，却绝口不提那些在空袭中死去的女性和孩子。在元暴力的逻辑下，这些生命被定义为“附带损害”，因为他们不在男人们的博弈维度之内。战争叙事永远在重复同一个 scam：用某种宏大的意义（如安全、主权、反恐）来合法化对肉体的直接摧毁。

当外交官们在纽约的联合国大会旁开会，讨论如何“管理危机”时，他们实际上是在讨论如何更高效地利用暴力三角。他们试图在 structural 层面上调整资源分配，好让 direct 层的杀戮在 cultural 层面上显得“必要”且“克制”。

Trump's remark to Fox News that “very big things are going to be happening” is a textbook example of a masculine-centric narrative. In the cognitive entry he constructs, the Middle East is not composed of actual people, families, or the right to exist, but a gaming board defined by “wiping out,” “letting them rot,” or “making a deal.” This mode of expression simplifies millions of lives into three options; this is the essence of meta-violence: those who control the interpretation power strip others of their subjectivity by defining the “possibilities.”

This so-called “escalation” is actually a grand-scale complicity. From the IRGC and the Houthis to the Saudi royalty, all parties are engaged in fierce sabre rattling. They use oil facility attacks, strait blockades, and threats to increase the “value of their vote” in this existential war. The US acts as an arrogant price-setter, manipulating regional anxiety through hints of bombing or sudden indifference (such as spurning Saudi pleas for help). The reward for this complicity is the redistribution of power, while the cost is the civilian population structurally pushed toward direct violence.

The irony is that in such grand narratives, the discourse focuses on “strategic ports” and “energy supplies,” while the women and children killed in airstrikes are silenced. Under the logic of meta-violence, these lives are defined as “collateral damage” because they exist outside the dimensions of the men's game. War narratives perpetually repeat the same scam: using some grand meaning—security, sovereignty, or counter-terrorism—to legitimize the direct destruction of the flesh.

As diplomats meet on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly to discuss “managing the crisis,” they are actually debating how to more efficiently utilize the Violence Triangle. They seek to adjust resource allocation at the structural level, ensuring that direct killing appears “necessary” and “restrained” at the cultural level.
