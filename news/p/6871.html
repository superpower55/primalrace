他讲藻类，因为藻类听话He Talked About Algae Because Algae Don't Talk Back

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要中期选举前一天，特朗普在林肯纪念堂倒影池前发表47分钟讲话，主题是藻类治理与喷泉修复。他声称藻害已被清除，但司法部和他以1500万美元无竞标合同聘请的公司都认定池体损伤是承包商失误。他还宣布国家英雄花园雕像从250座增至500座且「已经开工」，而司法部10月29日法庭文件称该项目尚未完成决策。讲话中他把民主党称为共产主义者，并称对委内瑞拉的军事接管让美国「每天进账数千亿美元石油」。
The newsOn the eve of the midterms, Trump spent 47 minutes at the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool talking about algae and fountain repairs. He claimed the algae were vanquished, though both the Justice Department and his own no-bid $15 million contractor attributed the pool damage to contractor error. He said his National Garden of American Heroes had grown to 500 statues and that work had "already started," while a Justice Department filing said no decision had been completed.
当现实不配合，就换一个自己能赢的现场。
When reality won't cooperate, pick a stage where you can declare victory.

中期选举前一天，一个总统把最后一场讲话给了倒影池里的藻类。这不是失态，这是选择——他选了一个自己可以宣布胜利的现场。

藻类是真的，池子的损伤也是真的。但司法部和他自己花1500万美元无竞标合同请来的公司都说了，损伤是承包商失误。他站在池边，拿着照片，把这件事说成破坏者干的。事实是什么不重要，重要的是谁在讲、讲给谁听。这是第三章那套东西：夺取解释权，夺取「什么是事实」的制造权。池子有没有修好，不由池子决定，由他手里的照片决定。

雕像花园更直接。他在台上说500座，「已经开工」。司法部的律师10月29日在法庭上写的是：国家公园管理局还没收到把这个花园纳入西波托马克公园的指示。一个在讲台上宣布开工，一个在法庭上说不存在。两个都是美国政府。哪个是真的？在认知入口的争夺里，先说的那个就是真的，等法院判下来，新闻周期已经过去了。

然后是委内瑞拉。「我们的征服」，「每天进账数千亿美元石油」。其他官员在淡化美国在那里的角色，他在台上直接把「征服」两个字说出来。战争叙事从来不需要包装成解放，有时候它连包装都懒得做，直接报账。

他累了，他自己说的那种「低能量」。他说「我们给了这很强的一击」——一个准备认输的人的语气。但认输的人还在讲藻类，还在讲拱门，还在讲比凯旋门更美的凯旋门。因为这些东西不会反驳他。选民担心的是物价、腐败、伊朗，这些会反驳他。藻类不会。

一个总统在选举前夜选择跟藻类对话，这不是他脱离现实，这是他精确地知道哪个现实对他最安全。代价是，那些担心物价的人，在这一刻没有被任何人代表。

On the eve of the midterms, a president gave his final scheduled appearance to the algae in the Reflecting Pool. This isn't a gaffe. It's a choice — he picked a stage where he could declare victory.

The algae are real. So is the damage to the pool. But both the Justice Department and the firm he hired through a no-bid $15 million contract said the damage was contractor error. He stood by the pool, held up photographs, and blamed vandals. What happened doesn't matter. What matters is who is telling it and to whom. This is the machinery from Chapter 3: seize the narrative, seize the power to manufacture what counts as fact. Whether the pool is fixed isn't decided by the pool. It's decided by the photos in his hand.

The statue garden is even more direct. On stage: 500 statues, work "already started." In a court filing dated October 29: the National Park Service has not received direction to include the garden in West Potomac Park. One man announces construction from a podium; a Justice Department lawyer tells a judge it doesn't exist. Both are the US government. Which one is real? In the fight over cognitive entry points, the one who speaks first is real. By the time a court rules, the news cycle has moved on.

Then Venezuela. "Our conquest." "Hundreds of billions of dollars of oil every single day." Other officials downplay the American role there. He says the word conquest out loud. War narratives don't always need to be dressed up as liberation. Sometimes they don't bother dressing up at all — they just read out the receipts.

He looked tired. "Low energy," as he once put it. He said, "We gave this a very strong shot" — the tone of a man bracing for a loss. But a man bracing for a loss is still talking about algae, still talking about the arch, still talking about the arch more beautiful than the Arc de Triomphe. Because these things won't argue back. Voters are worried about prices, corruption, Iran. Those argue back. Algae don't.

A president who spends the eve of an election talking to algae isn't out of touch with reality. He knows exactly which reality is safest for him. The cost is that the people worried about prices went unrepresented in that moment.
