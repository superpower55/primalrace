指责遗产的艺术与共谋者的回旋镖The Art of Blaming Legacies and the Complicity Boomerang

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-02 § 链接
政治上的“遗产”叙事是掩盖当前博弈失败的文化武器。
The "legacy" narrative in politics is a cultural weapon used to mask current gaming failures.

Andy Burnham 把英国经济的脆弱归咎于保守党的“legacy”，这在政治修辞上是标准动作，但在存在性战争中是极其低效的表达。当政府借口前任留下的烂摊子来解释当前的 market turmoil 时，它实际上是在利用一种 cultural violence：通过定义一个“原罪”时期，试图让自己在当前博弈中的失误变得合理化。

这场 PMQs 的本质是一次关于“谁在定义现实”的权力争夺。Kemi Badenoch 攻击 Burnham 是个 people pleaser，而 Burnham 则试图通过挖掘 Truss 时代的废墟来证明自己的“财政责任感”。有趣的是，Badenoch 将 Truss 时代的 mini-Budget 作为武器，而 Burnham 则试图通过指责对方任用 Truss 的旧部来反击。这种互掷“遗产”的行为，本质上是两拨共谋者在同一个 masculine-centric narrative 内部的内卷——他们争论的是如何更高效地管理债务和支出，而不是质疑这套以 GDP 和债券收益率为核心的、剥削底层的经济逻辑本身。

最讽刺的共谋发生在 Jim O’Neill 这种经济学家身上。他一方面是 Burnham 的盟友，另一方面却在投资者耳边低语，质疑谁能真正“get real”地削减开支。这种“内部建议”与“外部质疑”的共振，正是 structural violence 的运行方式：无论谁在台上，资本市场（the market）永远是那个最终的审判者。Burnham 试图通过“公共控制公用事业”来制造一种进步的可能性，但市场立刻用 10 年期国债收益率的跳升给了他一个耳光。这证明了在当前的权力结构中，任何试图脱离资本逻辑的表达，都会被迅速定义为“不专业”或“危险”。

所谓的“财政纪律”不过是一套武器化的语言，用来掩盖 Potential 与 Actual 之间巨大的差额。无论是指责前任的 legacy，还是承诺未来的 responsibility，只要这套叙事不触及资源分配的根本不公正，它就只是在给同一个旧机器更换不同颜色的油漆。

Andy Burnham blaming the Conservative "legacy" for the UK's economic vulnerability is a textbook political move, but in the existential war, it is a highly inefficient expression. By framing the current market turmoil as a result of a "legacy of failure," the government employs a form of cultural violence: creating an "original sin" period to rationalize its own failures in the current game.

This PMQs clash is essentially a struggle over who defines reality. Kemi Badenoch labels Burnham a "people pleaser," while Burnham attempts to prove his "fiscal responsibility" by exhuming the ruins of the Truss era. The irony is that both are fighting within the same masculine-centric narrative. They are not questioning the fundamental logic of an economy centered on GDP and bond yields—a logic that inherently exploits the bottom—but are instead arguing over who can manage debt and spending more "professionally."

The most telling complicity is seen in economists like Jim O’Neill. While remaining an ally to Burnham, he whispers to investors, questioning who will finally "get real" about cutting spending. This resonance between "internal advice" and "external doubt" is exactly how structural violence operates: regardless of who is in power, the Market remains the ultimate judge. Burnham’s attempt to create a new possibility through "public control of utilities" was immediately met with a spike in 10-year gilt yields. This proves that any expression attempting to deviate from capital logic is swiftly weaponized and labeled as "unprofessional" or "dangerous."

So-called "fiscal discipline" is merely a weaponized vocabulary used to mask the gap between Potential and Actual. Whether it is blaming a legacy or promising responsibility, as long as the narrative avoids the fundamental injustice of resource distribution, it is simply painting the same old machine in a different color.
