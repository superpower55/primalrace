防御性打击，这个词本身就是武器"Defensive Strikes" Is Itself the Weapon

国际 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要伊朗媒体报道，当地时间周一晚间，霍尔木兹海峡附近的格什姆岛和伦格港传出多起爆炸，美国媒体援引美方官员称美军实施了所谓“防御性打击”，目标是伊朗向美方资产发射地对空导弹的能力。美方称此举是为限制伊朗袭击海峡商业航运，并刻意保持低调以免被视为重启大规模作战。事件发生在中期选举前夕，特朗普同日称与伊朗的协议“可能很快达成”。
The newsIranian media reported several explosions late Monday on Qeshm Island and in the port city of Bandar Lengeh near the Strait of Hormuz. US media, citing US officials, said the military carried out "defensive strikes" against Iranian surface-to-air missile capabilities. Officials said the campaign was kept deliberately quiet to avoid signalling a return to major combat, on the eve of US midterm elections.
“防御”是开火方给自己贴的标签，不是事实。
"Defensive" is a label the side pulling the trigger gives itself. It is not a fact.

“防御性打击”这个词组本身就是一件武器。谁在防御？在别人海岸线边上、别人岛屿上空扔炸弹的一方，管这叫防御。定义权在谁手里，事实就长什么样。

报道里美方官员的说法是：打击是为了限制伊朗袭击海峡商业航运的能力，并且“刻意保持低调”，以免被看成重启大规模作战。翻译一下：既要把事情做了，又不要让它变成新闻。同一批官员转头就把消息喂给了《华尔街日报》、美联社、福克斯和 Axios。低调的意思是，不承认这是一场战争，但承认这是一次打击。这两句话不矛盾，它们服务于同一个目的——把“开战”从账面上抹掉，把“打击”留在账面上。

再看时间点。中期选举的前一天，特朗普在 Truth Social 上写油价“像石头一样往下掉”，因为人们觉得不会和伊朗打仗，还说伊朗想要协议，“可能很快达成”。同一天，海峡里有爆炸。这两件事在报道里并排放着，没有一句话把它们连起来，但它们本来就该连起来看：一边是选票要的“没有战争”，一边是军方要的“打了”。两边都拿到了自己想要的东西，代价由别人承担。

代价是谁？报道里没有出现一个死者的名字，没有伤亡数字，没有格什姆岛上居民的一句话。有的只是“目击者看到两架飞机飞过岛屿”。这不是报道的疏忽，这是这类新闻的标准结构：打击是可计数的，被打的人是背景。伊朗外交部发言人把球踢回给美国，说只要华盛顿解除海上封锁、确保停止敌对行动，重开海峡的方案两天内可以实施。海峡关着，油轮停着，保险费涨着，这些账最后落到谁的油箱和饭桌上，报道没写，但它一定会落到某些人的油箱和饭桌上。

“伊朗人用防空导弹对准美国资产”——这是美方给出的打击理由。一个主权国家在自己的海岸附近用防空系统对着靠近的军机，这被定义为需要被打击的行为。同一套逻辑在第三章里出现过很多次：中东女性需要被解放，所以打阿富汗。叙事入口换一个词，战争就能重来一次。这次换的词是“商业航运”。保护商船，听起来比保护人权更中性、更无可辩驳。

伊朗革命卫队同一天声称用电子战俘获了两架美军侦察无人机，并在海峡以东击落了一架 MQ-9。双方都在报自己的战果，双方都不报对方的伤亡。存在性战争打到国家机器这一层，就是这样：没有人赢，只有人付账。

特朗普说“伊朗想要和解，越快越好”。和解的条件由谁定，海峡什么时候重开，油价跌下去的那十美元会不会再涨回来——这些问题的答案不在格什姆岛上，在华盛顿和德黑兰的会议室里。岛上的人只负责听见爆炸。

"Defensive strikes" is a phrase doing weapons work. Defensive for whom? The side dropping bombs off someone else's coastline, over someone else's island, calls it defence. Whoever holds the definition holds the fact.

The US officials' account: the strikes were meant to limit Iran's ability to attack commercial shipping, and Washington deliberately kept the campaign quiet to avoid signalling a return to major combat. Put plainly: do the thing, but don't let it become news. The same officials then fed the story to the Wall Street Journal, the AP, Fox News and Axios. Quiet here means: don't admit it's a war, do admit it's a strike. Those two sentences don't contradict each other. They serve one purpose — erase "war" from the ledger, keep "strike" on it.

Then the timing. The day before the midterms, Trump writes on Truth Social that oil is "dropping like a rock" because people feel there will be no war with Iran, and that Iran wants a settlement, "It could happen!" The same day, explosions in the strait. The report sets these side by side without a sentence connecting them. They should be connected: one side needs the ballot-box story of no war, the other needs the strike. Both get what they want. Someone else pays.

Who pays? No name of a dead person appears. No casualty figure. Not one sentence from a resident of Qeshm. Only "witnesses saw two planes flying over the island." That isn't an oversight; it's the standard structure of this genre. Strikes are counted. The struck are scenery. Iran's foreign ministry spokesman says the ball is in Washington's court, that Tehran's plan to reopen the strait could be implemented within two days if the US lifts its naval blockade and secures an end to hostilities. The strait stays shut, tankers stay parked, insurance premiums climb — and the report never says whose fuel tank and dinner table absorb that. It will be someone's.

"Iranians using surface-to-air missiles against U.S. assets" — that is the stated reason. A sovereign state pointing air defence at approaching military aircraft near its own coast is defined as the thing that must be struck. The same logic runs through the playbook: Middle Eastern women need liberating, so bomb Afghanistan. Swap the entry word and war can start again. This time the word is "commercial shipping." Protecting merchant vessels sounds more neutral, harder to argue with, than protecting human rights.

The IRGC claims it captured two US reconnaissance drones with electronic warfare and destroyed an MQ-9 east of the strait. Each side reports its own hits; neither reports the other's dead. When the existential war reaches the level of state machines, this is what it looks like: nobody wins, somebody pays.

Trump says Iran wants a settlement, as soon as possible. Who sets the terms, when the strait reopens, whether the ten dollars a barrel come back — none of that is decided on Qeshm. The island's job is to hear the explosions.
