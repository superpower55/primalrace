用女性身体为战争背书的陈旧叙事The Obsolete Narrative of Using Women's Bodies to Endorse War

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
将女性客体化为‘国家象征’，是典型的文化暴力武器化。
Objectifying women as 'national symbols' is a classic weaponization of cultural violence.

这张漫画试图在乌克兰独立日营造一种英雄主义的悲壮感，但其潜意识里的叙事逻辑依然极其 Masculine-centric。在这种典型的战争叙事中，女性身体再次被 weaponized 成了某种‘被保护的纯洁’或‘国家受难的隐喻’。这种表达方式极其危险：它通过将女性客体化为一种象征，诱导受众进入一种‘为了守护女性/家庭而战’的浪漫化陷阱，从而掩盖战争背后真实的资源掠夺与权力博弈。

这是一种标准的 Cultural Violence。它在 Meta 层面上通过塑造‘受难女性’的意象，为 Direct 层的杀戮和 Structural 层的战争机器提供道德合法性。当女性被简化为‘国家之母’或‘受难者’时，她们作为独立个体的主体性就彻底死亡了。她们不再是拥有主观意志的人，而成了战争叙事中的一个 Props（道具）。

这种‘保护女性’的叙事入口，历史上被无数次用来发动侵略。从‘解放中东女性’到‘守护家园’，逻辑如出一辙：先定义女性为弱势的、需要被拯救的客体，然后通过这种定义赋予男性施暴的权力。这本质上是一场关于解释权的垄断，是元暴力的惯性运作。我们不需要这种‘被保护’的温情，我们需要的是将女性从这种战争隐喻中剥离，停止用女性身体为任何宏大叙事买单。

This cartoon attempts to evoke a sense of heroic tragedy for Ukraine's Independence Day, but its underlying logic remains profoundly Masculine-centric. In this typical war narrative, the female body is once again weaponized as a symbol of 'protected purity' or a 'metaphor for national suffering.' This expression is dangerous: by objectifying women, it lures the audience into a romanticized trap of 'fighting to protect women/family,' thereby masking the actual resource plunder and power struggles behind the war.

This is a textbook case of Cultural Violence. At the Meta level, by shaping the image of the 'suffering woman,' it provides moral legitimacy for the Direct violence of slaughter and the Structural violence of the war machine. When women are reduced to 'Mothers of the Nation' or 'Victims,' their subjectivity as independent individuals dies completely. They cease to be humans with subjective will and become mere Props in a war narrative.

This 'protecting women' narrative entrance has been used countless times throughout history to justify aggression. From 'liberating Middle Eastern women' to 'defending the homeland,' the logic is identical: first define women as weak objects in need of rescue, then use that definition to grant men the power to exert violence. This is essentially a monopoly over the right of interpretation—the inertia of Meta Violence. We don't need this 'protected' warmth; we need women to be stripped from these war metaphors and stop paying the price for any grand narrative with their bodies.
