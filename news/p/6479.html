慈善是她的布景，穷人当她的道具Charity Was Her Set, the Poor Were Her Props

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
万圣节的穷人装扮，暴露的是谁有权扮演苦难
A Halloween homeless costume shows who gets to perform suffering.

Martha Stewart 扮成流浪汉，脸上抹了泥，衣服撕破，举着一块牌子：「I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!」。照片发在她推销 QVC 蜡烛的 Instagram 帖子里，配文是「我的 QVC 蜡烛是万圣节绝配!!!!!」。

这不是一次失手的表达，而是一次准确的表达。她想要的东西很清楚：一个节日的氛围、一个商品的卖点、一张能让人停下滑动的手指的画面。流浪汉的身体在这套组合里是最好用的道具——脏、惨、一眼就懂，不需要解释，也不会说话。

然后她说，这是为了「让更多人关注无家可归这个非常严肃的话题」。这句话才是真正需要拆的东西。关注不是凭空产生的，注意力是定量的：她把一个流浪者的形象放进万圣节的娱乐序列里，观众记住的是 Martha Stewart 扮得有多像，而不是美国有多少人真的睡在街上。她占用了那个位置，真正该被看见的人就被挤出去了。

她还补充，自己花了很多时间和精力支持对抗这场社会流行病的慈善机构。这里要问的是：慈善是她的表达，还是流浪者的表达？前者她完全掌控，后者她连一句转述都没有。整篇报道里没有一个流浪者说话，也没有一个流浪者被提到名字——他们只以「无家可归」这个类别出现，作为她澄清声明的背景板。

批评她「tone deaf」「out of touch」的人说对了一半。真正的问题不是她不懂，而是她太懂了：她知道苦难的形象有传播力，知道把它包装成节日玩笑不会有人真的追究，知道自己事后发一段「我很痛心」的声明，这件事就翻篇了。这是共谋的日常形态——不需要恶意，只需要一个默认：有些人的处境可以被借来用一下，用完了还回去，不必付租金。

她说「never my intention」。意图在这里是最不重要的东西。一个拥有媒体帝国的人把贫穷做成 costume，这件事的代价不由她承担，也不由她的观众承担。

那件衣服撕破的地方，本来应该是一个人的住处。

Martha Stewart dressed as a homeless person: torn clothes, dirt on her face, a clipboard reading "I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!" The photo went into an Instagram carousel promoting the candles she sells on QVC. The caption: "My QVC candles are perfect for Halloween!!!!!"

This wasn't a slip of expression. It was an accurate one. What she wanted is obvious: a festive mood, a selling point, an image that stops the thumb. A homeless body is the ideal prop for that job — dirty, pitiful, legible at a glance, requiring no explanation and offering no backtalk.

Then she says it was meant to "bring more awareness to the very serious subject of homelessness." That's the sentence to take apart. Awareness is not free; attention is a fixed quantity. Put a homeless figure inside the entertainment sequence of Halloween and what viewers remember is how convincingly Martha Stewart played the part, not how many people in America actually sleep outside. She occupied the slot, and the people who should have been seen got pushed out of it.

She adds that she has spent significant time and effort supporting charities that combat this social epidemic. The question is whose expression the charity is. Hers, fully under her control. The homeless get not a single quoted line in the story, not a single name — they appear only as a category, as the backdrop for her clarification.

The people calling her tone deaf and out of touch are half right. The problem isn't that she doesn't understand. It's that she understands perfectly: that images of suffering travel, that wrapping one in a holiday joke carries no real cost, that a statement about being "distressed" closes the file. This is what complicity looks like in its everyday form — no malice required, just a default: some people's circumstances can be borrowed for a while and returned, rent-free.

"Never my intention," she says. Intention is the least relevant fact here. A woman with a media empire turned poverty into a costume, and the cost lands on neither her nor her audience.

The torn part of that outfit should have been somebody's home.
