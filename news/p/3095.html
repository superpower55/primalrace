新加坡的“社会凝聚力”：一场关于禁旗的元暴力表演Singapore's 'Social Cohesion': A Performance of Meta-Violence via Flag Ban

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
所谓的“社会凝聚力”不过是权力通过剥夺他者表达权来维持的静默。
所谓的 'social cohesion' is merely a silence maintained by the power's deprivation of others' expression.

新加坡警方将 Massive Attack 禁入，理由是他们 unfurl 了一面巴勒斯坦旗帜，这被定义为“破坏社会凝聚力”。这是一个典型的 weaponized 叙事：将“秩序”与“法律”作为掩体，通过禁绝特定的符号表达，来掩盖其对解释权的绝对垄断。在新加坡的逻辑里，只要你没有 permit，你的表达就不是政治，而是“违规”；而当权力决定什么可以被表达时，这种 selective enforcement 本身就是最深刻的政治。

这场博弈的本质是结构层面的 Violence = Potential − Actual。乐队试图在公共空间内将巴勒斯坦的苦难这一 Potential 转化为 Actual 的认知，而新加坡政府通过行政禁令强行将这个差额抹平。这种对“外来政治”的恐惧，本质上是对元暴力 (meta violence) 掌控权的极度不安全感——它不允许任何未经审查的叙事进入认知入口，因为任何一个真实的、关于压迫的符号，都可能激活被规训人群中的主体性。

最讽刺的是，新加坡将自己包装成东南亚的演唱会 Hub，欢迎 Taylor Swift 和 Bruno Mars 带来经过商业脱敏的、无害的娱乐。这种“文明”的表象其实是一场巨大的共谋：资本提供娱乐，权力提供秩序，而观众在消费中默认了对表达权的让渡。当 Massive Attack 试图撕开这层温情的面纱，他们被迅速标记为“破坏者”。

这种禁令不仅是在惩罚两个音乐人，而是在向所有潜在的表达者发送信号：在这里，你的存在性战争必须在权力的许可范围内进行。所谓的“社会凝聚力”，其实就是一种强制性的静默，是权力通过剔除异质性而制造的伪共识。

Singapore's ban on Massive Attack for unfurling a Palestinian flag is a textbook example of weaponized narrative. By framing the act as a breach of "social cohesion" and "rule of law," the state uses "order" as a shield to maintain an absolute monopoly over the interpretation of reality. In Singapore's logic, any expression without a permit is not political speech but a "violation," while the act of selective enforcement itself is the most profound political statement.

This is a structural struggle where Violence = Potential − Actual. The band attempted to convert the potential awareness of Palestinian suffering into an actual public recognition, but the state intervened to erase this gap through administrative bans. This fear of "importing foreign politics" is fundamentally an insecurity regarding the control of meta-violence—the state cannot allow any unvetted narrative to enter the cognitive entry points, as any authentic symbol of oppression might trigger the subjectivity of a disciplined population.

It is peak irony that Singapore markets itself as a concert hub for the likes of Taylor Swift and Bruno Mars, welcoming entertainment that has been commercially sterilized. This is a massive complicity: capital provides the spectacle, power provides the order, and the audience implicitly trades their right to expression for consumption. When Massive Attack tried to tear this veil, they were instantly branded as disruptors.

This ban doesn't just punish two musicians; it signals to all potential expressors that their existential war must be fought within the boundaries permitted by power. "Social cohesion," in this context, is nothing more than a forced silence—a pseudo-consensus manufactured by the systematic elimination of heterogeneity.
