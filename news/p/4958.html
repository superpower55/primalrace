沉浸式体验：用XR眼镜给废墟镀金Immersive Experiences: Gilding Ruins with XR Glasses

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-12 § 链接
当苦难被转化为“体验”，技术就成了抹除真实暴力的最高级美学共谋。
When suffering is converted into an 'experience,' technology becomes the ultimate aesthetic complicity in erasing actual violence.

Venice Immersive 把西岸的废墟和 17 世纪的阿姆斯特丹放在同一个展区，这种并列本身就是一种极其傲慢的 weaponization。在 XR 眼镜的滤镜下，被定居者暴力抹除的社区变成了“数字化保存”的 360 度纪录片，而殖民时代的贸易中心变成了“令人愉悦”的历史之旅。这种所谓的“沉浸感”，本质上是给结构性暴力（structural violence）镀了一层数字金边，让观众在舒适的威尼斯岛上，通过一种类似“电子旅游”的方式消费他人的绝望。

最讽刺的是那些创作者的自我感动。有人说这种技术是 advocacy tool，因为它可以被展示给世界银行或联合国的高官。这简直是典型的元暴力（meta violence）逻辑：受害者的生存状态必须被加工成一种符合精英审美、具有“冲击力”的数字化产品，才能换取权力阶层的短暂关注。这种“质量胜过数量”的叙事，实际上是将受害者的主体性再次让渡给了解释权的人——只有被“正确地”数字化，苦难才具有被拯救的价值。

当技术将现实折叠成 origami，把真实的血腥和绝望转化为一种“Lynchian nightmare”或“令人震撼”的视觉艺术时，它在文化层（cultural layer）上完成了一次高效的清洗。观众在摘下眼镜的那一刻，感受到的不是对暴力的愤怒，而是一种参与了“前沿艺术”的优越感。这就是技术共谋的最阴险之处：它让你以为自己在见证真实，实际上它在用一种极高分辨率的假象，掩盖那个 Potential 和 Actual 之间巨大的暴力差额。

Venice Immersive places the ruins of the West Bank alongside 17th-century Amsterdam in the same exhibition. This juxtaposition is a blatant weaponization of expression. Under the filter of XR glasses, communities erased by settler violence are transformed into 'digitally preserved' 360-degree documentaries, while the trade hubs of the colonial era become 'delightful' historical tours. This so-called 'immersion' is essentially gilding structural violence with a digital veneer, allowing visitors on a comfortable Venetian island to consume others' despair as a form of 'electronic tourism.'

The self-satisfaction of the creators is the most ironic part. Some claim this technology is an advocacy tool because it can be shown to World Bank or UN officials. This is classic meta-violence: the survival of the victim must be processed into a digitally polished product that fits elite aesthetics and possesses 'impact' to earn the fleeting attention of the power class. This narrative of 'quality over quantity' effectively surrenders the subjecthood of the victims to those who hold the power of interpretation—suffering only gains the value of being 'saved' when it is 'correctly' digitized.

As technology folds reality like origami, converting raw blood and despair into a 'Lynchian nightmare' or 'astounding' visual art, it completes an efficient cleansing at the cultural layer. The moment a visitor removes the glasses, they feel not anger toward violence, but a sense of superiority for having participated in 'cutting-edge art.' This is the most insidious aspect of technological complicity: it makes you believe you are witnessing reality, while it uses a high-resolution illusion to mask the massive gap of violence between Potential and Actual.
