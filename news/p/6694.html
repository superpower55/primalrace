选举的广告，观众的疲劳Election Ads, Viewer Fatigue

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要福克斯公司公布财季业绩，营收45.7亿美元、同比增长22%，广告收入增长47%，净利润9.57亿美元。首席执行官拉克伦·默多克称，中期选举政治广告收入已超过3.5亿美元，创中期选举周期纪录，其中地方台超2.5亿美元，Tubi与全国电视网各5000万美元。
The newsFox Corp. reported quarterly revenue of $4.57 billion, up 22 percent year over year, with advertising up 47 percent and net income of $957 million. CEO Lachlan Murdoch said political advertising passed $350 million, a record for a midterm cycle, including over $250 million at local stations and $50 million each at Tubi and national networks.
政治广告的每一块钱，买的都是你的注意力席位。
Every political ad dollar buys a seat in your attention.

福克斯这个季度赚了45.7亿美元，广告收入涨了47%。默多克在电话会上把原因说得很清楚：中期选举、世界杯、NFL开季。政治广告过了3.5亿美元，中期选举周期的新纪录。

这笔钱不是凭空长出来的。它是从观众的时间里抽出来的税。注意力是定量的，一个晚上只有那么几个小时，谁买下了这几小时，别人就买不到；谁买下了你在这几小时里看到的世界，你在这几小时里就没法看到别的世界。3.5亿美元买的不是广告位，是几千万人对「现在发生了什么」的默认版本。这就是认知入口的定价。

更有意思的是默多克自己承认的另一件事：福克斯新闻的收视率在跌。他的解释是「观众疲劳」——去年 inauguration、Charlie Kirk、DOGE、洪灾，新闻周期太密，观众看累了，不是换台，只是看得少一点。他还说这套剧本见过，2006年中期选举就是这样，我们知道怎么走过去。

翻译一下：观众的注意力被榨到疲劳，但榨取方不打算减产，只打算等他们缓过来再榨。疲劳被当成一种可以「走过去」的季节性天气，而不是一个信号。信号是什么？是这套机器已经在让它的原料——人——感到消耗了，而消耗不影响财报，所以不算问题。观众不是客户，是矿。

财报里还有一个细节值得盯：Tubi被默多克称为政治广告的「销售漏斗」，靠的是定向能力。定向的意思是，同一个选举，不同的人被投喂不同版本的政治叙事，各自以为看到的是全部。这不是阴谋，这是产品说明书上写着的功能，被当成卖点讲给华尔街听。

世界杯决赛是美国历史上收视最高的足球比赛，MLB季后赛开打，NFL开季——这些都被列进同一句话里，和选举广告并列，作为「强劲势头」的组成部分。体育、选举、新闻，在这份财报里是同一类东西：可出售的注意力容器。它们之间的区别，只在于卖给谁、单价多少。

所以这条新闻里真正在动的是文化层：谁有权力决定几千万人在选举前最后几周看见什么、看不见什么，并且把这件事作为季度业绩的亮点公布。结构层也在动——地方台那2.5亿美元，买的是本地新闻时段，而本地新闻是很多人唯一还会打开的新闻。

至于观众疲劳这件事，默多克说他们知道怎么走过去。他没说的是，走过去之后观众还剩多少。

Fox made $4.57 billion this quarter, advertising up 47 percent. Murdoch told analysts why: the midterms, the World Cup, the start of the NFL season. Political advertising passed $350 million, a record for a midterm cycle.

That money doesn't come from nowhere. It's a tax extracted from viewers' time. Attention is finite; an evening holds only so many hours. Whoever buys those hours denies them to someone else. Whoever buys what you see in those hours denies you every other version of the world. The $350 million doesn't buy ad slots. It buys the default account of what is happening, for tens of millions of people. That is the price of the cognitive entrance.

More interesting is the other thing Murdoch admitted: Fox News ratings are down. His explanation is viewer fatigue. Last year's news cycle was relentless, inauguration, Charlie Kirk, DOGE, flooding, and viewers are tired. Not switching channels, just watching less. He's seen this playbook before, he said, 2006, and they know how to move through it.

Translation: the audience has been squeezed to the point of exhaustion, and the squeezer has no plan to produce less, only to wait until they recover and squeeze again. Fatigue is treated as seasonal weather to be walked through, not a signal. The signal being that the machine is visibly consuming its raw material, people, and consumption doesn't show up in earnings, so it isn't a problem. The viewer is not a customer. The viewer is the mine.

One more detail in the filing deserves a stare: Murdoch calls Tubi a political ad sales funnel, thanks to its targeting. Targeting means the same election gets fed to different people in different versions, each believing they've seen the whole. This isn't a conspiracy. It's a feature, printed in the product manual, and pitched to Wall Street as a selling point.

The World Cup final, the most-watched soccer game in U.S. history, the MLB playoffs, the NFL season, all listed in the same breath as election ads, all part of the momentum. Sports, elections, news: in this filing they are one category of thing, sellable containers of attention. The only difference is who buys them and at what unit price.

So what moves in this story is the cultural layer: who has the power to decide what tens of millions of people see and don't see in the final weeks before an election, and who gets to report that power as a quarterly highlight. The structural layer moves too. The $250 million at local stations buys local news slots, and local news is the only news many people still open.

As for viewer fatigue, Murdoch says they know how to move through it. What he didn't say is how much of the viewer is left on the other side.
