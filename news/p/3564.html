拒绝自审：用直觉撕开结构性暴力的伤口Against Self-Censorship: Tearing Open Structural Violence with Intuition

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
真正的表达是停止扮演，将创伤从自审的囚笼中释放。
True expression is the cessation of performance, releasing trauma from the cage of self-editing.

Sullivan Fortner 的新专辑《Leave That in There》最核心的动作不是音乐上的实验，而是一次关于“表达”的夺权。他通过服用 edible 和放弃后期修剪，完成了一次从“假.最优解表达”向“真.最优解表达”的迁移。在主流工业的录音室里，无数次的 take 和 post-production 其实就是一种文化层面的 self-editing，它要求艺术家扮演一个“完美的、被认可的”角色，而这种扮演的代价就是主体性的死亡。

专辑标题源自卡特里娜飓风灾难中一个女性的冷漠回应：“Leave that in there（把它留在里面吧）”。这个瞬间极其有力，因为它标志着个体在面对结构性暴力（structural violence）——即政府失能、资源分配不均、对黑人社区的系统性遗弃——之后，产生的一种决绝的舍弃。当一个人意识到自己身处的环境本身就是一场巨大的 scam，且没有任何公正的救济时，不再追求“完整”或“体面”，反而成了唯一的生存策略。

Fortner 将这种决绝转化为艺术宣言：不再隐藏创伤，不再为了迎合某种“文明”的审美而修剪自己的破碎。这种不加修饰的表达，实际上是在对抗那种试图将灾难“浪漫化”或“韧性化”的叙事。很多好新闻喜欢歌颂受害者的 resilience（韧性），但韧性有时是结构暴力的遮羞布——因为你们足够坚强，所以我们不需要改善制度。Fortner 选择把那些不和谐的、疼痛的、未经编辑的碎片留在记录里，这本身就是对元暴力的反击：我不需要被你们定义的“优秀”来证明我的存在。

这种直觉的回归，是他在博弈中找到了自己的真.最优解。他不再试图通过扮演一个“完美的爵士钢琴家”来换取行业票票，而是通过呈现一个“破碎但真实的人”来确立身份。这种表达的武器化，不在于进攻，而在于拒绝被驯化。

Sullivan Fortner’s new album, *Leave That in There*, is less about musical experimentation and more about a reclamation of expression. By using edibles and abandoning post-production tinkering, he shifts from a fake optimal expression to a true optimal expression. In the mainstream industry, endless takes and editing are forms of cultural self-editing—a demand to perform a 'perfect, approved' role, the price of which is the death of subjectivity.

The title originates from a woman's terse response during the Hurricane Katrina disaster: 'Leave that in there.' This moment is powerful because it marks an individual's decisive abandonment after facing structural violence—government failure, systemic neglect of Black communities, and the collapse of resources. When one realizes the environment is a scam with no just remedy, abandoning 'completeness' or 'decency' becomes the only survival strategy.

Fortner transforms this resolve into an artistic manifesto: no more hiding trauma, no more pruning his brokenness to fit a 'civilized' aesthetic. This unedited expression resists the narrative that seeks to 'romanticize' or 'resilience-ize' disaster. Many good news stories praise the resilience of victims, but resilience is often a cloak for structural violence—suggesting that because you are strong enough, the system doesn't need to change. By leaving the dissonant, painful, and unedited fragments in the record, Fortner attacks meta-violence: he refuses to let his existence be validated by a definition of 'excellence' imposed by others.

This return to intuition is where he finds his true optimal expression. He no longer seeks to trade a performance of the 'perfect jazz pianist' for industry points; instead, he establishes his identity by presenting a 'broken but real human.' The weaponization of this expression lies not in aggression, but in the refusal to be domesticated.
