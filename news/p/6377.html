公开招募线人，是霸权在演给自己看An Open Casting Call Is a Confession

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
当秘密机构开始公开招人，说明它已经没有秘密可守。
When a secret agency recruits in public, it has no secret left to keep.

鲁比奥在 X 上发帖，请想「在古巴创造积极改变」的人「抓住这个机会，通过与 CIA 的 discreet（原文如此，他拼成了 discrete）合作，创造你想要的改变」。这条帖子被彭博的驻白宫记者核对了四遍才敢相信是真的。一个超级大国的情报机构，把招募线人做成了公开征召。

这不是力量的展示，这是无能的展示。报道里那位 CIA 官员对 ABC 说得很清楚：「在如今这个全面技术监控的世界里，我们没法像二十年前那样招募线人了。」对手建起了自己的监控网络，西方情报机构失去了地面的入口，于是只能上网喊话。拜登时期对俄罗斯用 Telegram 频道，对中国、伊朗、朝鲜发视频，都是同一个原因。公开招募不是新战略，是旧能力消失之后的替代品。

但掌握权力的人不这么看。报道点出了这一层：他们不把限制当限制，而把它当成新战略的证明——「炫耀国力，而不是把它藏在门后，据说藏着就没用了」。内塔尼亚胡把摩萨德的操作搬上台面，2024 年黎巴嫩的寻呼机袭击伤了超过两千人，一名摩萨德官员对《60 分钟》说这些人是「我们在整个中东优越性的行走证明」，另一名说「世界是我们的舞台」。黑水创始人埃里克·普林斯说自己「嫉妒」那次行动，公开敦促 CIA 向摩萨德学习。

这是表达被武器化到极致的形态：行动本身的价值，让位给了行动的叙事价值。寻呼机炸死炸伤的是具体的人，但在这套叙事里，他们只是道具，用来证明「我们能到达任何地方」。第三章讲的认知入口在这里被反过来用——不是投放给敌人看，是投放给本国的观众看，让国内的人相信帝国还在赢。鲁比奥在雅典卫城脚下说「我们选择毫不道歉地追求国家权力」，这句话的听众不在古巴。

而古巴这一头，报道写得很清楚：美国情报界的内部评估认为，就算把劳尔·卡斯特罗抓了，也「不太可能有务实的官员」接替他上台。古巴的军事结构和伊朗类似，靠的是去中心化和领导层的冗余，政治权力也不围绕单一个人。特朗普说「不管我是解放它、拿走它，我想我对它做什么都行」——这句话他在三月说过，当时说的是伊朗。现在那场「几周就能结束」的战争，几乎确定要拖过中期选举，再打一轮。

所以公开招募线人这件事，真正暴露的不是 CIA 有多强，而是它需要一个可以被看见的姿态。秘密机构一旦开始表演，说明它已经不确定自己还有没有实际的东西可以拿出来。

报道里缺席的是那些真正要付代价的人：被公开招募鼓动、真的去联系了 CIA 的古巴人，他们的名字不会出现在鲁比奥的帖子里，也不会出现在任何一份胜利通报里。帝国的舞台搭好了，上台的是官员，留在台上被记住的是官员，而那些被「机会」吸引的人，连群众演员的署名都没有。

Rubio posted on X inviting anyone who wants to "create positive change in Cuba" to "seize this opportunity to create the change you desire through a discrete [sic] partnership with the CIA." Bloomberg's White House correspondent checked four times that it was real. A superpower's intelligence agency has turned asset recruitment into an open casting call.

This is not a display of strength. It is a display of the lack of it. The CIA official who told ABC News in May 2025 put it plainly: "in today's world of [universal technical surveillance], we can't recruit sources the same way that we did 20 years ago." Adversaries built their own surveillance networks; Western agencies lost their ground entrances; so they shout online. Biden's Telegram channel aimed at Russia, the videos aimed at Chinese, Iranian and North Korean assets — same cause. Overt recruitment is not a new strategy. It is what remains after an old capability is gone.

But the people holding power don't read it that way. The report names the move: they treat the constraint as proof of a new strategy — boasting about national power rather than keeping it behind closed doors where it supposedly remains useless. Netanyahu dragged Mossad's operations into public view; the 2024 pager attacks in Lebanon injured more than 2,000 people, and one Mossad officer told "60 Minutes" they are "walking proof of our superiority all around the Middle East," another that "the world is our stage." Erik Prince, founder of Blackwater, said he was "jealous" of the pager attacks and publicly urged the CIA to learn from the Mossad.

This is expression weaponized to its endpoint: the narrative value of an operation displaces the operation itself. The people maimed by the pagers are real, but in this telling they are props, evidence that "we can reach anywhere." The cognitive entrance from Chapter 3 is being used in reverse — not aimed at the enemy, aimed at the domestic audience, to keep them believing the empire is still winning. Rubio, speaking at the Acropolis, declared "we choose the unapologetic pursuit of national power." His audience was not in Havana.

On the Cuban side, the report is clear: internal U.S. intelligence assessments do not see an easy overthrow. Even if Raúl Castro were captured, "few pragmatic officials" are likely to take power in his stead. Cuba's military structure resembles Iran's — decentralized, with redundancies in leadership — and its political power is not actually built around a single individual. Trump mused in March: "Whether I free it, take it, I think, I could do anything I want with it." He was saying similar things about Iran at the time. That war, supposed to last a few weeks, is now all but certain to extend past the midterms for another devastating round.

So what the open casting call really exposes is not how strong the CIA is, but that it needs a visible gesture. Once a secret agency starts performing, it is no longer sure it has anything real left to bring onstage.

What the report leaves out is who actually pays: the Cubans who are stirred by the public appeal and do reach out to the CIA. Their names will not appear in Rubio's post, nor in any victory dispatch. The imperial stage is built, the officials walk on, the officials are remembered — and the people drawn in by the "opportunity" do not even get a credit line.
