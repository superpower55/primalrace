迪士尼乐园不需要署名The Magic Kingdom Doesn't Need Credits

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要任天堂通过「Ask the Developer」文章确认，《塞尔达传说：时之笛》重制版由长期负责塞尔达系列的任天堂企划制作本部第3组主导开发，六位受访开发者中有四位来自该组。此前粉丝猜测该作由外包支持工作室负责，因为2011年3DS版时之笛重制由Grezzo操刀。游戏将于11月5日发售。
The newsNintendo confirmed via an Ask the Developer article that the Ocarina of Time remake is led by its own EPD Production Group No. 3, the longtime Zelda team; four of the six interviewed developers come from that group. Fans had speculated a support studio was handling it, since the 2011 3DS remake was made by Grezzo. The game launches Nov. 5.
神秘感是品牌资产，署名是劳工权利。
Mystery is a brand asset. A credit is a labor right.

任天堂把「谁在做这个游戏」做成了悬念，这本身就是一条产品线。

报道说得很清楚：这家公司对制作过程的保密「并非完全脱离行业惯例」，但它「把这件事提升到了另一个层次」，试图围绕品牌制造一种迪士尼乐园式的惊奇感。迪士尼乐园的惊奇感是什么？是你永远看不到扮演米老鼠的那个人摘下头套。你看见的是米奇，不是时薪多少、在三十八度的园区里站了几个小时的那个具体的人。任天堂的开发者署名，就是那个头套。

这次「官方揭晓」揭晓了什么呢？揭晓了《塞尔达》是任天堂自己做的。六位受访开发者里四位来自企划制作本部第3组，另外两位是青沼英二和藤林秀麿。也就是说，被当作新闻发布的这个事实，是「做塞尔达的组在做塞尔达」。粉丝此前之所以猜错，是因为3DS版时之笛重制是Grezzo做的，而Monolith Soft一直在给塞尔达当支持工作室。这些猜测不是空穴来风，它们是这家公司长期不署名的直接产物：当署名被系统性隐藏，猜测就会填补真空，而猜测填补的真空里，Grezzo和Monolith的工时、贡献、credit，全都不存在。

这就是文化暴力最经济的一种用法。它不撒谎，它只是不说。它把「谁做的」变成品牌叙事的一部分，而品牌叙事的所有权在任天堂手里。开发者被吸收进「任天堂」这三个字，就像米奇被吸收进「迪士尼」这三个字。玩家得到的是惊奇感，公司得到的是全部的功劳和全部的定价权。

报道最后一句是：「我们会在游戏11月5日发售时得知参与制作的完整工作室名单。」注意这个时间点。名单在发售时公布，也就是在预售、宣传、所有注意力都已经变现之后。这不是透明，这是把署名当成发售后的彩蛋。

所以，这条新闻里真正的悬念不是「谁在做时之笛重制版」，而是：那些没被采访、没被写进Ask the Developer、要等到11月5日才可能出现在滚动字幕里的名字，他们在这套惊奇感里值多少钱。

Nintendo has turned "who is making this game" into a product line of its own.

The report says it plainly: the company's secrecy isn't entirely out of step with an industry that hides how the sausage is made, but Nintendo takes it to another level, trying to create a Disneyland-like sense of wonder around its brand. What is the wonder of Disneyland? It's that you never see the person inside the Mickey costume take the head off. You see Mickey, not the hourly wage, not the hours standing in a thirty-eight-degree park. Nintendo's developer credits are that costume head.

So what did this official reveal reveal? That Zelda is made by Nintendo. Four of the six interviewed developers come from EPD Production Group No. 3; the other two are Eiji Aonuma and Hidemaro Fujibayashi. The fact published as news is that the group that makes Zelda is making Zelda. Fans guessed wrong because the 3DS remake of Ocarina of Time was handled by Grezzo, and Monolith Soft has long served as a support studio on Zelda. Those guesses weren't baseless. They were the direct product of a company that doesn't credit: when credit is systematically withheld, speculation fills the vacuum, and inside that vacuum the hours, the contributions, the credit of Grezzo and Monolith simply do not exist.

This is the cheapest use of cultural violence. It doesn't lie. It just doesn't say. It turns who-made-it into a piece of brand narrative, and the brand narrative is owned by Nintendo. Developers are absorbed into the three syllables of the company name, the way Mickey is absorbed into Disney. Players get wonder. The company gets all the credit and all the pricing power.

The last line of the report: we'll find out the full list of studios when the game comes out on Nov. 5. Note the timing. The list arrives after preorders, after marketing, after all the attention has been monetized. That isn't transparency. That's treating a credit as a post-launch easter egg.

The real mystery in this story isn't who is making the Ocarina of Time remake. It's what the names that weren't interviewed, weren't written into Ask the Developer, and may or may not surface in the scrolling credits on Nov. 5 are worth inside this sense of wonder.
