粮食价格的“自然”上涨与人为的暴力差额The 'Natural' Rise of Food Prices and the Calculated Violence of Scarcity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
饥饿不是自然灾害，而是结构性暴力在生物墙上的最终结算。
Hunger is not a natural disaster, but the final settlement of structural violence against the biological wall.

看到这类新闻，大众习惯于将目光投向“热浪” (heatwaves) 和“厄尔尼诺” (El Niño)，仿佛饥饿是上帝掷骰子后的随机结果。但用加尔通的暴力三角来看，这不过是 structural violence 的一次集中爆发。Potential（人类生产粮食的能力）与 Actual（实际喂饱人口的状态）之间的差额，就是最直接的暴力。

报道中提到的战争——乌克兰、中东，是典型的 masculine-centric narrative 的产物。男性统治者为了所谓的“宏大意义”或地缘权力，将粮食出口基础设施变成战场，将化肥运输通道变成筹码。在这种元暴力 (meta-violence) 的驱动下，粮食不再是生存权，而成了武器化的认知入口。当他们谈论“战略物资”时，代价是全球 5000 万人被推向 acute hunger。

最讽刺的是，这种 structural violence 具有极强的性别选择性。在粮食不安全 (food insecurity) 的家庭中，女性往往是最后一个人进食的人。由于生物墙的存在，孕产妇和儿童在营养匮乏面前最先崩溃，而这种崩溃在文化层 (cultural layer) 经常被简化为“家庭责任”或“自然损耗”。

所谓的“价格上涨”只是一个经济学掩体。本质上，这是一场关于生存权的博弈，而底层群体根本没有入场券。当供应商将成本 pass on to consumers 时，他们传递的不仅是价格，更是一个残酷的共谋逻辑：在这个系统中，少数人的权力游戏高于多数人的生存底线。

When reading such news, the public tends to focus on 'heatwaves' and 'El Niño,' as if hunger were a random result of a cosmic dice roll. But through the lens of Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is nothing more than a concentrated eruption of structural violence. The gap between Potential (the human capacity to produce food) and Actual (the reality of feeding the population) is, in itself, violence.

The wars mentioned—Ukraine, the Middle East—are classic products of a masculine-centric narrative. Male rulers, driven by so-called 'grand meanings' or geopolitical power, turn food export infrastructure into battlefields and fertilizer transit routes into bargaining chips. Driven by this meta-violence, food ceases to be a right to survive and becomes a weaponized cognitive entry point. While they discuss 'strategic assets,' the cost is 50 million people pushed into acute hunger.

Most ironically, this structural violence is highly gender-selective. In food-insecure households, women are often the last to eat. Due to the biological wall, pregnant women and children are the first to collapse under nutritional deficiency, a collapse that is frequently simplified in the cultural layer as 'family responsibility' or 'natural attrition.'

The so-called 'price increase' is merely an economic shield. In essence, this is a game of existential war over the right to survive, where the marginalized have no ticket to enter. When suppliers pass costs on to consumers, they are transmitting more than just a price; they are reinforcing a complicity logic: in this system, the power games of a few outweigh the survival baseline of the many.
