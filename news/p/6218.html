一个婊子，一个疯子A Whore and a Madwoman

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
她争来的是角色，争不来的是谁有资格定义她
She won the audition. She lost the right to define her own character.

格伦·克洛斯为《致命诱惑》里的 Alex Forrest 争了两样东西：试镜的机会，和那个她认为正确的结局。前者她赢了，道格拉斯在节目上承认整个剧组看错了人，说她“完全掌控”。后者她输了，输给了试映观众，也输给了坐在那间会议室里的其他人。

输掉的那一场更值得看。原版结局是 Alex 用一把沾着 Dan 指纹的刀自杀，栽赃他谋杀。试映观众恨透了这个女人，恨到剧组杀青六个月后还要回去重拍，最后改成由 Dan 的妻子 Beth 把她杀死。一个让女人自己动手、把男人拖进法律泥潭的结局，被换成一个好女人杀掉坏女人、家庭秩序恢复的结局。这不是创作分歧，这是观众在用票告诉制片方：这个角色只能以某种方式死。

克洛斯对这个角色的理解是“一个可怜的、失控的女人”，不是精神病。这个判断在电影里是成立的，Alex 要的不是性，是被看见，是被一个男人承认她存在过。而观众的愤怒恰恰来自这里：一个不肯安静退场的女人，比一个疯子更让人受不了。疯子可以被关起来，可以被同情；索要承认的女人只能被杀掉。

道格拉斯在这场争执里的位置很清楚。克洛斯复述他的原话：“宝贝，我是个婊子。”一屋子人里，唯一不需要为这个角色负责的人，用一句自嘲把自己摘了出去。他不是共谋者里最坏的那个，他只是那个有资格躺回去的人。会议室里真正拍板的是制片人和导演，但被记住的、被当成笑话讲的，是这句话。

克洛斯的抗争也不是什么英雄叙事。她争的是自己角色的完整性，这首先是演员的职业利益，其次才是别的。但这次职业利益刚好和一件更大的事重合：一个被写成疯女人的角色，有没有权利不被改写成更好杀的东西。她输了那一局，赢的那一局——试镜——反而被讲成了道格拉斯知错能改的段子。

这条新闻里最锋利的一句不是“我们错得离谱”，是试映观众“六个月后回来要求重拍”。六个月。一部电影已经拍完了，观众还是不肯放过这个女人，非要她死在另一个女人手里才安心。克洛斯说她当时不肯演，说她当场发了火。她发火的对象是一屋子决定她角色命运的人，而真正逼着他们改的人，不在那间屋子里，在放映厅里。

Glenn Close fought for two things on Fatal Attraction: the chance to audition, and the ending she believed was right. She won the first. Douglas admitted on the show that the whole production had misjudged her, that she was "in total command." She lost the second, to a test audience and to everyone else in that meeting room.

The lost fight is the one worth watching. In the original ending, Alex kills herself with a knife carrying her ex-lover Dan's fingerprints, framing him for murder. Test audiences hated her so much that the filmmakers came back six months after finishing the movie to reshoot it, and the final cut has Dan's wife Beth kill her. An ending where the woman acts on her own and drags the man into a legal mess was replaced by an ending where the good woman kills the bad woman and the family order is restored. That is not a creative disagreement. That is an audience voting on the only acceptable way this character is allowed to die.

Close's reading of the role was that Alex was not a psychopath but a "pathetic, out of control woman." That reading holds up in the film: what Alex wants is not sex, it is to be seen, to have one man acknowledge that she existed. And the audience's fury comes precisely from there. A woman who will not exit quietly is harder to tolerate than a madwoman. A madwoman can be locked up, can be pitied. A woman demanding recognition can only be killed.

Douglas's position in the argument is clear. Close recounts his line: "Babe, I'm a whore." In a room full of people, the one man who bears no responsibility for this character removes himself with a self-deprecating joke. He is not the worst of the co-conspirators. He is simply the one with the standing to lean back. The producers and the director were the ones who decided, but what gets remembered, what gets told as an anecdote, is that line.

Close's fight was not a heroic narrative either. She was defending the integrity of her own character, which is first of all an actor's professional interest, and only secondarily anything larger. But this time the professional interest happened to coincide with something bigger: whether a woman written as a madwoman has any right not to be rewritten into something easier to kill. She lost that round. The round she won, the audition, has been retold as a story about Douglas learning he was wrong.

The sharpest line in this story is not "we have never been so wrong in our lives." It is that the test audience came back six months later demanding a reshoot. Six months. The film was finished, and the audience still would not let this woman go, still needed her to die at another woman's hands before they could relax. Close says she refused, that she threw a fit in that room. She was angry at the people who decided her character's fate. The people who actually forced the change were not in the room. They were in the screening.
