改姓不是八卦，是表达A Surname Is a Receipt

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要安吉丽娜·朱莉与布拉德·皮特的女儿、18岁的薇薇安于11月2日获洛杉矶高等法院法官批准，将姓氏从「朱莉-皮特」改为「朱莉」，申请理由列为「个人原因」，无人提出异议。她是两人六个孩子中第四个改姓朱莉的，此前夏伊洛、扎哈拉、马多克斯已先后去掉「皮特」。两人2016年分居，离婚于2024年12月完成。
The newsVivienne, the 18-year-old daughter of Angelina Jolie and Brad Pitt, had her name legally changed from Vivienne Marcheline Jolie-Pitt to Vivienne Marcheline Jolie, approved by a Los Angeles judge on Nov. 2, with no objection filed. She is the fourth of the couple's six children to drop Pitt; Shiloh, Zahara and Maddox did so earlier. Jolie filed for divorce in 2016, and it was finalized in December 2024.
姓是父系私有制的收据，退回收据就是一次退出
A surname is the patriarchy's receipt; handing it back is an exit.

四个孩子改了姓，报道把它写成明星家事的连续剧。但它不是八卦，是一次表达的撤回。

姓是什么？第一章讲得清楚：表达决定你是谁。姓名是最省事的那一层表达，它天天被人叫、被人写、被人归档，你不需要开口就已经完成了站队。孩子姓父亲，是父系私有制最经济的一笔记账：血缘的归属、财产的传递、身份的默认值，全压缩进一个连字符里。朱莉-皮特，连字符两边不是对等的两家人，是一份合约的甲方乙方。

所以改姓不是情绪，是结算。报道里写，薇薇安2024年和母亲一起做《局外人》时，节目单上已经署的是「薇薇安·朱莉」；她七月刚满18岁就递交申请，理由一栏只写「个人」。18岁这个节点不是巧合——法律上第一次可以自己签字，她签的第一份文件就是把父亲那一半从自己名字里划掉。夏伊洛也是满18岁后第一个改的，扎哈拉、马多克斯随后。这不是四个孩子各自闹脾气，是一个接一个在同一件事上表态。

报道还写，诺克斯虽然没走法律程序，高中毕业证书上已经去掉了皮特。连没成年的那个都在用能用的方式做同一个动作。而报道引述的消息说，朱莉提出离婚十年后，皮特和六个孩子之间「几乎没有什么交流」，消息人士补了一句「这是个悲剧」。

悲剧这个词用得很讲究。它把一件有前因的事说成天灾。2016年那份离婚申请写的是国际航班上的一场激烈冲突，之后是八年的法律纠纷——这些是报道自己写的。孩子改姓不是从天而降的悲剧，是那八年之后长出来的结果。谁在承受代价，报道写得很清楚：六个孩子，一个接一个，用改名字这种最安静的方式，给一段关系投了票。

这里没有赢家叙事可讲。改姓不会让谁坐牢，不会让谁少拿一分钱，它连结构暴力的一根毛都没碰到。但它是文化层的一次动作：一个被默认的归属被当事人自己撤销了。第一章说身份的政治性是被动属性，身份政治是主动运用——一个18岁的人把自己的名字从「谁的女儿」改回「谁」，这就是最小单位、也最不可反驳的一次主动运用。

真正值得追问的不是他们为什么改，而是为什么改姓这件事到今天还要靠孩子满18岁、靠法官签字才能完成。默认值设在父亲那边，撤销它要走法院。这个默认值本身，就是一份没人投过票的合约。

至于那句「这是个悲剧」——说这话的人，心疼的是谁的名字被划掉了，而不是谁花了十年才敢划。

Four children changed their surname, and the coverage files it as the next episode of a celebrity divorce. It isn't gossip. It's an expression being withdrawn.

A surname is expression in its cheapest form: it gets said, written and filed for you every day, and you have already picked a side before you open your mouth. Children taking the father's name is the most economical entry in the ledger of patriarchal private property — lineage, inheritance, the default setting of identity, all compressed into one hyphen. Jolie-Pitt is not two families on equal footing. It's a contract with a Party A and a Party B.

So the name change isn't a mood. It's a settlement. The report notes that Vivienne was already billed as Vivienne Jolie in the playbill for The Outsiders, which she produced with her mother in 2024. She filed shortly after turning 18 in July, listing the reason as "personal." The timing isn't incidental: 18 is the first moment the law lets you sign for yourself, and the first document she signed removed her father's half of her name. Shiloh was the first to do it, also right after turning 18. Zahara and Maddox followed. This isn't four kids throwing separate tantrums. It's four people making the same statement, one after another.

The report adds that Knox hasn't filed, but has reportedly dropped Pitt from his high school diploma. Even the one who can't yet file is making the same move with the tools he has. And a source says that ten years after Jolie filed for divorce, there is "very little communication" between Pitt and his six children — followed by "It's a tragedy."

That word is doing careful work. It turns something with a cause into weather. The 2016 filing cited an allegedly heated confrontation on an international flight; then came eight years of legal disputes. The report says so itself. Children changing their names didn't fall from the sky. It grew out of those eight years. Who paid is right there in the piece: six children, one after another, voting on a relationship in the quietest way available.

There's no winner here. A name change puts no one in prison and costs no one a cent. It doesn't touch structural violence. But it is a move at the cultural layer: a default ownership, revoked by the person who was owned. Identity's political character is passive; identity politics is what you do with it. An 18-year-old taking her name back from "whose daughter" to "who" is the smallest possible unit of that — and the hardest to argue with.

The real question isn't why they changed it. It's why changing it still requires turning 18 and getting a judge's signature. The default sits with the father; undoing it takes a court. That default is itself a contract nobody voted on.

And "It's a tragedy" — the person saying it is mourning whose name got crossed out, not who needed ten years to dare cross it.
