免费证件照，付费的叙事A Free Headshot, a Paid Narrative

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 IndieWire ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要IndieWire 与 AT&T 于 10 月在洛杉矶合办 AFI Fest 电影人派对及「Future of Filmmaking」峰会，活动提供免费证件照拍摄与社交机会。导演 Lilian Mehrel 凭 2024 年 AT&T Untold Stories 竞赛的 100 万美元奖金拍出首部长片《Honeyjoon》，她在派对上致谢赞助方。该竞赛由 AT&T 与 Tribeca Festival 合作，总额 120 万美元。
The newsIndieWire and AT&T co-hosted a Filmmaker Party at AFI Fest in Los Angeles in October, alongside a "Future of Filmmaking" summit, offering networking and a complimentary headshot booth. Director Lilian Mehrel, whose debut feature "Honeyjoon" was funded by her $1 million win in the 2024 AT&T Untold Stories competition, thanked the sponsors at the party. The competition, run with Tribeca Festival, awards $1.2 million in total.
赞助商买下的不是电影，是「谁在讲故事」的解释权。
The sponsor isn't buying films. It's buying the right to define who gets to tell stories.

一场派对最诚实的地方，是它摆了什么。免费证件照。给谁拍？给那些还没被拍过的人。这是整篇通稿里唯一具体的资源分配——一个把人变成可被识别的职业主体的动作，由一家电信公司出钱。

AT&T 买的是「Untold Stories」这个词。100 万美元给了 Lilian Mehrel，让她拍出《Honeyjoon》；120 万美元总额、导师、全套支持，给「下一代电影人」。听起来像慈善。但慈善不会要求你在致谢里念出赞助商的名字，而她在派对上念了：「谢谢 AT&T，谢谢 AFI Fest，谢谢所有支持电影人的人。因为你们，更多未被讲述的故事得以被讲述。」这句话是这场交易的回执。

表达从来不是免费的。一个人能不能拍电影、拍什么、由谁出钱、拍完在哪个派对上被庆祝——这是一整套认知入口的分配。AT&T 不是在做电影，是在做「谁有资格讲故事」的筛选机制，顺便把自己写进筛选机制的名字里。Future of Filmmaking 峰会请来艺术家、高管、思想领袖，讨论好莱坞要往哪走；派对请来导演和电影节观众，聊电影。两个场合之间隔着一周，隔着的正是「谁决定方向」和「谁被方向决定」。

这不是说 Mehrel 的奖金是假的，她的片子是假的。100 万美元是真金白银，一个此前找不到落脚处的故事因此被拍出来，这是实打实的差额被缩小了。问题在于这个差额由谁定义、由谁结算。竞赛叫 Untold Stories，可「未被讲述」的标准由出钱的人划：什么样的故事算未被讲述，什么样的导演算下一代，什么样的电影值得被放进这个奖。企业不需要审查剧本，它只需要决定谁拿钱。

通稿里最响的一声，是没写出来的部分。派对在 Ovation Hollywood 办，出席的有导演、电影节观众、高管。没有名字的，是那些没进这个名单的人——没拿到 100 万、没被邀请、没在免费证件照前排队的人。他们不是新闻，他们是这个筛选机制的剩余项，而剩余项从不被写进赞助商的通稿。

「因为你们，更多未被讲述的故事得以被讲述。」这句话可以是真的。但它同时是一份定价单：故事的价格，由出钱的人报。

The most honest thing about a party is what it puts on the table. A complimentary headshot booth. For whom? For the people who haven't been photographed yet. That's the only concrete redistribution in the whole press release—an act that turns a person into a legible professional subject, paid for by a telecom company.

What AT&T is buying is the phrase "Untold Stories." One million dollars went to Lilian Mehrel so she could make "Honeyjoon"; $1.2 million in total, plus mentorship and support, goes to "the next generation of filmmakers." It sounds like charity. Charity doesn't require you to say the sponsor's name in your thank-you, and at the party she said it: thank you to AT&T, to AFI Fest, to everyone who champions filmmakers—because of you, more untold stories become told. That sentence is the receipt.

Expression was never free. Whether you can make a film, what you make, who pays for it, which party celebrates it afterward—this is a whole allocation of cognitive entry points. AT&T isn't making cinema. It's building a filter for who is allowed to tell stories, and writing its own name into the filter. The Future of Filmmaking summit gathered artists, executives, and thought leaders to discuss where Hollywood is heading; the party gathered directors and festival attendees to talk about movies. A week separates the two rooms, and what sits between them is the line between who decides the direction and who gets decided by it.

None of this makes Mehrel's prize fake or her film fake. A million dollars is real money, and a story that otherwise had no home got made—a real gap was closed. The question is who defines that gap and who settles it. The competition is called Untold Stories, but the criteria for "untold" are drawn by the people paying: which stories count as untold, which directors count as next-generation, which films deserve to be in this prize. A corporation doesn't need to censor scripts. It only needs to decide who gets the money.

The loudest thing in the release is what isn't written. The party was at Ovation Hollywood; directors, festival attendees, and executives were there. Unnamed are the ones who didn't make the list—didn't win the million, weren't invited, never stood in line at the free headshot booth. They aren't news. They're the remainder of the filter, and remainders never make it into the sponsor's release.

"Because of you, more untold stories become told." That can be true. It is also a price sheet: the price of a story, quoted by the one who pays.
