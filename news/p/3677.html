可持续的消费陷阱与人为制造的匮乏The Sustainability Scam and Manufactured Scarcity

科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
用环保叙事掩盖阶级筛选，用人为缺陷维持利润。
Using eco-narratives to mask class screening and manufactured defects to maintain profit.

Suri Core 1.0 的出现是一个典型的商业 scam。它在表面上通过降低价格和使用 bio-circular material 扮演一个“可持续”的进步者，试图通过缩小 Potential 与 Actual 之间的差额来吸引预算有限的消费者。但只要你拆开它的产品矩阵，就会发现这不过是一场关于认知入口的武器化操纵。

最恶心的地方在于那个故意被削短的机身。Suri 让 Core 1.0 无法适配 Pro 2.0 的充电盒，这绝不是为了环保，而是为了在 structural 层面上制造一种人为的匮乏。这种“人工弱化”是为了强迫用户在购买时就做出阶级选择：你要么接受一个被阉割的低端版本，要么支付溢价购买那个拥有“铝制外壳”和“UVC 消毒”的身份符号。这本质上是利用审美和功能定义权在进行阶级筛选。

至于所谓的“可持续”，不过是 cultural violence 的高级包装。用玉米淀粉和废弃食用油制造塑料，然后通过一个“免费回收”的闭环让消费者产生一种自己在拯救地球的幻觉。这种叙事将复杂的环境问题简化为个人的消费选择，让消费者在购买一件电子产品时获得了道德上的 anodyne。真正的可持续应该是延长产品生命周期，而不是通过不断迭代 1.0、2.0 并人为制造不兼容来驱动重复消费。

这是一个典型的共谋场域：品牌提供道德掩体，测评者在细节中赞美其“良心”，而消费者在潜意识中接受了这种被定义的“最优解”。当一个公司在报告中承认自己不是“perfect business”时，它实际上是在通过坦诚来换取更深层的信任，从而更高效地执行其利润最大化的逻辑。

The Suri Core 1.0 is a textbook commercial scam. On the surface, it plays the role of a 'sustainable' progressive by lowering prices and using bio-circular materials, attempting to close the gap between Potential and Actual for budget-conscious consumers. But once you dismantle its product matrix, it reveals itself as a weaponized manipulation of cognitive entry points.

The most repulsive detail is the intentionally shortened chassis. By making the Core 1.0 incompatible with the Pro 2.0 charging case, Suri isn't practicing sustainability; it is manufacturing scarcity at a structural level. This 'artificial weakening' forces users into a class-based choice at the point of purchase: either accept a castrated entry-level version or pay a premium for a status symbol defined by an 'aluminum finish' and 'UVC disinfection.' This is essentially using the power of aesthetic and functional definition to perform class screening.

As for the so-called 'sustainability,' it is merely a high-end packaging of cultural violence. Creating plastics from cornstarch and waste cooking oil, then offering a 'free recycling' loop, allows consumers to live in a hallucination that they are saving the planet. This narrative reduces complex environmental crises to individual shopping choices, providing a moral anodyne. True sustainability would mean extending product lifecycles, not driving repetitive consumption through constant iterations of 1.0 and 2.0 with artificial incompatibilities.

This is a field of complicity: the brand provides the moral cover, the reviewer praises its 'conscience' in the details, and the consumer subconsciously accepts this defined 'optimal expression.' When a company admits in its report that it isn't a 'perfect business,' it is using performative honesty to earn deeper trust, allowing it to execute its profit-maximization logic more efficiently.
