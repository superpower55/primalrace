Ted Lasso 的饼干与女性教练的 Catch-22Ted Lasso's Biscuits and the Female Coach's Catch-22

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Athletic ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
资格证是结构暴力的伪装，而机会才是存在性战争的入场券。
Certifications are the camouflage of structural violence; opportunity is the only ticket to the existential war.

《Ted Lasso》这部剧最讽刺的笑点在于：一个完全不合格的美国橄榄球教练，可以通过扮演一个“善良且乐观”的角色，在英超足球这个高度专业化的领域获得顶级的权力席位。在剧作逻辑里，这叫“鱼出水”的喜剧冲突；但在现实的性别博弈中，这就是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。男性可以凭借个性和某种模糊的“领导力”被赋予试错机会，而女性必须在拿到所有证书之后，才能被允许进入试错的门槛。

文中提到的那个 Catch-22 陷阱，本质上是 structural violence 的精准运作。系统要求女性教练拥有 Pro Licence 和精英执教经验，但系统同时通过垄断教练员培养路径（长期以来作为男球员的退休安置计划）和雇佣偏见，切断了女性获取这些经验的入口。当雇主声称“为了降低风险而选择有经验的人”时，他们实际上是在共谋维护一个由男性定义的“经验”标准。在这种逻辑下，资格证不再是能力的证明，而是一道生物墙的延伸——它被用来合法化对女性的排斥：你没有证书，所以你是不合格的；你无法获得证书，因为你没有被雇佣的机会。

最令人作呕的细节是，剧集试图用 Lasso 作为一个“啦啦队长”来拯救被挤掉席位的女性教练 Alice Chilton。这是一种极其典型的 weaponized 叙事：将结构性的剥夺包装成个体之间的人格魅力互动。女性不需要一个男性的“精神支持”或“鼓励”来获得权力，她们需要的是解释权的换手，是资源分配的公正表达。把女性的职业晋升寄托在某个男性的“慷慨”或“赏识”上，不过是让女性在父权结构中寻找另一种形式的生存最优解，而这恰恰是主体性的死亡。

The most biting irony of "Ted Lasso" is that an utterly unqualified American football coach can secure a top-tier power seat in the hyper-professional world of English soccer simply by playing the role of the "kind and optimistic" outsider. In screenwriting, this is a "fish out of water" trope; in the real-world gender game, it is a textbook masculine-centric narrative. Men are granted the luxury of trial and error based on personality or a vague sense of "leadership," while women are required to collect every possible certification before they are even allowed to stand at the threshold of that same trial.

The Catch-22 mentioned in the article is essentially the precise operation of structural violence. The system demands Pro Licences and elite experience from female coaches, yet simultaneously severs the pathways to acquire them by maintaining coaching as a retirement plan for male players and upholding hiring biases. When employers claim they are "reducing risk" by hiring experienced candidates, they are in complicity to maintain a definition of "experience" authored by men. In this logic, certifications cease to be proof of competence and become an extension of the biological wall—used to legitimize exclusion: you lack the license, therefore you are unqualified; you cannot get the license, because you are never hired.

The most nauseating part is the show's attempt to position Lasso as a "cheerleader" for Alice Chilton, the female coach displaced by his arrival. This is a weaponized narrative: packaging structural deprivation as a heartwarming interpersonal interaction. Women do not need the "spiritual support" or "encouragement" of a man to obtain power; they need a transfer of the power to interpret and a just expression of resource distribution. Relying on the "generosity" or "appreciation" of a man for professional advancement is merely seeking another survival optimal expression within the patriarchy—and that is precisely the death of subjectivity.
