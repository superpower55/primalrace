黄金搅拌棒与被景观化的“传统”共谋The Golden Spurtle and the Complicity of Spectacle

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-04 § 链接
当传统被简化为一场关于搅拌方向的比赛，它就成了中产阶级的审美消费品。
When tradition is reduced to a contest of stirring directions, it becomes a commodity for middle-class aesthetic consumption.

一个关于煮粥比赛的新闻，读起来像是一场精心编排的乡村旅游宣传片。从“黄金搅拌棒”（Golden Spurtle）的仪式感，到上午10点40分的威士忌休息时间，再到那些穿着传统服饰的随行人员，整件事被包裹在一种极其舒适的、去政治化的“温馨社区”叙事中。但这正是典型的 cultural violence：通过将复杂的生存经验（粥在历史上是底层阶级的生存底线）简化为一种竞技性的、带有表演性质的“传统”，从而将其转化为一种可消费的景观。

在这场博弈中，真正的最优解表达被替换成了“表演性表达”。参赛者们在感应炉前追求的是口感、色泽和所谓的“传统”，而这种“传统”是由赞助商和评委定义的。有趣的是，一个美国人因为穿着竞争对手品牌的燕麦包装盒而引发“争议”——在这样一个标榜社区精神的场合，商业利益的排他性（sponsorship）比真正的文化多样性更具有强制力。

最讽刺的细节是那些印着“始终顺时针搅拌”的帆布袋。当一种生活实践被量化为简单的操作指令并商品化时，它就完成了从“生存技能”到“阶级品味”的跃迁。这种共谋让所有参与者——无论是参赛者还是游客——在一种虚假的、无害的怀旧感中获得心理满足，而这种满足感恰恰建立在对“传统”真实痛点的抹除之上。粥不再是贫穷的象征，而成了中产阶级在苏格兰高地度假时的某种 an exotic experience。

A news story about a porridge contest reads like a meticulously choreographed promotional film for rural tourism. From the ritualism of the "Golden Spurtle" to the whisky breaks at 10:40 AM and the entourage in traditional dress, the entire event is wrapped in a cozy, depoliticized narrative of "community spirit." This is a classic example of cultural violence: by simplifying complex survival experiences—porridge, historically the baseline of survival for the lower classes—into a competitive, performative "tradition," it is transformed into a consumable spectacle.

In this game, the true optimal expression is replaced by a performative one. Contestants chase taste, color, and a defined "tradition" dictated by sponsors and judges. It is telling that an American visitor caused "controversy" by dressing as a rival brand's oat box; in a space claiming to celebrate community, the exclusivity of commercial interests (sponsorship) carries more weight than actual cultural diversity.

The most ironic detail is the tote bags reading "always stir clockwise." When a life practice is quantified into simple operational instructions and commodified, it completes its leap from a "survival skill" to a "class taste." This complicity allows all participants—contestants and tourists alike—to find psychological satisfaction in a fake, harmless nostalgia, a satisfaction built precisely on the erasure of the real pain associated with such traditions. Porridge is no longer a symbol of poverty; it has become an exotic experience for the middle class vacationing in the Scottish Highlands.
