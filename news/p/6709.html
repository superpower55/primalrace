先造一个敌人，再卖选票Invent an Enemy, Then Sell the Ballot

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要得克萨斯州中期选举前，州共和党以「禁止伊斯兰教法」为纲领，州长阿博特承诺全面禁止，州议会成立「无伊斯兰教法党团」并已有38名成员。穆斯林占该州人口不到2%，休斯敦伊斯兰协会在周五礼拜推动选民登记，希望用投票表明他们不是任何人的政治替罪羊。
The newsAhead of the US midterms, Texas Republicans have built a platform around banning Shariah law, with Governor Greg Abbott promising a full ban and a state caucus of 38 members. Muslims are under two percent of Texas's population; Houston's Islamic Society expanded voter registration drives, hoping to show they are no one's political boogeyman.
没有伊斯兰教法，只有需要伊斯兰教法的选举
There is no Shariah in Texas. There is only an election that needs one.

得州没有一部伊斯兰教法，一天都没有过。但州长阿博特在共和党中期大会上宣布「我们不等华盛顿，我们要在得州全面禁止伊斯兰教法」，州共和党把「Don't Sharia my Texas」写进官方纲领，州议会三月成立「无伊斯兰教法党团」，150 个席位里已经拉了 38 个人。禁一个不存在的东西，靠的不是法律，是叙事——先造出敌人，再把自己卖成唯一的守卫。

这是第三章的教科书操作：夺取认知入口，把「穆斯林公民的正常参与」重新定义成威胁的证据。乔治城「桥接计划」的报告写得清楚，政客们拿来当罪证的，是社区参与、人口增长、参与社区开发——一群人在自己的城市里正常生活，被读成了伊斯兰教法正在推进。休斯敦的医生 Sarah Dabbas 说得直白：美国历史上从来没有谁把伊斯兰法强加给政府，我们只是这样实践自己的信仰。

更精确地说，他们不是要禁一个东西，是要给一群人定价。穆斯林占得州人口不到 2%，这个数字在选举里几乎不值钱，但作为替罪羊非常值钱。阿博特要把 CAIR 得州分会定为「外国恐怖组织」——这个标签本该由联邦政府来贴，州长自己造了一个；州总检察长帕克斯顿干脆起诉 CAIR，要它停止在州内运作。CAIR 反过来以言论与表达自由起诉阿博特。注意这里谁在付代价：一个 42 岁的医生说她这两年考虑过离开美国，她不想自己的儿子被当成恐怖分子，而这件事她已经经历过了。政治人物拿到的是选票和捐款，代价记在一个孩子的档案上。

最值得看的是共和党穆斯林那一段。一位参加州党代会的穆斯林共和党人说，有党主席叫两名穆斯林代表退党，有州参议员在小组会上说穆斯林「按教法有义务撒谎」。他说这「非常伤人」，说自己认同共和党的商业价值、家庭价值，但他会把一些共和党候选人从选票上划掉。这就是共谋者理论里最标准的一幕：他站的那一边，正在把他从「我们」里划出去。他还在地方共和党政治里，只是这次不投某些人——这是他能做的最优解表达，代价是继续留在一个需要他闭嘴的联盟里。

而 CAIR 的 Sameeha Rizvi 点出了另一半共谋：很多社区领袖相信「埋头别说话」才是度过这一切的办法，他们叫人去投票，却不在公共舆论里开口。这是假.最优解表达的族群版本——用沉默换安全，换来的只是别人替你定义你是什么。

报道里还有一件不该被轻轻放过的事：得州教育委员会通过了高中课程，批评者说它不成比例地聚焦「激进伊斯兰」，并错误描绘了先知穆罕默德。选票能换掉官员，课程会在换掉官员之后继续教下去。元暴力就是这样运作的——它不在选票上，它在教科书里，等这一轮选举过去，它还在。

Texas has never had Shariah law, not for a day. Yet Governor Abbott told the state Republican convention, 「We are not waiting on Washington, DC. We will fully ban Sharia Law in Texas.」 The party wrote 「Don't Sharia my Texas」 into its official platform. In March the legislature launched a 「Sharia-free Texas Caucus」 that now counts 38 of 150 members. Banning something that does not exist isn't done with law. It's done with narrative: manufacture the enemy, then sell yourself as the only guard.

This is the textbook move from Chapter 3 — seize the cognitive entrance and redefine ordinary Muslim civic life as evidence of a threat. Georgetown's Bridge Initiative report is blunt about it: what politicians cite as proof is public participation, demographic growth, involvement in community development. People living normally in their own city, read as an advancing legal system. Sarah Dabbas, a Houston physician, puts it plainly: never in US history has anyone imposed Muslim law on the government. We just practise.

More precisely, they are not banning a thing. They are pricing a people. Muslims are under two percent of Texas — a number nearly worthless at the ballot box, and very valuable as a scapegoat. Abbott wants to designate CAIR Texas a 「foreign terrorist organisation」, a label normally applied by the federal government; the governor is minting his own. Attorney General Paxton has sued CAIR to stop it operating in the state. CAIR has sued Abbott on free speech and expression grounds. Note who pays. A 42-year-old doctor says she has contemplated leaving the US in the last two years, that she does not want her son viewed as a terrorist, that she has already dealt with it herself. The politician collects votes. The bill lands on a child's file.

The sharpest passage is the Muslim Republicans. One delegate recounts that the outgoing party chair told two Muslim delegates to leave the party, and a state senator told a panel Muslims are 「required by Sharia to lie」. He calls it 「very hurtful」, says he identifies with the party's business and family values — and will leave some Republican candidates off his ballot. This is the co-conspirator theory in its purest form: the side he stands with is editing him out of 「we」. Staying in local Republican politics while withholding some votes is his optimal expression. The price is remaining in a coalition that requires his silence.

Sameeha Rizvi names the other half of the complicity: many community leaders believe the way through is to put your head down and stay quiet. They tell people to vote but will not speak in the court of public opinion. That is the communal version of a false optimal expression — trade silence for safety, and let others define what you are.

One thing should not slide past: the State Board of Education approved a high school curriculum that critics say focuses disproportionately on 「radical Islam」 and misportrays the prophet Muhammad. Ballots can remove officials. Curricula keep teaching after the officials are gone. That is how meta-violence works — it isn't on the ballot. It's in the textbook, waiting for this election to end.
