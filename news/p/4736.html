储备罐头不能抵御结构性暴力的坍塌Canned Food Cannot Patch a Collapsing Structure

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-03 § 链接
将气候灾难个体化为“家庭储备”是典型的结构性暴力逃避。
Individualizing climate catastrophe as 'household stockpiling' is a classic evasion of structural violence.

英国政府让公民囤罐头来应对“千年一遇”的厄尔尼诺，这在叙事上是一个极其阴险的 weaponization。它把一个全球性的、由化石燃料驱动的 structural violence（结构性暴力），悄悄地置换成了个体的“resilience”（韧性）问题。翻译成人话就是：世界要崩了，但请你自行购买罐头，不要在断水断电时给政府添麻烦。

在这种叙事里，Potential（本可达到的安全状态）与 Actual（目前的脆弱状态）之间的差额，被政府定义为“准备不足”，而不是“治理失效”。当环境大臣 Angela Eagle 谈论水库和罐头时，她实际上在构建一种共谋：政府提供一个最低限度的、表演性的基础设施，而将生存的最终风险全部转嫁给个体。这是一种典型的 masculine-centric narrative（男性中心叙事）——用一种“面对危机要强韧”的英雄主义措辞，掩盖资源分配不均的元暴力。

最讽刺的是，这种“储备建议”在不同阶级之间有着完全不同的定价。对于中产阶级，这只是一个购物清单；但对于那些生活在结构性贫困中的人，所谓的“stock up”本身就是一个 scam。当 5000 万人面临饥饿，而英国政府在讨论如何让公民“更具韧性”时，这种 resilience 实际上成了弱势群体在被抛弃时的唯一安慰剂。

囤罐头能让你在停电的几天里不饿死，但它无法修补一个正在坍塌的生物圈。如果一个国家的危机管理方案就是让公民在家里囤粮，那么这个政府承认的其实只有一件事：他们已经放弃了对结构的掌控，只剩下对个体的规训。

The British government urging citizens to hoard tinned food for a 'once-in-a-millennium' El Niño is a sinister piece of weaponised expression. It stealthily displaces a global structural violence—driven by fossil fuels—into a matter of individual 'resilience.' In plain terms: the world is breaking, so please buy your own cans and avoid bothering the state during the blackout.

In this narrative, the gap between Potential (the safety we should have) and Actual (our current fragility) is defined as 'lack of preparation' rather than 'governance failure.' When Environment Secretary Angela Eagle speaks of reservoirs and tins, she is constructing a complicity: the state provides a minimal, performative infrastructure, while the ultimate survival risk is offloaded onto the individual. This is a textbook masculine-centric narrative—using the rhetoric of 'being tough' and 'resilient' to mask the meta-violence of systemic resource maldistribution.

The irony is that this 'stocking up' advice has different pricing across classes. For the middle class, it's a shopping list; for those in structural poverty, 'stock up' is a scam. While 50 million people face acute hunger, the UK government's solution for its citizens is 'resilience'—a term that, for the marginalized, is merely a sedative for being abandoned.

Canned food might keep you from starving during a few days of power loss, but it cannot repair a collapsing biosphere. When a government's crisis management boils down to telling people to hoard food, they are admitting one thing: they have lost control of the structure, and all they have left is the discipline of the individual.
