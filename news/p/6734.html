污染者出钱，病人出力The Polluters Pay, the Sick Pay Too

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要德国医生早在1974年就演示了塑料微粒能穿透动物肠道进入血液和大脑，与会企业随后数十年压下风险、抵制监管。半个世纪后，MAHA 运动既反对微塑料污染，又支持接受污染企业政治献金的共和党。今年6月环保署拒绝把微塑料列入饮用水监测清单，迄今只推出研究项目和象征性措施。
The newsA German doctor showed in 1974 that microscopic plastic particles can cross animal intestines into blood and brain; the companies present then spent decades downplaying risks and fighting regulation. Fifty years on, the MAHA movement campaigns against microplastics while backing Republicans funded by the same polluters. In June the EPA declined to put microplastics on its drinking-water watch list.
把毒贩和病人绑进同一个联盟，是最高级的共谋。
Binding the poisoned to the poisoners in one coalition is the highest form of complicity.

1974年，一位德国胃肠病学家向纽约 Delmonico 酒店的听众演示：动物吞下的塑料微粒能高效穿透肠道内壁，进入血液和淋巴，再到脑和肝。台下坐着 Dow Chemical 和 Exxon 的人。Union Carbide 的一位高管事后抱怨，这场演讲在与会者中间落了个「吞食 PVC 树脂的恐怖故事」的名声。半个世纪过去，这些公司还在，微粒也在，只是现在它们出现在人的血液、大脑、胎盘和睾丸里。

这不是意外，是产品设计之外的另一条生产线：先制造污染，再制造「污染没那么严重」的叙事。Vinyl chloride 是已知的人类致癌物，低剂量暴露的动物长出肿瘤，吸入的工人反复得同一种罕见癌症，而塑料生产商和行业协会继续抵制联邦监管、继续淡化风险。1984年 Conoco Chemical 的一位高管说：「我们生活在致癌物的海洋里，我们不仅活着——还活得更久了。」这句话是文化暴力的标准样品：把结构暴力造成的死亡，说成人类韧性的证明。

MAHA 的成立前提是承认这个海洋有毒。KFF 五月的民调里，41% 的美国人认同 MAHA，多数同时认同 MAGA。今年6月，环保署拒绝把微塑料列入饮用水优先监测清单，Moms Across America 的创始执行主任 Zen Honeycutt 公开表达「深切担忧」，要求环保署多做一点。然后呢？Kennedy 的 HHS 四月启动了一个 1.44 亿美元的「微塑料研究项目」，白宫的 MAHA 报告说微塑料可能让孩子生病，环保署同意研究。研究、研究、再研究——这是结构暴力最体面的形态：把「正在处理」当成「已经处理」。

钱的部分更直白。据《纽约时报》调查，特朗普第二任期一开始，工业污染者就排队申请豁免拜登时期的污染限制。环保署为「受监管群体」开了电子邮箱，2025年3月31日前正式提交申请。同年3月19日，代表美国最大塑料和石化制造商的 American Fuel and Petrochemical Manufacturers 协会和 American Chemistry Council 的政治行动委员会，向共和党候选人和共和党相关 PAC 捐了13笔共5万美元，同一天给民主党3笔共1万美元。月底，两家协会致信环保署，要求两年豁免，不必遵守限制 ethylene oxide 等毒素的空气质量规则——ACC 自己说这是塑料产品的「关键构建块」，也是致癌物。七月，在更多会议和更多捐款之后，它们拿到了想要的东西。

这就是共谋者理论的教科书案例，而且共谋不分党派。捐款是明码标价的，豁免是按时交付的，MAHA 的基层支持者被留在原地，手里拿着一份研究报告和一句「可能让孩子生病」。Honeycutt 上周在艾奥瓦的竞选集会上和 Kennedy 交谈，还和刚辞职的 Calley Means 合影。MAHA 峰会由 Kennedy 的长期出版商 Tony Lyons 运营的非营利组织主办，赞助商包括 Walmart 和 Hims & Hers——后者靠向焦虑的千禧一代卖 GLP-1、抗抑郁药和 finasteride 赚钱，去监管议程对它只有好处。Heritage Foundation 的 Jennifer Galardi 警告 MAHA 别沾 Bryan Johnson 那套「重新想象人性」的「反乌托邦宗教」，建议用「拥抱挣扎、痛苦甚至死亡」来替代。一个长期帮企业接管政府的机构，现在来教健康运动怎么保持纯洁，这本身就是一个笑话。

真正让人不安的不是 MAHA 的虚伪。虚伪是政治的正常代谢物。真正的问题是：我们手里有一整套记录完备的事实——1974年的演示、工人的罕见癌症、动物身上的肿瘤、血液和胎盘里的微粒、Nature 上关于微粒遇水释放有害化学物质的新论文——却几乎无法把它们兑换成问责、稳定的政治多数，或者一项能扛住换届的政策。Exxon 几十年前就知道化石燃料让地球升温，故事曝光后的十多年里它照旧录得创纪录利润。这条流水线太熟练了。

所以民主党人如果真想接住 MAHA 的基层，不必去迎合那些反疫苗的边缘信念。Ocean Conservancy 今夏的民调显示，近九成美国人对科学家在人体内发现微塑料感到担忧，63% 表示「非常担忧」。这个数字比任何左翼议题都更基础：这些东西在让我们生病，这应该停止。问题在于，说「应该停止」的人和有能力让它停止的人，是同一批收钱的人。MAHA 没能拆开这个结，它只是把自己也系了进去。

In 1974, a German gastroenterologist showed an audience at New York's Hotel Delmonico that microscopic plastic particles swallowed by animals infiltrate their intestinal linings and travel through blood and lymph to the brain and liver. Dow Chemical and Exxon were in the room. An executive at Union Carbide later complained that the talk had earned a reputation among attendees as "a horror story on ingesting PVC resin." Half a century on, the companies are still here, the particles are still here, and now they turn up in human blood, brains, placentas, and testicles.

This is not an accident. It is a second production line running alongside the first: manufacture the contamination, then manufacture the story that the contamination is fine. Vinyl chloride is a known human carcinogen; animals exposed at low levels grew tumors, workers who inhaled it kept developing the same rare cancer, and plastics producers and their trade associations kept fighting federal rules and downplaying risk. In 1984 a Conoco Chemical executive declared, "We live in a sea of carcinogens and not only are we surviving—we are living longer!" That sentence is a cultural-violence sample: structural death recast as human resilience.

MAHA exists because it admits the sea is toxic. In a May KFF poll, 41 percent of Americans identified with MAHA, most of them also MAGA. In June the EPA declined to add microplastics to its priority drinking-water watch list, and Zen Honeycutt, founding executive director of Moms Across America, voiced her group's deep concern and urged the agency to do more. What followed was a $144 million HHS "microplastics research program" launched in April, a White House report saying microplastics might be making kids sick, and an EPA promise to study them. Study, study, study—structural violence in its most presentable form, where "being handled" stands in for "handled."

The money is blunter. According to a New York Times investigation, industrial polluters began requesting exemptions from Biden-era pollution limits almost as soon as Trump's second term began. The EPA set up an electronic mailbox for the "regulated community" to file formal requests by March 31, 2025. On March 19, the political action committees of the American Fuel and Petrochemical Manufacturers association and the American Chemistry Council—representing the country's largest plastics and petrochemical makers—made 13 donations totaling $50,000 to Republican candidates and Republican-aligned PACs, and three totaling $10,000 to Democrats. At the end of the month the two associations wrote to the EPA asking for a two-year exemption from air-quality rules limiting ethylene oxide, which the ACC itself calls a "critical building block" for plastics and which is a carcinogen. In July, after more meetings and many more donations, they got what they wanted.

This is the co-conspirator theory in its purest form, and complicity has no party. The donations are priced, the exemptions are delivered on schedule, and MAHA's rank and file are left holding a research report and a maybe about sick children. Honeycutt spoke with Kennedy at an Iowa rally last week and posed for a photo with Calley Means, who had just resigned as Kennedy's senior adviser. The MAHA Summit was organized by a nonprofit run by Kennedy's longtime publisher, Tony Lyons, and funded by corporations including Walmart and Hims & Hers—the latter selling GLP-1s, antidepressants, and finasteride to anxious millennials, with everything to gain from deregulation. Jennifer Galardi of the Heritage Foundation warned MAHA away from Bryan Johnson's "dystopian religion" of reimagining human nature and suggested embracing struggle, pain, and even death instead. An institution that spent decades helping companies run the government now lecturing a health movement on purity is its own joke.

The unnerving part is not MAHA's hypocrisy; hypocrisy is normal political metabolism. The trouble is that we hold an arsenal of well-documented facts—the 1974 presentation, the workers' rare cancers, the tumors in animals, the particles in blood and placentas, the new Nature paper on chemicals released when microplastics meet water—and we can barely convert any of it into accountability, durable majorities, or a policy that survives an administration change. Exxon knew for decades that fossil fuels warm the planet and has posted record profits in the years since. The line is that well rehearsed.

So Democrats who want to reach MAHA voters need not indulge the anti-vaccine fringe. A summer poll from the Ocean Conservancy found nearly nine in ten Americans are concerned that scientists have found microplastics in human bodies, and 63 percent are very concerned. That is more basic than any lefty talking point: this stuff is making us sick, and it should stop. The problem is that the people who say it should stop and the people with the power to stop it are the same people cashing the checks. MAHA never untied that knot. It tied itself into it.
