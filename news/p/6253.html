保护政治盟友的避难权Asylum for Allies, Passports for Enemies

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
人道主义是给盟友的，护照是给敌人的。
Humanitarian parole is for allies; the passport is for the enemy.

巴西法院没收了博索纳罗的护照，美国给了他一张签证。鲁比奥说“我们没有促成任何事”，特朗普说“他想待多久就待多久”。这两句话不矛盾：一个负责否认动作，一个负责宣布结果。

博索纳罗在巴西面对的是腐败、洗钱、逃税的调查，警方认定他有潜逃风险，所以要求他交出护照。美国国务院在他输掉决选之后第二天就发了签证，他经阿根廷、厄瓜多尔到了迈阿密。鲁比奥说“他之前就有签证”，好像签证是天上掉下来的。一个被本国司法限制出境的人，在另一个国家的入境系统里畅通无阻——这不是“没有协助”，这是协助得足够干净，干净到不需要留下动作。

“人道主义假释”这个类别值得停一下。它通常给的是战乱里逃出来的、被遣返就会死的人。现在它给了一个拒绝承认败选、拿不出任何证据却反复说选举舞弊的前总统候选人。人道主义这个词被用在这里，功能是给一个政治决定套上法律外观。加尔通说文化暴力的作用就是让结构暴力看起来不像暴力——这里是让一个主权国家的司法程序看起来像可以绕过去的东西。

特朗普说“他受到了非常恶劣的对待”。恶劣在哪里？输掉选举，然后被调查。如果这就是恶劣，那么特朗普之前说巴西选举“被操纵”就不只是随口一说，而是在给这个结论预先铺路：选举结果不算数，所以追责不算数，所以护照不算数。这一套叙事在第三章里是标准动作——先夺取“什么是事实”的制造权，再从制造出来的事实里生产可能性。巴西的选票是事实，博索纳罗的“舞弊”是叙事，美国选择了叙事。

报道里有一个名字只出现了一次：Jason Miller，白宫官员，据美国媒体报道部分参与了这趟行程的安排。鲁比奥说政府“没有直接参与任何移动”，这句话和那个名字并排放着，读者自己判断。整篇报道里也没有巴西政府的任何一句话——鲁比奥在亚特兰大见了巴西外长维埃拉，谈了什么，报道没写。被冒犯的一方没有台词，这是这类新闻的常态。

最值得记住的是鲁比奥那句“我们没有促成任何事”的语法。它把国家行为拆成一个个可以否认的动作：没有安排航班，没有派人护送，没有打电话。但签证批了，人进来了，总统说想待多久待多久。一个国家能对一个逃开司法的人做的最大协助，就是让所有具体的协助都变得不必要。

Brazil's courts took Bolsonaro's passport. The United States gave him a visa. Rubio says "we didn't facilitate anything." Trump says he can stay "as long as he wants." These two statements are not in conflict: one denies the motion, the other announces the outcome.

Bolsonaro faces investigation in Brazil for corruption, money laundering and tax evasion. Police deemed him a flight risk and ordered him to surrender his passport. The State Department issued visas to him and his family the day after his run-off loss. He flew to Miami via Argentina and Ecuador. Rubio says he "previously held a visa," as if visas fall from the sky. A man whose own judiciary has restricted his travel passes through another country's border system without friction. That is not the absence of assistance. That is assistance clean enough to leave no gesture behind.

"Humanitarian parole" deserves a pause. It normally covers people fleeing war, people who would die if returned. Here it covers a former presidential candidate who refuses to concede, calls the results fraudulent, and offers no evidence. The word humanitarian is doing the work of dressing a political decision in legal clothing. Galtung's point about cultural violence is exactly this: it makes structural violence look like something else, and here it makes one country's judicial process look like something that can simply be stepped around.

Trump says Bolsonaro was "treated very shabbily." Shabbily how? He lost an election and then faced investigation. If that is shabbiness, then Trump's earlier claim that Brazil's election was "rigged" was not an aside. It was groundwork: the result doesn't count, so the accountability doesn't count, so the passport doesn't count. That is the standard sequence — seize the power to manufacture what counts as fact, then produce possibilities from the manufactured fact. Brazil's ballots are fact. Bolsonaro's "fraud" is narrative. The US chose the narrative.

One name appears exactly once: Jason Miller, a White House official, reported by US media to have partly arranged the journey. Rubio says the government was not "involved directly in any of the moves." Put that sentence next to that name and let the reader do the arithmetic. Nor does the report contain a single word from the Brazilian government. Rubio met Foreign Minister Vieira in Atlanta on Friday and Saturday. What was said is not in the text. The offended party has no lines. That is normal for this genre.

The sentence to remember is "we didn't facilitate anything." Its grammar breaks a state action into deniable motions: no plane arranged, no escort assigned, no phone call made. But the visa was granted, the man entered, and the president said he can stay as long as he wants. The greatest assistance a country can give someone fleeing justice is to make every specific act of assistance unnecessary.
