用“最高分”掩盖的结构性屠杀The Structural Massacre Behind 'Bumper' Results

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
平均分的上涨是 PR 叙事，地区差距的扩大才是真实的 Structural Violence。
Rising averages are a PR narrative; widening regional gaps are the actual Structural Violence.

看到“创纪录的高分”这种标题，第一反应应该是警觉。在加尔通的暴力三角里，只要 Actual（实际状态）低于 Potential（本可达到的状态），差额就是暴力。当伦敦的 A*/A 率冲到 32.7%，而东米德兰兹还在 23.1% 徘徊时，这 9.6 个百分点的差距不是什么“教育挑战”，而是一场精准的结构性剥夺。

这本质上是一场关于“机会定价权”的博弈。伦敦成为了一个 educational outlier，它通过更高的教师薪资、文化吸引力和移民家庭的竞争压力，构建了一座巨大的认知壁垒。而北东部和东米德兰兹的学生在面对同样的考试卷子时，他们面对的不是知识，而是被剥夺的公共服务、匮乏的基础设施和深陷贫困的社区环境。这种差距在疫情后被剧烈放大，从 2019 年的 3.9 个点扩到现在的 9.6 个点，这意味着结构性暴力在加速升级。

最令人作呕的是那些“激光聚焦” (laser focus) 的改进计划或所谓的“权力下放”。这种叙事试图将系统性崩溃简化为“管理问题”或“当地条件问题”。当教育被要求去“补偿”贫困、医疗匮乏和交通瘫痪时，学校就成了父权制和资本主义共谋下的一个临时收容所。试图用一点额外的资金来修补一个已经烂掉的系统，这本身就是一种 cultural violence——它让受害者相信，只要你足够努力，或者政府多给点钱，你就能跨越那道由出生地决定的生物墙和阶级墙。

人才分布是均匀的，但机会被垄断了。这种垄断通过教育成绩单被合法化，最终转化为职场上的定价权。这场战争的赢家在伦敦庆祝，而输家在北部的雨中被告知：这只是一个“地区差异”问题。

Whenever you see headlines celebrating 'record-breaking results,' be alert. In Galtung's Violence Triangle, any gap between the Actual and the Potential is violence. When London's A*/A rate hits 32.7% while the East Midlands lingers at 23.1%, that 9.6 percentage point gap isn't an 'educational challenge'—it is a precise structural deprivation.

This is fundamentally a game of 'pricing power' over opportunity. London has become an educational outlier, constructing a massive cognitive wall through higher teacher pay, cultural magnetism, and the competitive pressure of ambitious migrant families. Meanwhile, students in the North East and East Midlands aren't just fighting a curriculum; they are fighting a void of public services, crumbling infrastructure, and entrenched poverty. The fact that this gap exploded from 3.9 points in 2019 to 9.6 points in 2026 proves that structural violence is accelerating.

Most repulsive are the narratives of 'laser focus' improvement teams or 'devolution' plans. These scripts attempt to reduce systemic collapse to a 'management issue' or 'local conditions.' When schools are asked to 'compensate' for poverty, healthcare failure, and transport collapse, the school becomes a temporary shelter for the complicity between the state and capital. Trying to patch a rotten system with a bit of extra funding is a form of cultural violence—it tricks the victims into believing that if they just work harder, or if the government provides a few more grants, they can climb a wall built by the sheer accident of their birthplace.

Talent is spread evenly, but opportunity is monopolized. This monopoly is legitimized through grade sheets and eventually converted into pricing power in the labor market. The winners celebrate in London, while the losers in the North are told this is merely a 'regional disparity.'
