法袍下的拳脚Fists Under the Robes

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
法庭的合法性之争，最后靠谁先挤进大门定胜负
When the court's legitimacy is settled by who forces the door first, the law has already lost.

波兰宪法法院周五晚上演的不是法律剧，是摔跤。政府任命的一批法官投票停掉了法院院长和副院长，被停的一方称这是政变，PiS 的卡钦斯基说这是“袭击”，总统纳夫罗茨基把飞往塞内加尔的飞机掉头飞回华沙，凌晨一点穿过警察封锁线，跟安保和法官扭打进了大楼，四点还待在里面。谁对谁错先放一边——一个国家的最高法院，其权威最终靠的是谁身体先到、谁力气大，这已经把答案说出来了。

这场戏的台词是“法治”，道具是法袍，实际在争夺的是解释权。图斯克说“恢复法治是我们的职责”，西科尔斯基说法院在前政府手里被政治化了，所以要让它重新运转。听起来都对。但同一批人做的事是：用一群政府任命的法官，去停掉另一群被争议的法官。法律专家马特查克，一个公开批评 PiS 的人，说停职的法律依据“不存在”。连反对派都承认依据不存在，那这就是用程序的外壳装一个程序之外的动作。元暴力在这里不穿制服，穿西装，说的是“这是唯一的办法”。

再看另一头。PiS 八年执政期间往法院塞人，跟欧盟打了多年法治官司，法院屡屡判波兰法优先于欧盟法。现在他们喊“宪法秩序被侵犯”，喊得情真意切。可 2015 年那场塞人的操作，合法性同样可疑。两边都在用同一套逻辑：我赢的时候规则是规则，我输的时候规则是暴力。这不是法治，这是两党轮流把法院当战利品。

真正被消耗的是法院本身。一个法院的权威不来自警察多少，不来自总统能不能挤进门，来自人们相信它的判决有效。这场扭打之后，无论哪边赢，输的是那点残余的信任。明年议会选举，选民要选的不是谁更懂法，是谁更能把对方从大楼里赶出去。

还有一层没人提：法院门口站了几百人，三个因为试图冲进去被捕，警察列队，总统扭打。这些人里，谁在为堕胎权、为少数群体权利、为普通人的官司付代价？报道没写。报道写的是政客和法官的名字。法院停摆的这些年，那些等判决的人，那些被卡在程序里的案子，不在任何一方的声明里。

凌晨五点，纳夫罗茨基的声明说“一个宪法机关被武力占领”。他说对了一半。被占领的不只是机关，是“法律能解决问题”这个信念本身。而占领它的，不是哪一队警察，是两党共谋的这十年。

What happened at Poland's constitutional court on Friday night was not a legal drama. It was a wrestling match. A group of government-appointed judges voted to suspend the court's president and his deputy. The suspended side called it a coup. Kaczyński called it an "assault." President Nawrocki turned his plane around mid-flight to Senegal, returned to Warsaw, pushed through a police cordon shortly before 1am, tussled with security and other judges to force his way inside, and was still there at 4am. Set aside who is right. A supreme court whose authority is settled by who arrives first and pushes hardest has already told you the answer.

The script is "rule of law." The costume is a robe. What is actually being fought over is the power to define. Tusk says "restoring the rule of law is our duty." Sikorski says the tribunal was politicised under the previous government and must be made to function again. It sounds right. But the same people used one set of government-appointed judges to suspend another set of contested judges. Marcin Matczak, a prominent critic of PiS, said the legal basis for the suspension was "non-existent." When even the opposition's own legal critics say the basis does not exist, this is a procedure-shaped shell around an extra-procedural move. Meta-violence here does not wear a uniform. It wears a suit and says "there is no other way."

Now the other side. PiS spent eight years stacking the bench and fighting the EU over rule of law. The court repeatedly ruled Polish law over EU law. Now they shout "violation of Poland's constitutional order" with full conviction. But the 2015 appointments were legally questionable too. Both sides use the same logic: when I win, the rules are rules; when I lose, the rules are violence. This is not rule of law. This is two parties taking turns treating the court as spoils.

What is being consumed is the court itself. A court's authority comes not from police numbers, not from whether a president can squeeze through a door, but from people believing its rulings hold. After this scuffle, whoever wins, what loses is the remaining trust.

One layer goes unmentioned. Hundreds gathered outside. Three were arrested trying to force entry. Police lined up. The president tussled. Among all these people, who is paying the price for abortion rights, minority rights, ordinary lawsuits? The report does not say. It names politicians and judges. The people waiting on rulings during the court's years of paralysis, the cases stuck in procedure, appear in no one's statement.

At 5am, Nawrocki's statement said "a constitutional body was seized by force." He is half right. What was seized is not only an institution but the belief that law can solve things. And what seized it was not a police unit. It was a decade of collusion between two parties.
