爱丽丝被锁在旧主机里Alice Is Locked Inside Old Consoles

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
文化产品的死活，由版权方一句话决定
Whether a cultural work lives or dies is decided by whoever holds the rights.

一个游戏从货架上消失，不是因为它不好，是因为持有它的人算过账，觉得让它消失更划算。

Polygon 这篇写的是 American McGee 的两部爱丽丝游戏。2000 年的初代拿到 Metacritic 85 分，2011 年的《Madness Returns》6 月 14 日发售，评论界嫌它战斗烦人、画面过时，玩家照买。然后呢？2014 年之后没有然后了。McGee 在 2023 年的一段视频里说，发行方 Electronic Arts 拒绝出资、也拒绝授权做第三部《Alice: Asylum》。他一度离开开发，现在回来做 Plushie Dreadfuls 的 demo。

这里没有施暴者，没有人挨打，没有一条法律写着爱丽丝不许上现代主机。但初代就是买不到了——除非你多年前买过数字版，或者今天愿意折腾一圈。结构暴力最典型的形态：不是有人砸了你的东西，是资源的分配方式让某样东西自然地从你手里滑走，而且滑得悄无声息，没人需要为此负责。

EA 的算盘不难猜。2010 年蒂姆·波顿那部真人电影之后，这个 IP 的公众胃口就淡了，再投钱做重制，回报率不够漂亮。拒绝授权第三部，同时把旧作锁在一个世代的硬件里，等于把一整套表达按在抽屉最底层——不销毁，也不给你。

但这里还有一层更值得说的。爱丽丝这个角色，从卡罗尔那本童话开始，就是一个不听话的女孩：她质疑、她顶嘴、她砍掉红心皇后的头。McGee 的版本把她做成从精神病院回来的幸存者，Wonderland 腐烂了，只有她能处理。Polygon 写她“需要一种她不知道自己拥有的坚毅”。这样一个女主，黑暗、暴躁、不靠男人救，在 2000 年和 2011 年都属于稀缺品。现在它被版权和账本一起按住，等于告诉后来的人：这种故事可以存在，但不必流传。

Polygon 的结尾是“希望有人看到让新一代玩到最血腥的爱丽丝的智慧”。希望不是机制。真正决定这件事的，是 EA 愿不愿意把授权放出来，或者有没有人愿意为一次重制掏钱。在那之前，爱丽丝就待在旧主机里，做一个被怀念但不被交到手上的角色。

顺带一提，这篇文章挂在一期叫“Happily Never After”的成人童话专题下，由《The Wolf Among Us》重制版赞助，10 月 29 日发售。一边哀悼旧作买不到，一边给另一部重制版打广告——文化工业对“经典”的态度，从来是看谁能把它重新上架卖钱。

A game disappears from the shelf not because it's bad, but because the people holding it did the math and found disappearing more profitable.

Polygon is writing about American McGee's two Alice games. The 2000 original scored 85 on Metacritic; Alice: Madness Returns launched June 14, 2011, and critics found the combat irritating and the visuals outdated while players kept buying. Then nothing. After 2014, nothing. In a 2023 video McGee said publishing partner Electronic Arts refused to fund or license a third game, Alice: Asylum. He stepped away for a while; now he's back working on a demo for Plushie Dreadfuls.

No one is hitting anyone. No law says Alice can't run on modern hardware. But the first game is simply gone—unless you bought it digitally years ago, or you're ready to jump through hoops today. This is structural violence in its purest form: nobody smashes your things, the allocation of resources just lets a thing slide out of your hands, quietly, with no one accountable.

EA's arithmetic isn't hard to guess. After Tim Burton's 2010 film, public appetite for the property faded, and a remaster wouldn't return enough. Refusing the third game while keeping the old ones locked to a console generation is putting a whole body of expression in the bottom drawer—not destroyed, just not handed to you.

There's another layer worth naming. Alice, from Carroll onward, is a girl who talks back: she questions, she argues, she cuts off the Red Queen's head. McGee's version makes her a survivor back from the asylum, with Wonderland rotting and only she can fix it. Polygon writes that she needs "fortitude she never knew she had." A heroine like that—dark, angry, not rescued by a man—was scarce in 2000 and scarce in 2011. Now copyright and the ledger hold it down together, which tells everyone downstream: this kind of story may exist, but it needn't circulate.

Polygon ends hoping someone sees the wisdom in giving a new generation access to Alice at her bloodiest. Hope is not a mechanism. What decides this is whether EA releases the license, or whether someone pays for a remaster. Until then Alice sits in old hardware, a character remembered but never handed over.

One more thing: the piece runs in a special issue called "Happily Never After," sponsored by The Wolf Among Us Remastered, out October 29. Mourning a lost game while advertising another remaster—that's the culture industry's stance on "classics" in one frame: whoever can put it back on sale gets to call it timeless.
