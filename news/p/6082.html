军费是预算，教育是施舍Military Budget, Education Charity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
钱花在坦克上不叫浪费，花在学生身上才叫浪费。
Billions for tanks is policy; money for students is a scandal.

德国学生上街，三十四个城市、近十八万人报名，导火索不是抽象的政治，是一笔笔算得出来的账：教育经费在砍，生活费在涨，军费在涨。

这不是三条并列的诉求，是一条因果链。加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual，差额就是暴力。这个差额落在哪一层？结构层。大学在关研究所，老师在失业，教学楼在塌，同一个政府往军队里投几十亿欧元。学生没有被打，但他们的可能性被预算表削掉了一块，而削掉这块的理由被写成了“国家需要”。

更值得看的是叙事怎么先动手。抗议还没开始，政治人物已经在谈“抗议可能变得暴力”。这就是文化暴力最标准的动作：先把提出问题的人重新定义成问题本身。汉诺威来的那个二十三岁政治学学生说得很清楚，她打工是为了付学费，可舆论关心的不是她为什么打工，是她会不会砸东西。

科隆发生了推搡，柏林到下午还算平静。但报道里真正刺人的一句来自那位二十四岁的柏林学生：政府一边砍掉影响我们生活质量的开支，一边花几十亿在军费和加沙。这句话在德国公共讨论里是要被划红线的，而说这句话的人，正是被要求“被倾听”的那一群。

康斯坦茨那位研究员说，学生关于教育基础设施的担忧是“合理的”，“重要的是他们被倾听”。倾听。这个词本身就是权力关系的自白——一方说话，一方决定是否倾听。真正对等的关系里，不需要有人出来保证对方会被听见。

所以这场抗议的核心不是学生要钱，是谁有权决定钱流向哪儿，以及谁有权决定哪些不满算“合理”、哪些算“暴力倾向”。前者是结构，后者是解释权。近十八万人的签名，换来的是一句“应该被倾听”。

报道没写的是：这六个诉求里，有哪一个会被真正回应。

German students are on the streets in 34 cities, nearly 180,000 signed up, and the trigger isn't abstract politics. It's arithmetic: education funding is being cut, living costs are rising, military spending is rising.

These aren't three parallel demands. They're one causal chain. Galtung's formula is Violence = Potential − Actual, and the gap is the violence. Where does the gap land here? The structural layer. Universities are struggling, institutes closing, teachers losing jobs, buildings falling apart, while the same government pours billions into the military. Nobody is hitting these students. Their possibilities are being shaved off by a budget line, and the shaving is justified as "the nation needs it."

What deserves more attention is how the narrative moves first. Before the rally even starts, political figures are already talking about protests turning violent. That's the standard cultural-violence maneuver: redefine the people raising the problem as the problem. The 23-year-old political science student from Hannover says plainly that she took a part-time job to fund her studies. The public conversation isn't about why she needs one. It's about whether she'll break something.

There were scuffles in Cologne; Berlin stayed calm into the afternoon. But the sharpest line in the report comes from the 24-year-old Berlin student: the government cuts what affects their quality of life while spending billions on the military and on funding genocide in Gaza. That sentence is a red line in German public debate, and it comes from exactly the people who are supposed to be "listened to."

The researcher in Konstanz says students' concerns about educational infrastructure are legitimate and that "it's important that they are listened to." Listened to. The phrase is a confession of the power relation: one side speaks, the other decides whether to listen. Equals don't need someone to guarantee they'll be heard.

So the core of this protest isn't students wanting money. It's who gets to decide where money flows, and who gets to decide which grievances count as "legitimate" and which as a "propensity for violence." The first is structure. The second is the power to interpret. Nearly 180,000 signatures bought them one sentence: they should be listened to.

What the report doesn't say is which of the six demands will actually get an answer.
