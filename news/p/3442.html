Spritz 战争：被 Instagram 标价的“松弛感”骗局The Spritz War: A Scam of Instagram-Priced 'Effortlessness'

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
审美定价权是商业武器，所谓的“趋势”不过是新一轮的认知收割。
Aesthetic pricing is a weapon; what we call 'trends' are merely new rounds of cognitive harvesting.

这篇报道在讨论 Hugo 还是 Aperol 更有统治力，但本质上是在讨论谁能更高效地垄断 Gen Z 的“审美入口”。所谓的 Hugo spritz 爆火，并不是什么口味的自然演进，而是一场精准的 weaponized expression。从 Aperol 的亮橙色到 Sarti 的热粉色，这些酒液在杯子里的颜色，其功能不是为了味觉，而是为了在 Instagram 的滤镜下完成一次“阶级筛选”和“身份确证”。

注意文中提到的“daycap”趋势——年轻人把喝酒时间提前到下午。这被包装成一种轻盈、健康的社交生活方式，但实际上是商业资本在重新定义“松弛感”。当某种颜色、某种配方被定义为“今年夏天的必备”时，它就成了一个临时的身份符号。你喝的不是接骨木花或橙皮，而是在购买一张进入“时髦人群”阵营的入场券。这种通过定义“什么是酷的”来制造稀缺感并获取定价权的机制，是典型的商业 scam。

Campari 集团的总经理在文中表现得像个宽容的君主，称 Hugo 为“大卫”，而自己是“歌利亚”。这种叙事掩盖了资本共谋的真相：无论哪个品牌获胜，最终受益的都是掌握了认知入口的巨头。他们通过密集书写“夏季趋势”，让消费者在潜意识中完成自我规训——如果你这个夏天没在社交媒体上发一张 Spritz 的照片，你就失去了某种定义为“现代”的社交资格。

最讽刺的是，这种被精心制造的“自由选择”，其实是主体性的死亡。消费者以为自己在探索新口味，实际上只是在执行资本预设的剧本。所谓的“Spritz 运动”，不过是将消费主义包装成生活美学的一次成功演习。

This report debates whether the Hugo or Aperol holds the crown, but the real battle is over who can more efficiently monopolize the 'aesthetic entrance' of Gen Z. The explosion of the Hugo spritz is not a natural evolution of taste, but a piece of weaponized expression. From Aperol's vibrant orange to Sarti's hot pink, the color of the liquid is not for the palate—it is for the Instagram filter, serving as a tool for class screening and identity establishment.

Note the rise of the 'daycap'—shifting drinking to the late afternoon. This is packaged as a light, healthy lifestyle, but it is actually corporate capital redefining 'effortlessness.' When a specific color or recipe is branded as the 'must-have of the summer,' it becomes a temporary identity symbol. You aren't drinking elderflower or orange peel; you are buying a ticket into the 'trendy' camp. This mechanism of manufacturing scarcity by defining 'what is cool' to seize pricing power is a classic commercial scam.

The Managing Director of Campari Group plays the role of a benevolent monarch, casting the Hugo as 'David' to his 'Goliath.' This narrative masks the complicity of capital: regardless of which brand wins, the victors are the giants who control the cognitive entrance. By densely scripting 'summer trends,' they induce a subconscious self-discipline in consumers—if you haven't posted a Spritz photo this summer, you've lost your social qualification to be 'modern.'

Most ironically, this 'freedom of choice' is actually the death of subjectivity. Consumers believe they are exploring new tastes, while they are merely executing a script pre-written by capital. The so-called 'spritz movement' is nothing more than a successful exercise in packaging consumerism as lifestyle aesthetics.
