十年计划，一年一本的幻觉A Ten-Year Plan Dressed Up as Speed

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要奇幻作家布兰登·桑德森在万圣节直播中透露，《飓光志》系列后五部已有大致出版计划：可能连续推出两到三部，每部间隔两年，最后两部稍后跟进。该系列共十部，2010 年以《王者之路》开篇，前五部构成独立篇章，五、六部之间将有大跨度时间跳跃。他同时还在写《迷雾之子》第三纪元及苹果 TV+ 的多部改编作品。
The newsFantasy author Brandon Sanderson said on a Halloween stream that he has a loose schedule for the last five Stormlight Archive books: possibly two or three released back to back, two years apart, with the final two following a little later. The ten-book series began with The Way of Kings in 2010, and a big time skip will separate books five and six. He is also writing the third Mistborn era and multiple Apple TV+ adaptations.
产能叙事是出版业最成功的武器化包装
Productivity narrative is publishing's most successful weaponized packaging.

桑德森在万圣节直播里穿着史莱克装，说后五部《飓光志》可能两年一本、连着出两三本。直播间里欢呼，新闻标题写「更快了」。但把时间轴摊开：2031 年开写，最快两年一本，五本出来也是 2040 年前后。这不是加速，是把「十年后你会看到结局」包装成「我马上要动笔了」。

这里动的是文化层。出版业最擅长的武器化入口，不是色情、不是宗教，是产能叙事——把作者的写作速度、多项目并行、永不枯竭的产出，做成一种人格品牌。桑德森是这个品牌最完美的载体：他同时写《迷雾之子》第三纪元，2028、2029、2030 排满，还在给苹果 TV+ 写《迷雾之子》电影剧本，还拿下了最终剪辑权。读者看到的是「他在为我们拼命」，实际发生的是：注意力被产能本身吸走，作品什么时候出来反而成了次要问题。

更值得拆的是「时间跳跃」这个设定。第五部和第六部之间有大跨度跳跃，这在叙事上是聪明的——它让作者可以合理地换一批角色、换一套冲突，读者不会追问「上一部的伏笔呢」。但这也是一个共谋结构：作者需要时间，读者需要连续性，两边都同意用一个「跳跃」把十年的空档合法化。谁付代价？付代价的是那些从 2010 年《王者之路》开始追、到 2040 年才能看到结局的读者。他们的阅读生命被切成了两段，中间隔着一次官方认证的「时间跳跃」。

桑德森不是骗子，他确实在写，而且写得比大多数同行多。但「更快」这个词在新闻里出现的时候，从来不是在描述产能，是在描述期待管理。真正的最优解表达，是作者说「我需要多久就多久，你们别催」，读者说「好，我等」。现在双方都在演一场「我们都很努力」的戏，戏很好看，只是结局在十年后。

那条直播里他没说的是：后五部开写之前，他还要先写完《迷雾之子》的三本。所以「两年一本」的时钟，现在还没开始走。

Sanderson put on a Shrek costume for a Halloween stream and said the last five Stormlight books might come two years apart, two or three back to back. The chat cheered. The headline said faster. Lay out the timeline: start in 2031, two years per book, and five books land around 2040. That isn't acceleration. It's packaging "you'll see the ending in a decade" as "I'm about to start writing."

The cultural layer is what's moving here. Publishing's favorite weaponized entry point isn't porn or religion — it's the productivity narrative: the author's writing speed, the parallel projects, the inexhaustible output, turned into a personality brand. Sanderson is the perfect vessel. He's writing the third Mistborn era, with 2028, 2029, 2030 already booked, scripting the Mistborn film for Apple TV+ with final approval on the adaptations. Readers see a man killing himself for them. What's actually happening is that attention gets absorbed by the output itself, and when the books arrive becomes secondary.

The time skip is the sharper tell. A big gap between books five and six is narratively clever — it lets the author swap in new characters and new conflicts without readers asking where the old threads went. But it's also a co-conspirator structure: the author needs time, the readers need continuity, and both agree to legalize a decade-long gap with an officially sanctioned jump. Who pays? The readers who started with The Way of Kings in 2010 and will see the ending in 2040. Their reading lives get cut in two, with an authorized time skip in between.

Sanderson isn't a fraud. He writes, and he writes more than most. But when "faster" shows up in a headline, it's never describing capacity — it's describing expectation management. The real optimal expression is the author saying "it takes as long as it takes, stop rushing me" and the readers saying "fine, we'll wait." Instead both sides perform a play about how hard everyone is working. It's a good play. The ending is just ten years out.

What he didn't say on that stream: before the second half begins, he has to finish three more Mistborn books. The two-year clock hasn't started ticking.
