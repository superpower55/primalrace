观众疲劳是收视下滑的替罪羊Viewer Fatigue Is the Alibi for a Ratings Slide

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要福克斯CEO拉克伦·默多克在季度财报电话会上回应福克斯新闻收视下滑，称原因是观众「疲劳」，去年就职典礼、查理·柯克事件、DOGE、洪水等新闻周期太密集，观众只是整体看电视少了，并未转投其他频道。同期CNN和MS NOW在中期选举周期中显著上升。福克斯新闻收视仍高于两家之和，并从中期选举获得约5000万美元全国广告收入、2.5亿美元地方台收入和5000万美元Tubi收入。
The newsFox CEO Lachlan Murdoch told the company's earnings call that Fox News' ratings decline reflects "viewer fatigue" after an intense news cycle last year, and that these viewers are watching less television overall rather than switching channels. CNN and MS NOW are up significantly in the midterm cycle. Fox News still beats the two combined and booked about $50 million in national midterm ad revenue, $250 million at local stations and $50 million at Tubi.
把流失说成疲劳，是给解释权打的一针麻药
Calling lost viewers tired is a sedative for the people who own the narrative.

默多克把收视下滑解释成「观众疲劳」，这是把解释权攥在自己手里：不是我们出了问题，是观众累了。

「疲劳」这个词很妙。它把责任从内容生产者转移到观众身上，观众成了需要被体谅的病人，而福克斯是那个说「我们见过这套路，知道怎么走过去」的医生。可他没有说，观众到底对什么疲劳。是新闻太多，还是新闻太假；是事件太密集，还是叙事太单一。这两个问题他一个都不碰，因为一碰，财报电话会就变成了内容审查会。

更值得看的是他拿来对比的那串事件：就职典礼、查理·柯克事件、DOGE、洪水。这是一份清单，不是一份解释。它列的是「我们报过什么」，而不是「我们怎么报的」。观众疲劳的从来不是事件本身，而是事件被反复咀嚼、包装、加热到失去味道的过程。

但福克斯并不慌。收视下滑的同时，中期选举给福克斯带来了约5000万美元的全国广告收入、2.5亿美元的地方台收入和5000万美元的Tubi收入。也就是说，观众看不看，和钱进不进，是两条线。收视是注意力，广告是定价权，后者才是真正的战场。默多克说「他们没转台，只是少看了电视」，这句话的潜台词是：少看没关系，钱照收。

所以「观众疲劳」不是诊断，是公关。它把结构性的问题——内容同质化、叙事武器化、观众被当成投票机器——翻译成一句可以放在财报会上的体面话。真正疲劳的不是观众，是这套叙事本身。它已经疲劳到需要靠「疲劳」这个词来给自己续命了。

Murdoch calls the ratings decline "viewer fatigue." That is not a diagnosis. It is a way of keeping the explanation in-house: the problem is not us, it is that the audience is tired.

The word is doing a lot of work. It moves responsibility from the people who make the news to the people who watch it. The viewer becomes a patient to be pitied, and Fox becomes the doctor saying, "We have seen this playbook, and we know how to move through it." What he never says is what, exactly, the viewers are tired of. Too much news, or news that is too fake? Too many events, or a narrative that only has one setting? He touches neither, because touching either turns an earnings call into a content review.

Look at the list he offers as comparison: the inauguration, the Charlie Kirk tragedy, DOGE, flooding. That is an inventory, not an explanation. It lists what they covered, not how they covered it. Viewers do not fatigue on events. They fatigue on events being chewed, packaged and reheated until the flavor is gone.

And Fox is not panicking. Even as ratings fall, the midterms brought roughly $50 million in national ad revenue, $250 million at local stations and $50 million at Tubi. Ratings are attention; advertising is pricing power. Murdoch's line — they are not switching channels, they are just watching slightly less television overall — translates cleanly: less watching is fine, the money still arrives.

So "viewer fatigue" is not a finding. It is PR. It takes a structural problem — homogenized content, weaponized narrative, an audience treated as a voting bloc — and turns it into a sentence polite enough for an earnings call. The ones who are actually fatigued are not the viewers. It is the narrative itself, and it now needs the word "fatigue" to keep itself alive.
