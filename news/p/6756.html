数据中心先到，选票后到The Data Center Arrives Before the Ballot

其他 结构层 · 文化层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要佛罗里达州多个县居民反对在住宅附近建数据中心，西特鲁斯县居民桑迪·斯洛维组织反对团体，县官员否决了开发商800英亩的项目并通过一年禁令。数据中心议题正撕裂共和党初选选民，部分终身共和党人表示不会投票给接受开发商捐款的州长参选人拜伦·唐纳兹。
The newsResidents across several Florida counties are organizing against data centers built near their homes. In Citrus County, Sandie Slowey helped form an opposition group; officials rejected a developer's 800-acre project and approved a one-year moratorium. The issue is now splitting Republican primary voters, some of whom say they won't back Byron Donalds, who has taken millions from data center developers.
居民赢了禁令，但土地还在开发商手里。
They won a moratorium. The developer still owns the land.

斯洛维赢了一场。县官员否决了800英亩的数据中心，通过一年禁令，反对团体把两个备用会议室都坐满了。这是structural层的一次真实收缩：一个具体的入口被堵上了，不是许诺，是投票结果。

但报道里最诚实的一句话是她自己说的：「我们赢了这场战斗，但还没有赢下战争。」Deltona还持有她家旁边那块地，禁令一年后到期，她和指导委员会现在在帮县委员会起草条例——也就是说，防守方得自己动手写规则，因为规则本来不站在她这边。

这件事真正值得看的不是数据中心，是共谋者理论在佛罗里达的现场演示。唐纳兹公开支持数据中心，同时从开发商那里收了几百万竞选捐款。这不是什么隐蔽的交易，是明码标价的利益站队：开发商出钱，政客出政策，代价由住在活动房屋里、晚上开着窗睡觉的退休护士承担。噪音、水耗、飓风季里的核反应堆，这些成本没有出现在任何一份竞选财务报告里。

有意思的是共谋链条在这里断了一环。断的地方不是道德觉醒，是地理——数据中心建到了共和党选民自己的后院。斯洛维是注册共和党人，她要投给柯林斯，如果唐纳兹赢了初选，她十一月投给民主党人乔利。圣维森特是独立选民，她说「你属于哪个党，在这个问题上不重要」。这不是意识形态转向，这是暴力三角里最朴素的一条：当结构暴力开始直接作用在你家窗户上的时候，党派标签的边际收益突然变成负数。

麦克马纳斯点出了民主党真正的难题：这些保守的、虔信的乡村选民也许不会投给民主党，但他们会待在家里。待在家里也是投票，只是投给了缺席。

所以这不是一篇关于数据中心能耗或者AI基础设施的报道，这是一篇关于定价权的报道——谁有权决定一块土地值多少钱、值谁的安宁、值多少张选票。开发商已经给这块地定过价了，斯洛维们正在重新定价，用的工具是县委员会、Facebook群组和初选选票。

只是那个一年期限一直在走。条例还没通过，Deltona的地契还在，下一届州长还没选出来。她守住的是一条线，不是一块地。

Slowey won one. County officials rejected the 800-acre data center and approved a one-year moratorium; the opposition packed two overflow rooms. That's a real contraction at the structural layer—a specific entrance closed by an actual vote, not a promise.

The most honest line in the piece is hers: 「We won the battle, but we haven't won the war.」 Deltona still owns the land next to her home. The moratorium expires in a year. She and the steering committee are now drafting ordinances themselves—which means the defense has to write the rules, because the rules were never on her side.

The real story here isn't data centers. It's the co-conspirator theory doing a live demo in Florida. Donalds backs the industry and has taken millions from data center developers. That's not a hidden transaction; it's a priced alliance. Developers pay, politicians deliver policy, and the cost lands on a retired nurse in a mobile home who sleeps with her windows open. Noise, water use, a nuclear reactor in hurricane country—none of that shows up in a campaign finance report.

What's interesting is where the chain breaks. Not at a moral awakening—at geography. The data centers arrived in Republican backyards. Slowey is a registered Republican voting for Collins, and for Democrat Jolly in November if Donalds wins the primary. San Vicente, an independent, says party doesn't matter on this issue. This isn't an ideological turn. It's the Violence Triangle at its most literal: once structural violence starts humming outside your window, the party label's marginal value goes negative.

MacManus names the Democrats' actual problem: these conservative, heavily Christian rural voters may not vote Democratic—but they'll stay home. Staying home is still a vote. It's a vote for absence.

So this isn't a story about energy consumption or AI infrastructure. It's a story about pricing power—who gets to decide what a piece of land is worth, whose quiet is worth what, and how many votes that quiet is worth. The developer already priced the land. Slowey and her neighbors are repricing it with county commissions, Facebook groups, and primary ballots.

Except the one-year clock is running. The ordinances aren't passed. Deltona still holds the deed. The next governor hasn't been elected. She held a line, not a piece of land.
