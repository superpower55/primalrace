被抽血的“活化石”与生物权的定价权The 'Living Fossil' and the Pricing of Biological Rights

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
生存能力不等于议价能力，被需要的身体往往是被剥削的客体。
Survival capacity is not bargaining power; a body that is 'needed' is often a body objectified.

一个在地球上活了 4.45 亿年的物种，在面对现代人类时，其生存逻辑被简化成了一种“生物资源”的供应。鲎（Horseshoe Crab）的蓝血被生物医药产业垄断为疫苗检测的必需品，这在叙事上被包装成一种“跨越时代的联系”，但本质上是一场典型的结构性暴力：人类定义了它的价值，并以此为由将其身体客体化。

这种逻辑与原初种族的殖民逻辑高度同构。生物医药产业并不关心鲎作为生命主体的存在，只关心它血液中那个能检测毒素的化学机制。当一个生命的价值被锚定在它能为权力中心（人类医疗体系）提供多少便利时，它就从一个独立演化的物种变成了工厂里的“原材料”。

更讽刺的是，新闻在赞美它的“坚韧” (tenacity) 和在混凝土海岸线中寻找生存空间的能耐。这种赞美其实是一种掩盖。当一个物种必须在被人类混凝土封死的世界里卑微地寻找缝隙生存，且身体被定期抽血以服务于人类的健康时，这种“坚韧”不是进化优势，而是被压迫者的生存本能。我们将这种无奈的苟且称为“生命力”，这本身就是一种文化层面的 weaponized 叙事。

我们要问的不是它是否会灭绝，而是谁在定义它的生存状态。如果一个物种的“价值”仅仅在于它能被多少次抽血，那么这种价值其实是它的诅咒。

A species that has existed for 445 million years is now reduced to a 'biological resource' in the eyes of modern humanity. The blue blood of the horseshoe crab is monopolized by the biomedical industry for vaccine testing. This is framed as an 'incredible link in time,' but in reality, it is a textbook case of structural violence: humans define its value and use that definition to objectify its body.

This logic is isomorphic to the colonization of the Primal Race. The biomedical industry does not care about the horseshoe crab as a sentient subject; it only cares about the chemical mechanism in its blood. When a life's value is anchored to the convenience it provides to the center of power (the human medical system), it ceases to be an evolving species and becomes 'raw material' in a factory.

Even more ironic is the narrative praising its 'tenacity' and its ability to eke out a living among concreted coastlines. This praise is a mask. When a species must humbly search for cracks in a world sealed by human concrete, while its body is routinely drained to serve human health, this 'tenacity' is not an evolutionary advantage—it is the survival instinct of the oppressed. Calling this desperate struggle 'vitality' is a form of weaponized narrative at the cultural layer.

The question is not whether it will go extinct, but who defines its state of existence. If a species' 'value' lies solely in how many times it can be bled, then that value is, in fact, its curse.
