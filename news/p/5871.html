一封信在伦敦转了一圈A Letter That Circled London

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
伦敦警察厅把一桩涉嫌性侵的指控当成了王室家务事
The Met treated an abuse allegation as a family matter.

伦敦警察厅收到一封举报信，信里写着有人涉嫌在境外严重犯罪。他们没有调查，理由是案子发生在国外。他们也没有把信转给任何相关国家的执法机构。他们做了一件事：把信转给了苏塞克斯公爵夫妇的办公室。七年之后，信里的细节才开始寄往美国、菲律宾和泰国。

这不是执法疏漏，这是一次分类。同一封信，如果收信人不是公爵夫人的父亲，它会被归入刑事线索；因为收信人是公爵夫人的父亲，它被归入家庭纠纷。分类决定了它去哪个抽屉，而抽屉决定了它七年里没有动过。谁做的这个分类，IOPC 现在要查——查的是谁做的决定，以及程序有没有被遵守。注意这个措辞：程序。

报道里有一处细节比整件事更值得看：2019 年那封信被送到 Fixated Threat Assessment Centre，一个专门评估针对公众人物的骚扰和威胁的机构。指挥官 Adam Slonecki 说，写信人自称是出于对公爵夫人安全的担忧，但信里其实没有具体的安全信息，只有若干境外犯罪的指控。也就是说，这封信进的是一个保护要人的系统，而不是一个追诉犯罪的系统。系统按它自己的逻辑运转：它关心的是这位要人会不会被骚扰，不关心信里写的受害者是谁。

这就是元暴力在制度里的样子。它不是某个警察心怀恶意，而是一整套优先级：名人的安全排在儿童之上，王室的体面排在证据之上。信被转给公爵夫妇的办公室“供其知悉”——这一句话里，收信人变成了当事人，举报变成了家务，执法变成了通报。共谋不需要密谋，只需要每个人都在自己的岗位上做那个“合理”的决定。

现在菲律宾方面在查 Markle 涉嫌持有的数字图像，内政部长说所有迹象都指向一个“最恶劣类型的可能的性犯罪者”。Markle 本人否认，他承认付钱买性，否认持有非法材料。这些指控还没有被证实，这是事实；但七年的时间已经过去了，这也是事实。取证依赖的是硬盘、设备、还能不能找到的东西，而这些东西不会等人。

IOPC 的介入是真的，它动用了正式权力，查的是决策链和程序合规。但报道里也写了它不查什么：不查警方把信转给公爵夫人办公室是否违反数据规则。一个监督机构划出调查范围，本身就是一次分类。它查“为什么没转给外国警察”，不查“为什么转给了当事人家里”。后者才是这封信走错的那一步。

七年前那封信里如果真有受害者的名字，这些名字从来没有出现在任何一份警方通报里。它们出现在一封信里，那封信被评估、被转交、被放进一个办公室，然后安静了七年。伦敦警察厅的尴尬程度，按报道的说法，取决于菲律宾的调查结果。可那封信的尴尬，从 2019 年就开始了。

The Met received a letter alleging serious crimes abroad. It did not investigate, on the grounds that the alleged offences happened overseas. It did not pass the letter to law enforcement in any of the countries concerned. It did one thing: it forwarded the letter to the office of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex. Seven years later, the details are only now being sent to the US, the Philippines and Thailand.

This is not an oversight. It is a classification. The same letter, had the sender not been the Duchess's father, would have been filed as a criminal lead. Because the sender was the Duchess's father, it was filed as a family matter. The filing decided which drawer it went into, and the drawer decided that it would not move for seven years. The IOPC now wants to know who made that call, and whether procedure was followed. Note the word: procedure.

One detail matters more than the rest. In 2019 the letter went to the Fixated Threat Assessment Centre, a unit built to assess harassment and threats against public figures. Commander Adam Slonecki said the author claimed to be raising concerns about the Duchess's security, that the letter contained no information specifically related to security, and that it did contain claims of offending outside the UK. So the letter entered a system for protecting VIPs, not a system for pursuing crimes. That system ran on its own logic: it cared whether the VIP would be harassed. It did not care whose names were in the letter.

This is what meta-violence looks like inside an institution. Not a malicious officer, but a set of priorities: a celebrity's safety above a child's, a royal family's composure above evidence. The letter was passed to the Sussexes' office "for their awareness." In that phrase, the recipient becomes the party, a report becomes a domestic matter, and law enforcement becomes a notification. Conspiracy needs no conspiracy. It needs everyone making the reasonable call from their own desk.

Authorities in the Philippines are now examining digital images alleged to belong to Markle, and the interior minister says all signs point to a "possible sex offender of the grossest kind." Markle denies the claims. He has admitted paying for sex and denies possessing illegal material. None of this is proven. That is a fact. So is the seven years. Evidence lives on drives and devices, and drives and devices do not wait.

The IOPC's involvement is real: it has used formal powers and will examine the decision chain and procedural compliance. But the report also says what it will not examine: whether sending the letter to the Duchess's office breached data rules. A watchdog drawing the boundaries of its own inquiry is itself a classification. It asks why the letter was not sent to foreign police. It does not ask why it was sent to the family. The second question is where the letter actually went wrong.

If there were victims' names in that letter seven years ago, those names have never appeared in a police statement. They appeared in a letter, which was assessed, forwarded, placed in an office, and went quiet for seven years. The Met's embarrassment, per the report, depends on what the Philippines finds. The letter's embarrassment began in 2019.
