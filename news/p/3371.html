被物化为“猫”的身体与被阉割的叙事Bodies Objectified as 'Cats' and the Castrated Narrative

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
将人类身体通过 costume 降格为宠物，是典型的文化暴力掩体。
Reducing human bodies to pets via costumes is a classic cover for cultural violence.

这是一篇典型的、在元暴力（meta violence）中打转的剧评。评论者在讨论《Cats》时，关注点全部被锁定在“优雅”、“芭蕾感”以及那些“闪闪发光的紧身衣”（flamboyant leotard and catsuit）上。在这种叙事里，演员的身体不再是主体，而是一件被精心包装的商品，一种被物化为“猫”的视觉景观。当评论者赞美 Rum Tum Tugger 那种“像年轻时的 Mick Jagger 一样顶胯的 swagger”时，他实际上是在消费一种被高度性化且被驯化的男性特质——这种特质被包装成“猫的个性”，从而消解了真实的人类主体性。

最讽刺的是，这部剧的内核是一个关于“重生”的仪式，但这种重生是建立在由一个男性（Andrew Lloyd Webber）定义且垄断的解释权之上的。剧评中提到的“碎片化叙事”和“缺乏凝聚力的故事”，本质上就是这种男性中心叙事的后遗症：它不需要逻辑，不需要深层的个体存在性，只需要一系列的 vignette（小插曲）来填满观众的注意力。身体在舞台上进行着所谓的“仪式感”动作，但这种仪式是 corny 的，因为它没有触及任何真实的生存博弈，而是一场关于“谁更像猫”的扮演比赛。

这种将人类身体“宠物化”的表达，正是文化暴力（cultural violence）的高级版本。它通过创造一个虚构的、非人的设定，让观众在潜意识中接受一种“身体可以被随意定义、修剪并服务于某种审美景观”的逻辑。当 Grizabella 唱起《Memory》时，观众被要求共情，但这种共情是廉价的，因为她的痛苦已经被纳入了一个被设计好的、可消费的娱乐模版中。这不仅仅是一场糟糕的音乐剧，这是一次关于如何用“艺术”之名，将人的主体性彻底抹除并替换为“宠物表达”的示范。

This review is a textbook example of circling within meta-violence. The critic focuses entirely on 'grace,' 'balletic feel,' and 'flamboyant leotards.' In this narrative, the actors' bodies are no longer subjects but commodities—visual landscapes objectified as 'cats.' When the reviewer praises Rum Tum Tugger's 'hip-thrusting swagger' akin to a young Mick Jagger, he is consuming a highly sexualized and domesticated masculine trait, packaged as 'cat-like personality' to erase actual human subjectivity.

The irony lies in the show's core: a ritual of 'rebirth' defined and monopolized by a single man, Andrew Lloyd Webber. The 'fragmented narrative' and 'lack of cohesive storytelling' mentioned in the review are the symptoms of this masculine-centric narrative. It requires no logic or deep existential presence, only a series of vignettes to capture attention. The bodies perform 'ritualistic' movements on stage, but these rituals are corny because they touch no real existential game; they are merely a competition of who can best 'play the cat.'

This 'pet-ification' of the human body is a sophisticated version of cultural violence. By creating a fictional, non-human setting, it conditions the audience to accept a logic where bodies can be arbitrarily defined, trimmed, and served as an aesthetic spectacle. When Grizabella sings 'Memory,' the audience is asked to empathize, but this empathy is cheap, as her suffering is slotted into a pre-designed, consumable entertainment template. This is not just a bad musical; it is a demonstration of how to use 'art' to completely erase human subjectivity and replace it with 'pet expression.'
