假化妆品里的汞，和真化妆品里的账Mercury in the Fakes, Ledger in the Real

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
假货卖的是廉价，真货卖的是你不敢不买
Counterfeits sell cheap; the real ones sell the fear of not buying

一支假的修容膏里能测出高浓度铅，一瓶假香水里八成是甲醇——这些数字看着吓人，但它只是把美妆这门生意本来的账目，用最粗暴的方式摊开了一次。

先看真货这一边。报道里英国美容理事会的 CEO 说，这个行业把"巨大的用心、创造力和专业"投入到产品里，"安全和信任贯穿整个行业"。这句话本身没错，错的是它被当成了免责声明。真货的安全标准是真的，但真货卖给你的那套东西——你不够白、不够瘦、毛孔不够细、气味不够高级——从来没有经过任何安全测试。假货只是在复制这个前提，然后省掉了成本。它复制的不是配方，是那个"你必须买"的判决。

所以 18 到 24 岁里明知是假货还买的人从 2023 年的 6% 涨到去年的 20%，这个数字不该只读成"年轻人贪便宜"。这是一次很朴素的最优解计算：正品定价已经把她们排除在外，假货给了她们一个能进入这场游戏的入口。她们不是不知道有风险，是知道正品的价格本身就是另一种风险。

然后看执法这一边。查获 1,666 件假发产品，博尔顿一处窝点货值 200 万英镑，知识产权局要联合内容创作者搞一个叫 Face the Fakes 的宣传。这套动作全部落在"告知消费者"上：你要知道你的产品从哪来。可报道里的毒理学家说得很清楚，包装看着"极其逼真"，你从外面看不出里面是什么。也就是说，这场运动要求消费者承担一个她们在结构上不可能完成的任务——在购买的那一刻辨别化学成分。责任被完整地转移给了最没有能力承担它的那一端。

真正该被追问的名字在这篇报道里没有出现：那些被仿冒的品牌，它们的定价策略、它们的广告投放、它们年复一年制造出的那种"你欠自己一瓶"的焦虑，是整条假货产业链的需求端。假货商只是在收割它们种下的东西。品牌方在这里的位置很舒服——既是受害者，又是共谋者，还不用付任何代价。

再往下一层，是这张脸本身。美妆是把女性的身体表面变成一块可以被定价、被比较、被反复修正的场地，这个场地越贵，仿冒就越有利润。汞能让皮肤短期变白，铅能让颜色更饱和——假货商不是随便选的配方，他们精确地知道这门生意在卖什么：不是护肤品，是更接近标准的可能性。

一篇报道里最诚实的部分是那个毒理学家的话：有些反应你看得见，但汞和铅在身体内部做的事，不会立刻显形。这句话对真货同样成立。

One fake contour stick tests at high levels of lead, one fake perfume is about 80% methanol. The numbers are alarming, but they only expose, in the crudest form, the ledger that beauty was already keeping.

Look at the legitimate side first. The British Beauty Council's chief executive says the industry pours "enormous care, creativity and expertise" into its products, and that "safety and trust run right through our industry." That sentence is true, and that is exactly the problem: it is being used as an alibi. The safety standards are real. But the thing real cosmetics actually sell you — you are not pale enough, not thin enough, your pores are not fine enough, your scent is not expensive enough — has never been safety-tested by anyone. The counterfeiters just copied the premise and cut the cost. What they replicated was not the formula. It was the verdict that you must buy.

So when the share of 18- to 24-year-olds knowingly buying fakes rises from 6% in 2023 to 20% last year, that is not simply young people being cheap. It is a plain optimal-expression calculation: legitimate pricing has already priced them out, and fakes offer an entry ticket into the game. They know the risk. They also know the price tag is its own kind of risk.

Now the enforcement side. 1,666 counterfeit hair products seized, £2m of goods found in a Bolton facility, and an Intellectual Property Office campaign called Face the Fakes, made with content creators. Every bit of it lands on informing the consumer: know where your products come from. But the toxicologist in the piece says the packaging can look "incredibly convincing" — you cannot see what is inside. The campaign asks consumers to perform a task they are structurally unable to perform: identify chemical composition at the moment of purchase. Responsibility is handed in full to the party least equipped to carry it.

The names missing from this report are the brands being copied. Their pricing, their ad spend, the anxiety they manufacture year after year — you owe yourself a bottle — is the demand side of the entire counterfeit chain. The counterfeiters are harvesting what the brands planted. The brands get to be victim and co-conspirator at once, at no cost.

One layer down is the face itself. Beauty turns the surface of a woman's body into a site that can be priced, compared, and endlessly corrected. The more expensive the site, the more profit in faking it. Mercury whitens skin fast; lead makes color richer. The counterfeiters did not pick these ingredients at random. They know precisely what the business sells: not skincare, but the possibility of getting closer to the standard.

The most honest line in the report belongs to the toxicologist: some reactions are visible, but what mercury and lead do inside the body does not show up immediately. That is just as true of the real products.
