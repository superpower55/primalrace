撤军不是结束，是账单开始Withdrawal Is Not the End. It's When the Bill Comes Due

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
十天围城结束，被拆掉的房子不会自己长回来
A ten-day siege ends; the demolished homes do not grow back

以色列军队从希伯伦撤了。巴勒斯坦安全部队接管了地区，省长 Khaled Dudin 宣布被迫离家的家庭可以回去了。听上去像一条收尾新闻。它不是。

十天里发生的事，报道写得很清楚：围城、封锁城市和周边村庄、占领巴勒斯坦人的住宅改成军事哨所、强制驱离、拆毁房屋和商铺、破坏基础设施。超过 3300 名巴勒斯坦人流离失所或受到影响，15 所房屋及其他建筑被拆。学校停课，经济受损。这些不是撤军能撤回的东西。

Violence = Potential − Actual。撤军把 direct 层的一部分暴力停下来了——枪口移开，人可以回家。但 structural 层的账还在原地：房子没了，商铺没了，基础设施坏了，3300 多人的生活被打断。以色列军方完成了一次十天的行动，然后离开；代价留在希伯伦，由被驱离的人自己承担。这不是和平，这是把暴力从事件变成现状。

再看这场围城的由头。10 月 23 日，两名巴勒斯坦人发动枪击，打死两名以色列士兵、打伤三人，以军随后击毙了这两名袭击者。两个人做的事，一座城市付账。三千三百多人被驱离、被拆房，和那两名袭击者没有关系，和那两名死去的士兵也没有关系。集体惩罚在这里不是意外，是设计：它把“安全”变成一个可以无限透支的账户，透支的是别人的家。

报道里最值得盯住的不是撤军，是“可以返回”这句话由谁来说。省长说当局和巴勒斯坦安全部队正在监测局势、为居民安全返回创造条件。创造条件——房子被拆了，怎么创造？谁来赔？报道没写。十五所房屋和其他建筑被拆，三千三百多人受影响，这些数字后面没有一行是重建承诺、赔偿方案或者问责程序。撤军是军事决定，返回是行政说法，中间那段——谁拆的、拆了什么、谁该负责——消失了。

这就是 cultural 层在动的地方：一场十天的军事行动，最后被叙述成“撤军”和“返回”两个中性词。围城、占屋、拆房、驱离，全部被压缩进“行动”这个词里，好像它是一个天气过程。语言在这里做的事，是让 structural 暴力看起来像临时状况，而不是一次有施施者、有受益者、有代价承担者的行为。

还有一层没人问：这十天里，谁在共谋。以色列军方执行围城，这不用多说。但“巴勒斯坦安全部队接管”这句话里藏着一个安排——占领的结构没有变，变的只是哪支枪站在街口。希伯伦还在被占领，返回的人回到的是被拆过的家、被改成哨所又还回来的房子、断了的基础设施。

撤军是好事，direct 层的血止住了。但差额没有被缩小，只是被移到了别处：从枪口下移到废墟里，从十天的新闻移到没有期限的重建。三千三百多人回家，回到的是一个需要被重新证明可以住人的地方。谁付这笔账，报道没写。没写，就是答案的一部分。

Israeli forces have withdrawn from Hebron. Palestinian security forces have taken control, and Governor Khaled Dudin announced that families forced from their homes can return. It reads like a closing story. It isn't.

What happened over those ten days is in the report: a siege, closures on the city and surrounding villages, Palestinian homes seized and converted into military posts, forced displacement, demolitions of homes and commercial properties, damage to infrastructure. More than 3,300 Palestinians displaced or otherwise affected. Fifteen homes and other structures demolished. Schools disrupted. The economy hit. None of that is withdrawn by a withdrawal.

Violence = Potential − Actual. The withdrawal stops part of the violence at the direct layer — the guns move, people can go home. But the structural bill stays exactly where it was: houses gone, shops gone, infrastructure broken, 3,300 lives interrupted. The Israeli military ran a ten-day operation and left. The cost stayed in Hebron, payable by the people who were pushed out. This is not peace. It is violence converting from event into status quo.

And look at what triggered the siege. On October 23, two Palestinians opened fire, killing two Israeli soldiers and wounding three; Israeli forces then killed the two attackers. Two people did something. A city paid. The 3,300 displaced and the demolished homes have nothing to do with those two attackers, and nothing to do with the two dead soldiers either. Collective punishment here isn't a side effect. It's the design: "security" becomes an account you can overdraw indefinitely, and what gets overdrawn is other people's homes.

The line worth staring at isn't the withdrawal. It's who gets to say "can return." The governor says authorities and Palestinian security forces are monitoring the ground and working to create the conditions for residents to return safely. Create conditions — with what? The houses are demolished. Who pays? The report doesn't say. Fifteen homes and other structures demolished, 3,300-plus people affected, and not one line about reconstruction, compensation, or accountability. Withdrawal is a military decision. Return is an administrative phrase. The middle part — who demolished what, and who answers for it — has vanished.

That's the cultural layer doing its work: a ten-day military operation gets narrated as two neutral words, "withdrawal" and "return." Siege, seizure, demolition, displacement all get compressed into "operation," as if it were a weather event. Language here makes structural violence look like a temporary condition rather than an act with perpetrators, beneficiaries, and people who pay.

One more question nobody asks: who was complicit in these ten days. The Israeli military ran the siege; no need to belabor that. But "Palestinian security forces take control" contains an arrangement — the occupation's structure is unchanged, only which gun stands on the corner. Hebron is still occupied. People are returning to demolished homes, to houses that were turned into posts and handed back, to broken infrastructure.

The withdrawal is good news; the bleeding at the direct layer has stopped. But the gap hasn't shrunk. It has moved — from under the gun to inside the rubble, from a ten-day news cycle to a reconstruction with no deadline. Over 3,300 people go home to a place that now has to prove it's habitable again. Who pays that bill, the report doesn't say. Not saying is part of the answer.
