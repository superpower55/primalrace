用“年轻女性”作为药引的资本共谋Capital Complicity: Using 'Young Women' as the Bait

性别 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-29 § 链接
所谓的“吸引年轻顾客”，本质是资本在认知入口上的精准收割。
Attracting 'younger shoppers' is simply a precise harvest of cognitive entry points by capital.

M&S 和 Sephora 的这次联手，在商业分析师眼里是“战略转型”，但在我看来是一场极其典型的资本共谋 (complicity)。一个代表着中产阶级旧秩序的英国零售巨头，通过引入 LVMH 旗下的 Sephora，试图在 24-36 岁女性这个特定群体中重新夺回“认知入口”。

请注意这个年龄段的设定：24-36 岁，正是女性在存在性战争中压力最大、最容易被“浪漫爱”和“成功女性”叙事洗脑的阶段。Sephora 并不只是在卖化妆品，它在卖一套由 TikTok 和 Instagram 算法精心包装的审美权力。通过与 influencer 合作，它定义了什么是“酷”的、什么是“高级”的。而 M&S 此时入局，实际上是在利用 Sephora 已经完成的文化武器化 (weaponisation) 过程，将这些年轻女性引导进自己的生态位。

这种“重新定义高街美容”的叙事，其实是文化暴力 (cultural violence) 的温床。它告诉年轻女性：你的主体性可以通过消费特定的品牌来获得，你的身份可以通过在 M&S 门店里体验 Sephora 的“个性化服务”来确立。这是一种典型的假.最优解表达——通过扮演资本认可的“精致女性”角色来换取某种心理上的阶级认同，代价则是主体性的进一步消亡。

最讽刺的是，这场共谋的幕后推手是 LVMH 的 Bernard Arnault 这种级别的资本巨鳄。他并不在乎女性的赋权，他只在乎如何通过垄断审美定价权来榨取剩余价值。M&S 投入的 7 亿英镑所谓的“现代化”，本质上是升级一套更高效的收割机器，让年轻女性在不知不觉中完成从“被凝视的客体”到“自愿付费的客体”的转变。

The tie-up between M&S and Sephora is framed as a 'strategic transformation' by analysts, but it is a textbook case of complicity. An old-guard British retailer is leveraging LVMH's Sephora to reclaim the cognitive entry point of women aged 24-36.

Note the target: 24-36. This is the demographic most vulnerable to the weaponisation of 'romantic love' and 'successful woman' narratives. Sephora isn't just selling makeup; it's selling an aesthetic power structure curated by TikTok and Instagram algorithms. By partnering with influencers, it defines what is 'cool' or 'high-end.' M&S is simply piggybacking on this process to lure these women into its own ecosystem.

This narrative of 'redefining high street beauty' is a form of cultural violence. It whispers to young women that their identity can be established by consuming specific brands and that their expression is validated by a 'personalised experience' in a store. This is a fake optimal expression—playing the role of the 'sophisticated woman' approved by capital to gain a fleeting sense of class belonging, at the cost of their own subjectivity.

The irony is that the puppet master is Bernard Arnault of LVMH. He cares nothing for female empowerment; he only cares about extracting value by monopolising the pricing power of beauty. M&S's £700m 'modernisation' is nothing more than upgrading a harvest machine, transforming young women from 'objects of the gaze' into 'willingly paying objects.'
