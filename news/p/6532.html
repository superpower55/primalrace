影评人不是失意的艺术家Critics Aren't Failed Artists

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把批评者说成失败者，就是在取消批评本身。
Calling critics failed artists is how you cancel criticism itself.

Waititi 说影评人是"没能当成艺术家、只好退而求其次去当评论家"的"可怜迷失的灵魂"。这句话不是情绪失控，是一次解释权的抢夺：先把批评者的资格取消掉，批评的内容就不用回应了。

他的论证结构很清楚：我十五年没读过影评，但我听观众的。观众给了 78%，影评人给了 26%，所以影评人"无关、脱节、过时、可以扔进垃圾桶"。这套逻辑的问题在于，观众分和影评分本来测的就不是同一件事。观众进场前买的是"Jenna Ortega 和 Amy Adams 演的、石黑一雄改编的"这个包装，影评人看的是成片。用前者否定后者，等于用销量否定质检。

更值得说的是他顺手举的例子。他说这些人"当年也恨《乔乔的异想世界》"，可那部片的影评人分是 81%。他记错了，或者他根本没查——一个声称十五年不读影评的人，确实没有能力核对这件事。这不是细节失误，这是他自己论证的漏洞：他攻击的是一个想象中的批评群体。

然后是数字。首周末 880 家影院收 72 万美元，第二周 14.5 万，国内总票房 110 万，成本 2000 万。索尼缩减了上映规模。这些不是影评人造成的，是发行方在看到数据后做的决定。把商业失败归因于"脱节的评论家"，是把结构性的市场判断包装成文化战争，让粉丝去替他打。

Ortega 的做法反而诚实：她说读影评会让她"陷入某种精神病"，所以不读。这是承认自己在保护自己，没有把不读包装成对批评的审判。Waititi 做的是另一件事——他不读，但他要判批评死刑。

最讽刺的是他改编的原著。石黑一雄写的是一个被制造出来去爱、去观察、去承受误读的存在者。Klara 的一生就是不断被误读、又不断试图理解人类的过程。一部关于"被观看者如何理解观看者"的小说的导演，现在在说观看者的判断不作数。

他真正在意的不是评论，是定价权——谁有权说一部电影好不好。他的答案是：买票的人。这个答案对票房有利的时候他拿出来用，对票房不利的时候他就不提了。

Waititi says film critics are "sad lost souls who couldn't achieve their dreams of becoming artists, and thus settled, and became critics." This isn't a temper tantrum. It's a grab at interpretive authority: disqualify the critic, and you never have to answer the criticism.

The structure is clean. He hasn't read a review in almost 15 years, but he listens to the people. Audiences gave it 78%, critics gave it 26%, therefore critics are "irrelevant, out of touch, obsolete, in the bin." The problem is that audience scores and critic scores don't measure the same thing. Audiences buy the package — Jenna Ortega, Amy Adams, an Ishiguro adaptation. Critics watch the finished film. Using the first to overrule the second is using sales figures to overrule quality control.

Then there's the example he reaches for. He says these same people "also hated Jojo Rabbit." That film has an 81% critic score. He misremembered, or he never checked — and a man who claims not to have read a review in 15 years is in no position to check. This isn't a minor slip. It's the hole in his own argument: he's attacking an imagined critic, not the actual one.

And the numbers. $720,000 from 880 locations in week one, $145,000 in week two, $1.1 million domestic total against a reported $20 million budget. Sony scaled back the theatrical footprint. None of that was done by critics. It was done by a distributor reading data. Blaming "out of touch" reviewers for a commercial failure turns a market decision into a culture war and hands the fans a fight to fight on his behalf.

Ortega's version is more honest. She doesn't read reviews because "that would send me into a psychosis of some sort." That's someone protecting herself, and admitting it. Waititi does something else: he doesn't read, but he wants to pass sentence on criticism itself.

The irony sits in the source material. Ishiguro wrote a being manufactured to love, to observe, and to endure being misread. Klara's whole existence is the attempt to understand the humans who keep misreading her. The director of a novel about how the watched understand the watchers now says the watchers' judgment doesn't count.

What he's really after isn't the reviews. It's pricing power — who gets to say whether a film is good. His answer is: the people who bought tickets. He deploys that answer when the box office serves him and drops it when it doesn't.
