用“创新”掩盖的结构性贫困与男性叙事幻觉Innovation as a Mask for Structural Poverty and Masculine Delusion

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-10 § 链接
所谓的“创新红利”是典型的男性中心叙事，用宏大愿景掩盖资源分配的元暴力。
The promised 'innovation dividend' is a masculine-centric narrative using grand visions to mask the meta-violence of resource allocation.

戈登·布朗（Gordon Brown）这篇文章是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。他把国家的未来寄托在 AI、量子计算和合成生物学这些“硬核”领域，并试图用一个简单的数学计算——每户增加 5000 英镑——来诱导公众买单。这在本质上是一场关于“认知入口”的武器化操作：通过定义什么是“创新”，从而定义谁才是国家未来的“关键资产”。

请注意他提到的那些“资产”：顶级大学、研究论文、独角兽公司。这些领域在 structural 层面上是高度男性化的共谋场域。在 venture capital 和 scale-ups 的博弈中，谁在定义“天才”？谁在决定资金流向？依然是那群掌握解释权的男性精英。布朗提到的“lost Einsteins”（失落的爱因斯坦）听起来很温情，但这种叙事依然是在男性定义的“天才”框架下寻找补丁，而不是去质问为什么基础的 care work、社会照料和女性主导的领域在国家战略中永远是“次要”的。

这种叙事最阴险的地方在于它制造了一种“潜在状态”（Potential）。它告诉人们，只要我们“创新强度”提高，大家都会变富。但这实际上是 cultural violence：它将当前的经济困境归咎于“缺乏创新”，而非资源分配的结构性不公。它试图用一个关于“全球先锋”的浪漫化愿景，掩盖掉那些在生物墙和结构暴力中被榨干的底层劳动力——尤其是那些在 AI 自动化威胁下最先被抛弃的女性护理员和服务人员。

所谓的“国家使命”，往往就是一种让个体在宏大叙事中自我消解的 scam。当布朗引用肯尼迪登月计划时，他其实在呼吁一种集体性的顺从。在这种共谋中，个体不再是主体，而成了实现“创新强国”这个男性幻觉的燃料。真正的 good_news 不应该是每户多出 5000 英镑的许诺，而应该是那些被定义为“次要”的生命体验，终于拿到了定义“国家价值”的定价权。

Gordon Brown's piece is a textbook example of a masculine-centric narrative. By pinning the nation's future on 'hard' fields like AI, quantum computing, and synthetic biology, and attempting to sell it with a simple calculation—£5,000 more per household—he is engaging in the weaponisation of 'cognitive entry points.' He defines what 'innovation' is, and in doing so, defines who the 'critical assets' of the future are.

Look at the 'assets' he cites: elite universities, research papers, and unicorns. These fields are structural complicity zones dominated by men. In the game of venture capital and scale-ups, who defines 'genius'? Who directs the flow of capital? It remains the same group of men holding the power of interpretation. His mention of 'lost Einsteins' sounds compassionate, but it's merely a patch within a male-defined framework of 'genius,' rather than questioning why care work and female-led domains remain 'secondary' in national strategy.

The cruelty of this narrative lies in the manufacturing of 'Potential.' It tells the public that poverty is a result of 'lack of innovation' rather than structural injustice in resource distribution. This is cultural violence: using a romanticised vision of being a 'global vanguard' to mask the exploitation of those crushed by the biological wall and structural violence—especially the female caregivers and service workers who will be the first discarded by AI automation.

Such 'national missions' are often scams designed to make individuals dissolve into grand narratives. When Brown invokes JFK's moonshot, he is calling for a collective submission. In this complicity, the individual is no longer a subject but fuel for a masculine delusion of a 'strong innovation nation.' Real good_news wouldn't be a promise of £5,000; it would be the moment the lived experiences currently defined as 'secondary' finally seize the pricing power to define 'national value.'
