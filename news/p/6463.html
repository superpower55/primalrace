四十个高管，一个都不谈钱Forty Executives, Not One Word on Who Gets Paid

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要Billboard公布2026年40位40岁以下精英榜单，Backline联合创始人兼执行董事Hilary Gleason领衔。该榜单每年表彰音乐行业年轻高管，每人仅可入选一次，今年收到数百份合格提名。
The newsBillboard has revealed its 2026 40 Under 40 list, led by Hilary Gleason, co-founder and executive director of Backline. The annual list honors young music industry executives, who can appear on it only once, and drew hundreds of qualified nominations this year.
这份榜单不谈分配，只谈热爱。
A power list that talks about passion and never about distribution.

Billboard 的 40 Under 40 是一份权力榜，但它把权力写成了履历。四十个人，头衔从 CTO 到经纪人到厂牌合伙人，管的是版税系统、巡演结算、票务、AI 授权、艺人心理健康。这份榜单从头到尾没有一句话谈钱怎么分——除了 Nicki Shamel 让 TuneCore 的美加区收入涨了 20%、毛利涨了 25%，以及艺人累计拿到 50 亿美元。数字出现了，分配没出现。

这就是榜单这种体裁在做的事：把结构问题翻译成个人成就。音乐产业的硬问题榜单自己列了——AI、公平票务——然后说这些人是来“寻找解决方案”的。可解决方案由谁定、代价落在谁身上，一个字没有。Adam Cohen 促成的 112,408 张票是“美国史上最大售票演唱会”，这个数字被当成荣耀写出来；同一张票背后二级市场的溢价、服务费、动态定价，不在榜单的语法里。

Shirin Nury 那段最值得看。她说自己的“超能力”是还记得十六岁在 Roxy 看演出时是什么感觉，“我很清楚年轻女性作为粉丝在看什么”。这句话是真的，也是这门生意最精准的自我描述：一个女性粉丝的凝视被提炼成判断力，再拿去给 Gracie Abrams、Tate McRae、Charli xcx 排巡演。懂粉丝不是共谋，但把“懂年轻女性想要什么”变成可执行的商业直觉，是第三章那套东西——认知入口不需要撒谎，只需要选谁来解释需求。

Hilary Gleason 被放在榜首，理由是 Chappell Roan 在格莱美喊话厂牌承担艺人医保，然后给 Backline 捐了 25,000 美元，AEG 配捐，Noah Kahan、Sabrina Carpenter、Charli xcx 跟进。这是好事，具体的好事：一笔钱进了心理健康服务的入口。但把这件事放进一份“下一代领袖”榜单里，它同时完成了一次置换——一个艺人公开质问厂牌“你们管不管我们”，最后变成一家非营利组织的史上最大单笔募款，和“企业更认真地捐钱”。问题被接住了，但接住它的是慈善，不是那笔本该由厂牌出的医保。Gleason 自己都说她犹豫过：Roan 要的是更广的医疗可及性，Backline 只做心理健康。这个犹豫是整篇报道里最诚实的一句话，也是榜单最想赶紧翻过去的一句话。

再看这份名单的构成。四十个人里女性不少，非白人不少，拉丁、K-pop、乡村、电子各有代表，看起来很多元。但多元的是被管理的艺人池，不是被追问的问题。Nicole Kim 管 BTS 的全部运营，报道里出现的是“历史性回归专辑”和开到 2027 年 3 月的体育场巡演；BTS 背后那套练习生合约、行程密度、兵役与复出的商业安排，一个字没有。Juan S. Arenas 用 HYBE 的“艺人培养方法论”做出一个拉丁男团，150 亿次流媒体播放——方法论三个字被当成中性词写出来，可第三章里 K-pop 模型最核心的部分恰恰是这套方法论：从十二三岁签起，管饮食、管恋爱、管生活。

这不是说榜单上的人都是坏人。是说榜单这个形式，天然只能装下“谁上升了”，装不下“上升的梯子是谁的肩膀”。Blayke Ujihara 最骄傲的是辛纳屈的版权进了 Iconic 的资产包；Tara Finegan 在买作曲家的曲库；Andrew Baron 在评估并购。这些都是把音乐变成可交易资产的动作，而资产化的另一面——创作者一次性卖断、终身版税换一笔现金——正是这个行业过去十年最大的分配变化。榜单把它写成“合作有远见的创业者”。

报道里唯一接近代价的地方，是那些“如果我不做音乐我会去干嘛”的回答：开河边小店、去滑雪、当超市区域经理、当校长。四十个人里没有一个人说“如果我失败了会怎样”。因为这份榜单的前提就是没人失败——失败的人不在提名名单里，不在 Luminate 的数据里，不在 Boxscore 里。

所以这份榜单真正列出来的，是音乐产业对自己的一次自我描述：我们年轻、多元、懂粉丝、关心心理健康、在解决 AI 和票务。至于钱从谁流向谁，谁的身体被行程表用掉，谁的曲库被买断，谁的二十五万美元捐款替谁省下了一笔医保——那是另一份榜单，还没人做。

Billboard's 40 Under 40 is a power list, but it writes power as a resume. Forty people, titles running from CTO to agent to label partner, working on royalty systems, tour settlements, ticketing, AI licensing, artist mental health. Nowhere does the list say how the money gets split — except Nicki Shamel raising TuneCore's US/Canada revenue 20% and gross profit 25%, and artists crossing $5 billion in collective earnings. The number appears. The distribution doesn't.

That is what the list format does: it translates structural questions into individual achievement. The industry's hard problems are named right there — AI, fair ticketing — and then these people are said to be "finding solutions." Who defines the solution and who eats the cost is not in the grammar. Adam Cohen's 112,408 tickets are written as glory, "the largest ticketed concert in U.S. history." What sits behind that ticket — resale markup, service fees, dynamic pricing — is not in the grammar either.

Shirin Nury's entry is the one to read closely. Her "superpower," she says, is still remembering what it felt like to see shows as a sixteen-year-old in the San Fernando Valley: "I understand fandom and have a very strong barometer for what young women are looking at as fans." True, and also the most precise self-description this business has: a young woman's gaze gets distilled into judgment, then spent on routing tours for Gracie Abrams, Tate McRae, Charli xcx. Understanding fans isn't collusion. But turning "I know what young women want" into executable commercial instinct is the Chapter 3 move — the cognitive entrance doesn't need to lie, it only needs to decide who gets to interpret demand.

Hilary Gleason tops the list because Chappell Roan stood at the Grammys and told labels to cover artists' healthcare, then gave Backline $25,000. AEG matched it. Noah Kahan, Sabrina Carpenter and Charli xcx followed. That is a real, specific good: money into the door of mental health access. But placing it at the top of a "next generation of leaders" list performs a substitution. An artist publicly asked the labels "do you got us?" and it became the largest single fundraising moment in a nonprofit's history, plus corporations giving "in a more serious way." The question got caught — by charity, not by the label healthcare bill it was aimed at. Gleason admits she hesitated: Roan wanted broader healthcare access, Backline only does mental health. That hesitation is the most honest sentence in the piece, and the one the list most wants to move past.

Look at who's on it. Many women, many non-white executives, Latin, K-pop, country, dance all represented. It looks plural. But what's plural is the talent pool being managed, not the questions being asked. Nicole Kim runs all operations for BTS; what appears is a "historic comeback album" and a stadium tour running through March 2027. The trainee contracts, the schedule density, the military-service and comeback logistics — absent. Juan S. Arenas built a Latin boy band through HYBE's "artist development methodology," 150 million streams. Methodology is written as a neutral noun, but in the K-pop model the methodology is the thing: signing at twelve or thirteen, controlling diet, dating, daily life.

This doesn't make anyone on the list a villain. It makes the list a form that can only hold who rose, never whose shoulders the ladder stood on. Blayke Ujihara's proudest moment is Sinatra's catalog joining Iconic's roster. Tara Finegan is buying composers' catalogs. Andrew Baron is evaluating acquisitions. All of it turns music into tradable assets, and the other side of assetization — creators selling out, trading lifetime royalties for a lump sum — is the industry's biggest distribution shift of the past decade. The list calls it "partnering with visionary entrepreneurs."

The only place the piece comes near a cost is the "if I weren't in music" answers: a little joint by the water, ski industry, regional manager for Aldi, school principal. Not one of the forty says what happens if they fail. Because the premise of the list is that nobody fails — the failures aren't in the nomination pool, aren't in Luminate's numbers, aren't in Boxscore.

So what the list actually documents is the industry describing itself: young, plural, fluent in fandom, attentive to mental health, working on AI and ticketing. Who pays whom, whose body the tour schedule spends, whose catalog gets bought, whose $25,000 donation saves which company a healthcare bill — that's a different list, and nobody has made it yet.
