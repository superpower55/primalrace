谁在定义“冒险”：被低估的单身女性生存博弈Defining Adventure: The Underrated Existential Game of Solo Women

性别 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-04 § 链接
真正的冒险不是走过沙漠，而是女性在野外获得“不被定义”的自由。
True adventure isn't crossing a desert; it's a woman claiming the freedom to be undefined in the wild.

这篇文章表面在写徒步，其实在写一次关于主体性的夺回。Alice Morrison 走完沙特全境，那是需要后勤团队支撑的“世界第一”；但她这次在苏格兰高地的 solo hiking，才是真正意义上的存在性战争。因为在这里，她通过 self-supported 的方式，物理性地切断了所有需要向他人交代（answer to）的连接。

注意到文中那个细节：当她面对一个关闭的旅馆门时，对方女性的反应是“shriek”（尖叫）。这个生理反应精准地揭示了结构性暴力中的文化潜意识——一个单身女性出现在如此荒僻的野外，在既定叙事中要么是“迷路者”，要么是“受害者”。在这种元暴力的认知入口里，女性在野外是不被允许“自洽”的，她们必须被保护，或者被定义为某种异常。

但 Alice 把这种“异常”变成了她的最优解表达。她通过携带 12kg 的装备、面对连续五小时的暴雨、在野外露营，将自己从“被保护的客体”转化为了“掌控环境的主体”。这种 self-reliant 的状态，实际上是在挑战一种深层的共谋：即社会默认女性缺乏独立生存能力，从而将她们禁锢在私人领域或受控的公共空间中。

最后那句“Queen of the world”不是文学性的修辞，而是一个关于权力分配的判断。当她抛弃了“10英里”这种由外部定义的 manufactured goals 时，她完成了从扮演“成功探险家”到回归“生命本体”的跨越。这种在自然中感受到的安全感，本质上是对社会结构性不安全感的反向消弭。

This piece masquerades as a hiking travelogue, but it is actually about the reclamation of subjectivity. Alice Morrison's world-first walk across Saudi Arabia required a backup team; however, her solo hike in the Scottish Highlands is a genuine existential war. By being self-supported, she physically severs all connections that require her to answer to others.

Consider the detail where the hostel manager "shrieks" upon seeing her. This physiological reaction precisely exposes the cultural violence embedded in the subconscious: a solo woman in the remote wilderness is framed as either a "lost soul" or a "victim." In the cognitive entry of meta-violence, women are not permitted to be self-sufficient in the wild; they must be protected or categorized as an anomaly.

Alice transforms this "anomaly" into her optimal expression. By carrying 12kg of gear, enduring five hours of rain, and wild camping, she shifts from being a "protected object" to a "subject controlling the environment." This self-reliance challenges the deep-seated complicity that assumes women lack the capacity for independent survival, thereby confining them to private spheres or controlled public spaces.

Her final claim of being "Queen of the world" is not mere poetic flourish, but a judgment on the distribution of power. When she discards the "10-mile goal"—a manufactured goal defined by external standards—she transcends the role of a "successful adventurer" to return to her biological essence. The sense of safety she finds in nature is, in essence, a reversal of the structural insecurity imposed by society.
