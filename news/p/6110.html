把睡前故事拍成三部曲，谁在付账They Turned a Bedtime Story Into a Trilogy. Who Paid for It?

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
童话的框架被拆了，因为史诗比哄睡好卖。
The fairy-tale frame was dismantled because epic sells better than lullaby.

这篇文章说的是改编，但真正被拆穿的是另一件事：一个故事本来长什么样，是由谁在什么场合讲给谁听决定的。

《霍比特人》起源于托尔金在1930到1931年间写下的一行字，然后变成讲给孩子听的睡前故事。克里斯托弗会追着问比尔博的门是什么颜色、索林的流苏是什么材质，细节对不上就不干。托尔金后来把这些写下来，一部分原因就是被孩子催的。这不是花边，这是这个故事的原初形态：它是在一个房间里、被一个具体的人、讲给具体的听众、边讲边被追问出来的东西。

然后它被拍成了三部曲。作者说，怪企业贪婪太简单，因为杰克逊和菲利帕·博伊恩斯是同意把原定两部扩成三部的。这句话是对的，也正因为对，才更难看。贪婪不是一个人做的，是一群人达成的共识：多一部就是多一张票、多一轮宣发、多一个可以剪进预告片的战场。共谋不需要恶意，只需要每个人都觉得“这是行业惯例”。

作者提出的替代方案其实很准：《公主新娘》那种框架，祖父给生病的外甥念书，故事在“我们的世界”和“那个世界”之间来回切。托尔金自己在《论童话故事》里说过，仙境的魔法就在两个世界之间的那道门上。把讲故事的人放回画面里，虚构反而更可信。而托尔金的孩子真的参与过《霍比特人》的成形，他们帮忙打字、编辑。这一段如果拍进去，会是全片最有力量的镜头。

但这里有个更冷的东西。托尔金1967年对《纽约时报》说，孩子不是一个阶级，他们只是处在不同成熟阶段的人。他讨厌《霍比特人》里那些“当作写给小孩、而不是写给人看”的部分，他的孩子也讨厌。也就是说，把儿童读物降格成低配娱乐，不是孩子的需求，是成年人的定价。三部曲做的事是一样的，只是方向反过来：不降格，升格，把哄睡的故事升成史诗，因为史诗有票房。两次都是同一个动作——不问这个故事本来是什么，只问它该被包装成什么才卖得动。

报道里没有写的是：谁为这场升格付了钱。观众付了票钱，托尔金的名字被借去做了品牌，而那个在床边被追问门是什么颜色的场景，没有人拍。作者说这本来可以是一部更好的电影，我同意。但那部电影不会出现在排片表上，因为它不够大。

童话的定义里有一条是“逃逸”，托尔金说这种逃逸既是实际的，也是英勇的。三部曲逃向了另一个方向：逃向更大的预算、更长的时长、更响的战场。真正被留在原地的是那个房间，和房间里那个还没被定价的听众。

This article is about adaptation, but what it actually exposes is something else: what a story is depends on who tells it, to whom, and where.

The Hobbit began with a single line Tolkien wrote in 1930 or 1931, and then became bedtime stories for his children. Christopher nagged him about the color of Bilbo's door and the material of Thorin's tassel; inconsistencies were not tolerated. Tolkien eventually wrote it down, partly because his children pushed him to. This is not trivia. It is the story's original form: a thing told in a room, by a specific person, to specific listeners, revised on the spot by their questions.

Then it became a trilogy. The author says blaming corporate greed is too simple, because Jackson and Philippa Boyens agreed to expand two films into three. That is true, and because it is true, it is worse. Greed is not one person's act; it is a consensus. One more film means one more ticket, one more marketing cycle, one more battle to cut into a trailer. Conspiracy needs no malice, only everyone deciding this is how the industry works.

The alternative the author proposes is precise: The Princess Bride's frame, a grandfather reading to his sick nephew, the story moving between our world and that one. Tolkien himself wrote in On Fairy-Stories that the magic of Faerie lives in the door between the two. Putting the teller back on screen makes the fiction more believable. And Tolkien's children really did help shape The Hobbit, typing and editing. That process, filmed, would have been the most powerful scene in the movie.

But there is something colder here. In a 1967 New York Times interview, Tolkien said children are not a class, merely human beings at different stages of maturity. He disliked the parts of The Hobbit written "as for children instead of just for people," and his children disliked them too. So downgrading a children's book into low-grade entertainment is not what children want; it is how adults price it. The trilogy does the same thing in reverse: not downgrade but upgrade, turning a lullaby into an epic, because epics sell. Both moves are identical: never ask what the story is, only what it must be packaged as to move units.

What the article does not say is who paid for the upgrade. Audiences paid at the box office, Tolkien's name was borrowed as a brand, and the scene of a father being interrogated about a door's color was never filmed. The author says a better movie was possible. I agree. That movie would not have appeared on the schedule, because it would not have been big enough.

One definition of fairy story is escape, which Tolkien called both practical and heroic. The trilogy escaped in another direction: toward bigger budgets, longer runtimes, louder battlefields. What was left behind is the room, and the listener in it who had not yet been priced.
