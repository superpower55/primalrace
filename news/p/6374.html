一份片单，半部男性中心叙事A List, Half a Masculine Narrative

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Vanity Fair ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
二十五部"最佳"里，一半是男人在当主角
Of twenty-five "best" films, half are men being men

Vanity Fair 这份 Netflix 十一月片单，最值得看的不是那二十五部电影，是它自己。它是一份无意识的样本，展示了 2026 年一个文化权威机构认为什么是"best"。往下数一遍就知道了。

《Heat》，"brooding, masculine psychology"——原文自己写的，男性心理，还是阴郁的那种，被当成卖点。《The Irishman》，黑帮、工会、忠诚。《Lawrence of Arabia》，战争史诗，三个小时四十七分钟，原文还贴心地提醒你"the trick is not minding that it's long"。《Memento》《Looper》《Ad Astra》，三个男人在时间、空间、记忆里打转。《Stand by Me》，原文说它"gently touching on themes of death and masculinity"。连怪物片《Alien》的介绍，落点都是"meets its match in Sigourney Weaver's Ellen Ripley"——一个女性角色被写成了男主角的对手。

这不是说这些片子不好。是说当"最佳"的名单被这样拼出来的时候，它反映的不是品味，是认知入口的默认设置。第三章讲得很清楚：谁掌握书写权，谁就掌握"什么是好的"的定义权。Vanity Fair 不是恶意的，它只是在一个男性中心叙事里呼吸，呼出来的就是这些。

然后看那些女性主导的片子被怎么处理。《His Three Daughters》，三个女人挤在纽约的小公寓里等父亲死，Carrie Coon、Natasha Lyonne、Elizabeth Olsen，原文给了 98% 的烂番茄和"one of 2024's best films"——然后把它排在《Heat》和《The Irishman》后面，用一种"这也很好"的语气。好，但是次要的。软性的。补充的。

《Bridesmaids》更有意思。原文说它是"the best and funniest comedy of that decade"，这是极高的评价。但紧接着的描写是"a depressed woman whose best friend gets engaged, kicking off a slow-motion meltdown"——一个女人的崩溃被当成笑料。这不是批评这部电影，这部电影本身很好。是批评这份片单选择用"崩溃"来概括它，而不是用"友谊"。

最刺眼的是《May December》。原文的介绍是：一个演员痴迷于她即将扮演的真实人物，一个前教师，因为和一个青少年学生发生性关系而成为小报常客。然后原文说，这部电影"skewers tabloid culture"，同时"a darkly comedic look at performance and the lies we tell ourselves"。一个成年女性对未成年男性的性剥削，被包装成"表演"和"我们对自己说的谎"。如果性别对调，这份片单还会用"darkly comedic"来形容吗？

《Tár》是唯一一部让这份片单稍微诚实一点的电影。一个有权力的指挥家，做了坏事，从她自己的视角讲。原文说这是"dissection of a post-#MeToo downfall"。但注意，它被放在片单的末尾附近，像一个需要被小心处理的标本。

这份片单里有没有真正好的东西？有。《Strong Island》，Yance Ford 记录他兄弟 1992 年被谋杀、凶手逍遥法外，原文说它是"an antidote to the rise of the exploitative true crime documentary"，这是 cultural 层的一次真实的反向操作——一个被垄断的叙事被拆穿了。《Atlantics》，Mati Diop 的鬼故事，一个女孩被安排嫁给不爱的人，她的爱人在海上失踪，然后鬼魂回来向剥削性的公司复仇。原文说它"achingly romantic and fiercely original"，这是对的。

但这两部片子在这份二十五部的片单里，被放在什么位置？《Atlantics》在第四，《Strong Island》在中后段。它们存在，但它们不是这份片单的"骨架"。骨架是那些男人。

所以这份片单真正在教你的，不是"这二十五部电影好看"，是"好看的标准长什么样"。它长成一个中年男人的脸，阴郁、忠诚、在时间里打转、偶尔打一架。女性在这份片单里，要么是配角，要么是崩溃的喜剧演员，要么是被小报消费的教师，要么是需要被"解剖"的堕落者。

Netflix 的算法不会告诉你这些。它只会告诉你"因为你看了 X，所以你可能会喜欢 Y"。但 Vanity Fair 的编辑是人，他们选择、排序、写介绍。这个选择的过程，就是一次微小的、日常的、不被察觉的元暴力执行。

《Dick Johnson Is Dead》是这份片单里最接近"公正的表达"的东西。Kirsten Johnson 拍她父亲，用各种荒诞的方式预演他的死亡，原文引她的话："How can my father and I together confront the fact that he, who is irreplaceable, will disappear?" 这是一个人在面对不可逆的失去时，能做出的最诚实的表达。它不掠夺任何人。它只是存在。

这份片单需要更多这样的东西。不是更多女性导演（虽然也需要），是更多不把"男性心理"当成默认卖点的电影。是更多不把女性的崩溃当成笑料的喜剧。是更多不把性剥削包装成"表演"的剧情片。

但这份片单不会改。因为改它，意味着改"什么是好的"这个定义。而定义权，从来不在写片单的人手里。

The most revealing thing about Vanity Fair's November Netflix list isn't the twenty-five films. It's the list itself—an unwitting sample of what a cultural authority in 2026 considers "best." Count them.

Heat: "brooding, masculine psychology," the copy says, as if that's a selling point. The Irishman: mob, unions, loyalty. Lawrence of Arabia: war epic, three hours and forty-seven minutes, with the helpful note that "the trick is not minding that it's long." Memento, Looper, Ad Astra: three men circling through time, space, memory. Stand by Me: "gently touching on themes of death and masculinity." Even Alien's blurb lands on "meets its match in Sigourney Weaver's Ellen Ripley"—a female character framed as the hero's opponent.

This isn't an argument that these films are bad. It's that when "best" gets assembled this way, what's reflected isn't taste. It's the default setting of the cognitive entrance. Chapter Three is clear: whoever holds the writing hand holds the power to define what's good. Vanity Fair isn't being malicious. It's breathing inside a masculine-centric narrative, and this is what the exhale looks like.

Now watch how the women-led films get handled. His Three Daughters—three women crammed into a tiny New York apartment waiting for their father to die, Carrie Coon, Natasha Lyonne, Elizabeth Olsen, 98% on Rotten Tomatoes, "one of 2024's best films"—gets placed after Heat and The Irishman, in a tone that says "this is also good." Good, but secondary. Soft. Supplementary.

Bridesmaids is more interesting. The copy calls it "the best and funniest comedy of that decade," which is high praise. But the description that follows is "a depressed woman whose best friend gets engaged, kicking off a slow-motion meltdown." A woman's breakdown as the punchline. This isn't a critique of the film, which is excellent. It's a critique of a list that chooses "meltdown" over "friendship" as its summary.

The sharpest moment is May December. The blurb: an actor becomes obsessed with the real-life figure she's about to play—a former teacher who became a tabloid fixture for her sexual relationship with a teenage student. Then the copy says the film "skewers tabloid culture" while being "a darkly comedic look at performance and the lies we tell ourselves." An adult woman's sexual exploitation of a minor, packaged as "performance" and "the lies we tell ourselves." Swap the genders. Would the list still call it "darkly comedic"?

Tár is the one film that lets the list be slightly honest. A powerful conductor who has done bad things, told entirely from her myopic perspective. The copy calls it a "dissection of a post-#MeToo downfall." But notice where it sits—near the end, handled like a specimen requiring careful tweezers.

Is there anything genuinely good here? Yes. Strong Island, Yance Ford's documentary about his brother's 1992 murder and the killer who went free, is described as "an antidote to the rise of the exploitative true crime documentary." That's a real reversal at the cultural layer—a monopolized narrative getting dismantled. Atlantics, Mati Diop's ghost story about a girl set to marry a man she doesn't love, her lover lost at sea, spirits returning for revenge against an exploitative corporation—"achingly romantic and fiercely original," and the copy is right.

But where do these two sit in the twenty-five? Atlantics at number four. Strong Island somewhere in the middle. They exist, but they aren't the skeleton. The skeleton is the men.

So what this list actually teaches you isn't "these twenty-five films are good." It's "this is what good looks like." It looks like a middle-aged man's face—brooding, loyal, circling through time, occasionally throwing a punch. Women in this list are supporting players, or comedic breakdowns, or tabloid-consumed teachers, or fallen figures requiring dissection.

Netflix's algorithm won't tell you this. It'll just say "because you watched X, you might like Y." But the editors at Vanity Fair are human. They choose, order, and write the blurbs. That process is a small, daily, unnoticed execution of meta-violence.

Dick Johnson Is Dead is the closest thing here to a just expression. Kirsten Johnson films her father, staging his death in surreal, funny scenarios, and the copy quotes her: "How can my father and I together confront the fact that he, who is irreplaceable, will disappear?" This is the most honest expression a person can make in the face of irreversible loss. It plunders no one. It simply exists.

This list needs more of that. Not just more female directors (though that too), but more films that don't treat "masculine psychology" as a default selling point. More comedies that don't use women's breakdowns as punchlines. More dramas that don't package sexual exploitation as "performance."

But the list won't change. Because changing it means changing the definition of "good." And the power to define was never in the hands of the people writing the list.
