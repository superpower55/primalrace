一部关于赎罪的好笑剧集A Very Funny Show About Redemption

文娱 文化层 IndieWire ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要IndieWire 评论 NBC 情景喜剧《The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins》第二季，称其为今年最好笑的剧集。剧集讲的是因赌博被 NFL 除名的跑卫 Reggie Dinkins（Tracy Morgan 饰）在数十年后冲击名人堂，Daniel Radcliffe 饰的纪录片导演一路跟拍。第二季 11 月 2 日首播，每周更新，次日上线 Peacock。
The newsIndieWire reviews Season 2 of NBC's 'The Fall and Rise of Reggie Dinkins,' calling it the funniest series of the year. The show follows former NFL running back Reggie Dinkins (Tracy Morgan), decades after a gambling scandal, chasing a Pro Football Hall of Fame nod while Daniel Radcliffe's documentarian films him. Season 2 premieres November 2 on NBC, streaming next-day on Peacock.
被除名的人想回名人堂，这才是最好笑的地方
The funniest thing here is a banned man asking the same league to let him back in

这篇评论真正在说的事，藏在它的开头：评论者担心夸一部喜剧「最好笑」会变成明褒暗贬。因为「好笑」在这个行业里已经贬值了。艾美奖的最佳喜剧提名里，有一部温情催泪剧、一部重爱情轻喜剧的 rom-com、还有一部做饭的剧情片。喜剧这个类别，连装笑都懒得装了。

然后他花了半篇篇幅列名单：Adults、Colin From Accounts、Youth、The Comeback、It's Always Sunny、Abbott Elementary、The Lowdown、The Audacity、Trying、Hacks。这份名单不是背景，是证据。评论者要证明的不是「Reggie Dinkins 好笑」，而是「好笑这件事还值得被认真对待」。所以他把一部情景喜剧拆成 21 分钟一集的节奏表，数一个笑点怎么接一个笑点，讲 Monica 把 Pablo Torres 的播客和 Julio Torres 的「某个东西」搞混，Julio 问 Reggie「你觉得透明色怎么样」，Reggie 答「很多！」。三个笑点，一个镜头。这是手艺，评论者在为手艺作证。

但这里有个更有意思的东西。Reggie Dinkins 是谁？一个因为赌博被 NFL 踢出去的跑卫。几十年后，他想进名人堂。他的纪录片导演 Arthur 需要这部片子，不比 Reggie 想要那个头衔少。两个人一起做一部「复出电影」，一个要赎罪，一个要作品。评论者提到剧集愿意碰「纪录片对象要不要付钱给拍他的人」这种话题，然后马上滑向「一场荒唐的谋杀阴谋」和「灾难性的感恩节聚餐」。

这就是这部剧的机制：它把赎罪做成一个产业。名人堂是一个机构，纪录片是一个产品，复出是一个叙事。Reggie 要的不是原谅，是选票。Monica 在第二季第一集忙着招一个「公关主管」，去打通更年轻的市场。你看，连赎罪都要做市场细分。

这不是讽刺剧，评论者说得很清楚，它就是一个笑话机器，每个零件都为好笑服务。但笑话机器的燃料是真实的：一个被制度除名的人，想被同一个制度重新收编，而所有人——经纪人、导演、家人、对手的女儿——都在这条流水线上有自己的位置和分成。

评论者说，看到第四集，Reggie 和 Arthur 几乎像是被忘了，而这「完全没问题」。因为这部剧的机器已经转起来了，谁按按钮都行。这句话是夸奖，也是这部剧最诚实的地方：赎罪的故事里，主角从来不是最重要的零件。

评论者最后说，笑这么多次是一种礼物。他没说错。但礼物是有寄件人的：NBC、Peacock、Tina Fey 和 Robert Carlock 的制片公司，还有一套把「复出」包装成 21 分钟产品的播出机制。评论者数了每一个笑点怎么落地，没数的是：谁在为这套节奏付钱，以及为什么「好笑」需要一篇这么长的辩护。

The real subject of this review is buried in its opening worry: that calling a sitcom 「the funniest」 would be damning it with faint praise. Because 「funny」 has been devalued as a category. The most recent Emmy nominees for Best Comedy included a feel-good weeper, a rom-com heavy on the rom, and a cooking drama. The category has stopped pretending to laugh.

So the critic spends half the piece listing names: Adults, Colin From Accounts, Youth, The Comeback, It's Always Sunny, Abbott Elementary, The Lowdown, The Audacity, Trying, Hacks. That list isn't background. It's evidence. He isn't arguing that Reggie Dinkins is funny. He's arguing that funny is still worth taking seriously. Which is why he breaks a sitcom down into a 21-minute rhythm chart, counting how one joke hands off to the next: Monica confuses Pablo Torres's sports podcast with Julio Torres's 「something,」 Julio asks Reggie about 「the color clear,」 Reggie says 「A lot!」 Three laughs, one cut. That's craft, and the critic is testifying on its behalf.

But there's something better buried here. Who is Reggie Dinkins? A running back ousted from the NFL for gambling. Decades later, he wants into the Hall of Fame. His documentarian, Arthur, needs the comeback film as much as Reggie wants the honor. Two men making a redemption product: one wants absolution, one wants a movie. The review notes the show is happy to touch on documentary subjects paying their documentarians, then slides straight into a harebrained murder conspiracy and a calamitous Thanksgiving.

That's the mechanism: redemption as an industry. The Hall of Fame is an institution. The documentary is a product. The comeback is a narrative. Reggie doesn't want forgiveness. He wants votes. Monica spends the premiere hiring a new 「head of publicity」 to reach younger markets. Even atonement gets segmented.

This isn't a satire, and the critic is clear about that: it's a joke machine, every part tuned for laughs. But the fuel is real. A man banned by an institution wants to be re-admitted by the same institution, and everyone on the line — agent, director, family, the rival's daughter — has a seat and a cut.

By episode four, the critic says, Reggie and Arthur almost feel like an afterthought, and that's fine. The machine runs no matter who presses the buttons. That's a compliment, and it's also the show's most honest admission: in a redemption story, the lead was never the most important part.

Laughing this much is a gift, the critic concludes. He's not wrong. But gifts have senders: NBC, Peacock, Tina Fey's and Robert Carlock's production company, and a distribution machine that turns 「comeback」 into a 21-minute product. He counted how every joke lands. He didn't count who pays for the rhythm, or why 「funny」 needs a defense this long in the first place.
