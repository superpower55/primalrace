电影节的清单：在被精心包装的“禁忌”中消费客体Festival Lists: Consuming Objects within Curated 'Taboos'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-13 § 链接
将BDSM与芭蕾并列，是文化暴力将权力不对等转化为审美消费的典型scam。
Juxtaposing BDSM with ballet is a cultural violence scam, transforming power asymmetry into aesthetic consumption.

一份典型的电影节“必看清单”，本质上是一次关于认知入口的权力操演。看这个清单的排布：博尔赫斯的文学温情、芭蕾的肢体张力、以及BDSM的kink-positive。这种并列极其狡猾，它试图通过将“权力博弈”转化为“审美趣味”，把结构性的暴力消解在艺术的象牙塔里。

最值得追问的是《I Want Your Sex》。它被描述为“transgressive”且“kink-positive”，由一名“imperious dominatrix”和一名“bedazzled assistant”构成。在这种叙事里，BDSM被武器化为一种“前卫”的身份表达。但我们必须拆穿这个scam：当这种关系被放入电影节的快餐清单，它就成了一种被消费的符号。所谓的“积极”，如果没有对权力结构中真实共谋关系的解剖，就只是在为“支配与服从”这套男本位逻辑穿上艺术的外衣。

而《Pretty Babies》则揭露了另一种更直接的 structural violence：性产业与演艺圈的共生。两个女孩以为LA铺满黄金，结果成了产业的燃料。有趣的是，这种残酷的现实与前述的“BDSM审美”被放在同一个清单里。前者是血淋淋的被殖民，后者是精致的扮演。当观众在同一个电影节里同时消费这两种叙事时，他们实际上在共谋一场巨大的文化暴力——将女性身体的被剥夺与被支配，统一转化为一种可供观看的“视觉奇观”。

这种清单定义的“必看”，其实是在定义什么是“高级的禁忌”。它并不关心权力如何流动，只关心这种流动是否足够“viscerally intense”或“sensual”。

A typical film festival 'must-see' list is essentially a performance of power over cognitive entry points. Look at the arrangement: the literary warmth of Borges, the physical tension of ballet, and the 'kink-positive' nature of BDSM. This juxtaposition is cunning; it attempts to dissolve structural violence into artistic taste by converting power dynamics into aesthetic preferences.

'I Want Your Sex' is particularly telling. Described as 'transgressive' and 'kink-positive,' featuring an 'imperious dominatrix' and a 'bedazzled assistant,' BDSM is weaponized here as a form of avant-garde identity expression. But let's call this what it is: a scam. Without a dissection of the real complicity within power structures, 'positivity' is merely a decorative veil for the masculine-centric logic of dominance and submission.

Meanwhile, 'Pretty Babies' exposes a more direct structural violence: the symbiosis between the sex industry and show business. Two girls seek gold in LA and end up as fuel for the industry. The irony is that this brutal reality and the aforementioned 'BDSM aesthetic' occupy the same list. One is raw colonization; the other is a sophisticated performance. When the audience consumes both narratives at one festival, they become complicit in a massive cultural violence—unifying the deprivation and domination of the female body into a 'visual spectacle.'

This list doesn't define cinema; it defines 'high-class taboos.' It ignores how power actually flows, caring only if that flow is 'viscerally intense' or 'sensual.'
