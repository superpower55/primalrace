羊毛的“回归”：一场由石油驱动的生存博弈The Wool 'Comeback': A Survival Game Driven by Oil

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
所谓的行业复兴，不过是合成纤维成本上涨后的被动替代。
This industry revival is nothing more than a passive substitution triggered by rising synthetic costs.

不要被“回归” (comeback) 这种带有浪漫主义色彩的词汇欺骗了。从加尔通的暴力三角来看，这根本不是什么产业升级，而是一次典型的结构性被动获利。羊毛业在过去几十年里的衰落，本质上是全球资本在合成纤维（nylon, polyester）这个更高效的 weaponized 生产工具面前的溃败。当时人们选择化纤，不是因为羊毛不自然，而是因为化纤的定价权掌握在石油工业手中，它提供了绝对的成本优势。

现在的“好转”，逻辑极其简单且残酷：中东冲突 $\rightarrow$ 油价上涨 $\rightarrow$ 化纤成本增加 $\rightarrow$ 羊毛在价格博弈中重新获得竞争力。这不是因为世界突然变得更可持续 (sustainability)，而是因为石油这个“大他者”涨价了。所谓的“可持续性”叙事，不过是奢侈品牌（如 Chanel）和户外巨头（如 Patagonia）在资本运作时为了掩盖纯粹的成本套利而包裹的彩色糖衣。它们在寻找 premium wool，本质上是在通过垄断高品质原材料来构建新的阶级审美屏障。

最讽刺的是，新西兰的农业管理人员在呼吁国民“重新拥抱羊毛文化”。这种试图通过文化认同来稳定市场的做法，是典型的共谋尝试。他们试图将一个纯粹的经济博弈问题，转化为一种民族身份的认同问题。但事实是，当油价再次下跌，或者某种更廉价的生物合成材料出现时，这种基于“情怀”的复兴会瞬间崩塌。在存在性战争中，没有所谓的“回归”，只有在不同成本曲线之间地狱式地反复横跳。

Do not be deceived by the romanticized term 'comeback.' Through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this is not an industrial upgrade, but a typical case of passive structural gain. The decline of the wool industry over the past decades was essentially a defeat in the face of synthetic fibers (nylon, polyester)—a more efficient weaponized tool of production. The world shifted to synthetics not because wool lacked 'naturalness,' but because the pricing power was held by the petroleum industry, offering an absolute cost advantage.

The current 'improvement' follows a brutally simple logic: Middle East conflict $\rightarrow$ rising oil prices $\rightarrow$ increased synthetic costs $\rightarrow$ wool regains competitiveness in the pricing game. This isn't because the world suddenly became more committed to 'sustainability'; it's because the 'Other'—petroleum—became more expensive. The sustainability narrative is merely a colorful candy coating used by luxury brands like Chanel and giants like Patagonia to mask pure cost arbitrage. By securing premium wool, they are simply constructing new aesthetic barriers of class distinction.

Most ironic is the call for New Zealanders to 're-embrace wool culture.' This attempt to stabilize the market through cultural identity is a classic move of complicity. They are trying to transform a pure economic game into a matter of national identity. But the reality is that if oil prices drop again, or a cheaper bio-synthetic material emerges, this 'sentiment-based' revival will collapse instantly. In the existential war, there is no such thing as a 'comeback,' only a hellish oscillation between different cost curves.
