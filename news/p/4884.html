用虚构的战争给预算单写序言Writing Budget Proposals with Fictional Wars

国际 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-09 § 链接
恐惧是最高效的预算申请书，而战争叙事是权力的认知入口。
Fear is the most efficient budget application, and war narratives are the cognitive entry points of power.

这是一场极其典型的表达武器化操作。国防大臣 Wes Streeting 把一部 docudrama 称为他的“支出审查申请书” (spending review submission)，这句俏皮话背后是极其冷酷的逻辑：当真实的预算数据无法说服选民时，就制造一个足以令公众战栗的虚构现实，用恐惧来对冲税收增加或公共开支削减的愤怒。

这部剧的设定极其具有讽刺意味：让保守党的 Gove 和苏格兰民族党的 Sturgeon 这种死对头在虚拟的 Cobra 紧急会议室里结盟。这种“跨党派共识”的表演，本质上是在向公众传递一个信号：面对所谓的外部威胁，内部的所有政治分歧都应当被暂时抹除。这正是元暴力 (meta violence) 的运作方式——通过定义一个更高阶的“生存危机”，将权力阶层重新凝聚成一个共谋体 (complicity)，从而合法化大规模资金向军事机器的转移。

最荒诞的是 Gove 的反应，他惊讶于英国导弹防御能力的匮乏并感叹“WTF”。一个在政府核心层待了十多年的资深政客，竟然通过一部电视剧才意识到国防漏洞？这要么是拙劣的演技，要么就是权力结构中长期存在的认知脱节。他们不需要真实的预警，他们只需要一个能被大众消费的“觉醒时刻”，好让接下来的 300 亿英镑增支显得像是在拯救世界，而不是在填补他们此前数十年削减预算留下的坑。

这种叙事入口的精准投放，旨在将国防支出从一个“政治选择”转化为一个“生物本能”的生存问题。当民众开始在沙发上担心俄罗斯的潜艇时，他们就失去了质疑这笔钱是否应该用于医疗或气候灾难的权力。这就是制造可能性的艺术：先制造恐惧，再提供唯一的救赎方案——更多的军费。

This is a textbook case of the weaponisation of expression. Defence Secretary Wes Streeting calling a docudrama his “spending review submission” is a witty quip masking a cold logic: when real data fails to convince voters, manufacture a terrifying fictional reality to offset the anger over tax hikes or public spending cuts.

The show's premise is peak irony: pairing Michael Gove and Nicola Sturgeon—bitter political rivals—in a mock Cobra emergency room. This performance of “cross-party consensus” signals to the public that all internal political frictions must be erased in the face of an external threat. This is exactly how meta violence operates—by defining a higher-order “existential crisis,” the power elite reconstitute themselves as a complicity, legitimising the massive transfer of funds into the military machine.

Gove’s reaction is the most absurd part; he expresses shock at the UK's limited missile defence, asking “WTF.” A veteran politician who spent over a decade in the government core only realises these gaps through a TV show? This is either bad acting or a systemic cognitive disconnect. They don't need real warnings; they need a consumable “awakening moment” for the public, making the upcoming £30bn increase look like salvation rather than patching holes they dug themselves over decades.

This precision-targeted cognitive entry point aims to shift defence spending from a “political choice” to a “biological instinct” for survival. Once the public is on their sofas fearing Russian submarines, they lose the power to question if that money should instead go to healthcare or climate disasters. This is the art of manufacturing possibilities: first manufacture the fear, then provide the only perceived cure—more military spending.
