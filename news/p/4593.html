导弹技术转移与“玩火”叙事的认知博弈Missile Tech Transfer and the Cognitive Game of 'Playing with Fire'

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-27 § 链接
战争叙事是最高级的武器化表达，其目的在于夺取对“责任”的定义权。
War narratives are the ultimate weaponized expressions, aimed at seizing the power to define 'responsibility'.

俄罗斯外交部发言人扎哈罗娃的表态是典型的 weaponized expression。通过“玩火”（playing with fire）这种带有强烈情绪暗示的词汇，莫斯科试图在认知入口处建立一个简单的因果链：英国提供技术 $ ightarrow$ 乌克兰袭击 $ ightarrow$ 英国对袭击负有“直接责任”。这本质上是一场关于解释权的争夺战，旨在将英国从一个“技术供应商”重新定义为“恐怖袭击参与者”。

这种叙事逻辑的精巧之处在于它试图通过转移“责任”来制造恐惧。当俄罗斯威胁要打击英国在乌克兰及“其他地方”的军事设施时，它在进行一次存在性博弈。它不在乎导弹技术是否真的能被转移，它在乎的是通过这种公开的威胁，在西方内部制造分歧，迫使决策者在“支持乌克兰”与“自身安全”之间进行痛苦的权衡。这就是典型的利用认知入口来制造一种“不可预测的后果”之真实。

有趣的是，同一篇新闻中还夹杂着英国首相在威尔士谈论“绿色钢铁”和“青年就业”的琐碎叙事。这种强烈的反差揭示了现代政治的荒诞：在公共空间的表达中，一边是关于全球地缘政治的元暴力博弈，另一边是关于国内就业率的 PR 演出。对于大多数民众来说，后者是他们感知到的“现实”，而前者则是决定他们生存底线的结构性暴力。但无论是在工厂还是在战壕，这种由男性主导的权力博弈始终在运作，而具体的生命个体仅仅是被用来交换某种宏大意义的筹码。

Maria Zakharova's statement is a textbook case of weaponized expression. By using emotionally charged phrasing like "playing with fire," Moscow is attempting to establish a simplistic causal chain at the cognitive entry point: UK supplies tech $ ightarrow$ Ukraine strikes $ ightarrow$ UK bears "direct responsibility." This is essentially a battle for the power of interpretation, attempting to redefine the UK from a "tech supplier" to a "participant in terrorist attacks."

The sophistication of this narrative logic lies in its attempt to manufacture fear by shifting "responsibility." When Russia threatens to strike British military sites in Ukraine and "beyond," it is engaging in an existential game. It doesn't matter whether the missile tech is actually transferable; what matters is creating division within the West through public threats, forcing decision-makers into a painful trade-off between "supporting Ukraine" and "their own security." This is the art of using cognitive entry points to manufacture a reality of "unpredictable consequences."

Interestingly, the same news piece juxtaposes this with the UK Prime Minister talking about "green steel" and "youth employment" in Wales. This sharp contrast reveals the absurdity of modern politics: in the expression of public space, one side is a meta-violence game of global geopolitics, while the other is a PR performance about domestic employment rates. For most people, the latter is the "reality" they perceive, while the former is the structural violence that determines their survival baseline. Whether in a factory or a trench, this masculine-centric power game remains in operation, and individual lives are merely chips exchanged for some grand meaning.
