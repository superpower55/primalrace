怪物的定价权与被AI偷走的肉身The Pricing Power of Monsters and the Flesh Stolen by AI

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
艺术的真.最优解是肉身的存在感，而AI是同质化的元暴力。
The true optimal expression of art is physical presence; AI is just homogenised meta-violence.

Rick Baker 的职业生涯本质上是一场关于“真实表达”的博弈。在好莱坞这个巨大的共谋场域里，大多数人满足于用 CGI 这种廉价的数字掩体来制造所谓的“奇观”，但 Baker 坚持用胶水、橡胶和物理结构去构建怪物。这不仅是技术路径的选择，而是一次存在性战争：他拒绝让怪物的定义权交给算法，而是将其锚定在生物性的肉身触感上。

有趣的是，面对 AI 的入侵，Baker 表现出一种近乎温顺的 acquiescent。他说 AI 在“偷窃”所有人的作品，但随后又将其定义为“新玩具”。这种逻辑其实是一种危险的 cultural violence。AI 的本质不是在创作，而是在对人类既有表达进行大规模的采样和同质化（homogenised）。当所有的“创意”都变成了概率分布的平均值，艺术就失去了它的主体性。AI 制造的不再是怪物，而是一套符合大众认知的“怪物模版”。

Baker 提到的那句“希望到时候还会有艺术家存在”，其实揭示了目前认知入口的危机。当 AI 垄断了“什么是视觉事实”的制造权，人类的表达将被压缩成 AI 能够理解的标签。一个艺术家通过 40 年积累的、带有个人体温的“收藏癖”（hoarder），在 AI 看来只是可以被瞬间解构的 dataset。这种从肉身到数据的降格，正是 meta violence 的体现——它试图抹除个体表达的独特性，将其转化为可被量化的资源。

好在，物理世界的生物墙依然存在。AI 可以模拟皮毛的纹理，但无法模拟一个 13 岁少年在工作坊里触摸第一块弗兰肯斯坦面具时的那种生理性战栗。这种不可被数字化的“触感”，才是艺术家在存在性战争中最后的堡垒。

Rick Baker's career is essentially a game of 'authentic expression.' In the massive complicity of Hollywood, most are content with CGI—a cheap digital shelter for creating 'spectacles.' Baker, however, insisted on building monsters with glue, rubber, and physical structures. This isn't just a technical choice; it's an existential war. He refused to let the definition of monsters be handed over to algorithms, anchoring it instead in the biological tactility of the flesh.

Interestingly, Baker appears surprisingly acquiescent toward the invasion of AI. He notes that AI is 'stealing' from everyone, yet dismisses it as a 'new toy.' This logic is a form of dangerous cultural violence. AI doesn't create; it performs large-scale sampling and homogenisation of existing human expressions. When all 'creativity' becomes an average of probability distributions, art loses its subjectivity. AI no longer produces monsters; it produces 'monster templates' that satisfy mass consensus.

Baker's hope that 'there'll still be artists around' reveals the crisis of the current cognitive entry points. When AI monopolises the power to manufacture 'what is a visual fact,' human expression is compressed into labels that AI can understand. An artist's 40-year collection of personal, warm-blooded 'hoarding' is, to an AI, merely a dataset to be instantly deconstructed. This degradation from flesh to data is the embodiment of meta-violence—attempting to erase the uniqueness of individual expression and convert it into quantifiable resources.

Fortunately, the biological wall of the physical world still stands. AI can simulate the texture of fur, but it cannot simulate the physiological shiver of a 13-year-old boy touching his first Frankenstein mask in a workshop. This non-digitizable 'tactility' is the final fortress for artists in the existential war.
