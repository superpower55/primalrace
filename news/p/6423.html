他删掉的不是评分，是嘴He Didn't Delete Ratings. He Deleted His Mouth.

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要演员 Rider Strong 表示，他已停止在 Letterboxd 上公开自己的星级评分，以免影响与潜在合作者的关系。他在 Pod Meets World 播客中说，自己评分走极端，只给半星或五星，目前已删除约一半评分。
The newsActor Rider Strong said he has stopped revealing his star ratings on Letterboxd to avoid hurting relationships with potential collaborators. On the Pod Meets World podcast, he said he rates films either half a star or five stars, and has removed about half his ratings.
每个行业都有自己的Letterboxd，只是不叫这个名字
Every industry has its Letterboxd. Most just don't call it that.

Rider Strong 在播客上说，他不再在 Letterboxd 上公开打星了。理由很干净：他要请某个演员来演自己写的戏，而那个人翻到他的主页，发现自己的片子被他打了半颗星，这不是个好印象。所以他停下来，为了职业前途。

这不是一个关于影评的故事。这是一个关于表达如何被定价的故事。Letterboxd 表面上是一个影迷记录观影的地方，实际上是一个表达空间：你给什么打几星，等于在公开宣告你的品味、你的阵营、你愿意和谁站在一起。Strong 说得比谁都清楚——人们会互相查对方的 Letterboxd。查的不是电影，是你这个人。

他自称极端，只给半星或五星，评分曲线是一条平线。这种极端恰恰是最诚实的表达：喜欢就是喜欢，讨厌就是讨厌，没有中间地带的和稀泥。而现在他删掉了一半。他说“只删了一半”，剩下的“还在外面”。这句话听起来像宽慰，其实是自白：他知道剩下的一半也是风险，只是还没轮到来查的人。

所以这是一个行业里的共谋。不是阴谋，是默契。每个想在这个圈子里继续工作的人都明白，公开的表达是有代价的，而代价的定价权不在表达者手里，在那些可能成为合作方的人手里。Strong 没有抱怨，他只是陈述自己做了什么选择。这个选择很理性，理性到让人不舒服。

问题不在于他删了评分。问题在于，一个以表达为职业的行业，正在教人把表达收起来。演员、导演、编剧，所有人都在同一个平台上互相打量，而打量完之后，每个人都学会了只展示安全的部分。这不是审查，审查有明确的施暴者；这是更安静的东西，是每个人自己动手，把自己修剪成别人愿意合作的样子。

Letterboxd 有 1700 多万订阅者，今年还在探索出售。一个记录品味的平台，本身也成了一件被定价的商品。而在这个平台上，最贵的不是五星，是沉默。

Rider Strong said on a podcast that he stopped revealing his star ratings on Letterboxd. The reason is clean: he wants to ask an actor to be in a movie he's writing or directing, and if that actor checks his profile and finds his own film rated half a star, that's not a good look. So he stopped, for the sake of his professional career.

This is not a story about film criticism. It's a story about how expression gets priced. Letterboxd looks like a place where moviegoers log what they've seen. In practice it's an expression space: what you rate and how many stars you give is a public declaration of your taste, your camp, who you're willing to stand next to. Strong says it plainly — people check each other's Letterboxd. What they're checking isn't the films. It's you.

He calls himself an extremist, half a star or five, a flat line of a rating curve. That extremity is the most honest thing about it: love it or hate it, no mushy middle. Now he's removed half. He says "only half," the rest "are still out there." That sounds like reassurance. It's a confession: he knows the other half is risk too, just not yet audited by the right person.

So this is a conspiracy inside an industry. Not a plot — a tacit agreement. Everyone who wants to keep working in this business understands that public expression has a price, and the price is set not by the person expressing but by whoever might become a collaborator. Strong isn't complaining. He's describing a choice he made. The choice is rational, rational enough to be uncomfortable.

The problem isn't that he deleted ratings. The problem is that an industry whose whole business is expression is teaching people to put expression away. Actors, directors, writers all size each other up on the same platform, and after the sizing up, everyone learns to show only the safe part. This isn't censorship — censorship has a visible perpetrator. This is quieter: people trimming themselves into whatever shape someone else might agree to work with.

Letterboxd has over 17 million subscribers and has been exploring a sale this year. A platform for recording taste has itself become a priced commodity. And on that platform, the most expensive thing isn't five stars. It's silence.
