Drinkflation: 酒精浓度的剥夺与认知入口的欺诈Drinkflation: The Deprivation of Alcohol and the Fraud of Cognitive Entry

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-30 § 链接
当资本通过重新定义“品质”来掩盖成本削减，消费者在不知情中被剥夺了原有的获得感。
When capital masks cost-cutting by redefining 'quality,' consumers are stripped of their value without even knowing it.

这就是典型的 shrinkflation 升级版：Drinkflation。资本不再通过缩小包装体积这种肉眼可见的欺诈来占便宜，而是直接在生物化学层面动手，削减酒精浓度 (ABV)。这种暴力发生在 structural 层：通过利用 2023 年酒精税率的阶梯差额（3.5% 是分水岭），将成本压力精准地转嫁给消费者，而利润则被公司悄悄截留。

最令人作呕的是其 cultural 层的 weaponization。Carling 宣称 3.4% 的版本在口味测试中“排名更高”，这是一种极其卑劣的认知入口操控。它试图将“因为成本降低而导致的产品缩水”伪装成“为了口味升级而进行的优化”。这种叙事逻辑与很多公司宣称“为了环保而去掉塑料吸管”如出一辙——只要能把利润最大化包装成某种普世价值或用户体验，资本就敢把谎言写成真理。

这种机制在本质上是一场关于“存在性”的博弈。消费者在买单时，以为自己购买的是同一种产品，但实际上他们购买的是一个被阉割了的替代品。这种对“事实”的篡改，让消费者的主体性在不知不觉中被消解：你以为你在选择口味，其实你只是在被动接受一个被资本定价后的“劣质版本”。

所谓的“价值-高端价格阶梯” (value-premium price ladder) 更是典型的共谋陷阱。它通过制造一个更贵的 4.7% 版本作为锚点，让 3.4% 的缩水版看起来像是一个“合理的标准选项”。这不仅是商业策略，更是一次成功的心理操纵，让消费者在潜意识里认同这种被剥夺的状态。

This is a textbook evolution of shrinkflation: Drinkflation. Capital is no longer relying on the visible fraud of shrinking packaging to skim profits; instead, they are operating at the biochemical level by reducing alcohol by volume (ABV). This violence manifests at the structural layer: by exploiting the tax threshold of 3.5% set in 2023, companies precisely shift the cost burden onto consumers while silently hoarding the margins.

Even more repulsive is the weaponization at the cultural layer. Carling's claim that the 3.4% version "ranked higher for overall taste" is a vile manipulation of the cognitive entry. It attempts to disguise a product downgrade driven by cost-cutting as an optimization for flavor. This narrative logic is identical to corporations claiming they removed plastic straws "for the environment"—as long as profit maximization can be packaged as a universal value or a user experience upgrade, capital will rewrite lies as truth.

At its core, this is an existential game. The consumer believes they are purchasing the same product, but they are actually buying a castrated substitute. This tampering with "facts" erodes the consumer's subjectivity: you think you are choosing a taste, but you are passively accepting a "substandard version" priced by capital.

The so-called "value-premium price ladder" is a classic complicity trap. By introducing a more expensive 4.7% version as an anchor, the 3.4% watered-down version is framed as a "reasonable standard option." This is not just a business strategy; it is psychological manipulation, forcing consumers to subconsciously accept a state of deprivation.
