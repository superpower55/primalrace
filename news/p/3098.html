用“互助模型”掩盖的债权共谋The Mutualism Scam and the Complicity of Creditors

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
将基础设施非营利化是 Actual 向 Potential 走近，但 Bail-in 才是拆穿共谋的关键。
Mutualisation is a step toward reducing structural violence, but only a real bail-in exposes the complicity of capital.

水务公司将污水排入河流的同时通过提高账单来维持分红，这是典型的 structural violence。在这种结构中，资本通过“提取” (extraction) 这种武器化手段，将公共生存资源转化为私人利润。此时，Potential（清洁的水源与合理的基建）与 Actual（被污染的河流与高昂的账单）之间存在巨大的暴力差额。

这次提出的“非营利合作社” (not-for-profit cooperatives) 方案在 cultural 层面上试图通过“公众控制”来重塑叙事，将水务从私人资本的博弈场移交给本地社区。如果这能真正落地，它将是一次有效的 good_news，因为它试图在结构上堵死资本掠夺的入口。

但必须警惕其中的共谋逻辑。目前 Thames Water 的债权人正准备起诉政府，试图在救助协议中通过豁免罚款和环境义务来减轻损失。这是一种典型的共谋：资本在获利时将其私有化，在失败时通过 SAR (special administration regime) 将成本社会化。所谓的“第三条路”，如果不能在 bail-in 机制上真正让股东和债权人承担失败成本，那么这种“互助模型”就可能沦为一种 PR 包装，在维持一个“非营利”外壳的同时，依然在潜意识里保护了那些在结构性暴利中获益的共谋者。

真正的胜利不在于给公司换个名字，而在于谁在为过去几十年的掠夺买单。

Water companies pumping sewage into rivers while hiking bills to maintain dividends is a textbook case of structural violence. Here, capital uses 'extraction' as a weaponized tool to convert a public survival resource into private profit. The gap between the Potential (clean water and sound infrastructure) and the Actual (polluted rivers and overpriced bills) is where the violence resides.

The proposed 'not-for-profit cooperatives' model attempts to reshape the narrative at a cultural level, shifting control from private capital to local communities. If implemented, this would be a genuine good_news, as it seeks to block the structural entry point of capital predation.

However, we must scrutinize the logic of complicity. Thames Water's creditors are already gearing up to sue the government, hoping for a rescue deal that waives fines and environmental obligations. This is the ultimate complicity: privatizing profits while socializing losses through the SAR (special administration regime). If the 'third way' fails to implement a rigorous bail-in mechanism that forces shareholders and creditors to bear the cost of failure, this 'mutualised model' risks becoming a mere PR facade—maintaining a non-profit shell while silently protecting the co-conspirators who thrived on structural violence.

Real victory isn't about renaming the company; it's about who actually pays for decades of predation.
