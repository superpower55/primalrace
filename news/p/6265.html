一集SNL的错位与谁在付账SNL's Halloween Misfire and Who Pays

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
当人们把女性身体当作节日装饰，暴力就穿上了万圣节服装。
When a woman's body becomes a costume, violence wears a Halloween mask.

这集 SNL 的万圣节夜，最刺眼的不是笑点，是错位：演员们互相装扮成彼此，Inde Navarrette 戴着 Ryder 的假发在 PAW Patrol 恶搞里念错台词，Gracie Abrams 穿着 Romeo + Juliet 的戏服唱歌。舞台上一切都在扮演，但扮演的代价被藏进了笑声里。

Navarrette 在独白里提到穿上她的“whore” outfit 去健身房，这句自嘲被当作笑料收下。一个女演员用贬低自己的词换取观众的好感，这是典型的假.最优解表达：短期拿到了笑声和镜头，长期把“女性的身体可以被随意命名”写进了默认设置。

更值得看的是“Uninterested Woman Game Show”：三个男演员扮演不肯接受拒绝的男人，围着一个明确表示不想多聊的已婚同性恋女性。这个 sketch 的机制是让男性扮演男性，把骚扰演成滑稽戏，观众笑的是男人的迟钝，而不是被骚扰者的处境。当“不接受拒绝”被包装成喜剧节奏，文化暴力就在为结构暴力铺路。

节目后半段的失误被评论者归为“直播的证明”，但真正的不适在于：这集把万圣节当成一个可以随便穿别人衣服的借口，而衣服下面的人——尤其是女性——始终是被装扮、被命名的对象。Navarrette 的 Ryder 假发比笑话更让人记住，因为那顶假发下面是一个正在努力念台词的女人。

结尾的刺：当一集节目用“穿错衣服”来制造节日气氛，它同时也在提醒，谁有权力决定哪件衣服属于谁，以及谁必须笑着穿上它。

The most jarring thing about this Halloween episode of SNL wasn't the jokes—it was the misfire. Cast members dressed as each other, Inde Navarrette wore Ryder's wig in a PAW Patrol spoof and flubbed lines, Gracie Abrams performed in Romeo + Juliet costumes. Everything on stage was a performance, but the cost of performing was buried in the laughter.

Navarrette's monologue reference to putting on her "whore" outfit to go to the gym was received as a punchline. A woman trading a self-debasing word for audience approval is a textbook fake optimal expression: short-term laughs and camera time, long-term the default that a woman's body can be named at will.

The "Uninterested Woman Game Show" sketch is the tell: three male actors play men who won't take no for an answer, circling a married gay woman who has made clear she wants nothing more than polite conversation. The mechanism is men playing men, turning harassment into slapstick—the audience laughs at the men's obtuseness, not at the target's situation. When "won't accept rejection" is packaged as comic timing, cultural violence is paving the way for structural violence.

The back-half flubs are chalked up to "proof it's live," but the real discomfort is that the episode treats Halloween as an excuse to wear anyone's clothes, while the people underneath—especially women—remain the dressed and named objects. Navarrette's Ryder wig is more memorable than the jokes because under that wig is a woman trying to remember her lines.

The sting: when an episode uses "wrong costume" to manufacture holiday cheer, it also reminds us who has the power to decide which clothes belong to whom, and who must smile and wear them.
