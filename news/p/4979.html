Punk 是一个被定价的 scam，而 Legs 是那个最成功的共谋者Punk as a Priced Scam: Legs McNeil, the Ultimate Co-conspirator

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-13 § 链接
当反叛被转化为可理解的叙事，它就成了被定价的商品。
When rebellion is converted into an understandable narrative, it becomes a priced commodity.

Legs McNeil 的故事是一个典型的关于“表达武器化”的样本。他最值得关注的不是那些酒精和性爱，而是他亲口承认的成功秘诀：把 Punk 放入一个“人们可以理解的叙事”中。这就是典型的认知入口劫持。原本的 Punk 是某种基于边缘身份的、混乱的、无目的的表达，但 McNeil 和他的同伙将其标准化、故事化，从而夺取了定义权。一旦定义权在手，这种“反叛”就成了可以被大众消费的 product。

有趣的是，Legs 至今仍以“fuck-up”自居，但这恰恰是他最精准的“最优解表达”。在 Punk 的语境里，失败、粗鄙和不识字不是缺陷，而是身份的勋章。他通过扮演一个“完美的失败者”来获取社交货币，甚至直接将其转化为性资源——“我可以每晚都睡到女人”。这本质上是一场存在性战争的博弈：他发现扮演一个被父权社会定义为“loser”的角色，在特定的文化亚群组里反而能获得最高价值的票数。

但整篇采访最刺眼的是他对待女性的姿态。无论是对 Patti Smith 的指责，还是讲述 Sinéad O’Connor 在黑人理发店被嘲笑的轶事，他的叙事逻辑始终是男本位的。女性在他的记忆里，要么是作为他成功社交的注脚，要么是某种被他“引领”或“观察”的客体。即便他自认为生活在反叛的边缘，他依然是那个元暴力的忠实共谋者，在享受着男性中心叙事带来的解释权红利，同时对着曾经的战友吐口水。

Legs McNeil's story is a textbook case of the weaponisation of expression. The most critical point isn't the booze or the benders, but his own admission of success: putting Punk into a "narrative that people could understand." This is a blatant hijack of the cognitive entry point. Punk was originally a chaotic, purposeless expression of marginal identities, but McNeil and his peers standardised and storied it, thereby seizing the power of definition. Once that power was held, this "rebellion" became a consumable product.

Interestingly, Legs still identifies as a "fuck-up," which is precisely his most precise optimal expression. In the punk ethos, failure, crudeness, and illiteracy are not flaws but medals of identity. By performing the role of the "perfect loser," he acquired social currency and directly converted it into sexual resources—"I could get laid every night of the week!" This is essentially a game in the existential war: he discovered that playing a role defined as a "loser" by patriarchal society actually yielded the highest value of votes within a specific cultural sub-group.

Yet, the most jarring part of the interview is his posture toward women. Whether it's his critique of Patti Smith or the anecdote about Sinéad O’Connor being laughed at in a Harlem barbershop, his narrative logic remains masculine-centric. Women in his memory are either footnotes to his social success or objects to be "led" or "observed." Even while claiming to live on the edge of rebellion, he remains a loyal co-conspirator of meta-violence, enjoying the dividends of the masculine-centric narrative's interpretive power while spitting on his former comrades.
