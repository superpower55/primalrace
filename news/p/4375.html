用 Hyperpop 填补虚无：一场关于 anomie 的快餐式自救Filling the Void with Hyperpop: A Fast-Food Salvation for Anomie

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
当存在性战争被简化为一场 Comedy Rave，主体性成了被消费的演出道具。
When existential war is reduced to a Comedy Rave, subjectivity becomes a prop for consumption.

这场名为《Fully Loaded》的演出，本质上是一次典型的 Gen Z 式“表达博弈”。它把 existential angst（存在性焦虑）和 anomie（社会失范）直接快餐化，用 Hyperpop 的高频攻击和 queer(ish) 的标签，将深刻的虚无感包装成一种可以被观众消费的“酷”表达。这种表达方式在第一章定义的“表达 = 身份确立”中，其实是一种假.最优解：通过扮演一个“觉醒且虚无”的角色来获取同辈群体的认同，但这并非是对结构的挑战，而是在结构内部的一次快感交换。

剧本里提到的“建立巨型教会来填补精神深渊”，是对第三章中“宗教武器化”的一种轻量级反讽。但这种反讽是无力的，因为它并没有试图拆穿元暴力的运作机制，而只是在用一种“傻掉的” (silliness) 方式在权力边缘试探。最有趣的是文中提到的“直女作家对男同性爱之 fetishization”，这精准地捕捉到了某种权力不对等的共谋 (complicity)——将他者的身体和欲望客体化，作为一种审美或文学的养分，这本身就是一种微型的结构性暴力。

当演出将“生活是个洞，你得把它填满”作为核心叙事时，它实际上在潜意识里认同了消费主义的逻辑：缺失需要被填充，而填充的方式是更强烈的刺激。这不是在缩小 Potential 与 Actual 之间的暴力差额，而是在用一种高能量的文化噪音掩盖那个差额。这场 Rave 带来的快感是暂时的，而当灯光熄灭，那些被定义为“难民”的表演者和观众，依然被困在同一个由元暴力定义的、空洞的现实之中。

This show, 'Fully Loaded', is a classic Gen Z 'Expression Game'. It commodifies existential angst and anomie, using Hyperpop attacks and a 'queer(ish)' label to package profound void into a 'cool' expression for peer consumption. In terms of the first chapter's 'Expression = Identity', this is a fake optimal expression: gaining validation by playing the role of the 'awakened and nihilistic' subject, which is not a challenge to the structure, but a pleasure exchange within it.

The sketch about 'forming a megachurch to fill the spiritual chasm' is a lightweight satire of the weaponization of religion discussed in chapter three. However, this satire is toothless; it doesn't aim to dismantle the mechanism of meta-violence, but merely probes the edges of power through 'silliness'. The most telling part is the 'straight female writers fetishizing gay male sex'—it accurately captures a complicity of power imbalance, where the bodies and desires of others are objectified as aesthetic or literary nutrients, a micro-form of structural violence.

By framing the narrative as 'Life is a hole / You gotta fill it up', the show subconsciously aligns with consumerist logic: deficiency must be filled, and the method is stronger stimulation. This doesn't shrink the violence gap between Potential and Actual; it merely masks it with high-energy cultural noise. The pleasure of this Rave is transient, and when the lights go out, both the performers—styled as 'refugees'—and the audience remain trapped in the same void defined by meta-violence.
