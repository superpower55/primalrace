他不谈中期选举，因为选票不是他的工具He Won't Talk About the Midterms Because Votes Aren't His Tool

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要曾大力支持特朗普的右翼福音派领袖兰斯·沃尔瑙，在本月达拉斯-沃斯堡一场付费聚会上三度发言，却一次未提中期选举。他转而谈论萨尔瓦多、波兰、寻找查理·柯克的接班人以及饮食等话题，并在一场网络研讨会上称支持者「尽到了责任」，应把注意力放回自身。
The newsLance Wallnau, an influential rightwing evangelical backer of Donald Trump, spoke at three sessions at a paid Dallas-Fort Worth gathering this month and never mentioned the midterms. He instead discussed El Salvador, Poland, a successor to Charlie Kirk and even diet, and later told supporters they had "done your job" and should focus on themselves.
宗教动员的终点从来不是选票，是制度本身。
Religious mobilization doesn't end at the ballot. It ends inside the institutions.

Wallnau 这次不谈中期选举，不是退缩。中期选举对他没用。

报道说得很清楚：在达拉斯-沃斯堡那场给付费"伙伴"开的"As One"周末活动上，他讲了三场，讲萨尔瓦多的监狱、讲学区委员会、讲运动的未来，唯独一次没提三周后的选举。等到选前不到两周的网络研讨会上，他干脆把话挑明了："你们已经尽到责任了，良心是清的，中期选举出来什么就是什么，等发生了再说。"然后他建议听众关注自己——也许是生活方式，也许是饮食。

一个把特朗普从 2015 年就开始捧的人，在选前让支持者去管饮食。这不是失势，这是换战场。

他真正在讲的东西，报道里都有：萨尔瓦多的 Bukele 是"通过预言上任的总统"，在建一个"羊的国度"；匈牙利、斯洛伐克、波兰值得称道；德州 Southlake 的教会成员拿下了学区委员会，赶走了"进步马克思主义自由主义者和恶魔意识形态"；对附近 Irving 的清真寺不满的人，应该"去把市议会选掉"，他还建议运动"渗透伊斯兰"。

选票只是入口，学区委员会、市议会、监狱制度才是他要的东西。这就是表达的武器化最标准的形态：认知入口先拿下，然后一级一级往下走，走到制度里，走到谁有权力决定孩子在学校读什么。他 2013 年合写的那本书名字就叫《Invading Babylon: The 7 Mountain Mandate》，要基督徒接管社会的七个领域，从政府、媒体到教育、商业。

所以中期选举对他只是手段之一，而且是回报率正在下降的那种手段。报道里写得很直白：他承认自己的受众是"老龄化的人口结构"，房间里没几个四十岁以下的人；他也承认 Maga 运动在年轻人那里拉不动。一个七十岁的人，在选前把精力从当下撤回来，转向"三十六个月之后，很多事会变"。三十六个月，是下一个总统周期。

Kirk 死后他说"这让我不安"，然后说"我得找到那个人，这是我的工作"，又说自己更像撒母耳而不是大卫——撒母耳是给以色列国王膏油的那位。这话不是哀悼，是自荐。他要做的是给运动找下一个受膏者，而他自己站在膏油的那一边。

还有 Ziklag。报道提到这个德州的神秘团体，入会净资产门槛两千五百万美元，2024 年大选里它把行动分成三块：Checkmate 资助所谓选举诚信组织，Steeplechase 动员教会，Watchtower 炒作性身份和父母权利的紧张。2023 年泄露的会员视频里，Wallnau 自己说："你几乎不愿意把它讲得这么清楚，因为如果别人拿到这个，他们会疯掉。"

这句话是全篇最诚实的一句。他知道自己在做什么，也知道说清楚会怎样。所以他对记者说"他们跟踪我"，需要隐私——研究他的人说，这其实是在炫耀。

这里没有受害者叙事可以拆，也没有人在这场活动里被直接伤害。但结构层和文化层同时在动：一个净资产门槛两千五百万的团体，用教会网络和学区选举把神权政治装进地方治理的管道，而公共讨论还在问"他为什么不谈中期选举"。

他谈的比中期选举远得多。中期选举只是一次计票，他要的是计票之后谁在教室里、谁在市议会里、谁在决定什么算"恶魔意识形态"。

报道没写的是：那些被"渗透"和被"选掉"的人，在这场周末活动里一个字都没有。他们不在房间里，也不在邀请名单上。

Wallnau isn't dodging the midterms. The midterms are useless to him.

The reporting is plain: at the "As One" weekend for paying "partners" in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, he spoke across three sessions about El Salvador's prisons, school boards, the movement's future, and did not mention the election once, three weeks out. By the webinar less than two weeks before election day, he said it outright: you've done your job, your conscience is clear, whatever comes out of the midterms is going to be, we'll sort that out when it happens. Then he told followers to focus on themselves. Maybe lifestyle. Maybe diet.

A man who started boosting Trump in 2015, telling supporters to manage their diets before an election. This isn't decline. It's a change of battlefield.

What he actually talked about is all in the piece: Bukele in El Salvador is "a president through prophecy" building a "sheep nation"; Hungary, Slovakia and Poland are worth praising; churchgoers in Southlake, Texas took over the school board and kicked out "progressive Marxist liberals and demonic ideologies"; people upset about a mosque in nearby Irving should "go vote out this city council," and the movement should "infiltrate Islam."

Votes are an entrance, not the destination. School boards, city councils, prison systems are the destination. This is the weaponization of expression in its standard form: take the cognitive entrance first, then work downward, level by level, into the institutions, into who decides what children read at school. The book he co-wrote in 2013 is called Invading Babylon: The 7 Mountain Mandate, urging Christians to take control of seven spheres of society, from government and media to education and business.

So the midterms are one instrument among many, and the returns are falling. The piece says it directly: he admits his following is "an ageing demographic," few in the room under forty; he concedes Maga is struggling to appeal to young people. A seventy-year-old pulling back from current events before an election, toward "thirty-six months from now, a lot can change." Thirty-six months is the next presidential cycle.

On Kirk's death he says it bothers him, then says he has to find that guy, that's his job, and that he feels more like a Samuel than a David. Samuel is the one who anoints Israel's kings. That isn't mourning. That's a job application, with himself on the oil side of the ceremony.

Then there's Ziklag. The piece describes a secretive Texas group with a $25m minimum net worth for membership, which in 2024 split its operations into three: Checkmate funding so-called electoral integrity groups, Steeplechase mobilizing churches, Watchtower stoking tensions over sexual identity and parental rights. In leaked members-only videos from 2023, Wallnau himself says: "You almost hate to put it out this clearly, because if somebody else gets ahold of this, they'll freak out."

That is the most honest line in the whole piece. He knows what he's doing, and he knows what saying it plainly would cost. So he tells journalists they stalk him, that he needs privacy. The researcher who has followed him for years says that's bragging.

There's no victim narrative to dismantle here, no one directly harmed inside that weekend. But the structural and cultural layers are moving at once: a group with a $25m entry threshold routing church networks and school-board elections into a pipeline for theocratic local governance, while public discussion is still asking why he won't talk about the midterms.

He is talking about something much further out than the midterms. The midterms are a count. He wants who is in the classroom afterward, who is on the city council, who gets to decide what counts as a demonic ideology.

What the piece doesn't say: the people being "infiltrated" and "voted out" don't appear in that weekend at all. They aren't in the room. They aren't on the invitation list.
