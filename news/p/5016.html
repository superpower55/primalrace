身体的定价权与被窃取的叙事The Pricing of Bodies and the Hijacked Narrative

性别 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-14 § 链接
性化女性身体来兜售体育，是典型的用文化暴力抹除结构事实。
Sexualizing the female body to sell sports is a textbook use of cultural violence to erase structural facts.

这则广告是一个教科书级别的 scam。Novig 这家公司极其精准地捕捉到了一个认知入口：将体育这种关于力量、纪律和血汗的领域，强行通过 Sydney Sweeney 的裸体进行“视觉降级”。在广告商的逻辑里，女性运动员的身体只是一个背景板，而真正的“吸引力”来自于一个符合男性凝视的、被客体化的身体。这就是典型的 weaponized expression——用性快感覆盖掉对竞技体育主体性的认知。

Amy Hunt 等运动员的愤怒并非出于某种道德洁癖，而是一场关于“表达”的存在性战争。当她们展示肌肉、汗水和月经带来的不便时，她们是在试图确立一种基于生物墙和真实经历的身份认同。而广告商则在进行一次极其恶劣的共谋：利用女性身体作为诱饵，吸引男性用户为赌博买单，同时把“体育”这个词定义为一种视觉消费品。这不仅是 sexualization，更是对女性在体育领域通过 hard work 赢回来的主体性的一次结构性掠夺。

最讽刺的是 Sydney Sweeney 的回应。她通过列举其他女性运动员的裸照来试图证明“这很正常”。这是一种极其低级的逻辑陷阱：不能因为某些女性在特定语境下选择了展示身体，就证明将性化身体作为体育商业入口是正当的。这种回应本质上是在掩盖一个事实——她扮演的是那个被父权制定价的“最优解角色”，而运动员们在对抗的是那个试图定义所有女性身体的元暴力。

Novig 的 CEO 毫不掩饰地承认这种争议能带来 reach，这证明了在男性中心叙事中，女性的愤怒和身体的裸露都被量化成了流量指标。在这个博弈中，运动员们的反击是一次有效的文化层反攻，但只要“性依然能卖钱”这个共谋逻辑不崩塌，这种对女性身体的殖民就永远不会停止。

This advert is a textbook scam. Novig precisely captured a cognitive entry point: forcibly 'downgrading' sports—a realm of power, discipline, and sweat—through the nudity of Sydney Sweeney. In the advertiser's logic, the bodies of female athletes are mere backdrops; the real 'attraction' is a sexualized body crafted for the masculine gaze. This is weaponized expression—using sexual pleasure to overwrite the recognition of agency in athletics.

The fury of athletes like Amy Hunt is not about moral purity, but an existential war over expression. By showcasing muscles, sweat, and the discomfort of menstruation, they are attempting to establish an identity based on the biological wall and lived experience. Meanwhile, the advertiser engages in a vile complicity: using a woman's body as bait to lure men into gambling, while redefining 'sports' as a visual commodity. This is not just sexualization; it is a structural plunder of the agency female athletes have fought to win through hard work.

Sydney Sweeney's response is the most ironic part. By listing nude photos of other female athletes, she tries to normalize the act. This is a low-level logical trap: the fact that some women choose to show their bodies in specific contexts does not justify using the sexualized body as a commercial entry point for sports. Her response merely masks the truth—she is playing the 'optimal role' priced by the patriarchy, while the athletes are fighting the meta-violence that seeks to define all female bodies.

Novig's CEO candidly admits that controversy brings reach, proving that in the masculine-centric narrative, both female rage and female nudity are quantified as traffic metrics. In this game, the athletes' backlash is an effective counter-attack at the cultural layer, but as long as the complicity of 'sex sells' persists, this colonization of the female body will never end.
