所谓的“治愈”，不过是对阶级地狱的审美化消费The 'Feelgood' Scam: Aestheticizing the Class Hell

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-06 § 链接
当底层挣扎被包装成“Cult经典”，审美就成了掩盖结构暴力的麻醉剂。
When bottom-tier struggle is rebranded as a 'Cult Classic,' aesthetics become an anesthetic for structural violence.

这篇文章在谈论一部 80 年代的音乐剧电影，用词是 feelgood, miracle, exceptionally crafted。作者试图在赞美这部电影对资本主义的“智能批判”的同时，将其定义为一种可以让人安心入睡的“治愈”体验。这本身就是一个极其典型的 cultural violence 样本：将 Skid Row（贫民窟）的绝望、阶级斗争和血腥的生存博弈，通过 Motown 的节奏和木偶戏的奇观，转化为中产阶级客厅里的审美快感。

电影里的 Seymour 为了获得 Audrey 的爱和社会的认可，不惜喂养一个吃人的怪物。这在叙事上被处理成一个 B 级片的黑色幽默，但在结构层 (structural layer) 来看，这就是一个关于“最优解表达”的残酷寓言：在资源极度匮乏的底层，个体为了在存在性战争中获得一张入场券，唯一的路径就是与更庞大的暴力机器共谋，哪怕代价是出卖同类。作者赞美其中的“苏联宣传画风格”镜头，却在结尾将其归类为 feelgood movie，这种认知割裂恰恰证明了元暴力 (meta violence) 的强大——我们被允许在屏幕前欣赏“阶级斗争”的艺术形式，只要这种欣赏不会转化为真实的、对现状的愤怒。

最讽刺的是作者提到的“才华与个性统治的时代”。所谓的才华，其实是掌握了认知入口 (cognitive entrance) 的人们，将底层的苦难进行“武器化”包装，使其符合主流审美的能力。当我们将这部电影视为“治愈”时，我们实际上是在共谋一场巨大的 scam：我们通过消费底层的绝望来获得一种虚假的、带有道德优越感的心理慰藉。真正的 feelgood 不应该是看着一个吃人的植物在贫民窟生长，而应该是让 Skid Row 这种地方在现实中消失。

This piece discusses an 80s musical with terms like feelgood, miracle, and exceptionally crafted. The author attempts to praise the film's 'intelligent critique' of capitalism while defining it as a comforting experience that lets one fall asleep in peace. This is a textbook sample of cultural violence: transforming the despair of Skid Row, class struggle, and bloody existential gambles into an aesthetic pleasure for the middle-class living room through Motown rhythms and puppetry spectacles.

In the film, Seymour feeds a man-eating plant to gain Audrey's love and social recognition. While framed as B-movie black humor, on a structural layer, it is a brutal allegory of 'optimal expression': in the resource-starved bottom tier, the only path to a ticket in the existential war is complicity with a larger machine of violence, even if the price is betraying one's own kind. The author admires the 'Soviet propaganda' framing but concludes it's a feelgood movie; this cognitive dissonance proves the power of meta violence—we are permitted to appreciate the 'art' of class struggle as long as that appreciation doesn't translate into actual anger toward the status quo.

Most ironic is the mention of an era where 'talent and individuality ruled.' This 'talent' is simply the ability of those controlling the cognitive entrance to weaponize the suffering of the marginalized, packaging it to fit mainstream tastes. When we call this movie 'feelgood,' we are complicit in a massive scam: consuming the despair of the bottom tier to achieve a fake, morally superior psychological comfort. True feelgood is not watching a carnivorous plant thrive in a slum, but making sure places like Skid Row cease to exist in reality.
