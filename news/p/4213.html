特鲁普的“饼干罐”与共谋者的沉默Trump's Cookie Jar and the Silence of Complicitors

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
腐败不是个案，而是权力在缺乏监督时追求的真.最优解表达。
Corruption is not an anomaly, but the true optimal expression of power in a vacuum of oversight.

特朗普在白宫大肆敛财的行为，在很多自由派看来是“惊人的腐败”，但在博弈论视角下，这恰恰是他作为权力持有者的真.最优解表达：在法律边界模糊、监督机制失效的结构性空隙中，将公共权力直接转化为私人资本，这是收益最大化且成本最低的路径。

真正令人心惊的不是特朗普的贪婪——贪婪是生物本能，而真正的问题在于 structural violence 的共谋。国会选择放弃监督，司法部门被武器化，这种沉默构成了典型的共谋者理论（complicity）。当权力制定者发现“只要不触碰红线，就可以合法地掠夺”时，所谓的道德边界就成了掩体。这里的元暴力（meta violence）在于一种男性中心叙事的极致延伸：权力即掠夺，而能够毫无禁忌地掠夺且不被惩罚，被视为一种“强者的胜利”。

最阴险的武器化叙事（weaponized narrative）是特朗普对“主流媒体”的解构。通过将所有调查定义为“fake news”，他成功地在认知入口处建立了一道防火墙。当社会失去了对“事实”的共识，即便最惊人的丑闻也无法“移动指针”（move the needle）。这不仅是信息的战争，更是对真实（truth）的定义权的争夺。当解释权被垄断，腐败就变成了“建设美国经济”的某种怪诞逻辑。

人们期待中期选举能带来制衡，但这依然是在既定规则内的博弈。如果整个系统的底层逻辑依然是允许权力持有者通过扮演“强者”来定义正义，那么换一个人坐在那个位置上，依然会面对同一个饼干罐。这场存在性战争的赢家，永远是那些敢于定义规则并敢于打破规则的人。

Donald Trump's brazen enrichment within the White House is viewed by liberals as 'astonishing corruption.' However, from a game-theory perspective, this is his true optimal expression: in the structural gaps where legal boundaries are blurred and oversight is absent, converting public power into private capital is the path of maximum gain and minimum cost.

What is truly chilling is not Trump's greed—greed is a biological instinct—but the complicity within the structural violence. A Congress that chooses not to oversee and a weaponized justice department are textbook examples of complicity. When power holders realize they can plunder legally as long as they avoid a few red lines, moral boundaries become mere covers. The meta violence here is the extreme extension of a masculine-centric narrative: power is predation, and the ability to plunder without restraint or punishment is celebrated as the 'victory of the strong.'

The most insidious weaponized narrative is Trump's deconstruction of 'legacy media.' By branding all investigations as 'fake news,' he has built a firewall at the cognitive entry point. When a society loses a shared set of facts, even the most dramatic scoops fail to 'move the needle.' This is not just an information war; it is a struggle for the power to define truth. Once the power of interpretation is monopolized, corruption is rebranded as some grotesque logic of 'building the US economy.'

People hope the midterms will bring restraint, but this is still gambling within a rigged system. If the underlying logic of the system continues to reward those who play the 'strongman' to define justice, whoever sits in that chair will still find the same cookie jar. In this existential war, the winners are always those who dare to define the rules—and those who dare to break them.
