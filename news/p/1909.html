四天工作制不缺技术，缺的是老板点头The Four-Day Week Isn't Waiting on AI. It's Waiting on the Boss.

科技 结构层 · 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-07-07 § 链接
技术每抬高一次潜能，老板就把差额收走一次；AI只是下一次。
Every time technology raises the potential, the boss pockets the difference. AI is just the next round.

四天工作制不是一个等 AI 来兑现的预言，是一个十年前就已经被验证、然后被按住不放的事实。2015 年冰岛的试验，生产率持平或更高，员工满意度飙升，之后推广到全国；2022 年英国 61 家公司、近 3000 名员工的研究，营收上升，压力和倦怠骤降（来源：The New York Times）。Potential 早就在那里，Actual 停在五天。这个差额按加尔通的公式就叫暴力，不叫"技术尚未成熟"。

所以 Cohen 说四天、Yuan 说三天、Gates 说十年内两天、Musk 说工作会变成打球打游戏一样的爱好，这些话的功能不是预测，是延期。把一件今天就能签字的事挂到一个永远在前方的技术奇点上，差额就从"我的决定"变成了"时代的进程"。这是最省力的武器化叙事：不需要否认四天工作制可行，只需要把它的实现日期交给一个你无法催促的东西。

而同一批人的实际行为把这层包装撕得干干净净。Musk 要求办公室"至少"40 小时，并说真正有影响力得 80 小时以上；黄仁勋一边预测四天工作制一边宣布自己七天都在干；Dimon 预言 3.5 天，同时坚持员工五天全勤坐在办公室里，对远程办公骂声最大（来源：The New York Times）。face time 跟产出没关系，跟服从有关系。你出现在那栋楼里，是在表达你的存在归谁所有，而他们对这种表达的迷恋，是任何算力都替代不了的。

文章作者八十年代在《华尔街日报》实习时用的是手动打字机和复写纸，改错要剪刀加胶水。按理说数字工具应该让工时暴跌，结果反而暴涨，因为技术创造了全天候出版的可能，可能性立刻变成了要求。这就是机制本身：每一轮技术都把 Potential 抬高，而在现有的所有权结构下，抬高的那部分一分不留地流向定价的人，Actual 纹丝不动。差额没有缩小，是被人收走了。AI 不会改变这个流向，它只是让流量更大。

所以 AI 到来之后最可能发生的，不是你的周五空出来，而是你的周一到周五被重新定义成"算力辅助下的 80 小时才算真正有影响力"。那个被拿去做 PPT 的四天工作制，会继续以预言的形式存在，因为预言比承诺好用：承诺要兑现，预言只需要一直往后推。

The four-day workweek is not a prophecy waiting for AI to fulfil. It is a fact that was proven a decade ago and then sat on. Iceland's 2015 trial: productivity flat or better, satisfaction soared, later expanded nationwide. Britain's 2022 study of 61 companies and nearly 3,000 employees: revenue up, stress and burnout plunged (source: The New York Times). The Potential has been sitting there for years. The Actual is still five days. By Galtung's formula that gap is called violence, not "immature technology."

So when Cohen says four days, Yuan says three, Gates says two within a decade, and Musk says work will become a hobby like sports or video games, the function of those sentences is not forecasting. It is deferral. Hang something that could be signed today on a technological singularity that is always just ahead, and the gap stops being "my decision" and becomes "the march of history." This is the laziest form of weaponised narrative: you never have to deny that the four-day week works, you only have to hand its delivery date to something nobody can hurry.

And the same men's actual behaviour strips the wrapping clean off. Musk demands a "minimum" of 40 hours in the office and says real impact takes 80 or more. Jensen Huang forecasts a four-day week while announcing he works seven. Dimon predicts 3.5 days and insists on five full days in the building, the loudest critic of remote work in the room (source: The New York Times). Face time has nothing to do with output and everything to do with submission. Showing up in that building is an expression of whom your existence belongs to, and their attachment to that expression is something no amount of compute will replace.

The author interned at The Wall Street Journal in the early 1980s on manual typewriters and carbon paper, fixing mistakes with scissors and glue. Digital tools should have collapsed the hours. Instead they exploded, because the technology made round-the-clock publishing possible, and possibility became requirement overnight. That is the mechanism in one sentence: every round of technology raises the Potential, and under the existing ownership structure the raised portion flows entirely to whoever sets the price, while the Actual does not move. The gap was never narrowed. It was collected. AI will not reverse that flow. It will just widen the pipe.

So the most likely outcome of AI is not that your Friday opens up, but that Monday through Friday gets redefined as "80 compute-assisted hours is what real impact looks like now." The four-day week will go on living as a slide in someone's deck, because a prophecy is more useful than a promise: a promise has to be kept, a prophecy only has to keep being pushed back.
