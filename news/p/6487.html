没有孩子，就没有人替你签字No Children Means No One to Sign for You

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
他把照护危机说成个人规划问题，这就是共谋。
Framing a care crisis as a personal planning problem is complicity.

一对七十多岁的夫妻，结婚四十多年，没有孩子，失败的 IVF 之后留下创伤。他们现在要解决的问题是：万一有一天需要人替自己签字、替自己管钱，找谁。专栏作者很诚实，说自己也没法变出一个人来，然后把问题交给了一位心理治疗师和一位遗产规划律师。

这篇问答从头到尾没有出现过一个词：国家。社会照护滑向危机，这句话是提问者自己写的，也是全文唯一一句指向结构的话。回答把它接了过去，转成了 LPA 怎么设、可以设几个、要不要请律师、Age UK 有一本可以免费下载的 LifeBook。一个制度性的照护缺口，就这样被翻译成了一份待办清单。

这就是共谋的日常形态，不需要谁心怀恶意。专栏要交稿，律师要按专业规范回答，心理治疗师要处理情绪，每个人都在自己的位置上做对的事，合起来的效果是：照护这件事被确认为私事。你老了没人管，是因为你当年没生孩子、没经营好亲属网络、没提前做规划。加尔通那个公式在这里很好用：本可以达到的照护水平，和实际达到的之间那道差额，是结构暴力。差额被写成了个人失误。

更值得看的是这对夫妻的位置。妻子是亚裔，在英国没有亲属。这句话在信里只出现了一次，后面再没有被提起。一个没有本地家族网络的移民女性，在英国的照护体系里意味着什么，报道没写。谁的名字没有出现，往往比谁的名字出现更能说明问题。

回答里唯一有点分量的建议，是去连接处境相似的人，让焦虑不那么孤独。这话不难听，但它把集体困境处理成了情绪问题。处境相似的人聚在一起，能缓解孤独，不能变出签字权。真正能缩小那道差额的东西——公共照护预算、社区法定服务、无亲属者的公共监护人机制——一个字都没有。

律师那条建议里有一个细节值得记住：夫妻可以先互相指定为代理人，这会给其中一方超出配偶身份的“enhanced status”。也就是说，在没有子女的家庭里，婚姻被用来补上照护的缺口。婚姻在这里不是浪漫，是一份兜底的合同。而没有结婚、没有伴侣、也没有子女的人，连这份兜底都没有。

四十年婚姻，一次失败的 IVF，两个人把自己活成一个自足的单元。这个单元现在开始漏了，而他们得到的回答是：请你自己把漏洞规划好。

A couple in their late 70s, married more than 40 years, no children, still carrying the trauma of failed IVF. Their problem: when the time comes, who signs for them, who manages their money. The columnist is honest — she says she cannot magic up someone for them — and hands the question to a psychotherapist and an estate planning solicitor.

One word never appears in the entire exchange: the state. Social care slipping into crisis is the letter writer's own phrase, the only sentence in the piece that points at structure. The answer picks it up and converts it into a checklist: how to set up an LPA, how many attorneys you can appoint, whether to hire a solicitor, a free downloadable LifeBook from Age UK. A structural care gap gets translated into a to-do list.

This is what complicity looks like in its ordinary form. Nobody has to mean harm. The column has a deadline, the solicitor answers within professional norms, the therapist handles the feelings, and the combined effect is that care is confirmed as a private matter. You end up alone because you didn't have children, didn't cultivate a kin network, didn't plan ahead. Galtung's formula fits exactly: the gap between the care that could exist and the care that does is structural violence. The gap gets written up as personal failure.

Look closer at where these two people sit. The wife is Asian and has no family in the UK. That fact appears once in the letter and is never mentioned again. What a migrant woman without a local kin network means inside the British care system — the piece doesn't say. Whose name is missing often explains more than whose name is there.

The one suggestion with any weight is to connect with others in a similar position, so the anxiety feels less lonely. That isn't wrong, but it treats a collective predicament as a mood. People in the same position can ease each other's isolation; they cannot produce a signature. The things that would actually shrink the gap — public care budgets, statutory community services, a public guardian mechanism for people without relatives — go unmentioned.

One detail in the legal advice is worth keeping: the couple can initially appoint each other as attorneys, which grants an "enhanced status" beyond being a spouse. In a family without children, marriage is being used to plug the care gap. Marriage here is not romance; it is a fallback contract. And those with no spouse, no children, no partner have not even that.

Forty years of marriage, one failed IVF, two people who made themselves a self-contained unit. The unit is starting to leak, and the answer they get is: plan your leaks better.
