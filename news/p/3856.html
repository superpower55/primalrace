分数通胀与席位饥饿：一场被量化的存在性战争Grade Inflation and Seat Hunger: An Existential War of Quantified Merit

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
当评价标准被通胀，真实的资源分配便成了唯一的暴力。
When evaluation standards inflate, the actual distribution of resources becomes the only true violence.

这是一场典型的关于“票”的价值博弈。A-level 高分人数的激增，本质上是评价体系的通胀。当近三分之一的学生拿到 A 或 A*，这些原本代表“卓越”的符号就失去了原有的定价权。在系统的逻辑里，当每个人都持有“顶级入场券”时，这张票的实际价值就趋向于零。

但大学的物理席位——实验室、教室、导师的时间——是不可通胀的定量。这导致了 Potential（学生通过努力达到的高分状态）与 Actual（实际获得的录取席位）之间出现了巨大的差额。这个差额就是 structural violence。学生被要求 defer（延迟入学），或者在 clearing 中妥协，这不是因为他们不够优秀，而是因为系统在用一种“客观”的筛选机制，掩盖资源分配的匮乏。

最讽刺的是，像卡迪夫大学这样的机构，在用“详细建模”和“历史模式”来为自己的过载辩护。这是一种典型的 weaponized narrative：用技术性的词汇（modelling, historical patterns）将一次管理失败包装成不可抗力的统计学事件。而给学生提供 750 英镑的 bursary 来诱导其 defer，简直是一场极其廉价的共谋邀请——用极小的经济代价，换取学生在存在性战争中主动放弃一年的主体时间。

当 selective universities 的申请量增长 10% 而录取率仅增长 5% 时，这场战争已经变成了纯粹的存量博弈。所谓的“优秀”不再是通往机会的阶梯，而成了被系统用来进一步挤压个体的工具。

This is a classic game of the 'value of the vote.' The surge in top A-level grades is essentially an inflation of the evaluation system. When nearly a third of students hold A or A*, these symbols of 'excellence' lose their pricing power. In the system's logic, when everyone holds a 'top-tier ticket,' the actual value of that ticket trends toward zero.

However, university physical seats—labs, classrooms, professors' time—are a fixed quantitative. This creates a massive gap between the Potential (the high-grade status students achieved) and the Actual (the actual seats granted). This gap is structural violence. Students being forced to defer or settle in clearing isn't because they aren't 'good enough,' but because the system uses an 'objective' screening mechanism to mask the scarcity of resources.

The irony lies in institutions like Cardiff University defending their oversubscription with terms like 'detailed modelling' and 'historical patterns.' This is a weaponized narrative: using technical jargon to package a management failure as an inevitable statistical event. Offering a £750 bursary to induce students to defer is a cheap invitation to complicity—using a trivial economic incentive to convince students to surrender a year of their existential agency.

With selective universities seeing a 10% increase in applications but only a 5% increase in offers, this war has become a pure zero-sum game. So-called 'merit' is no longer a ladder to opportunity, but a tool used by the system to further squeeze the individual.
