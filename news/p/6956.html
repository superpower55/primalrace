省下的油钱不算赢Saving Fuel Isn't Winning

其他 结构层 · 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要受美国与伊朗开战影响，布伦特原油价格升至每桶116.72美元。嘉年华、皇家加勒比和挪威邮轮三家公司在最近一个季度均录得强劲业绩，但应对燃油成本的方式不同：皇家加勒比和挪威邮轮使用衍生品对冲，嘉年华则依靠降低油耗，称自2019年以来单位油耗下降26%，2026年因此节省约7亿美元。
The newsBrent crude hit $116.72 a barrel after the U.S.-Israel war with Iran began. Carnival, Royal Caribbean and Norwegian all posted strong quarterly results but handle fuel costs differently: Royal Caribbean and Norwegian use derivatives to hedge, while Carnival cuts consumption, saying unit fuel use is down 26% since 2019 and the savings are worth about $700 million in 2026.
对冲是给华尔街看的，省油才是给自己活的
Hedging is for Wall Street; burning less is for yourself

嘉年华说，控制燃油成本最好的办法就是少用油。这句话放在一家邮轮公司嘴里，朴素得几乎可疑——但它恰好是这篇报道里唯一一个没有把成本转嫁给别人的答案。

皇家加勒比和挪威邮轮走的是另一条路：对冲。用衍生品把油价波动的风险锁住。分析师说得很直白，对冲主要是为了管理华尔街的预期，防止股价跟着油价上蹿下跳。也就是说，这套操作服务的对象不是船上的乘客，也不是海上的空气，是估值倍数。

报道里有一句话值得多看一眼：分析师承认，长期来看对冲在数学上大概率更贵。更贵，但还是要做，因为它让模型里的波动更小，让交易更平静。这就是第三章那套东西的日常版本——一家公司花钱买的不是更便宜的燃料，是一个更好看的叙事。

嘉年华的数字是另一回事。单位油耗自2019年下降26%，2026年估计省下约7亿美元。这是真的把输入成本削掉了一块，不是把风险挪到未来的某张合约上。第三季度净利润19亿美元，历史新高。省油和赚钱在这里是同一件事，不需要额外包装。

但别急着鼓掌。嘉年华第三季度燃油花了6.15亿美元，占营收7.3%，比2025年第四季度的4.25亿、6.7%还高——因为油价涨得比省油快。省下来的7亿，是从一个还在膨胀的窟窿里抠出来的。而且这26%是怎么来的，报道没写：是换了更省油的船，是降了航速，是塞满了舱位摊薄了单位消耗，还是把某些航线砍了。省油的代价落在谁身上，这篇报道一个字没提。

真正被结构性藏起来的是另一笔账。油价上涨的源头是一场战争，战争的成本由中东的人付，由被通胀挤压的普通人付，邮轮公司只是在这条链条的末端调整了一下姿势。三家公司的CEO都在说消费者需求强劲、2027年预订量涨了两位数——翻译过来就是：无论油价多高，总有人愿意上船。

所以嘉年华的「少用」值得记一笔，但它是这家公司对自己成本结构的胜利，不是对任何人的公正。它省下的每一块钱，都没有变成更低的票价，也没有变成更干净的燃料。船还是烧着重油，海还是接着收。

韦恩斯坦说他们对省油「近乎偏执」。偏执是个好词。一家公司对一件事偏执，通常意味着它对另一件事不偏执——比如它烧的什么东西，排到哪里去。

Carnival says the best way to control fuel costs is to use less. From a cruise company, that sounds almost suspiciously plain — but it's the only answer in this story that doesn't push the cost onto someone else.

Royal Caribbean and Norwegian take the other road: hedging, using derivatives to lock down exposure to oil prices. The analyst says it plainly — hedging is mainly about managing Wall Street's expectations, keeping the stock from jumping around with fuel prices. The customer isn't the beneficiary here, and neither is the air. The valuation multiple is.

The same analyst admits that over the long term, hedging is most likely more expensive. More expensive, and still worth it, because it makes the model calmer and the trading quieter. This is Chapter 3 in its everyday form: a company isn't buying cheaper fuel, it's buying a better-looking narrative.

Carnival's numbers are a different thing. Unit fuel consumption down 26% since 2019, about $700 million saved in 2026. That's input cost actually removed, not risk shuffled onto a future contract. Third-quarter net income of $1.9 billion, an all-time high. Saving fuel and making money are the same act here; no packaging required.

Don't applaud too fast. Carnival spent $615 million on fuel in the third quarter, 7.3% of revenue — up from $425 million and 6.7% in the fourth quarter of 2025, because oil rose faster than the savings. The $700 million was scraped out of a hole that is still growing. And how the 26% was achieved, the report doesn't say: newer ships, slower speeds, fuller cabins diluting the per-unit figure, or routes quietly cut. Who paid for the savings goes unmentioned.

The bigger account stays structurally hidden. Oil rose because of a war; the war's cost is paid by people in the Middle East and by ordinary households under inflation. The cruise lines are just adjusting their posture at the end of that chain. All three CEOs talk about resilient demand and 2027 bookings up by a high-teens percentage — which translates to: whatever the price, someone will get on board.

So Carnival's 'use less' deserves a note, but it's a win over its own cost structure, not justice for anyone. None of those saved dollars became a cheaper ticket or a cleaner fuel. The ships still burn heavy oil, and the sea still takes it.

Weinstein says they are 'maniacal' about cutting fuel. Maniacal is the right word. A company that is maniacal about one thing is usually not maniacal about another — like what it burns, and where it goes.
