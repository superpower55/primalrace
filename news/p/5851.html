国防合同的抵消，是笔生意Offsets Are a Business Deal, Not Security

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把军费说成就业，是给暴力定价的第一步
Calling military spending jobs is the first step in pricing violence.

英国国防部的新规矩说得很直白：海外公司想拿下超过5000万英镑的国防合同，就得在英国花掉等值的钱——建厂、雇人、培训学徒、转移技术。国防大臣将其称为"支持英国"，并称过去那种只看成本和质量的做法是过时的"全球主义、自由市场"思路。

先把这笔账算清楚。一笔1亿英镑的合同，要求承包商在英国投入最多1亿英镑的"效益"。可这1亿英镑的效益不是凭空多出来的，它是合同价格的一部分，最终由英国纳税人支付。政府没有削减任何一笔军购，只是给这笔钱附加了一个回流条件。所谓"回流"，就是把同一笔钱数两遍，一遍算作国防开支，一遍算作就业成绩。

这是典型的structural层操作，但它的产出不是安全，而是叙事。BAE Systems的Scotstoun船厂是宣布这项政策的地点，也是这项政策最好的布景：国防大臣站在造船厂里谈学徒和工厂，画面本身就是论证。至于这些岗位是否真的新增、技术是否真的转移、合同到期后工厂是否还在，政策文件里写的是"将被考虑""可能达到"——考虑、可能，这两个词撑起了整条新闻。

真正值得追问的是：这项政策在保卫什么？它保卫的不是英国人的身体，而是英国国防工业在采购链条里的位置。把"买英国货"包装成"加强国家安全"，等于把一份产业补贴说成一份国防战略。波兰、挪威、荷兰也这么做，这不构成理由，只说明这套话术已经成了欧洲的通用货币。

那么谁在为这套话术付代价？报道里没有写。没有写这些抵消投资会不会挤占本来要投给民生项目的钱，没有写国防预算的总额是否因此上升，没有写那些拿不到合同的英国小供应商会怎样。报道写的是"英国工人、企业和学徒将受益"——用的是将来时，受益者有名有姓的，一个也没有。

Streeting说，每一英镑投进国防，都该用"它为英国做了什么"来衡量。这句话可以原样送回：每一英镑投进国防，都该问它本可以投到哪里去。Violence = Potential − Actual，这条政策缩小的差额，是国防工业的订单差额，不是任何一个具体的人的安全差额。

把军费换算成学徒岗位，是让战争看起来像就业政策的最省力办法。省力到没人需要问：这些岗位造出来的东西，最后会落在谁头上。

The UK Ministry of Defence has a new rule: foreign firms that win defence contracts over £50m must spend an equivalent amount in Britain — factories, jobs, apprenticeships, technology transfer. The defence secretary calls it backing Britain, and calls the old cost-and-quality approach an outdated "globalist, free market" idea.

Do the arithmetic. A £100m contract requires up to £100m of "benefits" delivered in the UK. That £100m does not appear from nowhere; it is part of the contract price, paid by British taxpayers. No purchase is cancelled. A condition is attached to the same money. "Return" here means counting the same pound twice — once as defence spending, once as jobs created.

This is a structural move, but its output is narrative, not security. BAE Systems' Scotstoun yard is the backdrop, and the backdrop is the argument: a defence secretary standing in a shipyard talking about apprentices. Whether the jobs are new, whether the technology actually transfers, whether the factory still exists when the contract ends — the policy paper says "will be considered" and "could reach." Considered and could: those two words carry the entire announcement.

What is actually being defended? Not British bodies, but the position of British defence industry inside the procurement chain. Selling "buy British" as "strengthen national security" turns an industrial subsidy into a defence strategy. Poland, Norway and the Netherlands do the same thing — that is not a justification, only evidence that the script has become standard European currency.

Who pays for the script? The report does not say. It does not say whether offset investment displaces money that would have gone to civilian programmes, whether the total defence budget rises, or what happens to the small British suppliers who lose the bids. It says British workers, businesses and apprentices "will benefit" — future tense, and not one beneficiary named.

Streeting says every pound invested in defence should be judged by what it does for Britain. Send it back: every pound invested in defence should be judged by where else it could have gone. Violence = Potential − Actual. The gap this policy closes is the order-book gap of the defence industry, not the safety gap of any actual person.

Converting military spending into apprenticeship places is the cheapest way to make war look like employment policy. So cheap that nobody has to ask what the things being built will eventually land on.
