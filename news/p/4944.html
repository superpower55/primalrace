The 'humiliation' of a superpower is the ultimate fuel for meta-violence.

Matthew Klimow 的回忆录提供了一个完美的样本，展示了男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）如何将具体的系统失效转化为一种情感上的“受辱感”（humiliation）。在他眼中，9/11 的恐怖不在于平民的死亡，而在于美国这台战争机器在那个瞬间的“无能”（impotent）。这种将国家安全等同于男性雄风的逻辑，正是元暴力的核心：它不关心受害者的痛苦，只关心掌控者的“面子”。

文中描述的混乱——无法联系上的国防部长、错误的飞行计划、重叠的指挥链——本应是结构性暴力（structural violence）的证据，证明了官僚主义对生命的漠视。但在这套叙事里，这些失败被包装成了“英雄在废墟中寻找答案”的戏剧冲突。Rumsfeld 冲向火海救人的“个人勇敢”掩盖了他在关键时刻失踪造成的运营灾难。这就是典型的武器化表达：用个体的 bravery 掩盖结构的 incompetence。

最讽刺的闭环在于，这种基于“受辱”而产生的情绪，迅速被转化为一场横跨二十年的“反恐战争”。从 2001 年五角大楼的烟雾到 2021 年喀布尔机场最后一名士兵登机，这不仅是一场军事行动，更是一次大规模的、由男性主导的存在性战争。他们试图通过在阿富汗和伊拉克的暴力输出，来修补那次被击碎的“雄性自尊”。

Klimow 在结尾处赞美美国的“韧性”和“梦想”，但这不过是文化暴力（cultural violence）的最后一道涂层。它将一场由误判和傲慢驱动的全球浩劫，美化成了一次关于“自由与民主”的周期性循环。在这种叙事中，被殖民者的血泪被彻底抹除，剩下的只有军官们在回顾往事时的淡淡忧伤。

Matthew Klimow’s memoir provides a perfect specimen of how the masculine-centric narrative transforms systemic failure into a sense of 'humiliation.' To him, the horror of 9/11 lay not in the civilian death toll, but in the 'impotence' of the American war machine. This logic, which equates national security with masculine potency, is the core of meta-violence: it ignores the suffering of the victims and focuses solely on the 'face' of the controllers.

The chaos described—an unreachable Secretary of Defense, erroneous flight plans, overlapping chains of command—should have been presented as evidence of structural violence, proving the bureaucracy's disregard for human life. Instead, these failures are packaged as dramatic conflicts of 'heroes seeking answers in the ruins.' Rumsfeld’s 'personal bravery' in rushing toward the fire serves as a narrative shield, masking the operational disaster of his disappearance during a national crisis. This is the weaponization of expression: using individual bravery to overwrite structural incompetence.

The most cynical loop is that this emotion, rooted in 'humiliation,' was rapidly converted into a twenty-year 'War on Terror.' From the smoke of the Pentagon in 2001 to the last soldier boarding a C-17 in Kabul in 2021, this was more than a military campaign; it was a massive existential war led by men. They sought to repair a shattered masculine ego through the violent export of power in Afghanistan and Iraq.

Klimow concludes by praising American 'resilience' and the 'American Dream,' which is merely the final coat of cultural violence. It beautifies a global catastrophe driven by miscalculation and hubris as a cyclical journey of 'liberty and freedom.' In this narrative, the blood and tears of the colonized are erased, leaving only the faint melancholy of officers reminiscing about the past.
