所谓的“型男短缺”：一场关于客体化定价权的闹剧The 'Hunk Shortage' Scam: A Farce of Objectification and Pricing Power

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-28 § 链接
男性拒绝被客体化，是因为他们习惯于作为主体去定义客体。
Men refuse objectification because they are conditioned to be the subjects who define the objects.

《卫报》这篇文章用一种近乎调侃的语气在讨论一个“谜团”：为什么 Mr England 选美赛没人报名？而同机构的 Miss England 却有数千人参与。组织者甚至得让 Miss England 的选手去海边“招募”男性，或者让女性提名自己的男友。这根本不是什么“短缺”，而是一次关于客体化 (objectification) 定价权的真实博弈。

在元暴力 (meta violence) 的逻辑下，男性中心叙事赋予了男性天然的“主体性”。他们习惯于作为凝视者、定义者和定价者。当一个比赛要求男性脱掉衣服，把自己变成一个被评分、被挑选的“肉块” (hunk) 时，这实际上是在要求他们放弃主体性，进入女性在父权结构中被强制扮演的“客体”角色。对于绝大多数男性来说，这种主体性的丧失比所谓的“成名机会”代价大得多。他们不愿参赛，是因为他们潜意识里拒绝被像女性那样被“消费”。

最讽刺的细节在于，Mr England 的报名入口居然在那个带有“皇冠 logo”的 Miss England App 里。这在结构层 (structural layer) 明确地告知了参与者：这场男性比赛只是女性盛会的附属品 (adjunct)。这种表达方式本身就是一种 weaponized 叙事——它试图将男性的身体美学纳入一个由女性审美（或定义女性审美的男性）主导的框架中。

女性在 Miss England 中数以千计的参与，并非因为她们更爱美，而是因为在长期的文化规训下，扮演“被挑选的客体”成为了许多女性在存在性战争中寻找的假.最优解表达。而男性在面对同样的剧本时，身体本能地产生了排异反应。这恰恰证明了：客体化从来不是一种审美，而是一种权力等级。当权力上位者被要求体验下位者的处境时，他们唯一的反应就是：没兴趣。

The Guardian treats the lack of entrants for Mr England as a 'mystery' while Miss England attracts thousands. The organizers even resorted to having Miss England contestants 'recruit' men on beaches or nominate boyfriends. This isn't a 'shortage'; it's a raw manifestation of the struggle over the pricing power of objectification.

Under the logic of meta violence, the masculine-centric narrative grants men innate 'subjectivity.' They are conditioned to be the gazers, the definers, and the pricers. When a contest asks men to strip down and become 'hunks' to be rated and picked, it demands they surrender their subjectivity and adopt the role of the 'object'—a role women are structurally forced to perform. For most men, this loss of agency is far costlier than any perceived 'opportunity for fame.' They refuse to participate because they instinctively reject being 'consumed' the way women are.

The most telling detail is that the Mr England registration is buried within the Miss England app, marked by a tiara logo. At a structural layer, this explicitly frames the men's competition as a mere adjunct to the women's event. This is a weaponized expression: an attempt to fold male physical aesthetics into a framework dominated by the gaze that defines women.

The thousands of women entering Miss England aren't doing so out of a greater love for beauty, but because, through long-term cultural conditioning, performing as the 'chosen object' has become a fake optimal expression for survival in the existential war. When men are handed the same script, their bodies react with instinctive rejection. This proves that objectification is never about aesthetics; it is about power hierarchy. When the dominant subject is asked to experience the condition of the object, their only response is: 'I'm not interested.'
