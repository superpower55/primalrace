河马死在旱季里The Hippos Died in the Dry Season

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
干旱不是天灾，是分配问题
Drought is not a natural disaster. It is a distribution problem.

28头河马在一个月里死在塞穆利基河，炭疽只是执行者。维龙加公园说，旱季植被稀少时，食草动物挤到仅剩的几处水源和草场，贴着地面啃草，于是接触到被孢子污染的土壤。炭疽孢子能在土里活几十年，等的就是这一刻。

所以这条新闻里真正的暴力不在细菌那一层。刚果东部打了近三十年仗，公园承认过去四年失去了大片土地的控制权，盗猎和砍伐随之而来。监测野生动物健康的难度上升，这句话在报告里是一行字，在河岸边是尸体漂在水里没人及时捞。武装冲突、保护区失守、气候恶化、动物挤在一起——结构层的每一环都松了，微生物才拿到了入口。

死的是河马，代价落在人身上。公园的公告说得很清楚：接触死畜或吃它的肉会传染。维楚姆比一带的居民已经在害怕，他们同时还在经历刚果历史上最致命的埃博拉疫情。一个社区被两种病夹着，一种来自医院和葬礼，一种来自河里漂来的尸体。他们被建议不要碰、不要剥皮、不要吃——这建议本身没错，但它假定这些人有别的蛋白质来源。

去年同一片公园，65头河马死于炭疽，工作人员调来挖掘机把尸体从水里拖走。今年28头。数字下降了，但公园2026年2月的普查说河马还有2700多头，分在171个群里。炭疽会反复来，因为孢子不会走，干旱会再来，冲突也不会因为河马死了就停。

M23说它在帮忙保护这片区域免遭盗猎者。一个武装团体用“保护”给自己定位，这是最老的一套叙事：先制造失控，再以秩序的名义接管。谁在共谋、回报是什么，报道没写；但公园失去土地控制权这件事，和河马尸体漂在河上这件事，中间没有隔多少层。

生态学家说河马是生态系统的工程师，它在陆地和水中搬运物质，它消失会打乱整条水系。这话是对的，只是说得太客气了。河马不是被炭疽杀死的，是被一个让土地无人看管、让水源越来越少、让人只能在病死畜和饿肚子之间选的世界杀死的。孢子只是最后签字的那个。

Twenty-eight hippos died in the Semuliki River in about a month. Anthrax was the executioner, not the cause.

Virunga National Park's own statement lays out the mechanism: when vegetation gets scarce, herbivores graze closer to the ground and crowd around the few remaining water points, and that is how they meet contaminated soil. Anthrax spores survive for decades in the earth. They were waiting for exactly this.

So the real violence here is not at the bacterial layer. Eastern DRC has been at war for nearly three decades, and the park admits it has lost control of a significant part of its territory over the past four years. Poaching and deforestation followed. "Difficulties in monitoring wildlife health" is one line in a press release; on the riverbank it means carcasses floating in the water with nobody there to pull them out in time. Armed conflict, a reserve slipping out of control, a worsening climate, animals packed together — every link in the structural layer came loose, and the microbe found its entrance.

The hippos die; the cost lands on people. The park is explicit that contact with a dead or sick animal, or eating its meat, transmits anthrax. Residents of the Vitshumbi enclave are already afraid, and they are doing it in the middle of the deadliest Ebola outbreak in the country's history. One community caught between two diseases: one arriving through hospitals and funerals, one arriving on a corpse floating down the river. Do not touch it, do not skin it, do not eat it — the advice is correct, and it assumes these people have another source of protein.

Last year, 65 hippos died of anthrax in the same park and staff brought in excavators to haul the carcasses out of the water. This year, 28. The number went down, but the park's February 2026 census put the population at more than 2,700 across 171 groups. Anthrax will come back, because the spores do not leave, the drought will return, and the conflict will not pause for the hippos.

M23 says it is helping protect the area from poachers and other threats. An armed group positioning itself as the protector is the oldest narrative there is: manufacture the breakdown, then take over in the name of order. Who is complicit and what they gain, the report does not say. But there are not many layers between the park losing its territory and dead hippos in the river.

The ecologist calls hippos ecosystem engineers, moving matter between land and water, and warns their disappearance would disrupt the whole aquatic system. True, and too polite. The hippos were not killed by anthrax. They were killed by a world that leaves the land unguarded, lets the water shrink, and leaves people choosing between diseased meat and an empty stomach. The spore only signed the document.
