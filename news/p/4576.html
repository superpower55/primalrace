当政治博弈退化为“幻想”的指控Politics Reduced to Accusations of 'Fantasy'

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
对女性政治家的攻击总是精准地从“认知能力”和“真实性”入手。
Attacks on female politicians always precisely target 'cognitive ability' and 'authenticity'.

Robert Jenrick 对 Kemi Badenoch 的攻击路径极其经典，甚至到了令人厌倦的程度。他没有在政策细节上进行 structural 层的博弈，而是迅速将战场转移到 personal attack，用“fantasist”（幻想家）和“pack of lies”（谎言之堆）这种词汇，试图将一个女性领导者的政治表达定义为某种精神层面的不稳定或认知偏差。

这是一种典型的 cultural violence。在 masculine-centric narrative 中，男性的野心被定义为“领导力”，而女性的政治抱负则被轻描淡写地归类为“幻想”。Jenrick 提到的 Stanford 大学录取 offer 争议，本质上是在通过质疑她的“背景表达”来解构她的主体性。他试图告诉世界：这个女性不配拥有她所主张的身份，她的存在是一个 scam。

最讽刺的是，Jenrick 此时在推销他所谓的“给白车厢男人（white van men）选择权”的政策。他一边在公共领域宣扬“选择权”和“自由”，一边在私人博弈中通过定义对方为“疯子”来剥夺一个女性政治家的解释权。这不过是两群男性共谋者在权力席位上的内斗，而女性在其中被物化为某种可以被随意定义为“虚假”的客体。

这场存在性战争的残酷之处在于，无论 Badenoch 赢得了多少权力，她依然要面对这种根深蒂固的元暴力：只要一个男性决定她是在“幻想”，那么她的所有政治表达都会被自动过滤为某种情绪化的表演，而非理性的决策。

Robert Jenrick's attack path against Kemi Badenoch is textbook, almost tedious. He avoids structural gaming over policy details and instead shifts the battlefield to personal attacks, using terms like 'fantasist' and 'pack of lies' to define a female leader's political expression as a form of mental instability or cognitive bias.

This is a classic manifestation of cultural violence. In a masculine-centric narrative, male ambition is defined as 'leadership,' while a woman's political aspiration is dismissed as 'fantasy.' Jenrick's reference to the Stanford university offer controversy is essentially an attempt to deconstruct her subjectivity by questioning her 'background expression.' He is attempting to tell the world that this woman does not deserve the identity she claims; her existence is a scam.

The irony is that Jenrick is simultaneously pitching policies about 'choice' for 'white van men.' While preaching 'choice' and 'freedom' in the public sphere, he uses the private game of labeling his opponent a 'madwoman' to strip a female politician of her right to interpretation. This is nothing more than an internal struggle between male complicity over power seats, where the woman is objectified as something that can be arbitrarily defined as 'fake.'

The cruelty of this existential war lies in the fact that no matter how much power Badenoch gains, she still faces this ingrained meta-violence: as long as a man decides she is 'fantasizing,' all her political expressions will be automatically filtered as emotional performance rather than rational decision-making.
