一首歌赢过一次，代价还在They Won Once, and Still Pay the Price

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要The Chicks 因疾病在洛杉矶 Dolby Theatre 演出开场前数小时宣布推迟，改期至 11 月 4 日，已购门票继续有效，无法到场者可退票。这场演出属于乐队围绕 2006 年专辑《Taking the Long Way》展开的二十周年巡演。当年专辑与单曲《Not Ready to Make Nice》回应了 2003 年 Natalie Maines 在伦敦批评布什与伊拉克战争后遭遇的抵制。
The newsThe Chicks postponed their Los Angeles show at the Dolby Theatre hours before doors opened, citing illness; it is rescheduled for Nov. 4, with tickets valid and refunds available. The date falls on their 20th-anniversary tour for Taking the Long Way, the 2006 album and single “Not Ready to Make Nice” that answered the backlash after Natalie Maines criticized Bush and the Iraq War in London in 2003.
她们赢了那场博弈，也从此付了定价。
They won that fight, and got priced for it ever after.

一条演唱会推迟的公告，本来没什么可说的。但这场巡演的名字是《Taking the Long Way》，二十周年。二十年前那张专辑卖到 Billboard 200 第一，拿下五座格莱美，包括年度专辑。这不是一张普通的专辑，它是一份不道歉的声明。

2003 年，Natalie Maines 在伦敦的演唱会上批评了当时的总统布什和伊拉克战争。随后乡村电台停播她们的音乐，抗议和抵制跟着来。一个歌手在台上说了句话，整个产业机器开动起来，要她收回。

这就是第三章说的武器化，入口是舆论，方式是让某个表达变得昂贵。电台不播、观众抵制、行业施压，成本一层层加上去，直到说真话的人自己算账：值不值。她们算出来的答案是《Not Ready to Make Nice》，在歌里直接回应了那股要她道歉的压力，以及她决定不退。

但别把这件事读成爽文。她们赢了那一局，代价是离开乡村音乐的主流位置，用一张专辑、二十年，重新给自己定价。报道写她们此后继续演出、发片，2020 年出了十四年来第一张录音室专辑《Gaslighter》。十四年这个数字放在那里，不用多解释。

值得注意的是，当年那套叙事——「中东女性需要被解放，所以打阿富汗」——和她们批评的战争是同一件事的两面。用女性身体做开战借口，是文化暴力最顺手的入口之一。一个乡村女歌手在伦敦把这句话拆了，产业的第一反应是让她闭嘴。

推迟到 11 月 4 日的那场演出，票依然有效。二十年后，她们还在唱那张专辑里的歌。这不是胜利的庆典，是账单的展示：赢过一次的表达，要用之后每一次登台来续费。

而当年那些停播她们的电台，报道里一个名字都没有。

A postponed concert notice is usually nothing to write about. But this tour is Taking the Long Way, twentieth anniversary. Twenty years ago that album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200 and took five Grammys, including album of the year. It was not an ordinary record. It was a refusal to apologize.

In 2003, Natalie Maines criticized then-President George W. Bush and the Iraq War at a London show. Country radio stations stopped playing the group, and protests and boycotts followed. A singer said one thing onstage, and the industry machine cranked up to make her take it back.

This is weaponization, chapter three: the entry point is public opinion, and the method is making an expression expensive. No airplay, boycotts, industry pressure — costs stacked one on another until the person speaking has to do the math on whether it is worth it. Their answer was “Not Ready to Make Nice,” a song that addressed the pressure to apologize and the decision not to back down.

Do not read this as a clean win. They won that round, and the price was leaving country music’s mainstream, then spending an album and twenty years repricing themselves. The report says they kept performing and releasing music, and put out Gaslighter in 2020, their first studio album in 14 years. That number needs no gloss.

The same narrative that sent them into exile — “Middle Eastern women need liberating, so we go into Afghanistan” — is the other face of the war Maines criticized. Using women’s bodies as a pretext for war is one of cultural violence’s handiest entry points. A country singer took that sentence apart in London, and the industry’s first move was to tell her to shut up.

The show is now set for Nov. 4, tickets still valid. Twenty years on, they are still singing those songs. This is not a victory lap. It is the bill, displayed: an expression that won once has to be renewed at every subsequent show.

And the radio stations that dropped them? Not one is named in the report.
