股市涨了，经济没涨The Index Rose, the Economy Didn't

国际 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
市场不是经济，涨幅不是产出。
Markets are not the economy; gains are not output.

这篇报道自己把答案写在了第二句：markets are not the economy。IMF 预测海湾地区 2026 年 GDP 收缩 4.8%，股市没跌那么多，报道给出的解释是企业盈利强于预期。问题在于，这个"企业盈利"是谁的盈利——沙特阿美一家占了 Tadawul 上市公司上半年合计盈利的约 72%（来源：Semafor）。

所以这不是"市场比经济更健康"，而是一个指数被一家卖油的公司绑架了。油价涨，它的净利润涨 33%，指数跟着好看；海湾地区的实际产出在收缩，这个收缩不会因为 Aramco 的财报而消失。把一家公司的盈利当成整个区域的体温计，等于把体温计插在暖气片上测量病人的状况。

更值得看的是那个因果链：胡塞武装对沙特的袭击加剧，油价上涨，Aramco 可能还有一个强劲的季度。也就是说，这场"市场韧性"的燃料是冲突升级。有人在中东挨炸，有人在利雅得财报上多出一行利润，而 Semafor 把这条链写成了一条利好。这是暴力三角里最典型的一层：直接层的暴力（袭击）转化成了结构层的收益（估值），中间不需要任何人做错什么，只需要市场照常运转。

阿曼被称作"脱颖而出"，脱颖而出的标准是它的股市跌得少。一个国家的经济状况被压缩成一个指数涨跌幅，而这个指数里权重最大的往往不是普通人每天生活所依赖的那部分。海湾国家有大量外籍劳工，他们的工资、居留、汇款和这个指数没有关系；GDP 收缩 4.8% 落到他们身上是什么，报道没有写，也不在"市场"这个词的射程之内。

市场不是经济，这句话是对的，但报道用它来安慰读者，而不是用它来追问：如果市场不能代表经济，那它代表的是谁的经济？答案是持有 Aramco 股票的人的经济。剩下的人不在这个指数里，也不在这篇报道里。

The piece answers itself in the second sentence: markets are not the economy. The IMF forecasts a 4.8% contraction in Gulf GDP for 2026, stocks haven't fallen as far, and the explanation offered is stronger-than-expected corporate earnings. The question is whose earnings. Saudi Aramco alone accounted for about 72% of the combined earnings of Tadawul-listed companies in the first half of the year (source: Semafor).

So this isn't "markets healthier than the economy." It's an index captured by one oil company. Crude goes up, its net profit goes up 33%, the index looks fine; the actual output of the region contracts, and that contraction does not disappear because Aramco had a good quarter. Treating one company's earnings as the region's thermometer is sticking the thermometer on a radiator and calling it a patient's temperature.

The causal chain is the real story: Houthi attacks on the kingdom intensify, oil rises, Aramco may post another strong quarter. The fuel for this "resilience" is escalation. People get bombed in one place, a profit line grows in another, and Semafor writes the chain up as a positive. This is the Violence Triangle at its most ordinary: direct violence converts into structural gain, and nobody has to do anything wrong for it to happen. The market just keeps running.

Oman "stands out" because its market fell less. A country's economy gets compressed into an index move, and the heaviest weights in that index are rarely the parts ordinary people live on. The Gulf runs on migrant labor whose wages, residencies, and remittances have nothing to do with this index. What a 4.8% GDP contraction means for them is not in the piece, and not within range of the word "market."

Markets are not the economy. True. But the piece uses that line to reassure, not to ask: if the market doesn't represent the economy, whose economy does it represent? The answer is the economy of people who hold Aramco stock. Everyone else is outside the index, and outside this article.
