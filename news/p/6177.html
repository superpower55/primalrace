悼词里的男人，和没被写进去的女人The Men in the Eulogy, the Woman Left Out

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Cut ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要伊恩·麦克莱恩发文悼念帕特里克·斯图尔特。斯图尔特于周四因阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆去世，两人相识于上世纪70年代的伦敦，此后合作半个世纪。
The newsIan McKellen paid tribute to Patrick Stewart, who died Thursday after a brief battle with Alzheimer's disease and Lewy body dementia. The two met as young actors in London in the '70s and collaborated for half a century.
一篇悼词写满了男性之间的爱与传承，妻子只是被照顾的那个。
A tribute full of men loving men; the wife is only a verb.

McKellen 写 Stewart，写的是 Yorkshire、是口音、是"有话直说"、是年轻演员的野心、是劝他别接《星际迷航》的那次街头偶遇。这是一份很好的悼词。它同时是一份完整的男性传承谱系：一个男人如何被另一个男人看见、提携、欣赏、爱。

然后 Sunny 出现了，一句话："where he met his adored Sunny who has cared for him through sickness and health, through thick and thin"。她照顾他，病中，健康时，顺境逆境。她是这句话里的动词，不是主语。悼词里没有她的名字之外的东西——没有她从哪来、她做过什么、她失去过什么。她在这篇文字里的全部功能是"cared for him"。

这不是 McKellen 的刻薄，恰恰相反，这是最善意的写法。问题就在这里：连最深情、最体面的悼词，默认的叙事结构也是男人是生平，女人是照料。一个女性在一个人生命最后阶段承担了最重的身体劳动——阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆，这两种病到最后需要的是二十四小时的看护——而这份劳动在公共叙事里只换来一个从句。

再看这篇报道本身。Jackman 说他是"a tremendous friend and man"，Shatner 说"Captain Picard stood for the best of us, and so did the man who played him"，Burton 说他是"family"，"deep down the silliest"。全是同行、全是男性、全是职业共同体内部的互相确认。这是共谋最温和的形态：没有人做错什么，所有人都在真诚地哀悼，而结构照常运转——男人的一生由男人的评价来定义，女人的一生由她照顾了谁来定义。

报道还提到他最后的银幕作品，会把 Magneto 和 Professor X 再凑到一起一次。两个男人，一段跨越几十年的关系，最后一次同框。这个收尾很漂亮，漂亮到几乎没人会问：Sunny 会出现在哪里。

阿尔茨海默病和路易体痴呆不会只折磨病人。照护者也在被这种病一点点拿走东西——睡眠、时间、自己的职业、自己的名字在公共记录里的位置。Stewart 的死讯里，这些代价一个字都没有。悼词是写给活人看的，而这篇悼词告诉活人：一个男人值得被记住的方式，是他爱过谁、被谁爱过、说过什么真话。一个女人值得被记住的方式，还没写出来。

McKellen writing about Stewart writes Yorkshire, writes accent, writes plain speaking, writes two ambitious young actors, writes the street-corner moment he told his friend not to take Star Trek. It is a good tribute. It is also a complete genealogy of men: how one man is seen, championed, admired, and loved by another.

Then Sunny appears, in one clause: "where he met his adored Sunny who has cared for him through sickness and health, through thick and thin." She cared for him. She is the verb in that sentence, not the subject. Beyond her name, the tribute gives nothing — not where she came from, not what she did, not what it cost her. Her entire function in this text is "cared for him."

This is not McKellen being cruel. It is the opposite: it is the most generous possible phrasing. That is exactly the problem. Even the warmest, most dignified tribute defaults to a structure in which the man is the life and the woman is the care. A woman carried the heaviest physical labor of his final years — Alzheimer's and Lewy body dementia end in round-the-clock care — and that labor earns one subordinate clause in the public record.

Look at the report itself. Jackman calls him "a tremendous friend and man." Shatner says "Captain Picard stood for the best of us, and so did the man who played him." Burton calls him "family," "deep down the silliest." All peers, all men, all mutual confirmation inside a professional guild. This is complicity in its mildest form: nobody does anything wrong, everyone grieves sincerely, and the structure runs exactly as designed — a man's life is defined by other men's judgment of him, a woman's life by whom she cared for.

The piece ends with his final film, which will put Magneto and Professor X together one last time. Two men, a relationship spanning decades, one last frame. It is a beautiful ending, beautiful enough that almost no one asks where Sunny will be in it.

Alzheimer's and Lewy body dementia do not only take the patient. They take the caregiver too — sleep, time, career, the place of her own name in the public record. None of that cost appears in the obituary. Eulogies are written for the living, and this one tells the living how a man deserves to be remembered: by whom he loved, who loved him, what true things he said. How a woman deserves to be remembered has not been written yet.
