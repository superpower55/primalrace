十句话术，卖的是顺从Ten Phrases, Priced to Obey

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要CNBC 刊出演讲培训师 John Bowe 的建议稿，列出十句「让人放下戒备」的话术，包括「多跟我说说」「不着急」「你怎么看」，称这样说话能让人更信任你、把竞争变成合作。文末附 CNBC 付费领导力课程推广。
The newsCNBC published advice from speech trainer John Bowe listing ten phrases—such as "Tell me more," "Take your time," and "What do you think?"—that supposedly make people trust you and turn competition into collaboration. The piece ends with a promotion for CNBC's paid leadership course.
所谓亲和力，是把听话包装成美德，再按份出售
Approachability is obedience repackaged as a virtue and sold by the phrase.

这篇东西与其说是沟通技巧，不如说是一份职场顺从的价目表。十条里没有一条教你说「不」，没有一条教你在被占便宜的时候把账算清楚。「不着急」「没关系，事情总会发生」「有什么我能帮的」——每一句都在训练你把别人的节奏、别人的错误、别人的需求放在自己前面，然后管这个叫「让人信任你」。

报道自己把底牌亮了：亲和力带来「友谊和职场联盟」，联盟带来「人脉和更多机会」，关系融洽时「竞争就变成合作」。看清楚，竞争变合作不是双赢，是有人先放弃了自己那一份议价权。这套话术的功能不是让你被喜欢，是让你在博弈里主动降价，还觉得自己在做好人。

这是第三章那个入口的教科书版本：表达被武器化，先给你一个「什么是好人」的定义，再把这个定义卖给你。定价权在谁手里？在写这份清单的人和卖课程的公司手里。你照着说，你变便宜；他们说，他们收钱。文末那句课程广告不是附赠，是这篇稿子的目的。

最狠的一层是它把结构问题翻译成个人修养。职场里有人不敢开口，报道的解释是「害羞的同事可能选择沉默」，解决办法是你多说一句「你怎么看」。可沉默的原因可能是说了没人听、听了不算数、算数了要担责——这些它一个字不提。把不敢说话归因于气氛不够温暖，等于让弱势的一方继续用情绪劳动去补制度的窟窿。

真.最优解表达不会教你把「谢谢您提出来」当口头禅。它教你的是：该争的争，该拒的拒，帮人是因为你愿意，不是因为帮了能换信任。这两套话术说出口可能一模一样，但说的人知道自己站在哪。

最后一句「我在这儿，想聊随时找我」——它没写的是，等你真去聊了，聊的是你的加班、你的委屈、你要的那份涨薪，对面还会不会用这十句话回你。

This isn't communication advice. It's a price list for workplace compliance. Not one of the ten phrases teaches you to say no, or to settle the score when someone takes advantage of you. "Take your time," "No worries, things happen," "How can I help?"—each one trains you to put other people's pace, other people's mistakes, other people's needs ahead of your own, and then calls the result being trusted.

The piece gives away its own game: approachability brings "friendships and alliances at work," which bring "a broader network of opportunities," and when relationships are friendly, "competition becomes collaboration." Read that again. Competition becoming collaboration isn't a win-win; it's someone quietly surrendering their bargaining power first. The function of this script isn't to make you liked. It's to make you discount yourself while feeling generous.

This is the textbook version of the weaponization entry from Chapter 3: first define what a good person sounds like, then sell you the definition. Who holds the pricing power? The person writing the list and the company selling the course. You talk like this, you get cheaper; they talk like this, they get paid. The course plug at the end isn't an appendix. It's the point.

The cruelest move is translating a structural problem into personal cultivation. Someone on the team won't speak up, and the article's diagnosis is that "shyer team members might choose to stay silent"—so the fix is for you to ask "What do you think?" more often. It never asks why they're silent: maybe because speaking got them nowhere, or nowhere that counted, or counted against them. Blaming the temperature of the room means the less powerful keep doing emotional labor to patch a hole in the system.

A true optimal expression doesn't make "Thanks for bringing that up" a reflex. It tells you to fight what's worth fighting, refuse what's worth refusing, and help because you want to, not because helping buys trust. The two scripts can sound identical out loud. The difference is whether the speaker knows where they're standing.

And that last line—"I'm here if you want to talk." What it leaves out is what happens when you actually do: your overtime, your grievance, the raise you're asking for. Will the other side still answer with these ten phrases?
