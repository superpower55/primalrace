被定义的“新物种”与被抹除的本地认知The 'New Species' Scam and the Erasure of Local Knowledge

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-17 § 链接
科学命名是对事实的二次殖民，认知的权力高于生物学事实。
Scientific naming is a secondary colonization of fact; the power to define outweighs biological reality.

一个在玻利维亚云雾林里生活了140万年的物种，直到2026年才被正式“命名”并被定义为“新物种”。这件所谓的科学突破，本质上是一次典型的认知入口争夺战。当地社区一直称它为 tilcayo，但在学术界的叙事里，只有通过基因测序、发表在《Current Biology》上、由来自比利时和巴西的科学家赋予其拉丁名 Leopardus tilcayo 之后，这个生物才在“客观事实”中获得了合法身份。

这就是典型的元暴力：解释权的垄断。学术界通过建立一套名为“科学”的筛选机制，将当地人的经验性认知贬低为非正式的，而将自己的实验室结果定义为真理。在这种结构下，tilcayo 并不是被“发现”了，而是被“重新定义”了。这种定义权决定了谁能获得研究经费，谁能决定该物种的保护等级，以及谁在关于自然历史的叙事中占据主导地位。

更讽刺的是，这次“发现”的契机竟然是一个被当地家庭养在家里、后来进入避难所的10岁雄性个体。这意味着这个物种在本地的表达早已存在，只是在男性中心、西方中心定义的“科学标准”中，它之前被错误地归类为巴西虎猫的子集。这种“用巴西的尺子量玻利维亚的猫”的逻辑，与用某种单一的文化模板去规训多元身份的逻辑完全同构。

虽然将物种细分能让 IUCN 的红皮书更精确地评估灭绝风险，从而在 structural 层面上获得更多保护资源，但这依然是一场用“命名权”换取“保护权”的博弈。我们庆祝的不是新物种的出现，而是学术系统终于意识到自己的认知盲区，并决定通过一次命名仪式来弥补这个差额。

A species that has inhabited Bolivia's cloud forests for 1.4 million years was only 'identified' as a new species in 2026. This so-called scientific breakthrough is essentially a struggle for the cognitive entry point. Local communities have always called it the tilcayo, but in the academic narrative, this creature only attained 'legal' identity after genetic sequencing, publication in Current Biology, and being granted a Latin name by researchers from Belgium and Brazil.

This is a textbook case of meta-violence: the monopoly over the power of interpretation. Academia uses a screening mechanism called 'science' to downgrade local experiential knowledge as informal, while defining laboratory results as the sole truth. In this structure, the tilcayo wasn't 'discovered'; it was 're-defined.' This power of definition determines who gets the grants, who decides the conservation status, and who dominates the narrative of natural history.

Cruelly, the catalyst for this 'discovery' was a 10-year-old male cat kept by a local family. This proves the species' expression already existed locally; it was simply categorized as a subset of Brazilian tiger cats because the 'scientific standard'—rooted in a masculine-centric, Western-centric gaze—used the wrong ruler. The logic of using Brazilian descriptions to measure Bolivian cats is identical to using a single cultural template to discipline diverse identities.

While splitting the species allows the IUCN Red List to more accurately assess extinction risks—potentially securing more resources at the structural level—it remains a gamble where 'protection' is traded for 'naming rights.' We are not celebrating a new species, but rather the moment the academic system acknowledged its own blind spot and decided to rectify the gap through a naming ritual.
