西敏斯特的“善意坟场”与无偿劳动的结构性共谋The Westminster Graveyard and the Structural Complicity of Unpaid Labor

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
所谓的“政治意愿”在结构性剥削面前，不过是另一场表演性让步。
So-called "political will" is merely a performative concession when faced with structural exploitation.

Andy Burnham 的这场演讲试图在社会护理（social care）这个死局中制造一种“希望”的叙事。但从加尔通的暴力三角来看，这不过是在 cultural 层面上通过表演“激情”与“人性”，试图掩盖 structural 层的深层暴力。所谓的“西敏斯特善意坟场”之所以存在，不是因为缺乏个别政治家的意愿，而是因为这套系统本身就是建立在对特定群体的剥削之上的。

注意正文中提到的“无偿照顾者”（unpaid carers）的牺牲。在社会护理的结构中，绝大多数的无偿劳动由女性承担。这种“牺牲”被浪漫化为家庭责任，实际上是父权制与资本主义最稳固的共谋：通过将护理工作定义为“爱”和“本能”，社会成功地将巨大的成本内部化给女性，从而维持了公共财政的低支出。Burnham 谈论投资、谈论薪资、谈论反对私有化公司的“牟利”，却始终没有触及这个问题的元暴力——即护理劳动在价值定义上的性别化缺失。

如果一个改革方案仅仅是“提高薪资”或“调整资金”，而没有重新定义护理劳动的主体性，那么它依然是在男性中心叙事的框架内打补丁。这种“令人兴奋的开始”往往是另一种 weaponized expression，用一个看似激进的姿态来对冲结构性不平等的愤怒，直到它再次陷入惯性，成为第 23 份被遗忘的审查报告。

Andy Burnham’s speech attempts to manufacture a narrative of "hope" within the dead-end of social care. However, applying the Violence Triangle, this is simply a performative display of "passion" and "humanity" at the cultural layer, designed to mask the deep-seated structural violence. The "Westminster graveyard of good intentions" exists not for lack of individual political will, but because the system itself is predicated on the exploitation of specific groups.

Note the mention of "unpaid carers" and their sacrifices. In the structure of social care, the vast majority of this unpaid labor is performed by women. This "sacrifice" is romanticized as family duty, but it is actually a stable complicity between patriarchy and capitalism: by defining care work as "love" and "instinct," the system internalizes massive costs within women to maintain low public spending. Burnham discusses investment, pay, and "profiteering" by private firms, yet he fails to address the meta-violence—the gendered erasure of value in care labor.

If a reform plan only focuses on "boosting pay" or "funding options" without redefining the subjectivity of care work, it remains a mere patch on a masculine-centric narrative. This "impressive start" is often another form of weaponized expression, using a seemingly radical gesture to offset anger over structural inequality, until it inevitably slides back into inertia and becomes the 23rd forgotten review.
