诺兰的《奥德赛》：一次关于“文明”的男性化洗白Nolan's The Odyssey: A Masculine Whitewashing of 'Civilization'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
将残酷的掠夺者修剪为“反思的男人”，是典型的元暴力叙事洗白。
Pruning a ruthless predator into a 'reflective man' is a classic meta-violence narrative scrub.

诺兰把《奥德赛》拍成了现代好莱坞的模版：一个技术精湛的 Imax 奇观，以及一个被修剪得温顺、内疚且充满“人性光辉”的男性主角。这不仅是改编，这是一次典型的 weaponized expression。原著中的奥德修斯是一个残酷的 schemer，一个在暴力中生存的掠夺者；但诺兰将其更新为类似现代 James Bond 的形象——一个“不情愿地使用暴力”且“深陷冲突”的深情男人。这种更新本质上是在用现代文明的伪装来消弭原初的暴力，让观众在一种虚假的 compassion 中完成对男性权力结构的再次认同。

最令人作呕的共谋在于对女性角色的处理。Penelope 被简化为一个在织布机旁等待 20 年的客体，Athena 被削弱为脆弱的配角，Circe 被异化为奇怪的巫婆。这种处理方式精准地维护了 masculine-centric narrative：女性要么是等待的奖赏，要么是需要被定义的他者。即便电影试图探讨“青铜时代崩溃”，它依然无法跳出父权制的认知入口——它在讨论权力的崩塌，却在视觉和叙事上继续巩固权力的垄断。

更讽刺的是，这部宣扬“共通人性”的电影竟然在西撒哈拉被占领土拍摄。这种 structural violence 的共谋达到了顶峰：在银幕上表演对战争的质疑，在现实中则通过向占领者付费来获取拍摄权限。这种“表演性进步”是典型的 scam，它用文化层面的伪善掩盖了直接层面的利益交换。所谓的“深邃”不过是高分贝音效掩盖下的空洞，而所谓的“救赎”则是让施暴者在观众的自我感动中获得了廉价的赦免。

Nolan has turned The Odyssey into a modern Hollywood template: an immense technical Imax spectacle paired with a protagonist pruned to be gentle, guilty, and radiating 'humanity.' This is more than an adaptation; it is a weaponized expression. The original Odysseus was a ruthless schemer, a predator surviving through violence. Nolan updates him into a modern James Bond—a man who uses violence reluctantly and is plagued by conflict. This update essentially uses the veneer of modern civilization to erase primal violence, allowing the audience to reaffirm the masculine power structure through a false sense of compassion.

The most egregious complicity lies in the treatment of women. Penelope is reduced to an object waiting 20 years at a loom, Athena is sidelined as a fragile being, and Circe is alienated into a 'weird witch.' This precisely maintains the masculine-centric narrative: women are either rewards to be won or others to be defined. Even as the film attempts to discuss the 'Bronze Age Collapse,' it cannot escape the patriarchal cognitive entry—it discusses the collapse of power while visually and narratively reinforcing its monopoly.

More ironic is the decision to film in the occupied lands of Western Sahara. This complicity in structural violence reaches its peak: performing a critique of war on screen while paying an occupying nation for filming permits in reality. This 'performative progressivism' is a total scam, using cultural hypocrisy to mask direct-level interest exchange. The so-called 'profundity' is merely a void hidden by high-decibel soundscapes, and the 'redemption' is simply granting the aggressor a cheap pardon through the audience's self-indulgent emotion.
