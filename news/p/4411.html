华夫饼的个性测试与被定义的“真实”Waffle Personality Tests and the Manufactured 'Real'

哲学 文化层 Wirecutter: Reviews for the Real World ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
当消费主义将“个性”量化为吃法时，主体性已沦为产品的注脚。
When consumerism quantifies 'personality' as a way of eating, subjectivity becomes a mere footnote to the product.

Wirecutter 这篇评测很有意思，它试图用一种温情且具有“人情味”的叙事来包装一次标准的商业筛选。作者把吃华夫饼的方式比作 Myers-Briggs 或 Enneagram 这种性格测试，试图通过描述童年时与父亲、姐妹在枫糖浆和黄油上的博弈，来赋予冷冻面团一种名为“个性”的灵魂。但请注意，这种表达方式本身就是一种典型的 weaponization。它在潜意识里告诉你：你可以自由地选择 topping，但你必须在它为你划定的“冷冻华夫饼”这个认知入口里进行选择。

最讽刺的是，这篇文章在定义什么是“好”的华夫饼。它把 Eggo 视为这个类别的 pioneer（开拓者），然后通过对比得出 Target 和 Aldi 的自有品牌“比 Eggo 更好”。这在逻辑上是一个典型的 scam：它并没有定义华夫饼的真.最优解是什么，而是直接把 Eggo 设为基准线，在 Eggo 制造的工业标准（薄、圆、蛋味）之内寻找一个性价比更高的替代品。这不叫发现更好的产品，这叫在既定结构内寻找更高效的共谋方式。

当作者写道 Trader Joe’s 的华夫饼像“从 Microsoft Office 剪贴画里跳出来的现实”时，她实际上在完成一次文化层的规训——将“工业化、 manufactured”的外观定义为一种可以被宽容的、甚至带有幽默感的缺陷，只要它能承载足够的枫糖浆。这种叙事将消费者的注意力从“食品工业如何通过高度加工的面团剥夺天然口感”转移到了“它是否足够 sturdy 能够承载我的个性化吃法”上。

所谓的“个性化吃法”，不过是给一个标准化的工业产品贴上的情感标签。真正的个性应该是对这种“冷冻便利”结构的质疑，而不是在 Target 和 Aldi 之间纠结哪个更像 Eggo。

This Wirecutter review is fascinating in how it wraps a standard commercial filter in a warm, 'human' narrative. The author frames the way one eats a frozen waffle as a personality test akin to Myers-Briggs or Enneagram, attempting to breathe a soul called 'individuality' into frozen dough through nostalgic anecdotes of syrup and butter battles with family. But notice: this mode of expression is a textbook weaponisation. It whispers that you are free to choose your toppings, provided you stay within the cognitive entry point of 'frozen waffles' they've defined for you.

The irony lies in how 'good' is defined. By positioning Eggo as the category pioneer, the review concludes that Target and Aldi's store brands are 'better than Eggo.' This is a logical scam. It doesn't define what a true optimal expression of a waffle is; instead, it uses Eggo as the baseline and searches for a more cost-effective alternative within the industrial standards (thin, round, eggy) that Eggo created. This isn't discovering a better product; it's finding a more efficient way of complicity within a predetermined structure.

When the author describes Trader Joe’s waffles as 'clip-art waffles plucked straight from Microsoft Office,' she is performing a cultural regulation—defining a 'manufactured' appearance as a tolerable, even humorous flaw, as long as it can hold enough syrup. This narrative shifts the consumer's attention from how the food industry strips natural taste through ultra-processing to whether the product is 'sturdy' enough to support a 'personalized' eating habit.

These 'personalized methods' are nothing more than emotional labels slapped onto a standardized industrial product. True individuality would be questioning the structure of 'frozen convenience' itself, not agonizing over whether Target or Aldi is a better Eggo clone.
