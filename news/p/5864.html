七十六支球队，零个新故事76 Teams, Zero New Stories

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
扩军不是让更多人有机会，是让组织者多收一轮钱
Expansion isn't more access. It's one more round of revenue for the organizers.

NCAA把三月疯狂从64队扩到76队，四位记者被问到这件事，一个人说"不是好事，也没必要"，一个人说"这是给不存在的问题找解决方案"，一个人说"也许挺酷，希望不只是NCAA又一次圈钱"，一个人说"还行吧"。没有一个人问：多出来的这12个名额，是从谁手里拿走的。

答案写在最后一位记者的回答里。扩军伤到的是那些低种子的自动晋级球队——他们原本直接进64强，现在要先在周二周三打"First 12"。名额变多了，但新名额给了谁、代价由谁承担，是两件事。这是结构层最标准的一次操作：把资源重新切一遍，切出来的增量归组织者，切出来的成本归最没有议价权的那一头。

更值得看的是这四个人怎么谈钱。有人直接说"更多球队意味着更多比赛意味着更多钱"，说完就过去了，像在陈述天气。这就是共谋者的语法：利益分配被讲成自然规律。NCAA不需要说服谁，它只需要让所有人默认"扩张是未来的方向，从世界杯到网球大满贯都是这样"，然后钱自己就流过去了。

同一篇预测里，球员被谈论的方式是另一条线。Tyran Stokes被形容成"像是为最大限度吸引职业球探而在实验室里设计出来的"，"身体条件看起来没有上限"。一个还没打一场大学比赛的新生，已经被拆解成球探可读的参数：爆发力、臂展、能不能换防任何位置。他不是一个人，他是一份资产说明书。

女篮这边，JuJu Watkins伤停19个月后回来，四个人里有三个把她选为年度最佳和WNBA状元。她被反复称作"presumptive No 1 draft pick"、"generational star"。这些词听着是赞美，功能是定价。一个人在被当成"一代一遇"的同时，也已经被放上了货架，只等2027年那一声落槌。

Hannah Hidalgo那条最露骨：5英尺6英寸，"打破了现代职业后卫的原型"，然后立刻接上"问题在于她矮小的身材会不会限制她的职业前景"。身高在这里不是描述，是估值模型里的一个变量。同一段话里，男球员的身体被夸成没有上限，女球员的身体被当成需要打折的风险项。

至于"连续性"那条讨论——密歇根赢了冠军，教练去了NBA，阵容散架；佛罗里达保住了核心。四位记者认真比较哪种模式更好。可这套讨论里从头到尾没有球员的位置：转学门户里流动的是人，被讨论的是"模式"。球员在这套语言里是 roster，是 core，是可以"disintegrate"的东西。

所以这篇预测真正预测的不是谁夺冠。它预测的是一个已经运转良好的系统会继续运转：钱往组织者那边流，身体被拆成参数，女球员被赞美着定价，而所有人都觉得这只是体育。

76支球队里多出来的那12支，会在周二周三打完，然后被忘掉。真正被记住的永远是那四强。差额就在这里：更多人获得了"参赛"，但没有获得任何新的东西。

The NCAA is taking March Madness from 64 teams to 76. Four writers were asked about it. One says it's "not good nor necessary." One says it's "a solution in search of a problem." One says it's "potentially cool" and hopes it's "more than just another money grab." One says "fine." None of them asks who the extra twelve spots are taken from.

The answer is in the last writer's response. Expansion hurts the low-seeded automatic qualifiers, who used to go straight into the 64 and now have to play in the First 12 on Tuesday and Wednesday. The field got bigger, but who gets the new slots and who pays for them are two different questions. This is the structural layer doing its most standard move: re-cut the resources, route the增量 to the organizers, route the cost to whoever has the least bargaining power.

What's more telling is how all four talk about money. One states outright that "more teams means more games means more money" and then moves on, as if reporting the weather. That's the grammar of the co-conspirator: distribution presented as natural law. The NCAA doesn't need to persuade anyone. It only needs everyone to accept that "expansion is the way of the future, from the World Cup to tennis majors," and the money flows on its own.

The way players get discussed is the other thread. Tyran Stokes is described as looking "like he was designed in a lab for maximum appeal to professional scouts," with "physical tools [that] appear to have no ceiling." A freshman who hasn't played a college game is already broken down into scout-readable parameters: explosiveness, wingspan, ability to guard any position. He isn't a person. He's an asset spec sheet.

On the women's side, JuJu Watkins returns after a 19-month injury layoff, and three of the four pick her as player of the year and the No 1 WNBA pick. She's called "the presumptive No 1 draft pick," a "generational star." These sound like praise. Their function is pricing. A person gets labeled once-in-a-generation and is simultaneously placed on the shelf, waiting for the 2027 hammer to fall.

The Hidalgo answer is the most naked. Five foot six, she "defies the prototype of a modern professional guard"—and then immediately: "the question is whether her diminutive frame limits her professional prospects." Height isn't description here. It's a variable in a valuation model. In the same piece, a male player's body is praised as limitless; a female player's body is treated as a risk to be discounted.

The "continuity" debate is the same story. Michigan won the title, lost its coach to the NBA, watched its roster disintegrate; Florida kept its core. Four writers seriously compare which model wins. Players have no position in this discussion. What moves through the transfer portal is people; what gets discussed is a "model." In this language, players are roster, core, things that can "disintegrate."

So this piece isn't really predicting a champion. It's predicting that a system already running smoothly will keep running: money flows to the organizers, bodies get broken into parameters, female players get praised and priced at the same time, and everyone agrees this is just sports.

The twelve extra teams will play on Tuesday and Wednesday and then be forgotten. What gets remembered is always the Final Four. The gap is right there: more people got to "participate," and nobody got anything new.
