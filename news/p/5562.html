谁在为“宏大叙事”的账单买单？Who Pays the Bill for Grand Narratives?

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-15 § 链接
所有的“全球经济冲击”本质上都是结构性暴力对底层生存空间的掠夺。
Every 'global economic shock' is essentially structural violence raiding the survival space of the bottom.

英国劳工联合会 (TUC) 给出的这个 £13,961 的数字，在主流新闻里被包装成一个关于“通货膨胀”和“全球冲击”的经济统计。但用加尔通的暴力三角来看，这就是一次教科书级别的 structural violence。所谓的“疫情后遗症”、“乌克兰战争”或“特朗普通胀”，不过是元暴力叙事中的掩体。当权力中心的人们在讨论地缘政治、资本利得税和财政赤字时，他们实际上在进行一场关于“谁来承担成本”的博弈。

最令人作呕的共谋在于，房东通过提高租金将抵押贷款的上涨成本转嫁给租客，而政府则在 IMF 的“建议”下，以维持市场信心为由，拒绝将资本利得税与劳动所得税平等化。这意味着：靠出卖劳动力生存的人在支付战争和贪婪的账单，而靠资本增值获利的人在享受制度的庇护。这根本不是什么“经济波动”，而是一次精准的资源重新分配——将底层人的 Potential 生活状态强行拉低，差额部分变成了权力者的利润。

在这种叙事里，劳动者被简化为“消费者”，他们的痛苦被量化为“消费支出疲软”对 GDP 的负面影响。这种 masculine-centric narrative 极其傲慢：它关心的不是人是否能体面地生活，而是这个“经济机器”是否还能高效运转。当 Healey 承诺给家庭提供“呼吸空间”时，他其实是在给一个正在溺水的人提供一次短促的吸气，而不是把他从水里拉出来。

这场存在性战争的胜负手不在于预算案里多给几英镑的补贴，而在于解释权的移交。只要“财政纪律”依然高于“生存权利”，只要资本利得依然被视为某种神圣不可侵犯的特权，那么每一次全球危机，都将成为一次合法化的掠夺。所谓的“呼吸空间”，不过是让受害者在被榨干之前，不至于太快地死掉而影响到下一个季度的统计数据。

The £13,961 figure from the TUC is packaged in mainstream news as a statistic on 'inflation' and 'global shocks.' But through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this is textbook structural violence. Terms like 'post-pandemic fallout' or 'Trumpflation' are merely bunkers for meta-violence. While power centers debate geopolitics and fiscal deficits, they are actually playing a game of 'who bears the cost.'

The most disgusting complicity lies in the chain of transfer: landlords pass mortgage hikes to renters, while the government, guided by the IMF, refuses to equalize capital gains tax with income tax to 'maintain market confidence.' In short: those surviving on labor pay the bills for war and greed, while those profiting from capital enjoy institutional protection. This isn't an 'economic fluctuation'; it's a precise reallocation of resources—forcibly lowering the Potential state of the poor to inflate the profits of the powerful.

In this narrative, workers are reduced to 'consumers,' and their suffering is quantified as 'weak consumer spending' dragging down the GDP. This masculine-centric narrative is profoundly arrogant: it cares not whether people can live with dignity, but whether the 'economic machine' still runs efficiently. When Healey promises 'breathing space,' he is giving a drowning person one short gasp of air rather than pulling them out of the water.

The winning move in this existential war is not a few pounds of subsidies in a budget, but the transfer of the power of interpretation. As long as 'fiscal discipline' remains superior to 'the right to survive,' and as long as capital gains are treated as a sacred privilege, every global crisis will remain a legalized raid. This so-called 'breathing space' is simply ensuring the victims don't die too quickly, lest they ruin the statistics for the next quarter.
