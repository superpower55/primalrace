把国家拆成公司，把人拆成数据He Wants to Turn the State Into a Corporation

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
技术威权不是新意识形态，是定价权的换手
Techno-authoritarianism isn't a new ideology. It's a change of ownership.

Yarvin 那套东西的核心不是"独裁"，是**把主权拆成公司股权**。一个国家变成一个 realm，一个 realm 是一家有绝对权力的公司，居民不是公民，是用户，用户没有投票权，只有搬迁权——而所有其他 realm 也都是公司，所以搬迁权等于零。这套设计里最诚实的一句话是：旧金山可以承诺不砍居民的手，但因为它是主权实体，没人能强制执行这个承诺。翻译过来就是：权力不需要对任何人负责，只需要对自己负责。

这不是什么"暗黑启蒙"，这是把公司治理的逻辑推到它的终点。公司是什么？是一个只对股东负责、不对居民负责的实体。Yarvin 只是问了一句：为什么国家不能这样？然后他发现，其实很多国家早就是这样了，只是还留着一层选举的皮。他要做的是把那层皮撕掉，并且诚实地告诉你：撕掉之后，你连抱怨的资格都没有，因为你已经签了用户协议。

Vance 从 Yarvin 那里拿走的不是"砍手"这种极端设定，是更实用的那一半：清洗。De-Baathification、de-woke-ification、fire every single midlevel bureaucrat、replace them with our people、无视法院命令。这一段不需要任何哲学包装，它就是夺权操作手册。Project 2025 要裁五十万联邦雇员，Vance 说这活儿他可以干。注意这里的结构：先宣布官僚系统是敌人，再宣布清洗是净化，最后宣布法院无权阻止净化。三步走完，制度就没了，剩下的只有人。

报道里最值得盯住的不是 Yarvin 的疯狂，是 Thiel 的钱怎么走。2011 年 Yale 一场演讲，Vance 发邮件，Thiel 把他叫到加州；Mithril Capital 给他位置；Hillbilly Elegy 他写，Thiel 写推荐语；Narya Capital 开张，Thiel 和 Andreessen 出钱；2022 年参议院选举，Thiel 砸 1500 万美元，还说服 Trump 背书；2024 年，Thiel 领头说服 Trump 挑 Vance 当副手。这条链条上每一环都是钱，每一环都有回报。Vance 不是"从俄亥俄底层爬上来的"，Vance 是 Thiel 造出来的产品，产品现在要进白宫了。

这里要看清的是：Thiel 和 Musk 早就有钱了，钱对他们已经不是稀缺品。他们现在买的是**解释权**——谁定义什么叫"生产力"，谁定义谁该被虚拟化，谁定义法院能不能被无视。Yarvin 提供的正是这套解释权的语法：国家是过时的软件，居民是数据，政府是待重写的代码。语法一旦被接受，剩下的只是执行。

所以这不是"技术亿万富翁支持 Trump 因为税和监管"的故事。税和监管是零钱。他们买的是把主权公司化的许可，是让"权力不需要对居民负责"这件事从疯话变成政策草案。Musk 每月 4500 万美元，外加把 Twitter 改造成右翼 misinformation 武器的那 440 亿美元实物投入——这笔账不能只算成选举捐款，它是认知入口的收购价。

报道最后一句说，旧金山也开启了 Harris 的政治生涯，现在她要接手把美国民主从技术威权手里救出来。这个对称很漂亮，但它藏了一个更冷的事实：Yarvin 的方案里没有"选民"这个角色。他的整个设计前提就是，你不需要说服任何人，你只需要接管系统，然后宣布系统是你的。民主能不能救回来，取决于还有没有人相信投票箱比用户协议更硬。

The core of Yarvin's scheme isn't "dictatorship." It's **turning sovereignty into corporate equity**. A nation becomes a realm, a realm is a corporation with absolute power, residents aren't citizens but users, and users don't vote—they relocate. Except every other realm is also a corporate government, so the right to relocate is worth nothing. The most honest line in the whole design: San Francisco can promise not to cut off its residents' hands, but since it's a sovereign power, no one can enforce that promise. Translated: power answers to no one but itself.

This isn't some "Dark Enlightenment." It's corporate governance pushed to its endpoint. What is a corporation? An entity that answers only to shareholders, never to residents. Yarvin just asked: why can't a state work like that? Then he noticed that plenty of states already do—they just keep a thin skin of elections over it. His contribution is to peel the skin off and tell you plainly: once it's gone, you don't even get to complain, because you already accepted the terms of service.

What Vance took from Yarvin isn't the hand-cutting. It's the practical half: purge. De-Baathification, de-woke-ification, fire every single midlevel bureaucrat, replace them with our people, ignore court orders. No philosophy required—that's an operations manual for seizing power. Project 2025 wants to fire an estimated 500,000 federal employees; Vance says he can run the job. Note the structure: first declare the bureaucracy an enemy, then declare the purge a purification, then declare the courts powerless to stop purification. Three steps, and the institution is gone. What's left is people.

The thing to watch in this story isn't Yarvin's madness. It's how Thiel's money moves. A 2011 speech at Yale, Vance sends an email, Thiel brings him to California; Mithril Capital gives him a seat; Vance writes Hillbilly Elegy, Thiel writes the blurb; Narya Capital opens, Thiel and Andreessen fund it; in 2022 Thiel spends an unprecedented $15 million on the Senate race and persuades Trump to endorse; in 2024 Thiel leads the push to make Vance the V.P. pick. Every link in that chain is money, and every link has a return. Vance didn't climb out of Ohio. Vance is a Thiel product, and the product is heading for the White House.

What's actually being bought here isn't influence—Thiel and Musk already have money. They're buying **the power to define**. Who defines "productive." Who defines who gets virtualized. Who defines whether a court order can be ignored. Yarvin supplies the grammar: the state is outdated software, residents are data, government is code awaiting a rewrite. Once the grammar is accepted, only execution remains.

So this isn't a story about tech billionaires backing Trump for tax cuts and deregulation. Taxes are pocket change. They're buying permission to incorporate sovereignty—to move "power owes residents nothing" from crank manifesto to policy draft. Musk's $45 million a month, plus the $44 billion in-kind contribution of gutting Twitter into a right-wing misinformation weapon, shouldn't be booked as campaign donations. That's the purchase price of a cognitive entry point.

The piece ends on a neat symmetry: San Francisco also launched Kamala Harris, who now inherits the job of saving American democracy from tech authoritarianism. It's a beautiful symmetry, and it hides something colder. In Yarvin's design there is no role called "voter." The whole premise is that you don't have to persuade anyone—you take over the system, then declare the system yours. Whether democracy can be saved depends on whether anyone still believes the ballot box is harder than a terms-of-service agreement.
