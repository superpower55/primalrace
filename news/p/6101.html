谁在替爆炸案买单Who Pays for the Blast

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
26条命换来一张合影，这就是共谋的价格。
Twenty-six lives bought one photo op. That's the price of complicity.

一个人炸死了26个人，18年后他穿着剪裁良好的西装，戴着阿尔巴尼亚和美国两面国旗的徽章，在达拉斯的共和党中期大会上和Ken Paxton合影。这不是新闻失职，这是新闻本身。Mediu被阿尔巴尼亚检方指控在2008年Gerdec军火库爆炸案中滥用职权——承包商雇用未经训练的当地工人、其中包括儿童，去拆解实弹，爆炸夷平了几千栋建筑，数百人受伤。他否认有罪，案子至今悬着，卡在议会豁免权的扯皮里。而他已经把自己活成了阿尔巴尼亚反对派在美国右翼的联络人。

加尔通的公式在这里是明码标价的：本可达到的状态是26个人活着、几千栋房子立着、一个国防部长为自己的失职负责；实际达到的状态是一个嫌疑人拿到了进入美国权力走廊的门票。差额就是暴力，而暴力的执行方式不是爆炸，是合影、晚宴、政治献金和一笔75万美元的游说费。

看清楚这条链子怎么转的。Berisha被美国国务院以“重大腐败”为由列入禁止入境名单，他的党雇了Continental Strategy——合伙人里有Rubio的前高级助手、白宫幕僚长Susie Wiles的女儿——去推动解除制裁，报酬75万美元，钱来自一个叫Make Albania Great Again Foundation的非营利组织，这个组织没有提交任何说明资金来源的文件。然后签证豁免下来了，Berisha飞到达拉斯，和RNC主席、Mehmet Oz、Ed Martin一一合影。

这就是第三章说的认知入口：不是买通某一个人，是买通“被看见”这件事本身。合影的功能不是留念，是制造事实——一个被本国检方追诉的人，站在美国司法部长旁边，照片发出去，阿尔巴尼亚国内的读者看到的是“他有靠山”。司法部后来说那只是在酒店大堂偶遇、同意合个影。可这张照片已经生产完了，它的政治效用不因为司法部的澄清而撤回。制造可能性的艺术，从来不需要谎言成立，只需要它先跑一圈。

钱的部分更直白。Paxton今年从新泽西的Ulaj兄弟那里收了Ahmet 2000美元、Astrit 8000美元，两个二十多岁的Arbi和Arlind各5000美元，这几位和Astrit共用一个地址。三兄弟共同拥有一家阿尔巴尼亚媒体公司和亚得里亚海沿岸一个争议豪华开发项目，当地居民抗议开发商非法夺取土地，开发商说合法取得。Astrit的回应是：捐款是私人的、公开申报的、和生意无关。9月9日，Paxton的竞选账户在达拉斯地区记入37000美元，来自六个人。其中一位Dritan Nokshigi对Mother Jones说得很老实：他捐5000美元，只为了参加和Berisha的那顿晚餐，“我不是有钱人”“我从不投票”“政客都一样”。两天后，Berisha和Mediu在他的披萨店拿到了Mesquite市长颁发的城市钥匙。

这不是腐败的例外，这是腐败的语法。一个从不投票的人花5000美元买一顿饭，因为他要的不是政策，是靠近。而靠近是可以被定价、被批发、被转手的。Mediu卖的就是这个：他把自己做成阿尔巴尼亚反对派通往MAGA世界的接口，然后拿这个接口去换自己案子的松动——去年他声称向阿尔巴尼亚法庭提交了六名美国议员的信，信没有公开；2025年6月他见了当时在司法部任职的Aaron Reitz，9月又见了另一名司法部官员，请求对方帮忙。报道没有写这些努力是否奏效，只写他没有放弃。

Paxton这边，道德问题已经排成了队：2012年涉嫌拿走一支1000美元的万宝龙笔（他说是搞混了）；2015年证券欺诈起诉（2024年支付30万美元赔偿后司法部撤诉）；2023年被德州众议院弹劾（州参议院宣告无罪）；2024年跨年夜在迪拜和两名国际逃犯一起狂欢；2015年以来350次出州行程花掉310万美元州财政，2022年那趟欧洲行德州付了近8万美元，其中一站是地拉那，接待人是Mediu。2025年1月，Mediu和房地产商Bashkim Ulaj住进了Paxton州办公室预订的华盛顿酒店房间，那批房间是给特朗普就职典礼和旁听最高法院案件的，同一批房间里还有Paxton的大金主。办公室的说法是：冬季风暴打乱了行程，不可退的房间本来会空着，最终入住的人把钱还给了州政府。那么阿尔巴尼亚人在那里做什么，办公室没有解释。

第四章的共谋者理论在这里不需要论证，它自己走出来了。共谋不分国籍，也不需要一个统一的意识形态：一边是需要美国背书来对冲本国司法的人，一边是需要外国金主和外国合影来装饰自己竞选的人，两边交换的不是信念，是彼此的存在性。回报清晰，代价也清晰——26个死者、几百个伤者、几千栋被毁的房子，他们的家属和阿尔巴尼亚反对党当年指控的“官员失职”，在这条链子上没有位置。报道里没有他们的声音，一个字都没有。

真正刺人的地方在这儿：Mediu不需要赢。他只需要一直出现在照片里，让追诉他的机构相信，动他是有成本的。案子拖了18年，每多拖一年，那26条命在账本上就便宜一点。

而Paxton那条关于1000美元钢笔的旧闻，和这件事被并排写在同一篇报道里，本身就是一个判断：在这个系统里，偷一支笔会被反复提起，26条人命可以被安静地放在括号里。

A man whose negligence was tied to an explosion that killed 26 people spent last month posing for pictures with Ken Paxton at a GOP convention in Dallas, wearing a pin with the Albanian and American flags. This is not a failure of journalism. This is the journalism.

Mediu was Albania's defense minister in March 2008, when a former army barracks at Gerdec packed with excess munitions went up in a series of blasts. Twenty-six dead, hundreds injured, thousands of buildings leveled or damaged. Investigators, including a US ATF team, faulted egregious safety violations: a private contractor using untrained local workers, some of them children, to dismantle live ammunition. Albanian prosecutors charged Mediu with abuse of position over his failure to oversee the contractor. He denies wrongdoing. The case remains unresolved amid wrangling over parliamentary immunity. Meanwhile he has made himself the Albanian opposition's liaison to the American right.

Galtung's formula is priced out in the open here. The potential was 26 people alive, thousands of homes standing, a defense minister answering for his oversight. The actual is a defendant with a security badge and a seat at the donor dinner. The difference is violence, and the instrument is not explosives. It is photographs, dinners, campaign checks, and a $750,000 lobbying contract.

Follow the chain. The State Department put Berisha on a persona non grata list over "involvement in significant corruption." His party retained Continental Strategy LLP, a firm that includes a former top aide to Marco Rubio and the daughter of White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles, to push for removal of the sanctions. Continental has been paid $750,000 for that work, money routed through a nonprofit called the Make Albania Great Again Foundation, which has filed no paperwork detailing its financing. Then came the visa waiver, the flight to Dallas, the photos with the RNC chair, Mehmet Oz, Ed Martin.

This is the cognitive entrance from Chapter 3. You don't buy a man. You buy the fact of being seen. A photograph is not a souvenir; it manufactures a fact. A man under prosecution at home, standing beside the US Attorney General, sends one message back to Albanian readers: he has cover. The Justice Department later said it was a lobby encounter, that the AG merely agreed to pose. The photo has already done its work. Manufacturing possibilities never requires the lie to hold. It only requires the lie to run first.

The money is just as plain. Paxton's campaign took $2,000 from Ahmet Ulaj and $8,000 from Astrit Ulaj of New Jersey, plus $5,000 each from Arbi and Arlind Ulaj, two men in their mid-twenties sharing an address with Astrit. The three brothers jointly own an Albanian media company and ABA Blue Borgo, a contested luxury development on the northern coast where locals have protested illegal land seizure. The developers say the acquisition was legal. Astrit says the donations were personal, publicly reported, and unrelated to business. On September 9, Paxton's campaign recorded $37,000 from six people in the Dallas area. One of them, Dritan Nokshigi, told Mother Jones exactly why he gave $5,000: to attend a dinner with Berisha. "I'm not a rich man," he said. "I never vote. I don't like politicians. They're all the same." Two days later Berisha and Mediu received keys to the city at his pizzeria.

This is not the exception to corruption. This is its grammar. A man who never votes pays $5,000 for a dinner because what he wants is not policy. It is proximity. And proximity can be priced, wholesaled, and resold. That is what Mediu is selling: himself as the interface between the Albanian opposition and MAGA world, and the interface as leverage on his own case. Last year he claimed to have filed a letter from six American congressmen urging Albanian prosecutors not to pursue him; the letter has not been made public. In June 2025 he met Aaron Reitz, then at the Justice Department. In September 2025 he met another DOJ official seeking help. The reporting does not say any of it worked. It says he has not stopped.

Paxton's own ethics file is a queue: the allegedly stolen $1,000 Montblanc pen in 2012 (a mix-up, he says); the 2015 securities fraud indictment (dropped in 2024 after $300,000 in restitution); the 2023 impeachment (acquitted by the state Senate); the Dubai New Year's Eve party with two international fugitives; 350 out-of-state trips since 2015 costing $3.1 million in state funds. The 2022 Europe trip cost Texas nearly $80,000, security detail included, and one stop was Tirana, hosted by Mediu. In January 2025, Mediu and real estate magnate Bashkim Ulaj stayed in Washington hotel rooms booked by Paxton's state office, rooms meant for the inauguration and for watching the AG's office argue before the Supreme Court, in the same block as major Paxton donors. The office says a winter storm disrupted travel, the nonrefundable rooms would have gone unused, and the occupants reimbursed the state. What the Albanian men were doing there, the office has not explained.

Chapter 4 does not need to be argued here. It walks out on its own. Complicity requires no shared ideology, only shared interest: a man who needs American cover against his own judiciary, and a man who needs foreign money and foreign photographs to decorate a Senate run. What they trade is not belief. It is each other's existence. The returns are legible. The costs are also legible: 26 dead, hundreds injured, thousands of homes destroyed, and the families whose allegation of official negligence has no seat at this table. Their voices do not appear in the reporting. Not once.

The sharpest part is this: Mediu does not need to win. He needs only to keep appearing in photographs, so that the body prosecuting him believes touching him carries a cost. The case has dragged for eighteen years. Every additional year makes those 26 lives a little cheaper on the ledger.

And the fact that the $1,000 pen story sits in the same article as all of this is itself the verdict. In this system, a stolen pen gets repeated. Twenty-six deaths get parentheses.
