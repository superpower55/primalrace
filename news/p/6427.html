把敌人造出来，再用它治国Manufacture the Enemy, Then Govern With It

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
先造出敌人，再拿敌人当执照。
First build the enemy; then use it as a license.

这篇报道列出的不是一串失言，是一份政策说明书。Ogles说穆斯林不属于美国社会，Gill说不再让穆斯林移民，Fine说要更多伊斯兰恐惧症而不是更少，Mace说每一个在公职上的穆斯林都是特洛伊木马，Clyde把顺序都排好了：取消归化、驱逐、重复。这不是"红肉"，这是把国家机器对准一群人的操作手册，而且已经在对准了——报道写得很清楚：用国家安全系统对付研究生，因为一座城市里有太多索马里裔而对其发动暴力占领，对穆斯林人口最多的那个州打经济战。

第三章讲的武器化，最有效的入口从来不是骂人，是重新定义谁是威胁。一旦"威胁"成立，剩下的都成了自卫。所以Johnson被问到同僚说伊斯兰是敌人时，回答的是"国内有很多能量、很多民意，认为在美国强加沙里亚法是个严重问题"。你看这个动作：把一群议员的种族主义，翻译成一种"民意"，再把民意翻译成"所以这有道理"。解释权就是这么被拿走的，不需要谁下命令。

报道里最值得盯住的一句是Gill的"伊斯兰 largely alien to American history，它当然不是坐五月花号来的"。这句话的功能不是陈述历史，是划定谁有资格算"遗产美国人"。而事实是第一个有记载的穆斯林比五月花号早到近一个世纪，他是被船运来的被奴役的非洲人，叫Estebanico。共和党人想把自己退回到那些老油画里去，可就算在画框之外，穆斯林也一直在那儿，在盖这个国家。原初种族那套逻辑在这里是同一个模具：先定义谁的身体天然属于这里、谁的身体是入侵物，然后暴力就不像暴力了，像清理。

注意这套话术的语法。Kirk早年还说"激进伊斯兰"，到2025年Mamdani在纽约上位，"激进"两个字和那层伪装一起被他扔了："伊斯兰是左派用来割开美国喉咙的刀。"Mamdani当选本身没有改变任何人的宗教实践，改变的是可见度——一个穆斯林当上了美国最大城市的市长。武器化的触发点从来不是对方做了什么，是对方被看见了。"文明危在旦夕"，"西方未来的战场"，"你输入什么就会变成什么"。一个城市的选举结果被叙述成文明的存亡，那么接下来任何手段都可以叫防卫。

报道里还有一层更冷的机制：说这些话的人不受惩罚。Ogles和Mace要离开国会，但不是因为她们说过的话。Gill是特朗普中期大会的演讲嘉宾。Stefanik，那位在校园里以反宗教歧视斗士形象出名的议员，反复把Mamdani叫作"圣战分子"。Scott被CNN追问时笑着说"我一直被叫Timothy Eugene"。这就是第四章说的共谋：不需要每个人都信，只需要每个人都不付代价。升迁的奖励、沉默的默许、一句玩笑带过，合起来就是把这条线一次次往前推。

报道的作者写了自己2013年那篇文章，说它没经受住时间。那时候把伊斯兰恐惧症挡在CPAC门外还算一件事，Norquist、McCain、Boehner这些人还在，虽然各有各的问题，但这不是他们的热情所在。现在挡的人不在了，或者adapt了。Rogers在密歇根竞选时搬出9/11和本·拉登，全国共和党参议院委员会把对手的名字写成"Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed"。当年被当作边缘的东西，现在是党的目的本身。

特朗普2015年说那道禁令是暂时的，"直到我们的代表搞清楚到底他妈发生了什么"。报道最后一句是：现在是2026年，这就是他们搞清楚之后干的事。

搞清楚的是什么呢。不是安全，不是移民，是一件事：只要一群人可以被叙述成不属于这里，国家暴力就有了永久的执照。而这份执照不会只用在一群人身上。

What this piece lists is not a string of gaffes. It is a policy prospectus. Ogles says Muslims don't belong in American society. Gill says no more Muslim immigration. Fine says we need more Islamophobia, not less. Mace says every Muslim holding public office is a Trojan horse. Clyde has the sequence ready: denaturalize, deport, repeat. This isn't red meat for the base; it's an operating manual for pointing the state at a group of people—and it's already pointed. The reporting is explicit: the national-security apparatus aimed at grad students, a violent occupation of an American city because there are too many Somali Americans, economic warfare against the state where many of them live.

The most effective entry point for weaponized expression was never insults. It's redefining who counts as a threat. Once the threat is established, everything else becomes self-defense. So when Johnson is asked about his colleagues calling Islam the enemy, he answers that there's a lot of energy in the country, a lot of popular sentiment that imposing Shariah law is a serious problem—that's what animates this. Watch the move: the bigotry of a group of congressmen gets translated into public sentiment, and public sentiment into justification. That's how the power to interpret gets taken. Nobody has to issue an order.

The line to hold onto is Gill's: Islam is largely alien to American history, certainly didn't come over on the Mayflower. The function of that sentence isn't history. It's drawing the border of who gets to be a Heritage American. The first known Muslim arrived nearly a century before the Mayflower—an enslaved African named Estebanico, brought on a ship. Republicans want to roll the country back to the old paintings they pass around. But even outside the frame, Muslims were always there, building the thing. This is the same mold as the Primal Race: first define whose body naturally belongs and whose body is an intrusion, and then violence stops looking like violence. It looks like cleaning.

Notice the grammar. Kirk used to say radical Islam. By 2025, as Mamdani rose in New York, he dropped both the radical and the pretense: Islam is the sword the left is using to slit the throat of America. Mamdani's election changed nobody's religious practice. What changed was visibility—a Muslim became mayor of the largest city. The trigger for weaponization is never what the other side does. It's that the other side becomes seen. Civilization's on the line. Battlefield for the future of the West. You become what you import. Once a city election is narrated as civilizational survival, any means can be called defense.

There's a colder mechanism underneath: saying these things costs nothing. Ogles and Mace are leaving Congress, but not because of what they said about Muslims. Gill was a featured speaker at Trump's midterm convention. Stefanik, the nationally recognized crusader against religious discrimination on campus, repeatedly calls Mamdani a jihadist. Scott, pressed on CNN, smiles and says he gets called Timothy Eugene all the time. This is the co-conspirator structure: nobody has to believe it. They only have to pay no price. Promotions, silences, a joke that closes the subject—together they push the line forward, one notch at a time.

The reporter notes that a piece he wrote in 2013 did not age well. Back then, keeping the biggest Islamophobes out of CPAC was still a thing. Norquist, McCain, Boehner were flawed, but this wasn't their passion. Now the people who pushed back are gone, or they adapted. Rogers invokes 9/11 and bin Laden in his campaign; the National Republican Senatorial Committee writes the opponent's name as Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed. What was fringe is now the party's purpose.

In 2015 Trump called the Muslim ban a placeholder, until our representatives can figure out what the hell is going on. The piece ends: it's 2026 now. This is what happens when they finally figure it out.

What did they figure out? Not security. Not immigration. One thing: once a group can be narrated as not belonging here, state violence gets a permanent license. And that license never stays pointed at just one group.
