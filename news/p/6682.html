一杯抹茶拿铁里的定价权Pricing Power in a Matcha Latte

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要阿根廷歌手Emilia在迈阿密接受Billboard采访，谈到新单曲「Bodypainting」、在MP3之后推出下一张专辑的压力、与Zara Larsson合作「Girl's Girl (Girls Trip)」的经过、在Coachella与Luísa Sonza同台，以及她如何靠健身和在故乡Nogoyá的生活保持状态。
The newsArgentine singer Emilia spoke to Billboard in Miami about her new single "Bodypainting," the pressure of following up MP3, how her Zara Larsson collaboration "Girl's Girl (Girls Trip)" came together, performing at Coachella with Luísa Sonza, and staying grounded in her hometown of Nogoyá.
她的身体是产品，她的松弛是卖点，采访只是包装纸。
Her body is the product, her ease is the selling point, and the interview is just the wrapping.

这篇采访里唯一真实的动作是点单：一杯提拉米苏拿铁要skinny，一杯冰抹茶拿铁换燕麦奶。其余全是产品说明。

「Bodypainting」这个歌名已经说明了这门生意的入口在哪——身体作为画布，作为被涂抹、被观看、被定价的表面。采访里她说健身是therapy，说自己在Nogoyá保持grounded，这两句被并置在一起，功能是同一个：把一个正在出售的身体包装成「她自己的选择」「她自己的生活哲学」。读者看到的是自律和松弛，看不到的是这套自律背后谁在定标准。

「Girl's Girl (Girls Trip)」的合作经过被她讲成一个惊喜：Zara Larsson的团队发来邮件，她不敢相信。这个叙事很可爱，也很有效——它把一次商业计算讲成了命运的安排，把两个女性歌手之间的合作讲成姐妹情。女性之间的联盟当然可以是真的，但联盟一旦进入产业，它同时就是一次双方粉丝盘的合并，一次注意力的置换。采访没有问这次合作谁让了多少，只问了它怎么发生的。

真正值得看的是采访结构本身。Billboard派来的记者和她喝咖啡、聊健身、聊时装周，最后端出「Hot Coffee Pot」这种问题：你会不会吃醋，你用不用酒店化名。一个正在发新单曲、筹备新专辑、上过Coachella的歌手，被访问的落点是她的嫉妒心和她的假名。这是典型的feminine采访格式——男性音乐人谈的是创作方法、产业判断、下一张专辑要突破什么，女性音乐人被问的是她怎么保持身材、她的感情是否稳定。她的表达被收窄成一个可消费的人设，而人设的定价权不在她手里。

她说MP3之后有压力，要level up。这句话是整篇里最接近真实的一句，但它被埋在了抹茶和化名之间。压力来自哪里、level up的标准是谁定的、这个标准奖励什么样的女性表达——这些问题采访一个都没碰。

一杯skinny的提拉米苏拿铁，一杯换燕麦奶的抹茶。她在点单时精确控制着进入自己身体的东西，而这篇采访精确控制着进入公众视野的她。两件事同时发生，没有一句提问指向后者。

The only real action in this interview is the ordering: a tiramisu latte made skinny, an iced matcha with oat milk. Everything else is product copy.

The song is called "Bodypainting." The title already names the entry point of the business—the body as canvas, as a surface to be painted, watched, and priced. In the interview she says fitness is her therapy and that she stays grounded in Nogoyá. Placed side by side, the two lines do the same job: they wrap a body currently for sale in the language of her own choice, her own philosophy. The reader sees discipline and ease. The reader does not see who sets the standard that discipline is measured against.

She tells the Zara Larsson collaboration as a surprise—an email from the other team, and she couldn't believe it. Cute, and effective: it turns a commercial calculation into fate, and a deal between two women singers into sisterhood. Alliances between women can be real. But once an alliance enters the industry, it is also a merger of two fan bases, a trade in attention. The interview never asks who conceded what. It only asks how it happened.

What's worth watching is the structure of the interview itself. Billboard sends a reporter to drink coffee with her, talk fitness, talk fashion week, and then serve up "Hot Coffee Pot" questions: Do you get jealous? Do you use a hotel alias? A singer releasing a single, preparing an album, who has played Coachella, is finally asked about her jealousy and her fake name. This is the standard feminine interview format. Male musicians get asked about craft, industry judgment, what the next record breaks open. Women musicians get asked how they stay in shape and whether their love life is stable. Her expression gets narrowed into a consumable persona, and the pricing power over that persona is not hers.

She says there is pressure after MP3, that she has to level up. That is the closest thing to a true sentence in the whole piece, and it sits buried between the matcha and the alias. Where the pressure comes from, who sets the standard for leveling up, what kind of feminine expression that standard rewards—the interview touches none of it.

A skinny tiramisu latte, a matcha with oat milk. While ordering, she precisely controls what enters her body. The interview precisely controls what enters public view of her. Both happen at once, and not one question points at the second.
