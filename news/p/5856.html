战争账单上的龙虾The Lobster on the War Bill

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
普通人付代价，解释权在共谋者手里
Ordinary people pay; the co-conspirators hold the解释权.

缅因州的龙虾渔民 LaMontagne 去年捕四十只龙虾够加满一箱油，今年要捕一百零四只。油箱的价格从每周三百五十美元涨到近七百美元。报道把原因写得很清楚：伊朗战争和关税。这是他付的账，不是他做的决定。

再往下看，一个七十七岁的退休校长说，她灌了一百三十一加仑取暖油，花了七百二十九美元，油箱还没满，她说她害怕。缅因州近一半家庭靠取暖油过冬，均价超过六美元一加仑，创了纪录。战争和关税的成本，就这样一层层压到最没有议价能力的人身上——加尔通那个公式里，被削掉的那部分，就是暴力。

但这场选举真正的机制不在油价，而在解释权。Collins 在竞选中拿出的政绩清单是：五角大楼给巴斯钢铁厂的合同、释放三十万桶取暖油、给缅因产业争取到的关税豁免。每一条都是真的，每一条也都是她参与的这套秩序在制造问题之后，再从指缝里漏一点回来。她反对了那项削减 Medicaid 的法案最终通过，却支持了让它进入参议院的关键程序性投票。纽约时报的分析说，她与本党决裂的比例是百分之十七，和第一个任期差不多，但这些决裂很少真正拖慢总统的议程。

这就是共谋者理论的教科书样本：不需要她百分之百站队，只需要她在关键节点上让路，然后在选区里以“独立”的形象收账。回报是什么？是第六个任期。代价是谁付？是那位说“我害怕”的七十七岁老人，是那位要多捕六十四只龙虾的二十七岁渔民。

Jackson 的竞选语言是“我关心普通人的家庭”，他把自己包装成工人阶级的替代选项。但报道里最诚实的一句话来自他的支持者 Cellucci：她不是冲着他来的，“说实话，Troy 不会是我的第一选择”，她只是要投给任何一个能刹住特朗普的民主党人。另一位选民说得更直接：Jackson 在某种程度上几乎无关紧要，问题在于选民能不能忍受再给特朗普一票。

一场参议院选举，候选人成了次要变量，这是表达被武器化到极致的状态——选票不再是选择谁来代表你，而是选择谁来挡谁。挡得住挡不住，另说。

报道里还有一处值得停一下。前民主党候选人 Platner 因为一系列争议退选，最后一根稻草是性侵和强奸指控，他否认。这条信息在文中只占一句，但它说明了一件事：一个政党在推举谁、在什么条件下放弃谁，这套筛选机制本身就是权力分配的一部分，而它和油价、和关税一样，最终由选民承担后果。

LaMontagne 还没决定。他说现状很烂，说 Collins 确实是龙虾业的盟友，说投 Jackson 需要“一次信心的跳跃”。他要的不是更好的政策，是一个值得信任的人。这个需求本身，就是被这套秩序训练出来的——当结构性的问题无法在选票上被解决，人们只能把希望寄托在某个人的品性上。

那位退休校长说，Collins 在国会三十年，“够了”。她这句话比任何民调都准。但“够了”之后呢？报道没写，因为报道也回答不了。

Last year, forty lobsters filled LaMontagne's tank. This year it takes over a hundred and four. His fuel bill went from about $350 a week to nearly $700. The Guardian names the cause plainly: the war with Iran and the tariffs. He pays the bill. He did not make the decision.

Further down, a 77-year-old retired school principal put 131 gallons of heating oil into her tank, spent $729, and it wasn't even full. She says she's scared. Nearly half of Maine households heat with oil; the average gallon now tops $6, a record. The cost of war and tariffs gets pressed down, layer by layer, onto the people with the least bargaining power. That gap between what is and what could be is the violence.

But the real mechanism of this race isn't the price of oil. It's who gets to explain it. Collins's case for herself is a list: a Pentagon contract for Bath Iron Works, 300,000 barrels of heating oil released, tariff exemptions for Maine industries. Every item is real. Every item is the system that created the problem leaking a little back through its fingers. She opposed the final passage of the Medicaid-cutting bill, then backed the procedural vote that let it reach the floor. The Times analysis found she breaks with her party 17% of the time, roughly her usual rate, and those breaks have rarely slowed the president's agenda.

This is the co-conspirator playbook in its purest form: you don't need to vote with him every time. You need to step aside at the key node, then collect the credit back home as an independent. The reward is a sixth term. The cost is paid by the woman who says she's scared and the fisherman who has to haul sixty-four more lobsters.

Jackson's pitch is that he cares about people's families. But the most honest line in the piece comes from his own supporter, Cellucci: she wasn't voting for him, exactly. "Troy would not have been my first pick." She'd vote for almost any Democrat who could put brakes on Trump. Another voter says Jackson seems almost irrelevant; the question is whether people can tolerate giving Trump one more Senate vote.

When the candidate becomes the secondary variable, expression has been weaponized to its limit. The ballot stops being a choice of who represents you and becomes a choice of who blocks whom. Whether the block holds is another matter.

One more thing worth pausing on. The former Democratic nominee, Platner, withdrew after a series of controversies, the last being a sexual assault and rape allegation he denied. That gets one sentence. But it tells you something: who a party promotes, and under what conditions it drops them, is itself a distribution of power, and the voters absorb the consequences just as they absorb the fuel bill.

LaMontagne hasn't decided. He says the status quo sucks, says Collins really is an ally of the lobster fishery, says voting for Jackson would take a leap of faith. He isn't asking for better policy. He's asking for a person he can trust. That need is itself a product of the order he's stuck in: when structural problems can't be solved at the ballot, people pin their hopes on character.

The retired principal says thirty years in Congress is enough. That line is more accurate than any poll. What comes after "enough" the report doesn't say, because it can't.
