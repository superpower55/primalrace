赦免是货，价钱谈崩了The Pardon Was For Sale; the Cut Wasn't His

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要特朗普在 Mar-a-Lago 万圣节活动上对记者说，前私人律师 Alina Habba Mildon 为一名委内瑞拉亿万富翁家族争取赦免而收取 5000 万美元是「完全令人作呕、不可接受」，并表示她本应把钱捐给他的总统图书馆。他同时说，他期待的捐款对象「不是她」。
The newsAt a Mar-a-Lago Halloween event, Trump told reporters that his former personal attorney Alina Habba Mildon's reported $50 million contract to secure a pardon for a Venezuelan billionaire's family was 「totally obnoxious and outrageous,」 and said she should have donated the money to his presidential library. He added that she was not the person he expected a donation from.
他骂的不是腐败，是抽成没到他账上。
He's not angry about corruption. He's angry the cut didn't reach his account.

特朗普骂 Alina Habba Mildon 的那 5000 万美元「完全令人作呕、不可接受」。这话从一个刚把赦免当政治回报发出去的人嘴里说出来，本身就是这场交易最诚实的一部分：赦免是可以定价的，问题只在于谁定价、谁收钱。

他的原话里有一句漏了底——「我不想要她拿钱」。不是不想要有人拿钱，是不想要她拿。他说他期待捐款的对象「不是她」，等于把价目表当众摊开：这笔钱该流进他的总统图书馆，或者「任何想捐的人」。赦免在这里不是权力，是货；Habba 不是律师，是没交份子钱的中间商。

报道没写 Habba 怎么回应，没写那 5000 万最后去了哪，也没写那位委内瑞拉亿万富翁的赦免到底批没批。这些空白不是报道的疏漏，是这套交易的结构：赦免的价格从来不在台面上，只在事后被人不小心说漏嘴。

真正被定价的从来不是总统的签名。是那个签名的买家以为自己在买法律，其实买的是一个随时可以被公开骂一顿、再被要求补交捐款的席位。Habba 拿到钱的那一刻，就已经不是共谋者，是共谋者里没分匀的那一个。

特朗普说「一个人拿法律费用是一回事」——他把这条线划得很清楚：律师费可以拿，权力抽成不行，除非抽成的人是他自己。这条线不是道德，是账本。

Trump called Alina Habba Mildon's reported $50 million contract to secure a pardon 「totally obnoxious and outrageous, not acceptable—at all.」 Coming from a man who has openly admitted trading a pardon for political support, that's the most honest part of the whole transaction: pardons have a price. The only question is who sets it and who collects.

One line gives it away: 「I don't want her paid.」 Not nobody should be paid. Not her. He said the person he expected a donation from was 「not her,」 and left the door open for 「anybody who wants to make it」—meaning his presidential library, or anything attached to him. The pardon isn't power here. It's merchandise. Habba isn't a lawyer. She's a middleman who forgot to pay dues.

The report doesn't say how Habba responded, where the $50 million went, or whether the Venezuelan billionaire's pardon was ever granted. Those blanks aren't gaps in the reporting. They're the structure of the trade: the price of a pardon never sits on the table until someone says it out loud by accident.

What got priced was never the president's signature. It was the buyer's belief that he was purchasing law, when what he bought was a seat that can be publicly scolded and invoiced for a donation at any moment. The moment Habba took the money, she stopped being a co-conspirator and became the co-conspirator who didn't get a fair share.

Trump drew the line himself: legal fees are one thing. A cut of the power is another—unless the one taking the cut is him. That's not a moral line. It's a ledger.
