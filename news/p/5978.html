道歉视频挡不住起诉书An Apology Video Doesn't Answer an Indictment

文娱 直接层 Vulture ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
律师教她少说，检察官替她说完了
Her lawyer told her to say less. The prosecutor will say the rest.

Selma Blair 发了一段道歉视频，开头先说律师建议她少讲。这句话本身就是整件事的缩影：一个人在法庭上被两名二级攻击罪指控，在 Instagram 上被自己的法律团队按住了嘴。

报道列出的指控很具体：攻击两名空乘，咬了其中一人；飞行途中试图打开舱门；飞机落地后被拘押期间踢了一名警察和一名护士。这不是情绪失控的模糊描述，是一份有时间、有对象、有动作的清单。

她的律师在传讯时给出的解释是：她少服了一种重要药物，又在机上撞到了隔板，加上高空气压，导致严重的神志混乱和定向障碍。这个说法能不能成立，要看证据。但值得注意的是它的结构——把行为归因于身体状态，而不是意图。Blair 自己在视频里说的也是同一件事：她这一生中没有一天想过要伤害或惊吓任何人。

两套说法指向同一个方向：我不是那个人。

这里有一个真实的医学问题，也有一个真实的暴力问题，它们不互相取消。一个处于严重定向障碍中的人，确实可能做出她清醒时绝不会做的事；同时，被咬、被踢、在飞行途中面对有人试图开舱门的空乘和警察，承受的是实实在在的直接暴力，不管施暴者当时脑子里在想什么。

报道没有写那两名空乘和那名护士后来怎么样了。没有写他们是否受伤、是否停工、是否被列为受害者。整篇报道的叙事重心在 Blair 身上：她的道歉、她的律师、她的下一次开庭日期。这是名人新闻的常规操作，但常规操作本身就是一种分配——注意力被分配给有名的那一方，承受暴力的一方留在句子的宾语位置上。

Blair 的道歉里有一个词值得停一下：她向“失望的、被伤害的、被惊吓的、被惊动的人”道歉。这四个词涵盖了粉丝、公众、旁观者，也顺带涵盖了那几个人。但道歉的对象越宽泛，责任就越稀薄。对被她咬的那名空乘，需要的不是一段视频，是法庭上的东西。

下一次开庭是 1 月 19 日。在那之前，视频会一直在，起诉书也会一直在。

Selma Blair posted an apology video that opens with her saying her lawyers advised her not to say much. That sentence is the whole story in miniature: a person facing two counts of second-degree assault, with her own legal team holding her mouth shut on Instagram.

The charges in the report are specific: attacking two flight attendants, biting one of them; trying to open the plane doors mid-flight; kicking a police officer and a nurse while detained after landing. This is not a vague description of a meltdown. It is a list with times, targets, and actions.

Her lawyer's explanation at arraignment: she had gone without an important medication, had hit her head on the plane's bulkhead, and the altitude compounded it into severe confusion and disorientation. Whether that holds up is a question for evidence. What matters here is the structure of the argument — it attributes the behavior to a bodily state, not to intent. Blair says the same thing in her video: not one day in her life had she intended to hurt or frighten anyone.

Both accounts point the same way: I was not that person.

There is a real medical question here and a real violence question, and they do not cancel each other out. A person in severe disorientation may genuinely do what she would never do lucid. At the same time, the flight attendants and the officer who were bitten and kicked, the people facing someone trying to open a cabin door in flight, absorbed direct violence regardless of what was going on in the perpetrator's head.

The report does not say what happened to the two flight attendants or the nurse. Not whether they were injured, not whether they lost work, not whether they are listed as victims. The narrative weight sits entirely on Blair: her apology, her lawyer, her next court date. That is standard celebrity coverage, and standard coverage is itself a distribution — attention goes to the famous party, and the people who took the violence stay in the object position of the sentence.

One word in Blair's apology is worth pausing on: she apologizes to the people she disappointed, hurt, frightened, alarmed. Four words that cover fans, the public, bystanders, and incidentally those few. The broader the apology, the thinner the responsibility. What the flight attendant she bit needs is not a video. It is what happens in court.

The next hearing is January 19. Until then, the video stays up, and so does the complaint.
