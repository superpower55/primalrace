替天行道的剧本，法律不认Vigilante Fantasy, Not Justice

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
把他供成神，等于替他把杀人的账抹平
Making him a god erases the murder he's accused of.

Luigi Mangione 的律师在打一场程序仗：联邦那两项跟踪罪他已经认了，州里的谋杀罪就不该再审一遍，纽约州法写着"同一行为不得被分别起诉"。检察官说跟踪罪和谋杀罪不重叠，法官把庭期排到了 12 月 10 日。这些都是法律技术问题。

真正值得看的是这套技术问题外面包着的那层叙事。Thompson 在曼哈顿一家酒店外被杀，全美对营利医疗的怒火跟着烧起来——这个愤怒是真的，被保险公司拒赔、拖死的人是真的。但愤怒被接到了一张脸上：一个长得好看的、被指控杀人的男人。于是舆论开始给他洗地，审讯还没结束就把他英雄化了，用他自己的话说都不需要，别人替他说了。这在书里叫偶像产业的情感入口：女性（这里也包括所有被医疗系统坑过的人）被训练出的情感投入模式，被精准地导向一个男性偶像，判断力让渡给了信仰。粉丝维护"神"的本能压过了最基本的是非。

他的律师在法庭上争的是"同一个行为不能被审两次"，公众在舆论场上争的是"他是不是替我们出了口气"。前者是程序，后者是神话，两者互不干扰，甚至互相喂养——每一次出庭都是新一轮造神。

这里没有受害者缺席的问题：Thompson 死了，这是报道里写着的。但报道里没有写的是，那些真正被营利医疗拖垮的人，他们的愤怒被谁拿去用了。愤怒被一个杀人嫌疑人代言，等于愤怒本身被消音——你骂保险公司，最后骂出了一尊神像。

州法能不能挡住第二次起诉，12 月 10 日见分晓。但无论法官怎么判，有一件事已经判完了：一个被指控杀人的人，靠一张脸和一套"劫富济贫"的想象，把自己从被告席上搬到了神坛上。这不是正义，这是共谋——所有替他洗地的人，都在用他的脸替自己消化对医疗系统的恨，而那个恨本来该指向制度，不该指向一个嫌疑人。

Mangione's lawyers are fighting a procedural battle: he has already pleaded guilty to two federal stalking charges, so the state murder case, they argue, would prosecute him twice for the same act—and New York statute says a person may not be separately prosecuted for two offenses based on the same criminal transaction. Prosecutors say stalking and murder don't overlap. The judge set the next date for 10 December. All of this is legal technique.

What's worth watching is the narrative wrapped around that technique. Thompson was killed outside a Manhattan hotel, and the rage against for-profit healthcare caught fire—that rage is real. People denied claims, people dead because a bottom line came first: real. But the rage got attached to a face: a good-looking man accused of murder. So the discourse started scrubbing him clean, heroizing him before the trial even ended. This is the emotional entry point of the idol industry: a trained pattern of emotional investment gets redirected onto a male idol, and judgment is handed over to faith. The instinct to protect the god overrides basic right and wrong.

His lawyers argue in court that one act can't be tried twice. The public argues in the discourse that he settled a score for us. The first is procedure, the second is myth, and they feed each other—every hearing is a new round of canonization.

The victim is not absent here: Thompson is dead, and the report says so. What the report doesn't say is who took the rage of the people actually crushed by for-profit healthcare. That rage got spoken for by a murder suspect, which means the rage itself got muted—you curse the insurance company and end up with a shrine.

Whether state law blocks a second prosecution will be decided on 10 December. But one verdict is already in: a man accused of murder, through a handsome face and a Robin Hood fantasy, has been moved from the defendant's dock to the altar. That isn't justice. It's complicity—everyone scrubbing him clean is using his face to digest their hatred of the healthcare system, when that hatred should point at the institution, not at a suspect.
