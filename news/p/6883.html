谁在背叛，谁在定义背叛Who Betrays, Who Defines Betrayal

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要密歇根州民主党参议院初选落败的众议员海莉·史蒂文斯发布视频，背书共和党候选人迈克·罗杰斯，对阵民主党提名人阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德。她此前曾称罗杰斯是「特朗普危险议程的橡皮图章」。民调显示埃尔-赛义德在密歇根民主党选民中支持率约95%，参议院少数党领袖舒默称此举是「可鄙的背叛」。
The newsHaley Stevens, who lost Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, released a video endorsing Republican Mike Rogers over Democratic nominee Abdul El-Sayed. She had previously called Rogers a "rubber stamp for Donald Trump's dangerous agenda." Polls show El-Sayed backed by 95 percent of Michigan Democratic voters; Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer called the endorsement "a despicable betrayal."
「温和」是标签，「背叛」是判断，两样都是解释权的产物。
"Moderate" is a label. "Betrayal" is a judgment. Both are products of who holds the naming rights.

史蒂文斯输了初选，然后录了一段视频，在车里，用手机，角度歪着，宣布背书共和党人。The Intercept 的定性是「为以色列而背叛」。这个定性没错，但它停在了半路。

她此前说罗杰斯是「特朗普危险议程的橡皮图章」，现在她说「我不属于阿卜杜勒的竞选」。两句话之间没有逻辑，只有站队。站队的回报是什么，报道没写。谁给她算的这笔账，报道也没写。一个初选落败的众议员，在选举前两天把票往对面推，这不是个人情绪，这是把表达当武器用出去——武器化的表达不需要自己赢，只需要让对手输。

报道说这是「名义上的中间派才是真正的极端分子」，这句话是对的，但它漏了一层：所谓「中间」，从来不是位置，是定价。谁被叫作中间派，谁被叫作激进左翼，取决于谁握着标尺。埃尔-赛义德拿到九成五的民主党选民支持，仍然被要求「向以色列支持者作出更大姿态」——这个要求跟选民数量无关，跟定价权有关。

舒默骂她「可鄙的背叛」，报道说这反映了政治风向在变。我同意风向在变，但舒默没有变：他仍然支持以色列的军事行动，仍然反对党内左翼。他骂史蒂文斯，不是因为她的立场错了，是因为她把党内矛盾摆到了台面上，在关键选举前两天。这是共谋者之间的纪律问题，不是原则问题。共谋者不反对共谋，只反对共谋者把账算到台面上。

报道说史蒂文斯的举动「不太可能对周二的选举结果产生重大影响」，又说在势均力敌的选战里，拉走任何一点支持都暴露出所谓中间的极端性。这两句放在一起，等于承认：她的实际影响可能很小，但她的象征影响很大。象征影响就是解释权的影响——她用一次背书告诉所有人，民主党内部谁才是可以被牺牲的那一方。

埃尔-赛义德说「这些都不重要」，然后去谈学校、医疗、食品价格。这是正确的应对，也是无力的应对：他只能谈政策，因为叙事战场已经被别人占了。他谈的是密歇根人的日子，别人谈的是他够不够格。两套语言不在同一个层面上，一套是生活，一套是资格审查。

报道的结尾说，希望史蒂文斯这样的立场在到达最极端版本之前就被视为负资产。这个希望很温和，温和得像它批评的那些人。真正的问题不是史蒂文斯会不会被惩罚，是谁有权力定义「背叛」这个词——舒默用它，是因为他还能用；等他用不动的时候，这个词会换一个主人，但不会消失。

Stevens lost the primary, then recorded a video, in her car, on her phone, at an awkward angle, endorsing the Republican. The Intercept calls it a betrayal for Israel. That's accurate, but it stops halfway.

She had called Rogers a "rubber stamp for Donald Trump's dangerous agenda." Now she says, "I don't belong in Abdul's campaign." There is no logic between those two sentences, only a side. What the side pays, the report doesn't say. Who did the math for her, the report doesn't say either. A primary loser pushing votes to the other column two days before the election isn't a mood. It's expression used as a weapon. A weaponized expression doesn't need to win. It only needs the other side to lose.

The report says the so-called centrists are the real radicals. True, but it misses a layer: "the center" was never a location. It's a price. Who gets called moderate and who gets called radical left depends on who holds the ruler. El-Sayed has 95 percent of Democratic voters and is still told to make "greater overtures to Israel supporters." That demand has nothing to do with voter counts. It has everything to do with who sets the price.

Schumer calls her endorsement "a despicable betrayal," and the report reads this as a shifting wind. The wind is shifting. Schumer isn't. He still backs Israel's military campaign, still fights the party's left. He isn't angry that her position is wrong. He's angry that she put the internal fight on the table two days before a key race. That's discipline among co-conspirators, not principle. Co-conspirators don't oppose the conspiracy. They oppose putting the ledger on the table.

The report says her theatrics are "unlikely to make a significant difference" to Tuesday's results, then says that in a close race, pulling any support away reveals the extremism of the purported center. Put together, that admits her practical impact may be small while her symbolic impact is large. Symbolic impact is the impact of explanatory power. With one endorsement she told everyone which side of the party can be sacrificed.

El-Sayed says "none of this matters" and talks about schools, healthcare, food prices. That's the right response and the powerless one: he can only talk policy because the narrative battlefield is already occupied. He talks about Michigan lives. Others talk about whether he's qualified. Two languages on two floors. One is life. The other is a vetting.

The report ends hoping positions like Stevens's are seen as a liability before they reach their most extreme iterations. That hope is mild, mild like the people it criticizes. The real question isn't whether Stevens gets punished. It's who has the power to define the word "betrayal." Schumer uses it because he still can. When he can't, the word will change hands. It won't disappear.
