Ridley Scott 的“阳刚之气”是一场过时的 ScamRidley Scott’s ‘Masculinity’ is an Obsolete Scam

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-02 § 链接
所谓的“不够阳刚”不过是元暴力在审美层面的权力审计。
The ‘not masculine enough’ critique is merely a power audit of meta-violence in aesthetics.

Ridley Scott 对 Paul Mescal 的不满简直是教科书级别的 meta violence。一个快 90 岁的老男人，试图用 2000 年前罗马角斗士的刻板印象来审计 2026 年的男性表达。在他眼里，阳刚之气 (masculinity) 不是一种特质，而是一套必须执行的指令：要像 Russell Crowe 那样用额头开门，要把悲伤转化为暴力，要把所有复杂的情绪简化为一次停车场斗殴。

这本质上是一场关于“解释权”的博弈。Scott 试图定义什么是“真正的男人”，而 Mescal 的磁场在于他敢于在男性表达中注入灰色地带——悲伤、脆弱、敏感。在元暴力的逻辑里，这种对主体性的挖掘被视为“不够阳刚”，因为这种表达削弱了男性作为“钝器”的纯粹性，从而威胁到了那种由男性中心叙事构建的权力美学。

最讽刺的是，电影的失败被归咎于演员“不够阳刚”，而不是剧本的空洞或导演对现代认知的脱节。这是一种典型的 weaponized narrative：当产品本身失败时，通过攻击一个人的“身份表达”来转移矛盾。将商业 flop 转化为一场关于 masculinity 的定义权战争，让观众在讨论“谁更像男人”的陷阱里打转，而忽略了那个 90 岁导演其实是在用过时的生物墙思维在指挥一个现代灵魂。

所谓的“正确工具”并不存在，存在的只有谁在定义工具。如果一个角色必须通过“砍碎对方”而非“像成年人一样反思”来证明阳刚，那么这种阳刚本身就是一种结构性暴力。Scott 想要的不是演员，而是一个能够完美执行父权指令的生物样本。

Ridley Scott’s dissatisfaction with Paul Mescal is a textbook example of meta-violence. A man nearly 90 is attempting to audit 2026’s male expression using a Roman gladiator stereotype from two millennia ago. In his eyes, masculinity is not a trait but a set of commands: open doors with your forehead like Russell Crowe, translate grief into violence, and flatten all complex emotions into a car park brawl.

This is essentially a game for the right of interpretation. Scott tries to define the ‘real man,’ while Mescal’s magnetism lies in his courage to introduce grey areas into masculine expression—sadness, vulnerability, sensitivity. Within the logic of meta-violence, this excavation of subjectivity is labeled as ‘not masculine enough’ because it dilutes the purity of the male as a ‘blunt instrument,’ thereby threatening the power aesthetics constructed by the masculine-centric narrative.

The irony is that the film’s failure is blamed on the actor’s lack of masculinity rather than a hollow script or the director’s cognitive lag. This is a classic weaponized narrative: when a product fails, shift the blame by attacking an individual’s expression of identity. By transforming a commercial flop into a war over the definition of masculinity, the audience is lured into a trap of debating ‘who is more of a man,’ ignoring that a 90-year-old director is simply using an obsolete biological wall to direct a modern soul.

There is no such thing as the ‘right tool’; there is only who defines the tool. If a character must prove his masculinity by ‘chopping someone to shreds’ rather than ‘reflecting like an adult,’ then that masculinity is itself a form of structural violence. Scott doesn’t want an actor; he wants a biological specimen capable of perfectly executing patriarchal commands.
