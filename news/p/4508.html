肌肉、紧身衣与民主党的“性吸引力”骗局Biceps, Tight Tees, and the Democrats' Sex Appeal Scam

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
用男性身体的性化掩盖政治能力的缺失，是元暴力的审美升级。
Using the sexualization of the male body to mask political incompetence is just an aesthetic upgrade of meta-violence.

民主党试图通过在 TikTok 上投放“thirst traps”来挽救其糟糕的公众形象，这本质上是一次极其拙劣的表达武器化尝试。当他们意识到技术官僚的 PowerPoint 无法赢得选票时，决定祭出最古老的认知入口：男性身体的性吸引力。通过展示 Abdul El-Sayed 的肱二头肌和紧身黑 T 恤，民主党试图将政治博弈简化为一场视觉消费，这实际上是在承认：我们的 philosophy 无法说服你，但我们的肌肉可以诱惑你。

这里最值得警惕的是这种“vibe shift”背后的性别共谋。Axios 明确指出，民主党“有意选择展示最迷人的男性成员”。在这种叙事中，男性身体被塑造为一种权力与魅力的符号，而像 AOC 这样的女性政治家则被简化为“漂亮的微笑”和“红唇”。这再次证明了男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 的统治力：即便在自称进步的民主党内部，权力的表达依然锚定在男性的生物墙之上。男性通过展示肌肉来获得“强力”的暗示，而女性则被要求在“美丽”的客体框架内寻找空间。

这根本不是什么“学习特朗普”的娱乐化政治，而是一次典型的 cultural violence。它在潜意识中向选民传递一个信号：政治领袖的合法性部分来自于其表型 (Phenotype) 的优越感。当政治表达从“如何解决问题”退化为“谁看起来更 Hot”时，这种表达就成了掩盖 structural violence 的遮羞布。用性吸引力去赢取“低信息量选民”的票数，是对民主最深刻的嘲讽。

所谓的“性销售” (Sex sells) 永远是一场 scam。它让人们在多巴胺的冲动中忘记审计权力者的真实能力。如果一个政党需要靠紧身衣来证明自己没有“脱离群众”，那么它真正缺失的不是 sex appeal，而是与现实世界建立公正表达的能力。

The Democrats' attempt to rescue their image via TikTok "thirst traps" is a clumsy exercise in the weaponisation of expression. Realizing that technocratic PowerPoint presentations fail to win votes, they have pivoted to the oldest cognitive entry point: the sexual appeal of the male body. By showcasing Abdul El-Sayed’s biceps in tight black T-shirts, the party is reducing political discourse to visual consumption. It is a tacit admission: our philosophy cannot convince you, but our muscles might seduce you.

What is most alarming here is the gendered complicity driving this "vibe shift." Axios explicitly notes an "intentional choice to feature the most attractive male members." In this narrative, the male body is constructed as a symbol of power and charisma, while female politicians like AOC are reduced to a "pretty smile" and "red lipstick." This reinforces the masculine-centric narrative: even within a "progressive" party, the expression of power remains anchored in the biological wall of masculinity. Men project strength through muscle; women are relegated to the role of aesthetic objects.

This is not "learning from Trump"—it is pure cultural violence. It signals to the electorate that a leader's legitimacy is partially derived from phenotypic superiority. When political expression devolves from "how to solve problems" to "who is hotter," it becomes a smokescreen for structural violence. Courting "low-information voters" through sex appeal is a profound mockery of democracy.

"Sex sells" is always a scam. It encourages voters to trade critical auditing of power for a dopamine hit. If a party needs tight T-shirts to prove they aren't "out of touch," what they lack isn't sex appeal, but the capacity for Just Expressions in the real world.
