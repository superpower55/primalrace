她赢了比赛，Netflix赢了叙事She Won the Match. Netflix Won the Story.

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
一场业余挑战赛，把职业运动员变成了节目道具
An amateur challenge show turned a professional athlete into a prop.

Fallon Sherrock击败Luke Littler这件事，在竞技层面是真的：一个曾经拿过PDC巡回赛卡、赢过女子世界对抗赛的人，在一局定胜负的赛制里赢了世界第一。但这条新闻真正的主角不是她，是Netflix。

节目叫Beat Luke Littler，规则是20个业余挑战者中任何一人只要赢下一局，就能拿走501,000英镑。这套设计的前提是：Littler几乎不可能输。所以Netflix准备了应急方案，万一他在前几轮就被打败，还有第二名的比赛可以顶上。换句话说，整个节目的悬念是被计算过的，Sherrock的胜利是系统里的一个bug，不是设计目标。

然后争议来了：她不是真正的业余选手。她有MBE，打过世锦赛，2022年赢过首届女子世界对抗赛。Netflix的回应是，只要没拿过PDC巡回赛卡就算业余。这个定义很聪明，它把“业余”重新定义成“没有拿过那张卡”，而不是“水平不在职业层级”。改变的不是她的能力，是评价她的尺子。这和科技公司做不出小芯片就宣布新定律是同一套机制：现实不配合，就重新定义现实。

但这里还有一层更值得说的。Sherrock被放进这个节目，恰恰因为她是女性。一个女性击败世界第一，是这档节目能拿到的最好剧本，比她赢下任何一场正式比赛都更有传播价值。她自己也说：“给我一个机会，我每次都会抓住。”问题是，为什么她需要靠一档娱乐节目来获得这个机会？她早就证明过自己能在职业赛场上赢，但职业巡回赛的门票、奖金、曝光，从来没有因为她的胜利而重新分配。

Netflix把“业余挑战职业”包装成平民逆袭的叙事，实际上是在用她的身体和她的胜利，为一个流媒体平台的直播首秀引流。节目带来的“更多工人阶级观众”是Netflix的收益，501,000英镑是节目预算里的一笔支出，而Sherrock拿到的是钱和一次拥抱，不是一张巡回赛卡。

这场胜利里被削掉的，是“女性只能在被设计好的舞台上证明自己”这个假设的一角。但没被削掉的是：那个舞台的门票，仍然由平台发放。

还有一个赢家是Banijay，它同时拥有制作这两档节目的公司。Beat Luke Littler和Celebrity Traitors对打，收视率谁高谁低，钱都进同一个口袋。所谓“直播电视的竞争”，是同一家公司左手打右手。Sherrock赢了Littler，Netflix赢了注意力，Banijay赢了两边。她拿走了奖金，他们拿走了她赢的这个事实。

Fallon Sherrock beating Luke Littler is real at the competitive level: someone who has held a PDC tour card and won the inaugural Women's World Matchplay beat the world No 1 in a single leg. But the protagonist of this story isn't her. It's Netflix.

The show was called Beat Luke Littler. The rule: any of 20 amateur challengers who won a single leg would take £501,000. The premise was that Littler almost certainly wouldn't lose. Netflix had contingency plans for a second-place competition in case he fell in the early rounds. Sherrock's win was a bug in the system, not a design feature.

Then the controversy: she wasn't a true amateur. She has an MBE for services to darts, has played the PDC World Championship, and won the inaugural Women's World Matchplay in 2022. Netflix's answer was that anyone who has never held a PDC tour card counts as eligible. That definition is clever. It redefines "amateur" as "hasn't held that card," not "isn't at professional level." What changed wasn't her ability. It was the ruler used to measure her. Same mechanism as a tech company that can't make small chips and so announces a new law: when reality won't cooperate, redefine reality.

But there's a layer beneath that. Sherrock was put in this show precisely because she is a woman. A woman beating the world No 1 is the best script this programme could get, more valuable for broadcast than any official match she could win. She said it herself: "Give me an opportunity and I'll take it every time." The question is why she needs an entertainment show to get that opportunity. She had already proven she could win on the professional circuit, but the tour cards, the prize money, the exposure were never redistributed because of her victories.

Netflix packaged "amateur versus professional" as an underdog narrative, while using her body and her win to drive traffic to its first UK live entertainment event. The "more working-class audiences" are Netflix's gain. The £501,000 is a line item in the production budget. What Sherrock got was money and a hug, not a tour card.

What this victory chips away at is the assumption that women can only prove themselves on a stage someone else designed. What it leaves intact is that the tickets to that stage are still handed out by the platform.

There's one more winner: Banijay, which owns the production companies behind both shows. Beat Luke Littler and Celebrity Traitors went head to head, and whichever drew more viewers, the money went to the same pocket. The "live TV battle" was one company's left hand fighting its right. Sherrock beat Littler. Netflix won the attention. Banijay won both sides. She took the prize. They took the fact that she won.
