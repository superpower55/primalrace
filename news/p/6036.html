取消的不是面试，是被人看见What Got Cancelled Was Being Seen

科技 结构层 · 文化层 404 Media ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要Proton CEO Andy Yen于周五取消了近全公司的「AI重新面试」，该面试原本要求员工说明如何使用AI。此前404 Media周四的调查报道称，Yen在Proton内部强力推行AI，并存在令员工恐惧的文化。Yen在全员会上承认沟通失误，但表示仍将继续推进AI。
The newsProton CEO Andy Yen on Friday cancelled nearly company-wide "re-interviews" that would have required employees to explain how they use AI. The move followed a Thursday 404 Media investigation reporting that Yen aggressively pushed AI at Proton and fostered a culture of fear. Yen acknowledged communication failures at an all-hands meeting but said he will keep pushing AI.
暴力被曝光后收回去的那部分，不算缩小。
A violence withdrawn after exposure is not a violence reduced.

Proton 的 CEO Andy Yen 周五取消了那场几乎覆盖全公司的“AI 再面试”，理由是这些面试“以一种无意的方式已经达到了目的”——员工现在开始谈论和学习 AI 了。这句话本身就是整件事最诚实的地方：再面试从来不是真的为了评估谁，它是一次示范，让每个人看见不拥抱 AI 的人会有什么下场。目的达到，工具就可以收起来。

这是结构暴力最典型的运作方式：不需要真的开除谁。只要让全公司相信“你的旧工作已经没了”（CTO Bart Butler 在 Slack 上的 AI 宣言原话），让工程师在安全频道里请求先花几天做渗透测试时被 CEO 回一句“那是狗屎、是失败者的思维”，恐惧就已经完成了分配。不用落到任何一个人头上，它落在所有人头上。

而这一切发生在什么名义下？更多年收入。404 Media 的调查写得很清楚：Proton 大举推进 AI，很大程度是为了提高年度营收。一个卖“隐私保护”的公司，在营收压力下把安全往后排，这不是什么 AI 时代的阵痛，这是把用户买的那个承诺重新定价了。用户买的不是软件，是“有人会替我把关”这件事；现在把关的人被要求“别再充满爱意地手工打磨我们的代码，多用 AI”。

所以真正被取消的是什么？不是再面试，是这件事被公开之前的那几个月。员工说得很准：再面试不是被取消的，是“在这件事公开之后”才被取消的，此前许多人提过担忧，没人理会。取消的动作发生在 404 Media 发稿之后，发生在 Reddit 上他的回应被踩了一千两百多次之后，发生在付费用户公开说要退订之后。共谋者不是良心发现，是成本变了。

Yen 在全公司会议开头承认“我这边有沟通上的失误”，紧接着一句“往前走，我还是会继续推”。Butler 说“我们搞砸了，我们知道我们搞砸了”。这两句话合起来是完整的：道歉的是沟通方式，不道歉的是方向。方向不变，只是把测量从一场公开的面试挪进了绩效考核——一位员工说，会上紧接着就讲了再面试要衡量的东西会成为绩效评估的一部分。入口没有关上，只是从正门挪到了侧门。

董事会“密切关注着局势”。董事会里有 Yen 本人，员工也说了，他持有 Proton 的多数股份，所以“不会有什么后果落到 Andy 头上”。这就是元暴力最朴素的样子：解释权、定价权、免责权握在同一个人手里，于是问责这件事从一开始就没有入口。

用户那边的反应倒是这场里唯一没被收编的部分——退订、公开要求辞职，这是拿钱投票，是唯一让成本真的动起来的语言。但要注意，这仍然是被损害之后才启动的防守。

这场里被削掉的差额是多少？是“公开曝光”这一个变量带来的、一次可撤回的姿态调整。真正的差额——工程师能不能在不被羞辱的前提下说“这段代码需要先测几天”——一点没动，只是以后不会在全员面前问了。

Proton CEO Andy Yen cancelled the near-company-wide "re-interviews" on Friday, explaining that they had, "in an unintentional way," already served their purpose: employees are now talking about and learning AI. That sentence is the most honest thing in the whole story. The re-interviews were never an evaluation. They were a demonstration, so that everyone could see what happens to people who don't embrace AI. Purpose achieved, tool put away.

This is structural violence at its most standard: nobody has to actually be fired. Just make the whole company believe "your old job is gone" — the CTO's own words in his AI manifesto on Slack — and have the CEO answer an engineer who asks for a few days of pentesting with "that's bullshit and loser thinking." Fear is now distributed. It doesn't land on one person. It lands on everyone.

And all of it in the name of what? More annual revenue. The 404 Media investigation is explicit: Proton is barreling ahead with AI in large part to hit revenue targets. A company that sells privacy protection deprioritizing security under revenue pressure is not an AI-era growing pain. It is the promise being repriced. Users weren't buying software. They were buying "someone is checking." Now the checkers are told to "stop lovingly handcrafting all our code and use AI more."

So what actually got cancelled? Not the re-interviews. The months before this went public. The employees put it precisely: the re-interviews weren't cancelled until this became public, despite many of them raising concerns. The cancellation came after 404 Media published, after Yen's Reddit response was downvoted more than 1,200 times, after paying users said publicly they were leaving. The co-conspirators didn't have a change of heart. The cost changed.

Yen opened the all-hands by acknowledging "a communication failure on my part," then added: "Going forward, I'm still going to push." Butler said, "We fucked up, we know we fucked up." Put the two together and you have the full shape of it: the apology is for the communication, not the direction. The direction is unchanged; the measurement just moved from a public interview into performance reviews — one employee says the call made clear that what the re-interviews were meant to measure would become part of performance reviews. The entrance wasn't closed. It was moved from the front door to the side door.

The board is "closely monitoring the situation." The board includes Yen. Employees note he holds the majority of Proton's shares, so there won't be consequences for Andy. That is meta-violence in its plainest form: the power to interpret, to price, and to exempt all sit in the same pair of hands, so accountability never had an entrance to begin with.

The users are the one part of this that didn't get absorbed — cancelling plans, publicly calling for his resignation. That's money voting, the only language that makes cost actually move. But note that it's still a defense launched after the damage.

How much of the gap did this close? One variable — public exposure — produced one retractable gesture. The real gap, whether an engineer can say "this code needs a few days of testing" without being humiliated in front of everyone, hasn't moved at all. It just won't be asked in the all-hands anymore.
