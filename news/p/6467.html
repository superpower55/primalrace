人道停火是谈判桌上的道具，不是目的
A humanitarian truce is a bargaining chip, not a goal.

奥莱什基的六天停火结束了。报道里写得很清楚：双方都称这次撤离行动成功，数百名居民在周末与亲人团聚，乌克兰人权监察员称其为“我们共同人道努力的重要一步”，教皇希望这次合作能为更广泛的和平铺路。

然后同一篇报道的下一段写着：乌克兰观察人士认为，俄军利用这次停火加固阵地、轮换驻守奥莱什基的疲惫部队，新的攻势不可避免。

这就是人道主义停火在战争里的真实位置。它不是暴力的暂停，是暴力的换挡。六天里被运走的是饥饿疲惫的士兵，被运回来的是数百个家庭，两件事同时发生，两件事都被称为成功。加尔通的算式在这里格外干净：Potential 是这六天里本可以撤出的人、本可以谈的事，Actual 是六天之后一切照旧，还多了一批休整好的守军。差额就是暴力，而差额被“人道”这个词盖住了。

梅拉尼娅·特朗普斡旋的这场停火，和贾里德·库什纳要求乌克兰暂停炼油厂打击三十天，是同一套动作的两个面。报道说《华尔街日报》曾称乌克兰屈服于白宫压力停止打击俄能源设施，乌克兰官员则对《基辅独立报》说从未同意库什纳的要求；克里姆林宫抱怨基辅现在“完全无视”美国的意愿。特朗普把全球油价上涨归咎于泽连斯基，上个月还解除了对俄罗斯柴油的制裁。

把这些放在一起看：美国要的是油价，乌克兰要的是俄罗斯炼油厂烧起来，俄罗斯要的是顿涅茨克全部。三方都在谈“停”，没有一方在谈“谁少死”。停火、暂停打击、人道走廊，全是筹码的不同包装。泽连斯基不承认最新的炼油厂袭击，只说打了罗斯托夫一家造导弹的国防企业、摧毁一套铠甲系统，还给它起了个名字叫“远程制裁”——他很清楚，承认了就要挨白宫的骂。

泽连斯基给出的数字是：十月俄军伤亡五万四千四百人，是全面战争以来单月最高；乌军上月收复九十六点二平方公里，比俄军夺取的多十点四平方公里。他说“俄罗斯在输”。这些数字可能是真的，但“俄罗斯在输”和“乌克兰在赢”之间隔着的，是基辅周日八到十小时的停电，是被击中的热电厂，是四个州几十万户被切断的电力。

报道里没有出现任何一个乌克兰平民的名字。有数字：几十万户、八到十小时、四个州。有机构：人权监察员、教皇、克里姆林宫官员。有地名：奥莱什基、赫尔松、顿涅茨克。唯独没有一个具体的人在承受这些。这不是记者的疏忽，这是战争报道的常规语法——伤亡被统计，被统计的东西就不再是个体。

普京愿意和泽连斯基直接通电话，但要求支持乌克兰的欧洲领导人不得参与。三方会谈可能本月晚些时候举行。普京的底线没变：整个顿涅茨克，加上赫尔松和扎波罗热他控制不了却宣称“已吞并”的土地。

所以下一轮“人道”安排已经在路上了。它会有一个好听的名字，会有双方都称成功的声明，会有教皇的祝福。而在它生效的六天里，某一方的阵地会被加固，某一方的士兵会被换下来吃饱睡足，某一方的平民会在停电中度过第八个小时。

报道说双方都称撤离行动成功。成功的是撤离，不是和平。这两件事被写成同一句话，就是这次停火最成功的部分。

The six-day ceasefire in Oleshky is over. The report is clear: both sides called the evacuation a success, hundreds of residents were reunited with loved ones over the weekend, Ukraine's human rights ombudsman called it "an important step in our joint humanitarian efforts," and the pope hoped the cooperation would pave the way for broader peace.

Then, a few paragraphs later, the same report says Ukrainian observers believe the Russian military used the truce to reinforce positions and rotate out hungry, exhausted troops garrisoned in Oleshky. A renewed Russian onslaught is inevitable.

That is the actual position of a humanitarian ceasefire in a war. It is not a pause in violence; it is a gear shift. What moved out in those six days was starving soldiers; what moved back was hundreds of families. Both happened. Both are called success. Galtung's arithmetic is unusually clean here: the Potential is the people who could have been evacuated and the things that could have been negotiated in those six days; the Actual is everything unchanged afterward, plus a rested garrison. The gap is violence, and the word "humanitarian" is the lid on the gap.

The truce brokered by Melania Trump and Jared Kushner's demand that Ukraine pause refinery strikes for thirty days are two faces of the same move. The Wall Street Journal reported Ukraine had bowed to White House pressure and stopped hitting Russian energy infrastructure; Ukrainian officials told the Kyiv Independent they never agreed to Kushner's demand; the Kremlin complains Kyiv is now "simply ignoring" US wishes. Trump blamed Zelenskyy for rising global fuel prices and last month lifted US sanctions on Russian diesel.

Put together: Washington wants oil prices down, Kyiv wants Russian refineries burning, Moscow wants all of Donetsk. All three are talking about "stopping." None is talking about who dies less. Ceasefires, strike pauses, humanitarian corridors — different wrappers on the same bargaining chip. Zelenskyy won't acknowledge the latest refinery attacks; he says his forces hit a missile plant in Rostov and destroyed a Pantsir system, and he calls it "long-range sanctions." He knows that admitting the refineries means a scolding from the White House.

His numbers: 54,400 Russian killed and wounded in October, the highest monthly figure since the full-scale war began; 96.2 sq km recaptured last month, 10.4 more than Russia seized. "Russia is losing," he says. The numbers may be true. But between "Russia is losing" and "Ukraine is winning" sit eight to ten hours of power cuts in Kyiv on Sunday, a damaged thermal plant, hundreds of thousands of households across four regions without electricity.

Not a single Ukrainian civilian is named in the report. There are figures: hundreds of thousands, eight to ten hours, four regions. There are institutions: the ombudsman, the pope, Kremlin officials. There are place names: Oleshky, Kherson, Donetsk. There is no specific person bearing any of it. That is not a reporter's oversight; it is the standard grammar of war coverage — casualties get counted, and what gets counted stops being a person.

Putin is willing to talk to Zelenskyy by phone, provided European leaders who back Ukraine are kept out. Trilateral talks may happen later this month. His maximalist demands are unchanged: all of Donetsk oblast, plus territory in Kherson and Zaporizhzhia he does not control but claims to have annexed.

So the next "humanitarian" arrangement is already on its way. It will have a good name, a joint statement calling it a success, and a papal blessing. And in its six days, someone's positions will be reinforced, someone's troops will be fed and rested, and someone's civilians will spend their eighth hour in the dark.

The report says both sides called the evacuation a success. What succeeded was the evacuation, not the peace. Writing those two things as one sentence is the most successful part of this ceasefire.
