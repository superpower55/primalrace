拟人化的幽默，是人类最高级的傲慢Anthropomorphic Humor: The Ultimate Human Arrogance

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-03 § 链接
将他者‘拟人化’以获得快感，本质是权力的认知殖民。
Finding humor in 'human-like' animals is a form of cognitive colonization by the powerful.

所谓的“喜剧野生动物摄影奖”，其实是一场大规模的认知殖民。人类通过快门，将野生动物强行塞进人类的文化模版里：长了棵树的长颈鹿被定义为“戴了顶时尚帽子”，水牛头上的花被解读为“精致的遮阳帽”，而两只打闹的棕熊则被赋予了“微笑”的拟人叙事。这种所谓的‘幽默’，本质上是人类在行使一种定义权——定义什么是‘好笑’，定义什么是‘个性’。

这种表达方式是典型的 weaponized 叙事。它通过将野生动物‘客体化’为人类娱乐的素材，掩盖了物种之间真实的生存博弈。当摄影师在描述一只青蛙被对手‘殴打’出水面时，将其称为‘杂技’，这种叙事上的置换就完成了一次 cultural violence：将残酷的生存竞争转化为一种轻盈的、可消费的视觉快感。动物不再是独立的存在，而成了人类用来投射情感、确认自身‘文明’与‘幽默’的镜像。

最讽刺的是，这种‘幽默’建立在一种极不对等的权力关系之上。人类拥有昂贵的 Nikon z8 镜头，在安全距离外捕捉他者的‘窘迫’或‘滑稽’，然后将其冠以‘个性’之名进行全球展出。这和历史上殖民者记录‘异域原住民’的猎奇视角如出一辙：通过剥夺对方的自主定义权，将其转化为一种被审视的、带有娱乐属性的‘景观’。

当我们对着这些照片大笑时，我们实际上是在共谋一场对非人类物种的认知霸凌。我们并不在乎动物本身的生命逻辑，我们只在乎它们在什么时候‘看起来像个傻瓜’。这种拟人化的快感，正是人类在元暴力结构中，习惯性地将所有非我者‘客体化’的惯性体现。

The so-called 'Comedy Wildlife Photography Awards' is essentially a large-scale exercise in cognitive colonization. Through the lens, humans forcibly cram wild animals into human cultural templates: a giraffe with a tree on its head becomes 'wearing a fancy hat,' and the flowers on a buffalo are interpreted as a 'bonnet.' Even the play-fighting of brown bears is narrated as 'smiling.' This 'humor' is, in reality, the exercise of a power—the power to define what is 'funny' and what is 'personality.'

This mode of expression is a weaponized narrative. By objectifying wildlife into materials for human entertainment, it masks the raw existential struggle between species. When a photographer describes a frog being 'pummelled' out of a pool as 'acrobatics,' a substitution of narrative occurs, completing an act of cultural violence: transforming a brutal survival fight into a light, consumable visual pleasure. The animals cease to be independent entities; they become mirrors for humans to project emotions and confirm their own 'civilization' and 'wit.'

The irony lies in the extreme power imbalance. Humans, armed with expensive Nikon z8 lenses, capture the 'embarrassment' or 'absurdity' of the Other from a safe distance, then label it as 'personality' for global exhibition. This mirrors the exoticizing gaze of historical colonizers documenting 'indigenous peoples': stripping the subject of their right to self-definition and transforming them into a spectacle of entertainment.

When we laugh at these photos, we are complicit in a cognitive bullying of non-human species. We do not care about the biological logic of the animal; we only care when they 'look like a fool.' This anthropomorphic pleasure is a manifestation of the habit within the meta-violence structure to objectify everything that is not 'us.'
