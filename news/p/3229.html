用“高级感”包装的特权共谋与叙事陷阱The Glamorized Complicity of Privilege and Narrative Traps

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-04 § 链接
将特权阶级的自我毁灭美化为“艺术”，是典型的文化暴力。
Beautifying the self-destruction of the privileged as 'art' is a textbook case of cultural violence.

这部剧的宣发逻辑简直是一个标准的 weaponized expression 样本。它把 80 年代洛杉矶富家子弟的 cocaine 派对、毒品滥用和精神空虚，通过 Ryan Murphy 的“奢华”包装，转化为一种所谓的“elevated”审美。这种操作的本质是夺取解释权：将结构性的阶级特权和毁灭性的自我放纵，重新定义为一种关于“身份隐藏”和“青春阵痛”的艺术探讨。

最讽刺的是，剧组在讨论“不要模仿毒品派对”的同时，却在挑选演员时将“aspirationalism”（令人向往感）作为核心标准。这种共谋非常阴险——它在文化层面上向受众兜售一种“如果你足够富有且美丽，即使在毁灭边缘地带地打转也是高级的”幻觉。这不仅是审美的筛选，更是对特权阶级生活方式的二次神化，让底层在潜意识中将这种病态的 hedonism 内化为一种追求目标。

至于剧中对同性欲望的处理，那种所谓的“身体代码切换”（code-switching），不过是将 gay 身份标签化、表演化。当一个演员通过“拱起眉毛”和“挺起胯部”来区分直男与同志时，他其实是在强化一种男性中心叙事下的刻板印象。这种“自然而然”的表演，恰恰证明了元暴力如何深植于个体的身体记忆中，使得主体性在扮演既定角色时达到了某种伪最优解。

最后，那个关于“第二季会有更多性”的承诺，揭露了这部剧最底层的逻辑：它并不在乎探讨什么，它在乎的是如何利用 sex, drugs and rock'n'roll 这套经典鸡尾酒配方来制造流量。这不过是一场关于“被看见”的欲望博弈，而真正的受害者——那些在 80 年代真正被 Aids 摧毁的边缘群体，在这里被简化成了一个便捷的“隐喻”，成为了特权阶级文艺创作的装饰品。

The promotional logic of this show is a textbook sample of weaponized expression. By wrapping the cocaine parties and spiritual void of 80s LA rich kids in Ryan Murphy's "extravagance," it transforms systemic class privilege and destructive hedonism into a so-called "elevated" aesthetic. The essence here is the seizure of interpretation: redefining a life of decadent privilege as an artistic exploration of "hidden identities" and "youthful trauma.

It is profoundly ironic that the production warns against imitating drug parties while simultaneously using "aspirationalism" as a key casting criterion. This complicity is insidious—it sells the illusion that if you are rich and beautiful enough, even spiraling toward destruction is "high art." This is not just aesthetic filtering; it is the secondary sanctification of a privileged lifestyle, encouraging the marginalized to internalize this pathological hedonism as a goal.

As for the portrayal of queer desire, the so-called "physical code-switching" is nothing more than the tokenization and performance of gay identity. When an actor distinguishes a straight teen from a gay one via "arched eyebrows" and "crotch forward" posture, he is merely reinforcing stereotypes within a masculine-centric narrative. This "natural" performance proves how meta-violence is embedded in bodily memory, allowing subjectivity to vanish into the performance of a pre-defined role.

Ultimately, the promise of "more sex" in a second season reveals the show's baseline logic: it doesn't care about exploration; it cares about using the classic cocktail of sex, drugs, and rock'n'roll to manufacture attention. It is a game of visibility where the actual victims—the marginalized decimated by Aids in the 80s—are reduced to a convenient "metaphor," serving as mere ornaments for the creative whims of the privileged.
