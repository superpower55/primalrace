七百个狗屎桶和一场财政表演Seven Hundred Bins and a Fiscal Pantomime

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
砍掉十万的桶，掩盖两千六百万的税。
Cut £100k of bins to hide £26m of untaxed revenue.

Hillingdon 议会砍掉700个狗屎桶，省下10万英镑。同一个议会，连续多年冻结市政税，O'Neill 算过账：哪怕只提到伦敦平均水平，一年能进2600万。10万对2600万，这不是省钱，这是拿最显眼、最恶心、最容易被居民骂出声的东西做一场财政表演。

看这个差额。加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual。Hillingdon 的潜在状态是：税收到位、服务维持、700个带盖的桶继续收狗屎。实际状态是：桶没了，狗屎进普通垃圾桶，普通垃圾桶没盖，狐狸能扒，味道散出来，居民前院挂满“狗屎彩球”。中间那一截，就是结构暴力。它没有施暴者，没有一滴血，只有一份议会报告和一句“我们只是改变了收集方式”。

结构暴力的狡猾之处，是它总能找到一个更弱的对象来承担代价。这次的对象是狗屎，以及每天遛狗的人。Punja 说树林里的狗屎桶早就满出来了，普通垃圾桶又不够。她自己是反对党工党议员，这话有立场，但立场不取消事实：容量不够是容量不够。议会说普通垃圾桶“已有近1500个，容量翻倍还多”，可容量翻倍的前提是有人把袋子准确投进去，而没盖的桶连气味都留不住，遑论留住行为。

更值得看的是谁在共谋。保守党议会冻结市政税多年，这是给选民的礼物，也是给中央政府的姿态：我们不需要加税。代价被推迟，被折叠，最后落在700个桶上。Bridges 说“我们理解一些居民有担忧”，然后继续执行。这句话的功能不是回应，是让回应看起来已经发生过。

O'Neill 讲了一句最准的话：他讲赤字、讲审计问题，没人听；一讲桶要没了，居民炸了。这不是居民短视，这是结构暴力的可见性政治——抽象的560万年度债务增长、1.015亿的学校拨款赤字，没有形状；一个消失的狗屎桶有形状，有气味，有画面。议会知道这一点，所以它挑了这个。

这件事没有受害者倒下，没有法庭，没有刑期。它小。但小不妨碍它精确：一个财政决策把成本从预算表转移到人行道，从议会转移到遛狗的人，从今年转移到每一个没盖的垃圾桶。省下的10万是真的，转移出去的东西没有出现在任何报表里。

那2600万还在桌上，没人去拿。

Hillingdon council is removing 700 dog-waste bins to save £100,000. The same council has frozen council tax for years. O'Neill, who has sat through the last 150 council meetings, did the arithmetic: raising it even to the London average would bring in £26m. Ten thousand against twenty-six million. This is not thrift. This is picking the most visible, most revolting, most guaranteed-to-anger-residents item on the list and calling it a budget decision.

Look at the gap. Violence = Potential − Actual. The potential Hillingdon is: revenue collected, services maintained, 700 lidded bins still doing their job. The actual is: bins gone, waste redirected into ordinary litter bins, no lids, foxes can get at them, the smell escapes, front gardens acquire what O'Neill calls a Christmas tree of dog-shit baubles. That gap is structural violence. No perpetrator, no blood, just a council report and a line about "changing how it is collected."

The cleverness of structural violence is that it always finds something weaker to carry the cost. This time it is dog waste, and the people who walk dogs every day. Punja says the bins in the woods are already overflowing and there aren't enough general ones. She is a Labour opposition councillor, so she has a position — but a position does not cancel a fact. Not enough capacity is not enough capacity. The council says there are nearly 1,500 ordinary bins, "more than doubling existing capacity." Doubling capacity assumes the bag lands inside. A bin without a lid cannot even hold the smell, let alone the behaviour.

More interesting is who is complicit. A Conservative council froze council tax for years — a gift to voters, a gesture to Whitehall: we don't need to raise taxes. The cost was deferred, folded, and finally placed on 700 bins. Bridges says he understands some residents have concerns, then proceeds. That sentence does not answer the concern; it makes it look answered.

O'Neill says the sharpest thing in the piece: tell people about the deficit, the audit problems, and nobody cares; tell them the bins are going and they go nuts. That is not residents being short-sighted. That is the visibility politics of structural violence — £5.6m of annual debt growth and a £101.5m schools grant deficit have no shape; a vanished dog-waste bin has a shape, a smell, an image. The council knows this. That is why it chose this one.

No one falls down here. No court, no sentence. It is small. Small does not stop it from being precise: a fiscal decision moves cost off the budget sheet onto the pavement, off the council onto dog walkers, off this year onto every lidless bin. The £100,000 saved is real. What got displaced appears in no report.

The £26m is still on the table. Nobody is reaching for it.
