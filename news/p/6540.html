独立选民是一场统计幻觉The Independent Voter Is a Statistical Illusion

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国中期选举投票日到来，近半数选民自认独立派，约1.17亿成年人。皮尤调查显示44%美国成年人认同独立身份，但多数仍倾向两党。缅因州独立州长候选人贝内特等第三方候选人仍难突破两党格局，内布拉斯加和蒙大拿被视为独立候选人最有机会的州。
The newsUS midterm elections are underway, with nearly half of voters—about 117 million adults—identifying as independent. Pew puts the figure at 44 percent, though most still lean toward a major party. Independent candidates like Maine's Rick Bennett still struggle against the two-party system, with Nebraska and Montana seen as their best chances.
1170万独立派，被两党按票仓瓜分
117 million independents, already allocated to two parties.

美国有近一半选民自称独立派，报道给出的数字是1170万成年人。听上去像一场对两党制的造反。但同一篇报道里，真正没有任何党派倾向的"真独立派"只占全体选民的7%到10%。剩下那一大截，是登记表上写了independent、投票时照样投给民主党和共和党的人。这不是身份，这是姿态。

姿态是有用的。当一个人说"我是独立派"，他其实在表达对华盛顿那潭沼泽的厌恶，同时保留了对具体议题的立场。共和党策略师Lesley Shedd说得直白：这些人说到底还是共和党或民主党，只是不认同本党每一条主张。所以那1170万不是一个新族群，是一批在两党之间来回滑动、随时可以被拉回去的存量。两党争的从来不是他们的忠诚，是他们的出场率。

真正被这套系统挡在外面的，是独立候选人。报道里Bernard Tamas的判断很清楚：法律门槛不是主要障碍，钱才是。2024年联邦选举花了148亿美元，2026年中期选举光政治广告就预计116亿。DNC筹了约2.523亿，RNC约3.41亿。独立候选人"筹到的钱很少"，知名度也低。赢家通吃的选区制把票分散到各个单一选区，即使有可观的少数党支持，也很少能在一个选区里集中到足以拿下席位。

于是出现一个闭环：选民知道独立候选人赢不了，所以不投，怕浪费票、怕当spoiler；独立候选人因为没有票，所以筹不到钱；因为筹不到钱，所以更赢不了。报道里说选民"在不知情中强化了这个系统"，这话说得客气了。这不是不知情，这是被反复训练出来的理性计算。一个把"投了也没用"内化成常识的选民，不需要任何法律来阻止他。

这也是表达被武器化的标准样本。两党不需要立法禁止第三党，它们只需要垄断"谁是可赢的人"这个定义。定义一旦垄断，选票自己就会流向被定义的一方。报道里那个唯一的例外是内布拉斯加的Dan Osborn，51岁的海军退伍军人，民主党在该州干脆没推替代候选人，让他成了共和党人Pete Ricketts的主要挑战者，RealClearPolling把这场选战列为五五开。蒙大拿的Seth Bodnar则可能被同属中左的民主党候选人Alani Bankhead分走选票，反而把共和党人送上去。

报道给出的最诚实的判断来自Simmons：独立派最大的影响，历来是逼两党"实施新政策或治理得更有效"。也就是说，他们的功能是压力，不是替代。1170万人被允许愤怒，被允许在民调里被反复统计，被允许成为两党争夺的对象，但不被允许成为选项本身。

那个7%到10%的"真独立派"，是这套系统里唯一没有被任何一方预定的票。他们被称作"可说服的"，是摇摆票的核心。可说服，换个说法就是还没有被定价。谁先去定价，谁就赢。

Nearly half of US voters call themselves independent — as many as 117 million adults, per Al Jazeera. It sounds like a revolt against the two-party system. In the same article, the "true" independents with no partisan leaning at all are only about 7 to 10 percent of the electorate. The rest are people who wrote independent on a form and will still vote Democrat or Republican. That is not an identity. That is a posture.

The posture is useful. Saying "I'm independent" expresses disgust with the swamp while keeping every specific position intact. Republican strategist Lesley Shedd puts it plainly: these people are really Republicans or Democrats who don't agree with their party on absolutely every issue. So the 117 million are not a new tribe. They are a stock that slides between the two parties and can be pulled back at any time. What the parties contest is never their loyalty. It is their turnout.

What the system actually keeps out is independent candidates. Bernard Tamas is clear in the piece: legal barriers are not the main obstacle, money is. The 2024 federal cycle cost $14.8bn; the 2026 midterms are projected to spend up to $11.6bn on political advertising alone. The DNC raised nearly $252.3m this cycle, the RNC about $341m. Independent candidates raise very little and are less known. Winner-take-all districts scatter minor-party support so thin that it rarely concentrates enough in one district to take a seat.

Hence the loop: voters know independents can't win, so they don't vote for them, fearing a wasted ballot or a spoiler; independents get no votes, so they raise no money; with no money they can't win. The article says voters unknowingly reinforce the system. That is too kind. This is a rational calculation trained into people. A voter who has internalized "my vote doesn't matter" needs no law to stop him.

This is the textbook weaponization of expression. The two parties don't need to outlaw third parties. They only need to monopolize the definition of who is electable. Once the definition is monopolized, the votes flow on their own toward whoever is defined as viable. The one exception in the piece is Dan Osborn in Nebraska, a 51-year-old Navy veteran, left as the principal challenger to Republican Pete Ricketts after the state Democratic Party declined to field a replacement; RealClearPolling calls the race a toss-up. In Montana, Seth Bodnar risks having votes siphoned by Democratic candidate Alani Bankhead from the same centre-left pool, potentially handing the seat to Republican Kurt Alme.

The most honest line comes from Simmons: the greatest impact of independents has historically been pressure on the major parties to implement new policy or govern more effectively. Their function is pressure, not replacement. 117 million people are permitted to be angry, permitted to be counted in polls, permitted to be courted by both parties — but not permitted to be an option.

The 7 to 10 percent of true independents are the only ballots in this system not already reserved by one side. They are called persuadable, the core of the swing vote. Persuadable means not yet priced. Whoever prices them first wins.
