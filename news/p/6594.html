宠物滤镜下面藏着刀The Pet Filter Has a Knife Under It

科技 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
真正的产品不是滤镜，是女人的身体。
The real product isn't the filter. It's women's bodies.

一个自称做宠物肖像的 app，真正的功能是生成性虐待深度伪造。这不是新闻里的意外发现，这是它的商业模式：宠物滤镜是壳，壳用来过审、用来投广告、用来收钱，壳下面才是货。

报道里的数字摆得很清楚：400 多个关联网站和 app，同一套代码，同一批密钥，互相引用。今年春天一周注册约 30 个，9 月有 27 个还活在 Google Play 上，合计下载超过 35 万次，很多标注适合所有年龄段。一款叫 DiffAI 的，2025 年 12 月上线，下载超过 10 万次。模板里写着"bedroom rape""threatening knife"——一个男人把刀架在女人脖子上。

这不是色情，这是把"男人拿刀架在女人脖子上"做成一个可以点选、可以付费、可以反复生成的菜单项。第三章讲色情产业是身体入口的武器化，Aylo 那种垄断还算是有个公司实体可以追责；这里连实体都省了，一个运营者，几百个一次性 app，假名字，免费邮箱，注册在多个司法管辖区之间。武器化的门槛被 AI 降到了几乎为零，代价却仍然由同一个群体承担。

平台的说辞值得逐句看。苹果说，这些 app 上架时是合规的，是开发者事后把内容源换成了色情内容，公司去年因此下架了数万个 app。翻译一下：审核发生在门口，门里面发生什么，平台说自己不知道。Google 在被 WIRED 问询前删掉了大部分 app，没有回应置评请求。Meta 说 2025 年就禁止了 KeLing Technology Limited 投放广告，收到 WIRED 给的域名后又处理了 11600 个广告账户、4700 多个商业主页、5200 个用户、15000 个页面，屏蔽 209 个 app 投放。数字很大，但报道最后一句是：WIRED 完成这篇报道时，类似的广告和 app 还在继续发布。

这就是加尔通三角里文化层和结构层的联动。文化层提供叙事："这只是一个宠物滤镜""这是用户自己上传的照片""这是第三方平台的问题"。结构层提供通道：应用商店的审核、支付系统、广告投放、跨境注册。直接层提供伤害：那些被上传照片的人，可能永远不知道自己被放进了哪个场景里。三层各司其职，每一层都声称自己只负责自己那一段。

报道里有一个细节最值得停一下：这些 app 会向服务器发送用户来源、语言、唯一标识，然后服务器决定给不给看真实内容。从 Meta 广告点进来的，更容易看到。也就是说，投放广告不只是获客，它本身就是筛选机制——愿意为这种广告点击的人，就是它要的客户。广告、app、支付，一条完整的商业闭环，每一环都在平台的规则之内运行，直到有人把规则翻过来看。

谁的名字没有出现？那些被上传照片的人。报道里没有她们的声音，没有她们是否知情、是否求助、是否被删除的记录。她们是这条产业链的原材料，却不是这条新闻里的角色。这本身就是元暴力的一次运作：解释权在研究者、平台、记者手里，被生成进"bedroom rape"场景里的女人，连一个化名都没有。

苹果说这些 app"在审核时符合我们的规则"。规则是平台写的，审核是平台做的，广告是平台卖的，支付是平台收的，然后平台说这是开发者的欺骗。共谋不需要开会，只需要每一方都恰好在自己的职责边界内行事，而边界恰好把所有责任都挡在外面。

KeLing 在被联系后删掉了公开的关联信息，Veyra 网站上的邮箱被换掉，社交媒体频道被改名。这不是慌乱，这是熟练——一个已经运营了一年、每周注册几十个域名的组织，知道被盯上之后该做什么。报道说，这个网络"不指望执法能追上"，而到目前为止，确实没追上。

最后一句留给那个数字：35 万次下载。每一次下载背后，都可能有一张被上传的脸。她们不知道自己在哪里，而生成她们的人，正在用 4 美元 130 个硬币的价格，为下一次点击付费。

An app that claims to make pet portraits has one real function: generating sexually abusive deepfakes. This is not an incidental discovery in the reporting. It is the business model. The pet filter is the shell—the shell that passes review, buys ads, collects payments. What's underneath is the product.

The numbers in the WIRED investigation are precise: more than 400 connected domains and apps, running the same code, the same encryption keys, referencing one another. Around 30 were being registered each week this spring; 27 were live on Google Play in September, downloaded more than 350,000 times collectively, many rated suitable for all ages. One app, DiffAI, launched in December 2025 and has over 100,000 downloads. The templates include "bedroom rape" and "threatening knife"—a man holding a knife to a woman's neck.

This is not pornography. It is a menu item: a man holding a knife to a woman's neck, selectable, payable, repeatable. Chapter 3 treats the porn industry as weaponization through the body entrance; Aylo's monopoly at least has a corporate entity you can pursue. Here there is no entity—one operator, hundreds of disposable apps, fake names, free email addresses, registration spread across jurisdictions. AI has dropped the barrier to weaponization to nearly zero. The cost still lands on the same group.

The platforms' statements deserve a close read. Apple says the apps complied when reviewed, and that developers later swapped the content source for pornography; the company says it has suspended tens of thousands of apps for this bait-and-switch. Translation: review happens at the door; what happens inside the door, the platform says it doesn't know. Google removed most of the apps before WIRED contacted it and did not respond to a request for comment. Meta says it banned KeLing Technology Limited from advertising in 2025, and after WIRED shared the domains it took action against 11,600 ad accounts, more than 4,700 business pages, 5,200 users, and 15,000 pages, blocking 209 apps from advertising. Large numbers. The last line of the story: as WIRED finished reporting, similar ads and apps continued to be published.

This is the Violence Triangle's cultural and structural layers working in tandem. The cultural layer supplies the narrative: it's just a pet filter, users uploaded the photos themselves, it's a third-party platform problem. The structural layer supplies the channels: app store review, payment systems, ad delivery, cross-border registration. The direct layer supplies the harm: the people whose photos were uploaded, who may never learn which scene they were placed in. Each layer does its part, and each claims to be responsible only for its own part.

One detail is worth pausing on: the apps call a server with the user's location, language, and a unique identifier, and the server decides whether to show the real content. Coming from a Meta ad makes the explicit version more likely. Advertising is not just customer acquisition—it is the filter. Whoever clicks that ad is the customer they want. Ad, app, payment: a complete commercial loop, every link running inside platform rules, until someone turns the rules over.

Whose names are missing? The people whose photos were uploaded. The story has no record of their voices, whether they knew, whether they sought help, whether anything was deleted. They are the raw material of this supply chain and not characters in this story. That is meta-violence operating: the interpretive power sits with researchers, platforms, and the reporter, while the woman generated into the "bedroom rape" scene does not even get a pseudonym.

Apple says the apps complied with its rules when reviewed. The platform writes the rules, performs the review, sells the ads, collects the payments—and then says the developer deceived it. Conspiracy requires no meeting. It only requires each party to act precisely within its own boundary of responsibility, where the boundary happens to block every claim from getting through.

After WIRED reached out, KeLing removed public connections, the Veyra website's email was changed, social channels were renamed. That is not panic. That is fluency—an operation that has run for about a year, registering dozens of domains a week, knows what to do when it is noticed. The network, the reporting says, does not expect enforcement to catch up. So far it hasn't.

One number to end on: 350,000 downloads. Behind each one, possibly a face that was uploaded. She does not know where she is. The person generating her is paying for the next click, 130 coins for around $4.
