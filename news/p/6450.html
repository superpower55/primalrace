被偷六十三亿，然后说没事$6.3bn Stolen, and They Say It's Fine

科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
一家交易所的储备证明，证明不了它替谁保管什么
A proof of reserves proves nothing about whose money it is holding.

六十三亿美元被偷，Binance 补上了九成，剩下的百分之十它说「承诺覆盖」。投资者净提走三十六亿，占其管理资产总额一千七百一十亿的百分之二点一。这个比例被拿来当稳定证据。稳定不稳定我不知道，但一家机构在被史上最大规模盗窃之后，最重要的产出是让数字看上去不大，这本身就是一条信息。

钱去哪了，报道写得很清楚：FBI 认定是朝鲜的 TraderTraitor，正在把赃款换成 ethereum，再用去中心化金融和即时兑换继续洗。被偷的是客户账户里的资产，被追的是链上地址，夹在中间的是用户。他们既没有参与攻击，也没有参与追赃，却要在一个「分阶段恢复提现」的公告下面等自己的钱。

Binance 的应对是教科书式的：先冻结提现，再宣布已补充九成，再让联席 CEO Richard Teng 出面说「用户不会承担这次黑客攻击的财务影响」。这句话值得逐字读一遍。用户不承担财务影响，那谁承担？公司自有资金、十亿美元的安保资产基金、期货保险基金、企业金库。听起来很有担当。但这些钱原本就是用来做这件事的，动用它们不叫慷慨，叫履行。而被挪用的部分，是平台本可以用来应对下一次的缓冲。

真正的问题不在这次补没补上，而在这套结构本身。一家公司替几千万人保管资产，它的安全性由它自己发布、自己确认、自己解读。报道里说，这些数字「由公司在其最新的储备证明快照中确认」。快照是它拍的，报告是它写的，星期一再出一份完整报告，还是它写的。这不是审计，这是自证。当保管人同时是记账人、是公告发布者、是危机公关的作者，储备证明就只是一种修辞。

有意思的是报道最后那句：这次黑客事件发生时，许多观察者正担心 AI 模型不断扩张的能力可能危及密码学和 IT 安全协议。这句话被轻轻放在结尾，像一个远景。但朝鲜这个国家，被国际制裁围了这么多年，靠的从来不是最强的技术，而是最会找制度缝隙的人。它挑中的不是密码学的弱点，是中心化托管的弱点：把所有人的钱放在一个房间里，然后只派一个人守门，而且这个守门人的笔记本电脑被入侵了。

比特币跌了近百分之五，ethereum 跌了近百分之六，BNB 跌了近百分之十。价格会回来。但「你的资产由一家公司保管，而这家公司告诉你一切稳定」这件事，不会因为价格回来就变得合理。

所以这条新闻里最该被追问的，不是朝鲜怎么偷的，而是一个用户在看到「净流出仅占百分之二点一」时，为什么会觉得安心。

Six point three billion dollars stolen, Binance says it has replenished ninety percent, and it is "committed to covering the remaining 10%". Investors pulled a net $3.6bn, which the company reports as 2.1% of its $171bn in assets under management. That ratio is offered as evidence of stability. I don't know about stability, but after the largest heist in history, the most important output of this institution is making the number look small. That itself is information.

Where the money went is clear from the report: the FBI says a North Korea-affiliated group called TraderTraitor is converting the stolen assets into ethereum and will keep laundering them through decentralised finance and instant swap services. What was stolen sat in customer accounts. What is being chased is an on-chain address. In between stand the users, who neither attacked nor investigated, and who now wait under a notice about a "phased reopening" of withdrawals.

Binance's response is textbook: freeze withdrawals, announce ninety percent replenished, then have co-chief executive Richard Teng say users "would not bear the financial impact of the hack". Read that sentence slowly. If users don't bear it, who does? Company funds, a $1bn secure asset fund for users, part of its futures insurance fund, its own corporate treasury. It sounds like accountability. These funds existed precisely for this. Drawing on them isn't generosity, it's obligation. And what gets spent is the buffer that was supposed to absorb the next one.

The real problem isn't whether this particular hole got plugged. It's the structure. One company holds assets for millions, and the safety of those assets is published, confirmed and interpreted by that same company. The report says the numbers were "confirmed by the company in its latest proof of reserves snapshot". The snapshot is taken by them, the report is written by them, Monday's full report will also be written by them. That is not auditing. That is self-certification. When the custodian is also the bookkeeper, the press office and the crisis PR, a proof of reserves is a rhetorical device.

One line near the end deserves more weight than it gets: the hack came as observers worried that expanding AI capabilities could compromise cryptography and IT security. North Korea, sanctioned for years over its nuclear and missile programmes, has never relied on the strongest technology. It relies on finding the seams in institutions. What it exploited here wasn't a weakness in cryptography. It was a weakness in centralised custody: put everyone's money in one room, post one guard, and compromise his laptop.

Bitcoin fell nearly 5%, ethereum nearly 6%, BNB nearly 10%. Prices recover. But the arrangement itself, your assets held by a company that tells you everything is stable, does not become reasonable because the price comes back.

The question this story should leave isn't how North Korea did it. It's why a user reading "net outflow of about 2.1%" is supposed to feel reassured.
