赔钱不是修好铁路Paying Out Is Not Fixing the Line

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
延误赔付在涨，说明准点这个差额一直没被补上
Compensation is rising because the gap between promised and actual arrival is never closed.

Avanti 一个夏天赔了超过两千万英镑，四周就赔掉九百三十万——接近它过去两年各自一整年的利润。这不是什么慷慨，这是 Violence = Potential − Actual 里那道差额被明码标价了：本该准点到达的乘客，和实际到站时间之间的那段距离，被换算成了英镑。铁路没有变好，只是开始为变坏付一点钱。

注意这笔钱从哪来。疫情后取代特许经营的那套合同里，运营商的收入是交通部付的费用，票款和退款带来的盈亏由纳税人兜着。所以 Avanti 赔出去的每一镑，都不是股东的痛，是税金的转移。赔付额翻倍，看上去是乘客的权利被兑现了，实际上是结构暴力换了个记账方式：延误的成本从乘客身上，挪到了公共账本上，而延误本身一天都没有减少。

再看数据。七月 Avanti 不到一半的列车准点，平均延误十九分钟。官方数字说全网络 2024-25 年延误赔付总额一亿五千万，比上一年涨 8%，其中约 27% 是 Avanti 的。赔付上涨的原因，报道自己列了：票价通胀、乘客更知道自己的权利、赔付自动化。三个原因里两个和准点率无关。也就是说，这条新闻里涨得最猛的那个数字，量的是索赔机制的效率，不是铁路的可靠性。把申诉渠道修得丝滑，和把轨道修好，是两件事，前者便宜得多。

Avanti 说今年五月到八月它直接负责的延误只有 23%，其余归 Network Rail、其他运营商，还有闯入轨道的人，以及那一群在轨道上待了好几个小时的牛。这套说法把责任切碎，切到没有任何一个主体需要为“不到一半准点”负责。这就是结构暴力的标准形态：没有施暴者，只有一个由很多环节组成的现状。而现状被当成天气。

真正值得盯的是明年。Avanti 明年随 Great British Railways 收归公有，本财年预计赔付超过七千万。国有化之后，赔付账单和运营亏损会进同一个口袋，那时候“赔给乘客”和“修好铁路”就成了同一笔预算里的两个选项。如果这笔钱继续用来买乘客的沉默，而不是买准点，那这次国有化就只是把共谋的双方合并成了一个。

报道里最诚实的一句是交通部的研究：大约一半受影响的乘客会去索赔。另一半没有。赔付总额再高，也只覆盖了那道差额的一半——剩下的一半，是不知道、不会填表、或者懒得为一个十九分钟去填表的人，替这套系统省下来的钱。

Avanti West Coast paid out over £20m in a single summer, £9.3m in the four weeks to 25 July alone — close to its annual profit in each of the past two years. This is not generosity. It is the Violence = Potential − Actual gap given a price tag: the distance between the arrival passengers were sold and the one they got, converted into pounds. The railway did not improve. It started paying a little for getting worse.

Follow the money. Under the contracts that replaced franchising after the pandemic, operators are paid fees by the Department for Transport, and the taxpayer absorbs the profit or loss from fares and refunds. Every pound Avanti hands back is not a shareholder's pain; it is a transfer from the public purse. Payouts doubling looks like passenger rights being honoured. It is structural violence changing its bookkeeping: the cost of delay moves from the passenger to the public accounts, while the delay itself does not budge.

The numbers. In July fewer than half of Avanti's trains ran on time, with passengers delayed by an average 19 minutes. Across the network, compensation reached £150m in 2024-25, up 8%, about 27% of it Avanti's. The report lists why payouts are climbing: fare inflation, passengers knowing their rights, automated claims. Two of those three have nothing to do with punctuality. The fastest-rising figure here measures the efficiency of the claims machine, not the reliability of the railway. Making the complaint channel seamless is far cheaper than making the track work.

Avanti says it was directly responsible for only 23% of delays between May and August; the rest belongs to Network Rail, other operators, trespassers, and a herd of cows that spent several hours on the tracks. This chops responsibility into pieces small enough that no one has to answer for "fewer than half on time." That is structural violence in its standard form: no perpetrator, only a present condition assembled from many parts — and the condition gets treated like weather.

Watch next year. Avanti goes into public ownership under Great British Railways, expecting to pay out over £70m this financial year. Once nationalised, the compensation bill and the operating loss land in the same pocket, and "paying passengers" and "fixing the line" become two line items in one budget. If the money keeps buying silence instead of punctuality, nationalisation will have merged the co-conspirators into one.

The most honest line in the report is the DfT finding that about half of disrupted passengers claim. However high the payout total climbs, it covers only half the gap. The other half is the people who don't know, can't fill in the form, or won't bother for 19 minutes — and their share is what the system saves.
