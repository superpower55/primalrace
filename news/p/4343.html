骑行者的“自由”与文明的掩体The 'Freedom' of Cyclists and the Shield of Civility

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-21 § 链接
将违规美化为“人性纪律”，是特权阶层对公共空间的再次殖民。
Beautifying rule-breaking as 'human discipline' is just another form of privileged colonization of public space.

Simon Jenkins 的这篇文章是一次典型的“特权阶层自白”。他坦承闯红灯，但迅速将锅甩给英国的交通法，并搬出 Hans Monderman 的“共享空间” (shared space) 理论作为遮羞布。这在逻辑上是一个极其阴险的 scam：他把个人的违规行为包装成对某种“更高阶文明”的践行，将对规则的践踏定义为“人性纪律” (human discipline)。

我们要看清这里的 Violence Triangle。在 structural 层，交通法是所有路权博弈的底线；而在 cultural 层，Jenkins 试图制造一种新的叙事——即资深骑行者可以通过“灵活处理”规则来获得某种“快感”和“自由”。这种所谓的自由，本质上是建立在对他人的风险转嫁之上的。当他“灵活地”在红灯前骑行时，他是在利用自己的认知优势和身体机能，强行在公共空间中挤占原本属于他人的安全冗余。这就是一种微观的殖民：我定义什么是“智能的规制”，而你必须在我的“自由”中小心翼翼地生存。

最讽刺的是，他一边享受着这种“精英式”的违规快感，一边又在文中对 e-bike 配送员的鲁莽表示厌恶。这揭示了一个残酷的真相：他并不在乎规则本身，他在乎的是“谁在破坏我的秩序”。当一个像他这样具有社会资本的骑行者闯红灯时，那是“对官僚主义的抗争”；而当一个底层配送员骑着电单车疾驰而过时，那就是“混乱”和“缺乏管理”。

这种双标正是元暴力的体现。他试图定义一套只有“懂行的人”才能参与的非正式规则，从而在公共空间中获得某种隐形的优先权。文明在这里成了他的掩体，而所谓的“共享空间”，不过是他用来合法化自身特权的叙事武器。

Simon Jenkins' piece is a classic confession of the privileged. He admits to running red lights, but swiftly shifts the blame to British traffic laws, using Hans Monderman's 'shared space' theory as a rhetorical shield. This is a sophisticated scam: he packages personal misconduct as the practice of a 'higher civilization' and redefines the violation of rules as 'human discipline'.

Let's apply the Violence Triangle. At the structural level, traffic laws are the baseline for all road-right negotiations. At the cultural level, Jenkins attempts to manufacture a new narrative: that seasoned cyclists can achieve a certain 'exhilaration' and 'freedom' by 'flexibly' handling the rules. This so-called freedom is essentially the externalization of risk onto others. When he 'flexibly' rides through a red light, he is using his cognitive and physical advantage to forcibly seize the safety margin that belongs to someone else. This is micro-colonization: I define what 'intelligent regulation' is, and you must survive cautiously within my 'freedom'.

The peak of irony is his simultaneous disgust toward the recklessness of e-bike delivery drivers. This reveals a brutal truth: he doesn't care about the rules; he cares about 'who is disrupting my order'. When a high-capital cyclist like himself runs a red light, it is a 'protest against bureaucracy'; when a precarious delivery driver speeds past, it is 'shambles' and 'chaos'.

This double standard is a manifestation of meta-violence. He seeks to establish a set of informal rules accessible only to the 'initiated', thereby gaining an invisible priority in public space. Civility here serves as his cover, and the so-called 'shared space' is merely a weaponized narrative used to legitimize his own privilege.
