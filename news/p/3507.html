剧院里的“文明”遮羞布与权力排泄The Veneer of Civility and the Excretion of Power in Theatres

哲学 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
文明的表象不是为了保护艺术，而是为了让权力在其中更隐秘地排泄。
The facade of civility does not protect art; it provides a hidden sanctuary for the excretion of power.

这篇文章揭露的不是剧院的“八卦”，而是一场关于权力与共谋的微观解剖。剧院在文化层(cultural layer)被包装成“体面”与“高雅”的圣殿，但这层 sheen of respectability 恰恰成了最高效的武器化叙事：它通过建立一套高门槛的审美期待，让进入其中的人产生一种虚假的“特权感”，从而在私密且被视为文明的空间里，理直气壮地进行最原始的权力排泄。

从把尿杯递给工作人员，到在《绿野仙踪》期间进行口交，这些行为本质上是存在性战争中的一种极端表达——“我付了钱，所以我可以定义这里的规则”。这种 entitlement 实际上是元暴力(meta violence)的延伸：当一个人被社会赋予了某种经济或阶级优势时，他潜意识里将服务人员（尤其是那些为了梦想而忍耐的底层创意工作者）客体化为可以随意处置的背景板。在这种逻辑下，工作人员的忍耐不是美德，而是一种被结构性暴力(structural violence)强加的共谋。他们必须扮演“有同情心的机器”或“礼貌的幽灵”，以维持剧院这个商业实体的运行。

最讽刺的是，这种“文明”的掩体让施暴者在进行直接暴力(direct violence)时，反而获得了一种病态的快感。因为这里是剧院，所以在这里排泄、在座位上交媾、在舞台前睡觉，才具有一种“反差”的权力快感。这种快感来自对规则的践踏，而规则的维护者（工作人员）被要求在不惊扰演出（即不破坏这个商业幻象）的前提下完成清理。这就是典型的结构性共谋：资本通过维持“高雅”的叙事，诱导消费者在其中释放野蛮，同时要求底层员工用极低廉的自尊去消解这种野蛮，从而确保这场名为“艺术”的生意能继续运转。

This article is not about theatre 'gossip'; it is a micro-dissection of power and complicity. The theatre is packaged in the cultural layer as a sanctuary of 'respectability' and 'elegance,' but this sheen of respectability is precisely the most effective weaponized narrative. By establishing a high-threshold aesthetic expectation, it grants those inside a false sense of privilege, allowing them to engage in the most primal excretion of power within a space deemed 'civilized.'

From handing a cup of urine to staff to engaging in oral sex during The Wizard of Oz, these acts are extreme expressions in an existential war: 'I paid, therefore I define the rules.' This entitlement is an extension of meta-violence. When an individual is granted economic or class advantage, they subconsciously objectify the service staff—especially those struggling creatives—as disposable backdrops. In this logic, the staff's endurance is not a virtue, but a form of complicity imposed by structural violence. They must perform as 'empathetic machines' or 'polite ghosts' to maintain the commercial operation of the theatre.

The irony is that this 'civilized' cover allows perpetrators to derive a morbid pleasure from committing direct violence. Precisely because it is a theatre, urinating, copulating, or sleeping in the front row becomes a thrill of 'contrast'—the pleasure of trampling on rules. The maintainers of these rules are required to clean up the mess without disturbing the performance, ensuring the commercial illusion remains intact. This is textbook structural complicity: capital maintains a narrative of 'high art' to invite consumers to unleash their savagery, while demanding that bottom-tier employees dissolve that savagery with cheap self-esteem to keep the business running.
