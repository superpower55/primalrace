钢铁厂的闭店与宏大叙事的血肉祭坛Steel Mill Closures and the Altar of Grand Narratives

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
战争叙事将人类身体降格为可消耗工具，而资本在计算完损益后迅速撤离。
War narratives degrade human bodies into consumable tools, while capital exits swiftly after calculating the loss.

ArcelorMittal 的撤离是一场精准的资本博弈。对于跨国巨头来说，所谓的“安全且可持续” (safe and sustainable) 并非是对工人生死权的关怀，而是一次损益审计后的止损决定。当工厂变成俄罗斯导弹的靶标，五个工人的死亡只是报表上的一个损耗项，而“不可持续”则是资本在意识到无法通过结构性套利获得利润后，迅速抽离的体面措辞。

这场战争的本质是极致的 masculine-centric narrative。从普京声称“没有动机”升级冲突的伪装，到泽连斯基在 WhatsApp 上紧急催促无人机订单，整个叙事空间被完全地“武器化”了。在这些男性主导的政治博弈中，无论是钢铁厂的工人，还是麦当劳仓库里的食品，都被客体化为一种“资源”或“目标”。在这种元暴力的逻辑下，人的存在被简化为战争机器的零件：要么是生产导弹的劳动力，要么是用来交换政治筹码的受害者。

最讽刺的共谋发生在外交辞令之间。一边是美国参议员在讨论是否邀请普京参加 G20 这种名为“外交”实为“合法化”的社交派对，另一边是乌克兰的工业脊梁在物理层面被拆解。这种将“文明”与“秩序”作为掩体的共谋，让直接暴力（direct violence）在结构暴力（structural violence）的掩护下显得像是一场可以被谈判、被抵消的财务赤字。在这种叙事里，人类的身体被降格为可消耗的手段，而真正的赢家永远是那些掌握着解释权、定义权，并能随时决定在哪个节点撤离的权力中心。

The exit of ArcelorMittal is a precise game of capital. For a multinational giant, the phrase “safe and sustainable” is not a concern for the workers' right to life, but a stop-loss decision following a profit-and-loss audit. When the plant became a target for Russian missiles, the death of five workers was merely a loss item on a spreadsheet, and “unsustainable” is the polite euphemism used by capital to withdraw once structural arbitrage is no longer possible.

This war is the ultimate expression of a masculine-centric narrative. From Putin’s facade of having “no motive” to escalate, to Zelenskyy’s urgent WhatsApp briefings for drone orders, the entire expression space has been weaponized. In these male-dominated political gambles, whether it is the steelworkers or the food in a McDonald’s warehouse, everyone is objectified as a “resource” or a “target.” Under this meta-violence, human existence is reduced to a component of the war machine: either labor producing missiles or victims used as bargaining chips.

The most cynical complicity occurs within diplomatic rhetoric. On one side, US senators debate whether to invite Putin to a G20 summit—a social party masquerading as “diplomacy” to legitimize a regime; on the other, Ukraine’s industrial backbone is being physically dismantled. This complicity, using “civilization” and “order” as bunkers, allows direct violence to appear as a financial deficit that can be negotiated or offset via structural violence. In this narrative, the human body is degraded into a consumable means, while the true winners remain the power centers who hold the right of interpretation and the ability to decide exactly when to exit.
