换一把尺子，再拆一次重建Change the Ruler, Dismantle Reconstruction Again

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把宪法变成白人的护身符，把种族隔离叫作风俗。
Turning the Constitution into a white man's shield, calling segregation custom.

最高法院在 Callais 案里干的不是重新划选区，是把宪法修正案调了个头。第十四修正案的平等保护条款，原本是给被解放的黑人用的，现在被用来保护白人不受黑人议员的"冒犯"——六比三的判决认定，一个黑人占多数的选区"侵犯了白人选民的权利"。Alito 写下的理由是：种族主义已经结束，社会已经变了。这句话本身就是元暴力的标准句式：由掌握解释权的人宣布，你们已经没有资格再抱怨了。

路易斯安那的历史把这句话拆得很干净。重建时期，黑人男性在几乎所有南方州的登记率超过八成，1868 年 John Willis Menard 当选国会议员，众议院以 130 比 57 决定不让他就座，理由据说是"让黑人进国会还为时过早"。然后 Campbell 用第十三、十四修正案去起诉一家屠宰场，不是为了屠宰场，是为了让联邦政府退出对南方州权的监督。Slaughterhouse 案判下来，联邦的后盾就没了。1873 年复活节，Colfax 的白人民兵烧了法院、杀了几十个投降的黑人，Cruikshank 案三年后判决，凶手一个没罚，理由是宪法只约束政府行为，不约束私人暴力。之后路易斯安那 114 年没有一个黑人在国会。

这条线一路连到 Roberts。2013 年 Shelby County 案拿掉预先审查，2019 年 Rucho 案让党派划区不受联邦法院管辖，2024 年又要求法院假定立法者出于善意。到 Callais，州议会只要说"我是按党派划的，不是按种族划的"，就能合法抹掉任何一个黑人有机会赢的选区。这套逻辑的祖师爷就是 Cruikshank 法院：除非你能证明立法者心里想的是种族，否则什么都不算数。Kagan 在异议里说，这等于让投票权法"几乎成为一纸空文"。

Fields 这个人的履历就是这条线的刻度。1992 年当选，1996 年选区被拆，2024 年回来，2025 年又被拆，2026 年 7 月 21 日宣布彻底退休。他说自己"在国会没待过一天，头上没有联邦诉讼悬着"。路易斯安那黑人占人口近三分之一，重建以来只选出过四名黑人国会议员，他一个人占了两段。这不是个案，报道说南方十几个黑人占多数的国会选区可能在 2028 年前被划掉，多达 140 个州议会选区也悬着。选区没了，投票权还在，只是选不出任何人——这是结构暴力最干净的做法：不禁止你投票，只让你的票永远凑不够数。

文化层在同步动。共和党在最近一个会期不只拆了 Fields 的席位，还废掉了奥尔良堂区刑事法院书记员的职位，好让一个曾被错判谋杀的黑人当选者坐不进去；取消了两名黑人法官的席位；推进法案把过去二十年拆掉的邦联纪念碑重新立起来。1995 年 Fields 竞选州长时，巴吞鲁日一家商场卖过一件 T 恤，画着一个黑人被戴头罩、拿钉棍的人追着跑，上面印着"跑啊，Cleo，跑"。三十年后，路易斯安那仍然没有一个黑人在全州选举中胜出。

Alito 说"南方尤其发生了巨大的社会变化"。Fields 的回应是请大法官们来路易斯安那，作为一个黑人竞选国会议员，看看能走到哪一步。这句话问的不是事实，是解释权：谁有资格宣布种族主义结束了。宣布的人从来不是被宣布的那一方。

Callais 案的原告之一 Bert Callais 参加过 1 月 6 日前的"Save America"集会，那天有人举着邦联旗。他们起诉的理由是自己的"个人尊严"受了冒犯。Fields 的反驳很朴素：路易斯安那一年选出的白人国会议员，比他这辈子选出的黑人国会议员还多。宪法被拿来保护这种"尊严"，而 Colfax 的枪声、Menard 被拒之门外的那 130 票、Nash 在几乎空荡的议场里讲到深夜的那番话，全被归进"过去"。历史学家 Michael Ross 说，Campbell 此刻正在某处咧嘴笑。

真正被拆掉的不是几张地图，是"联邦可以管州"这件事本身。重建修正案被重新解释成只保护白人、只约束政府、只认主观故意，那么下一次，被划掉的可以是任何一群人的席位。Fields 退休了，Menard 的画像还挂在他办公室的墙上，而那张画像里的席位，1868 年被 130 比 57 否决过一次，2026 年又被六比三否决了一次。

What the Supreme Court did in Callais was not redraw districts. It turned the Reconstruction amendments around. The equal protection clause of the 14th Amendment, written for the formerly enslaved, now protects white voters from the offense of a Black congressman—the 6–3 ruling held that a majority-Black district violated white voters' rights. Alito's justification: racism is over, the country has changed. That sentence is the standard grammar of meta-violence: the people who hold the power to interpret announce that you no longer have standing to complain.

Louisiana's history takes that sentence apart. During Reconstruction, more than 80 percent of Black men registered in nearly every Southern state. In 1868 John Willis Menard won a House seat; the House voted 130 to 57 to keep the seat empty rather than seat a Black man—it was, reportedly, "too early to admit a Negro to the US Congress." Then Campbell sued a slaughterhouse under the 13th and 14th amendments, not for the butchers but to push the federal government out of supervising Southern state power. Slaughterhouse withdrew the federal backstop. On Easter Sunday 1873, a white militia burned the Colfax courthouse and shot dozens of surrendering Black men; three years later Cruikshank ensured no one was punished, on the logic that the Constitution restrains government, not private violence. After that, Louisiana went 114 years without a single Black member of Congress.

The line runs straight to Roberts. Shelby County in 2013 removed preclearance. Rucho in 2019 put partisan gerrymandering beyond federal courts. In 2024 the justices ordered courts to presume legislators acted in good faith. By Callais, a legislature only has to say "party, not race" and any district where Black voters could win disappears legally. The ancestor of that logic is the Cruikshank court: unless you can prove what was in the legislator's mind, nothing counts. Kagan, dissenting, said the VRA is now "all but a dead letter."

Fields's career is the scale on which this line is marked. Elected in 1992, district dismantled in 1996, returned in 2024, dismantled again in 2025, announced his final retirement on July 21. He says he has never served a single day in Congress without a federal lawsuit hanging over his head. Black people are nearly a third of Louisiana's population; the state has elected four Black congressmen since Reconstruction, and he is two of those stints. This is not an individual case. The report says more than a dozen Black-held Southern congressional seats could be drawn away before 2028, and up to 140 Black- and Hispanic-majority state legislative districts with them. The districts vanish; the right to vote remains. You can still cast a ballot, you just can never elect anyone—structural violence at its cleanest: no prohibition, only permanent arithmetic.

The cultural layer moves in step. In the most recent session Republicans also abolished the clerk of criminal court post in majority-Black Orleans Parish so a Black man once wrongly convicted of murder could not be seated, eliminated two Black-held judgeships, and advanced a bill to put removed Confederate monuments back up. In 1995, when Fields ran for governor, a Baton Rouge mall sold T-shirts showing a Black man chased by hooded figures with nail-studded clubs: "Run, Cleo, Run." Thirty years later, no Black candidate has won statewide office in Louisiana.

Alito says vast social change has occurred, particularly in the South. Fields's answer is to invite the justices to come to Louisiana as a Black man and run for Congress, and see where that gets them. The question is not about facts. It is about who gets to declare that racism is over. The declarer is never the declared.

One of the Callais plaintiffs, Bert Callais, attended the "Save America" rally before January 6, where some carried Confederate flags. The plaintiffs' claim was an affront to their personal dignity. Fields's reply is plain: Louisiana elects more white congressmen in one year than it has elected Black congressmen in his whole lifetime. The Constitution is being used to protect that dignity, while the gunfire at Colfax, the 130 votes that shut Menard out, and Nash speaking late into a nearly empty chamber are all filed under past. The historian Michael Ross says that somewhere, Campbell is grinning his evil genius grin.

What is being dismantled is not a few maps. It is the proposition that the federal government may supervise the states at all. Once the Reconstruction amendments are read to protect only white people, restrain only government, and recognize only explicit intent, the next district erased can belong to anyone. Fields is retiring. Menard's portrait still hangs on his office wall, and the seat in that portrait was voted down 130 to 57 in 1868, and again 6 to 3 in 2026.
