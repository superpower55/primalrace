Richard Tice 是一个完美的样本，展示了当一个男人拥有足够的 cash 但完全缺失 self-awareness 时，是如何在权力结构中扮演一个“Beta 顺从者”的。他试图在公共空间表达一种“Apex Male”的强势，但实际上，他所有的表达都指向一个事实：他只是 Nigel Farage 权力游戏中的一个 beta supplicant。在这种博弈中，Tice 的最优解表达不是成为领导者，而是成为一个好用的、没有主体性的真空容器。

最讽刺的是他对着 DEI（多元、平等、包容）政策的抱怨。在一个真正的 meritocracy（精英政治）中，Tice 这种程度的 dim and incompetent 根本没资格站在起跑线上。他的存在本身就是一种 structural violence 的结果——金钱作为一种认知入口，为他制造了“政治人物”的假象。他用财富掩盖了能力的缺失，用扮演“强势右翼”来掩盖在 Farage 面前的绝对卑微。

而他对待气候危机的态度——在干旱和山火中庆祝能喝到更好的起泡酒——是典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 逻辑。对他这种阶层来说，现实不是用来面对的，而是用来定义和消费的。既然现实不配合（地球在燃烧），那就重新定义现实（这只是个享受阳光的好天气）。这种将他人生存危机转化为个人审美享受的能力，正是 masculine-centric narrative 的极致体现：世界是我的游乐场，他人的痛苦只是背景噪音。

Tice 不是在政治博弈，他是在进行一场极其低劣的表演。当他试图填补 Farage 留下的权力真空时，他发现自己本身就是一个真空。这种主体性的死亡，是所有试图通过扮演他人认可的角色来换取短期利益的“假.最优解表达”的必然结局。

Richard Tice is a perfect specimen of how a man with ample cash but zero self-awareness functions as a 'beta supplicant' within a power structure. He attempts to project the expression of an 'Apex Male' in the public sphere, yet every action reveals the truth: he is merely a tool in Nigel Farage's game. In this existential war, Tice's optimal expression is not leadership, but acting as a useful, void-like vessel with no subjectivity.

His complaints about DEI policies are particularly farcical. In a genuine meritocracy, someone as dim and incompetent as Tice wouldn't even make it to the starting line. His very existence is a product of structural violence—where capital serves as a cognitive entry point, manufacturing the illusion of a 'politician.' He uses wealth to mask a lack of competence and performs a 'strong right-wing' persona to hide his absolute subservience to Farage.

His reaction to the climate crisis—celebrating better sparkling wine amidst droughts and wildfires—is a textbook manifestation of meta violence. For his class, reality is not something to be faced, but something to be defined and consumed. Since reality refuses to cooperate (the planet is burning), he simply redefines it (it's just great barbecue weather). This ability to convert the survival crises of others into personal aesthetic pleasure is the pinnacle of a masculine-centric narrative: the world is a playground, and the suffering of others is merely background noise.

Tice is not engaging in politics; he is performing a low-grade act. As he attempts to fill the power vacuum left by Farage, he discovers that he himself is the vacuum. This death of subjectivity is the inevitable end for anyone pursuing a 'fake optimal expression'—trading their own essence to play a role approved by the powerful.
