偶像工业的账本，从来不记练习生的年龄The Idol Industry's Ledger Never Records a Trainee's Age

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
四条独立生产线，一台机器没变
Four solo pipelines, one machine unchanged.

Billboard 这篇把 BLACKPINK 四个人的个人作品按年份排了一遍，从 2012 年 ROSÉ 在 G-Dragon 歌里当匿名女声，排到 2026 年 LISA 的 EP。读起来像荣誉榜。实际上是一份劳动履历：谁在哪一年被放出来单独变现，谁先谁后，谁和谁合作。

名单里最诚实的一行是 ROSÉ 那条：2012 年，她还是 YG 练习生体系里的“年轻练习生”，被放在 G-Dragon 的歌里当一个不署名的女声。JENNIE 2013 年那三首合作，原文自己写了：当时她也还是练习生。这就是 K-pop 模型的核心工序——练习生在出道之前就已经在产出，产出的收益先归公司。名单把这段写成“有些人出道前就开始做音乐了”，语气是励志的。

然后是 JISOO。她是四个人里最后一个发个人作品的，2023 年，出道七年之后。原文给她的位置是“finally joined in on the fun”。fun 这个词用得很轻。四个人同一家公司、同一个团、同一套合约，个人作品的时间表却差了五年，这不是巧合，是排期。谁的 solo 先上、谁的先压着，和音乐能力无关，和团体的商业节奏、和每个人被允许占用的认知入口有关。

值得单独拎出来的是 LISA 那条：和 RCA 签约，“in partnership with her own LLOUD company, which will allow her to retain ownership of her master recordings”。母带所有权，这是整篇名单里唯一一条关于谁拥有作品的条款。它出现在 LISA 身上，不出现在别人的条目里。也就是说，这份名单里绝大多数作品，所有权结构是不写的——不写，通常意味着不在艺人手里。

至于这些歌在唱什么：JENNIE 的“Mantra”被描述成给所有“pretty girls”的宣言，“Born Again”被写成“channel luxury and confidence”。这是第三章那套东西的当代版本——把女性包装成自信、奢侈、被凝视的完美客体，然后卖回去。它不制造直接的伤害，它制造标准。

名单结尾停在 2026 年 8 月的公告上：LISA 的 EP 两个月后发。一条产品线的排期表，被写成了四个女孩的成长史。而那个 2012 年在别人歌里不署名的练习生，和 2026 年拥有自己母带的艺人，中间隔着十四年——这十四年里，谁拿走了差额，名单一个字没提。

Billboard has laid out BLACKPINK's four solo careers in chronological order, from ROSÉ singing uncredited on a G-Dragon track in 2012 to LISA's EP in 2026. It reads like an honor roll. It is actually a labor record: who was released to be monetized alone, in which year, in what order, with whom.

The most honest line in the list is ROSÉ's: in 2012, still a "young trainee" inside YG's development program, she was placed on a G-Dragon song as an unnamed vocalist. JENNIE's three 2013 features — the piece says it itself — happened while she was also still a trainee. That is the core工序 of the K-pop model: trainees produce before debut, and the returns go to the company first. The list frames this as "some of them started working on music before they rose to fame." The tone is inspirational.

Then JISOO. She was the last of the four to release solo music, in 2023, seven years after debut. The piece gives her the position of "finally joined in on the fun." Fun is a light word. Same company, same group, same contract structure — and a five-year spread in solo timelines. That is not coincidence, it is scheduling. Whose solo goes first and whose is held back has nothing to do with musical ability and everything to do with the group's commercial rhythm and how much cognitive entry each member is permitted to occupy.

One entry deserves to be pulled out: LISA signing with RCA "in partnership with her own LLOUD company, which will allow her to retain ownership of her master recordings." Master ownership — the only clause in the entire list about who owns the work. It appears for LISA and for no one else. Which means for most of the catalog here, the ownership structure is simply not written. Not written usually means not with the artist.

As for what the songs say: JENNIE's "Mantra" is described as a manifesto for all "pretty girls," "Born Again" as channeling "luxury and confidence." This is the contemporary version of the Chapter 3 apparatus — packaging women as confident, luxurious, perfectly gazeable objects and selling them back. It inflicts no direct harm. It manufactures a standard.

The list ends on an August 2026 announcement: LISA's EP drops two months later. A product pipeline schedule, written as the coming-of-age story of four girls. And between the trainee who sang uncredited on someone else's song in 2012 and the artist who owns her masters in 2026 lie fourteen years — fourteen years in which someone captured the difference, a difference the list does not mention once.
