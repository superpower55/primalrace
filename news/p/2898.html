断头台、歌词与政治表演：谁在定义“煽动”？Guillotines, Lyrics, and Political Performance: Who Defines 'Incitement'?

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
法律被武器化为政治博弈的工具，而“文明”则是掩盖这种掠夺的遮羞布。
Law is weaponized as a tool for political gambling, with 'civilization' serving as the veil for this predation.

这是一场典型的关于“解释权”的权力博弈。Nigel Farage 并不在乎那件 T-shirt 到底是在致敬 1979 年的 XTC 乐队还是在策划谋杀，他在乎的是通过将对方定义为“煽动谋杀者”，从而在公共空间中完成一次身份的置换：将自己从一个极右翼煽动者，包装成一个受害者和法治的捍卫者。

在这种叙事中，Police 的“conducting inquiries”成了最完美的武器。当警察介入时，事实本身（歌词、讽刺、亚文化）已经不重要了，重要的是“被调查”这个标签所带来的结构性压力。这是一种典型的文化暴力（cultural violence）：利用法律程序的严肃性，将政治对手在认知入口处直接定格为“危险分子”。

而绿党 spokesperson 的回应则是一次极其低级的“假.最优解表达”。他们试图通过强调“kindness”和“不鼓励分歧”来寻求公正，但这在残酷的存在性战争中毫无意义。在 Farage 这种熟练运用武器化表达的玩家面前，谈论“善良”无异于在战场上递交投名状，它不仅不能消弭暴力，反而通过承认对方的叙事逻辑，进一步让渡了主体性。

最讽刺的是，这场关于“安全”和“谋杀”的表演，精准地锚定了前任议员被杀的社会情绪。当“安全”被定义为“不准用断头台图案做讽刺”时，真正的结构性暴力——那些由极右翼叙事驱动的、针对边缘群体的真实暴力——反而被掩盖在了这种表演性的法治狂欢之中。

This is a classic power game over the 'right to interpret.' Nigel Farage doesn't care whether that T-shirt is a nod to a 1979 XTC song or a murder plot; he cares about repositioning his identity in the public sphere. By labeling his opponent an 'inciter of murder,' he transforms himself from a far-right agitator into a victim and a defender of the rule of law.

In this narrative, the police 'conducting inquiries' becomes the perfect weapon. Once the authorities intervene, the actual facts—lyrics, satire, subculture—become irrelevant. What matters is the structural pressure exerted by the label of 'under investigation.' This is a textbook example of cultural violence: using the solemnity of legal procedures to freeze a political opponent as a 'dangerous element' at the cognitive entry point.

Meanwhile, the Green party spokesperson's response is a pathetic 'false optimal expression.' Trying to seek justice by emphasizing 'kindness' and 'avoiding division' is meaningless in a fierce existential war. In the face of a player like Farage, who skillfully weaponizes expression, preaching 'kindness' is akin to surrendering. It doesn't eliminate violence; it merely validates the opponent's narrative logic and erases one's own agency.

The irony is that this performance of 'security' and 'murder' precisely anchors itself to the public anxiety following the death of a former MP. When 'security' is defined as 'not using guillotine imagery for satire,' the real structural violence—the actual violence against marginalized groups driven by far-right narratives—is hidden beneath this performative celebration of the law.
