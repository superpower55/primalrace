不让你就座，也是一种暴力Not Seating You Is Also Violence

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国智库「美国复兴中心」（CRA）发文主张，即便部分民主党候选人赢得选举，也可以因其被指与「红绿联盟」有关联而拒绝其就任国会议员；该文点名「小队」四名进步派有色人种女性议员及纽约市长马姆达尼、密歇根参议员候选人埃尔-赛义德。CRA由现任白宫管理和预算办公室主任沃特创立，多名法学教授认为该主张在法庭上难以成立，但指出其目的是制造恐惧。
The newsA post by the Center for Renewing America, a right-wing think tank founded by current OMB director Russell Vought, argues that some Democratic candidates—including Muslim and progressive politicians—could be prevented from taking office even if they win. Law professors say the legal theory would likely fail in court, but that floating it still creates fear.
他们不打算赢下辩论，只打算取消对手的座位。
They don't plan to win the argument. They plan to cancel the seat.

这篇东西最值得注意的地方，不是它会不会在法庭上成立——好几位法学教授已经说了，不会——而是它把「谁有资格被算作政治对手」这件事，从选票手里收回到了一小撮人手里。

CRA的文章说，一个当选的议员如果「个人接受了这些群体所主张的对宪政秩序的摧毁」，就不能诚实地宣誓支持宪政秩序，因此可以不予就座。翻译一下：先由我们定义你信奉什么，再由我们判定你信奉的东西违宪，最后你赢了多少票都不算数。定义权、审判权、执行权，一条龙。

它点名的对象很说明问题。「小队」四名女性——奥卡西奥-科尔特斯、特莱布、普雷斯利、奥马尔——加上马姆达尼、埃尔-赛义德，被归进一个叫「红绿联盟」的筐里：「新马克思主义进步派」和「反西方的穆斯林」。报道写得很清楚，这些人里没有一个自称马克思主义者。筐是外面的人编的，编筐的人不需要你同意，你只需要被装进去。

这就是共谋者理论里最典型的一幕：规则看上去是中立的——「宣誓支持宪法」，多正当——但谁被判定为违背了它，由制定规则的人说了算。CRA的创始人沃特现在管着白宫预算办公室，报道提到他扣留、延迟、拒绝拨付国会批准的资金，包括去年冻结50亿美元对外援助、今年收回超过8亿美元。一个正在被指违反拨款法的人，写文章论证别人不配宣誓遵守宪法。

再往里一层。为什么是穆斯林，为什么是有色人种的女性？因为「反西方的穆斯林」这个标签一贴，宗教审查就变成了安全问题，「进步派女性」这个标签一贴，政治竞争就变成了文明存亡。报道里布朗大学的布雷特施奈德说得很直接：宪法明文禁止以宗教测试作为任职条件，这条路更难走。但难走不等于没人走，走不通的诉讼照样能制造效果。

效果是什么，布雷特施奈德说得很清楚：威胁和打压对手会制造恐惧，让人担心「下一个会不会是我」。这才是这篇东西真正的产品。它不生产判决，它生产寒意。一个候选人赢了选举，却要在接下来几个月里准备应诉、准备被质疑资格、准备面对一场「国会诉法院」的对峙——这些成本不会写进任何判决书，但它实实在在地消耗掉了一个人本可以用来做事的时间和位置。

加尔通的公式在这里很好用：本可以达到的状态是，赢得选举的人按时就座、开始工作；实际的状态是，她或他得先证明自己有资格被允许坐下。这个差额，就是暴力。它不在直接层——没有人挨打；它在结构层和元层之间来回滑动：用一套看似中立的资格审核程序，把「谁算合法的政治参与者」的解释权，从选民手里挪到一小群自称「激进宪政主义者」的人手里。

格罗斯说得很实在：如果民主党只赢一两个参议院席位，共和党可能真的会去争；如果赢四席，「就更不可能，也更荒谬」。也就是说，这套策略的可行性不取决于法律，取决于票数差距。法律在这里不是原则，是余量。

所以这篇文章真正暴露的，不是它有多荒谬——皮尔兹斯一句话就够：「这不会发生。」——而是它假定了一个前提：选举结果是可以被重新审核的，只要审核的人够坚决。这个前提一旦被公开写出来、被一个有影响力的智库署名发表、被现任政府官员创办的机构推广，它就已经在改变游戏的规则了，不管法庭最后怎么判。

报道里没有写的是：那些被点名的候选人，尤其是埃尔-赛义德的竞选团队，对这件事没有回应。沉默不等于没有代价。他们大概正在算，要不要为一场还没赢的选举，先准备一场关于自己有没有资格就座的官司。这笔账，没有人替他们付。

The most telling thing about this piece isn't whether it would hold up in court—several law professors have already said it wouldn't—but that it moves the question of who counts as a legitimate political opponent out of the hands of voters and into the hands of a small group of people.

The CRA post argues that an elected member who has "personally embraced the destruction of the constitutional order" cannot truthfully swear to support that order, and therefore need not be seated. Translated: first we define what you believe, then we rule that what you believe is unconstitutional, and finally your votes don't count. Definition, judgment, execution—one stop.

The targets are instructive. The four women of "the Squad"—Ocasio-Cortez, Tlaib, Pressley, Omar—along with Mamdani and El-Sayed, are packed into a box called the "Red-Green Alliance": "neo-Marxist progressives" and "anti-Western Muslims." The report notes that none of them has self-identified as a Marxist. The box is built from the outside. The people building it don't need your consent; they just need you inside it.

This is the conspirator's playbook in its purest form: the rule looks neutral—"swear to support the Constitution," how reasonable—but who gets judged to have violated it is decided by the people who wrote the rule. Vought, who founded the CRA, now runs the White House budget office. The report notes he has impounded, delayed, and refused to disburse funds Congress approved, including freezing $5 billion in foreign aid last year and clawing back more than $800 million this year. A man accused of violating the Impoundment Control Act is writing articles about who is unfit to swear an oath to the Constitution.

Go one layer deeper. Why Muslims, why women of color? Because the label "anti-Western Muslims" turns a religious test into a security question, and the label "progressive women" turns ordinary political competition into a civilizational emergency. Brettschneider says it plainly: the Constitution explicitly forbids a religious test for office, so that route is even harder. But hard to win doesn't mean nobody walks it. A lawsuit that fails still produces effects.

What effects? Brettschneider again: threatening and shutting down opponents creates fear, makes people worry "they might come after me next." That's the real product here. It doesn't manufacture verdicts; it manufactures a chill. A candidate wins an election and then spends months preparing to defend their eligibility, bracing for a Congress-versus-courts showdown—costs that appear in no ruling but consume the time and the seat a person was elected to use.

Galtung's formula fits: the potential is that the winner takes the seat and gets to work; the actual is that she first has to prove she's allowed to sit down. The gap is violence. Not direct—nobody is beaten. It slides between structural and meta: a neutral-looking eligibility review that moves the power to define "who counts as a legitimate participant" from voters to a small group of self-described "radical constitutionalists."

Grose is blunt about the math: if Democrats win one or two Senate seats, Republicans might actually fight it; if they win four, it becomes "more preposterous." The strategy's viability doesn't depend on law. It depends on the margin. Law here isn't a principle; it's a remainder.

So what this piece really exposes isn't how absurd it is—Pildes needs one sentence: "This isn't going to happen."—but the premise it assumes: that election results are subject to re-audit, if the auditors are determined enough. Once that premise is written down, signed by an influential think tank, and promoted by an institution founded by a sitting administration official, it has already changed the rules of the game, no matter what a court later says.

What the report doesn't say: the named candidates, El-Sayed's campaign in particular, did not respond. Silence is not the same as no cost. They are probably calculating whether to prepare, for an election not yet won, a lawsuit about whether they are allowed to be seated. Nobody is paying that bill for them.
