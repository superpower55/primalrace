在米其林鸽子餐与殖民怀旧中，被消解的“奥德赛”The 'Odyssey' Dissolved in Michelin Pigeons and Colonial Nostalgia

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-02 § 链接
用中产阶级的审美快感掩盖历史暴力，是最高级的文化共谋。
Using bourgeois aesthetic pleasure to mask historical violence is the ultimate form of cultural complicity.

一个穿着宽大亚麻裤子的资深演员，在马耳他的米其林餐厅舔盘子，在殖民时期的酒店喝无酒精鸡尾酒，然后对着巴洛克式教堂的黄金涡卷赞叹不已。这哪里是寻找奥德修斯的足迹，这分明是一次标准的、被精心包装的男性中产阶级审美巡礼。整个叙事把一个关于战争、流离失所与苦难的史诗，简化为了一个“充满惊喜”的旅游Vlog。

最讽刺的是，当他面对那些埋葬了400名“战士骑士”的墓地时，他将其定义为“死亡与荣耀的杰作”。这种表达是典型的武器化叙事：通过将死亡美学化，把直接暴力（direct violence）转化为一种可供消费的文化景观（cultural violence）。至于那个所谓的“真实且血腥的战争”现场，在Grant巨大的笑容和“模糊的事实与虚构”之间，被轻描淡写地处理成了一个背景板。

这种“chill”的姿态其实是一种极其傲慢的共谋。他利用自己的阶级身份和文化资本，在认知入口处定义了什么是“现代的奥德赛”——即：只要我有足够的审美能力和经济能力，历史的血腥就可以被转化为“启示”，殖民的阴影可以被转化为“怀旧”。

至于片尾提到的难民、气候变化和政治危机，在这一整套由奢华酒店和米其林餐厅构建的叙事逻辑中，不过是用来增加一点点“深刻感”的装饰品。这种所谓的“相关性”是表演性的，它不打算触碰任何结构性暴力，而只是为了让这场贵族式的狂欢在道德上显得更体面。

A veteran actor in voluminous linen trousers licking plates at a Michelin-starred restaurant in Malta, sipping lime mocktails in a colonial-era hotel, and marveling at the golden whorls of a Baroque cathedral. This isn't a search for Odysseus; it's a textbook bourgeois male aesthetic pilgrimage. The entire narrative reduces an epic of war, displacement, and suffering into a 'surprising' travelogue.

The irony peaks when he describes the tombs of 400 'warrior knights' as a 'masterpiece of death and glory.' This is a textbook weaponization of expression: by aestheticizing death, direct violence is converted into a consumable cultural landscape, a form of cultural violence. The 'real, bloody war' at Troy is treated as a mere backdrop, conveniently blurred into the gap between Grant's massive grin and the 'blurring of fact and fiction.'

This 'chill' attitude is a manifestation of arrogant complicity. Leveraging his class status and cultural capital, he defines the 'Modern Odyssey' at the cognitive entry point: as long as one possesses sufficient aesthetic and economic power, historical bloodshed can be rebranded as 'revelation' and colonial shadows as 'nostalgia.'

As for the mentions of refugees, climate change, and political crises at the end, they are merely decorative ornaments used to add a veneer of 'profundity' to a narrative built on luxury hotels and fine dining. This claimed relevance is purely performative; it refuses to engage with structural violence, serving only to make this patrician carnival appear more morally palatable.
