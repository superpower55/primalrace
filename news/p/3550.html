凝视的政治学：谁在定义“看”的权力The Politics of Gaze: Who Defines the Power of Seeing

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
凝视不是审美，而是一种权力分配的政治行为。
Gazing is not aesthetics; it is a political act of power distribution.

约翰·伯格 (John Berger) 在《观看之道》 (Ways of Seeing) 中撕开了艺术史最核心的 scam：所谓的“纯粹审美”其实是男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 的掩体。当他指出女性裸体画是为满足 male gaze 而产出时，他实际上是在揭露一种 cultural violence —— 女性的身体在艺术中被物化为客体，其存在性被让渡给了观看者的权力。这种凝视本质上是元暴力的具体实践：谁拥有定义“美”的权力，谁就垄断了对事实的解释权。

但有趣的是，这本传记揭示了伯格本人的存在性战争。他在书页上是激进的马克思主义者，在现实中却是分裂的：一边哀悼农村社区的消亡，一边在瑞士和法国的豪宅间切换，在不同女性之间分配时间。这种 contradiction 恰恰证明了，即便是一个看穿了结构性暴力的知识分子，在私域博弈中依然难以逃离父权结构的惯性。他解构了油画中的 male gaze，却在生活中扮演了那个掌控资源和选择权的 gaze 主体。

伯格的伟大在于他把“观看”从审美领域拉到了政治领域。他告诉我们，每一次看向某物的行为，都是一次关于权力的投票。但我们今天阅读他的传记时，必须意识到：仅仅意识到凝视是权力，还不够。真正的 Just Expression 应该是打破这种不对称的凝视结构，让被观看者拿回定义自己的权力，而不是在另一个男性的叙事框架里被重新“定义”为进步。

John Berger, in Ways of Seeing, tore through the core scam of art history: the so-called "pure aesthetics" is merely a cover for a masculine-centric narrative. By pointing out that female nudes were produced for the male gaze, he exposed a form of cultural violence—the female body is objectified, and her existence is surrendered to the power of the observer. This gaze is a concrete practice of meta-violence: whoever holds the power to define "beauty" monopolizes the right to interpret reality.

Yet, this biography reveals Berger's own existential war. On the page, he was a radical Marxist; in reality, he was contradictory: mourning the loss of rural communities while shuttling between villas in Switzerland and France, splitting his time among different women. This contradiction proves that even an intellectual who sees through structural violence struggles to escape the inertia of patriarchal structures in the private sphere. He dismantled the male gaze in oil paintings, yet played the role of the gazing subject who controlled resources and choices in his own life.

Berger's greatness lies in dragging "seeing" from the realm of aesthetics into the realm of politics. He taught us that every act of looking is a vote on power. But as we read his biography today, we must realize: merely recognizing that the gaze is power is not enough. A truly Just Expression requires breaking this asymmetrical structure of gazing, allowing the observed to reclaim the power of self-definition, rather than being "redefined" as progressive within another masculine narrative.
