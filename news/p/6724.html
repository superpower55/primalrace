投票前一天，谁在制造事实The Day Before the Vote, Who Manufactures Facts

国际 结构层 · 文化层 NPR ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国司法部在选举日前一天请求最高法院介入，要求加州将约两万名「疑似」非公民的选票搁置、暂不计票。联邦地区法院和第九巡回法院此前均驳回该请求，认定联邦名单不可靠，且选举前夕更改规则违反 Purcell 原则。司法部近期还警告地方选举官员可能被刑事起诉，并向洛杉矶县选民登记处员工发出大陪审团传票。
The newsA day before Election Day, the Justice Department asked the Supreme Court to order California to set aside and stop processing the ballots of 20,000 people it calls "likely" noncitizens. Lower courts rejected the request, calling the federal list unreliable and citing the Purcell principle. DOJ has also warned local election officials of criminal prosecution and subpoenaed Los Angeles County registrar staff.
他们不需要赢官司，只需要让计票看起来可疑。
They don't need to win the case. They need the count to look suspect.

司法部在选举前一天要求最高法院把两万张票先扣下来。注意这个时间点：不是选举前三个月核查名单，不是选举后走法律程序，是投票日前一天。这不是在纠错，这是在制造一个「这次选举有问题」的先例。

法院已经说了两件事。第一，联邦那份名单不可靠。第二，第九巡回法院引用了 Purcell 原则——选举前夕不改规则——还引用了《全国选民登记法》，联邦选举前九十天内不得系统性移除选民。两个法官，一个特朗普任命，一个拜登任命，写了同一份意见：照司法部的要求做，「可能导致剥夺选举权，并可能危及该县整场选举」。连特朗普自己任命的法官都不同意。

但司法部要的可能从来不是这两万张票。报道里写得很清楚：实际有争议的选票「很可能远少于两万张」，因为县选举官员发现名单上很多人根本没有有效登记，也没收到选票。一个数字被反复引用，从两万变成一个事实上的零，中间没人纠正。这就是武器化的标准操作：先占据认知入口，把「两万疑似非公民投票」变成公共记忆，至于后来法院怎么说、名单准不准，传播上已经不重要了。

更值得看的是它同时在做什么。警告地方选举官员不配合就可能被刑事起诉，向洛杉矶县选民登记处员工发大陪审团传票。一边打官司，一边让办事的人害怕。这不是法律行动，这是让执行选举的人在投票日当天掂量自己的处境。结构层的暴力不需要改法律，只需要让法律执行者知道谁在盯着他们。

第九巡回法院的两位法官守住了 Purcell 原则，这是实打实的一层：在投票日之前，规则没有被改掉。但这场胜利的代价是它本来就不该发生——一个联邦部门动用刑事起诉的威胁和大陪审团传票，去追一份自己都承认「很可能」的名单，而法院只能赶在投票前一天把它挡下来。

最高法院接不接、来不来得及在选举日前表态，报道没说。它没说的事还有一件：名单上那两万个人里，有多少是公民，有多少根本没登记，报道只给了「很多」。谁在被点名，谁在为自己的投票权辩护，报道里一个名字都没有。他们不在文章里，但他们才是这场官司真正的当事人。

The Justice Department asked the Supreme Court to hold back twenty thousand ballots one day before Election Day. Not three months before, when a list could be checked. Not after the vote, through ordinary litigation. The day before. This is not error correction. This is the manufacture of a precedent: that this election was suspect.

The courts have already said two things. The federal list is unreliable. And the Ninth Circuit invoked Purcell — no changing election rules on the eve of an election — along with the National Voter Registration Act, which bars systematic removal of voters within ninety days of a federal election. Two judges, one appointed by Trump, one by Biden, wrote the same opinion: granting the request "could result in disenfranchisement and could jeopardize the County's entire election." Even Trump's own appointee would not go along.

But the twenty thousand ballots were probably never the point. The report says the actual number in dispute is "likely much smaller," because county officials found many people on the list had no active registration and were never mailed ballots. A number gets repeated until it becomes a fact, then turns out to be close to zero, and nobody corrects the record. That is the standard operation: seize the cognitive entry point, plant "twenty thousand likely noncitizens" in public memory, and let the courts' findings arrive too late to matter.

Watch what else is happening at the same time. Local election officials are warned they could be criminally prosecuted for not cooperating. Grand jury subpoenas go to staff at the Los Angeles County registrar's office. A lawsuit on one hand, fear delivered to the people running the election on the other. Structural violence does not need to change the law. It only needs the people enforcing the law to know who is watching them.

The two judges held the line on Purcell, and that is real: the rules were not altered before the vote. But the price of this win is that it should never have been necessary — a federal department deploying criminal prosecution threats and grand jury subpoenas against a list it itself calls "likely," with courts left to block it the day before the election.

Whether the Supreme Court takes the case, or rules in time, the report does not say. Neither does it say how many of the twenty thousand are citizens, how many were never registered — only that "many" were not. No one on that list has a name in this story. They are the actual parties to this case, and they are not in it.
