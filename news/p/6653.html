万圣节墓碑立错了地方The Tombstones Are on the Wrong Lawn

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
粉丝在给工作室立碑，资本在给表达定价。
Fans build graves for studios; capital prices expression.

粉丝在EA门口立了一排墓碑，Bullfrog、Pandemic、Visceral、Westwood、Origin、Maxis，还有BioWare。每个碑上刻着成立和关闭的年份、一句墓志铭、顶上一行RIP。报道说做的人时间不够，没做完。

这是表达的葬礼，不是公司的葬礼。一个工作室死了，死的不是几十个岗位，是一整套做事的方式，谁来决定什么游戏值得做、做给谁、做多久。BioWare在《质量效应》宇宙里泡了五年多，然后被取消。五年多的表达，一句话就没了。

关掉它的是谁？报道写得很清楚：沙特主权基金、Jared Kushner的Affinity Partners、私募股权公司Silver Lake参与的收购。一个前EA经理早就预见了这次关闭。私募股权买工作室不是为了做游戏，是为了在资产负债表上做加减法。做游戏是慢的、不确定的、要养人的；加减法是快的、确定的、可以裁人的。这两种时间尺度根本不在一个博弈里，粉丝在万圣节刻石头，资本在季度报表上划掉一行。

所以EA沉默。报道说EA认为《星球大战》是更可靠的科幻动作游戏IP。可靠。这个词就是全部的答案。不是质量，不是玩家，不是工作室的历史，是可靠。可靠意味着可预测的回报率，意味着风险被压到最低，意味着表达被压成产品线。

粉丝做了什么？马德里有人在EA办公楼外喷了“Save Mass Effect”，纽约有人买了时代广场的广告牌。报道说这比很多人想的传播得更广。但传播广不等于有力量。这些表达进不了那个做决定的房间。买广告牌的钱、喷漆的勇气、刻墓碑的时间，全都投进了一个不收这种货币的市场。

墓碑上给BioWare选的那句话是Mordin Solus说的：“It had to be Me. Someone else might have gotten it wrong.” 用在这里很妙，也很讽刺。Mordin说的是责任，是有人得去做那件难的事。而现在，做那件难的事的人被赶走了，留下的是做容易的事的人。

粉丝的愤怒是真的，社区的团结也是真的。但团结起来保卫一个已经被买走的东西，这个结构本身就有问题。你保卫的不是你的工作室，是别人的资产。资产的主人算过账了，你的眼泪不在账上。

墓碑立得再整齐，也是立在别人的门口。真正该问的是：这些工作室为什么可以被这样买来卖去？谁给了私募股权把五年多的表达当废纸撕掉的权力？粉丝在给死者立碑，没人去问是谁签的死亡证明。

Fans set up a graveyard outside EA's offices. Bullfrog, Pandemic, Visceral, Westwood, Origin, Maxis, and BioWare. Each tombstone has the studio's logo, founding and closure dates, a quote, and RIP across the top. The creator ran out of time to make more.

This is a funeral for expression, not for companies. When a studio dies, what dies isn't just jobs. It's a whole way of doing things: who decides which games are worth making, for whom, and for how long. BioWare spent over half a decade on the Mass Effect Universe. Then it was canceled. Five-plus years of expression, gone in a sentence.

Who shut it down? The report is clear: a buyout involving Saudi Arabia's private investment fund, Jared Kushner's Affinity Partners, and private equity firm Silver Lake. A former EA manager saw the closure coming. Private equity doesn't buy studios to make games. It buys them to do arithmetic on a balance sheet. Making games is slow, uncertain, and requires keeping people. Arithmetic is fast, certain, and requires cutting them. These two time scales aren't playing the same game. Fans carve stone on Halloween; capital crosses out a line on a quarterly report.

So EA stays silent. The report says EA sees Star Wars as a more reliable franchise for a sci-fi action game. Reliable. That word is the whole answer. Not quality, not players, not the studio's history. Reliable means predictable returns, means risk compressed to a minimum, means expression flattened into a product line.

What did fans do? Someone in Madrid spray-painted "Save Mass Effect" outside an EA office building. In New York, fans bought a billboard in Times Square. The report says the reach is wider than many would've thought. But reach isn't power. None of this enters the room where the decision was made. The billboard money, the spray-paint courage, the hours spent carving tombstones—all of it went into a market that doesn't accept this currency.

The quote chosen for BioWare is Mordin Solus: "It had to be Me. Someone else might have gotten it wrong." It fits, and it stings. Mordin is talking about responsibility, about someone having to do the hard thing. Now the people who did the hard thing are gone, and the people who do the easy thing remain.

The anger is real. The community coming together is real. But rallying to defend something that's already been bought—that structure is the problem. You're not defending your studio. You're defending someone else's asset. The owner has done the math. Your tears aren't on the ledger.

The tombstones are neat, but they're standing on someone else's lawn. The real question is: why can these studios be bought and sold like this? Who gave private equity the power to tear up five-plus years of expression like scrap paper? Fans are erecting headstones for the dead. Nobody's asking who signed the death certificate.
