Zyzz：一个关于“最优解表达”的绝望模拟Zyzz: A Desperate Simulation of 'Optimal Expression'

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
当一个人必须扮演一个神格化的角色才能获得存在感，主体性就已死亡。
When one must perform a deified persona to be seen, subjectivity is already dead.

Zyzz 的故事不是一个关于健身或药物滥用的个案，而是一场典型的存在性战争。对于一个库尔德裔移民、在社会边缘挣扎的少年来说，真实的自我（Aziz）在既定结构中没有票价。于是他制造了一个名为 "Zyzz" 的虚拟人格——一个极度自负、掌控审美定价权、嘲讽他人嫉妒的 "Aesthetics" 之神。这就是他在当时环境下找到的假.最优解表达：通过扮演一个被男性中心叙事认可的“强者”角色，来对冲身份认同的缺失。

这场博弈的代价是主体性的全面让渡。正文中那句 "Zyzz is a person who took over who I am" 揭示了最残酷的真相：当 Aziz 成功地将 Zyzz 武器化并以此夺取认知入口时，他自己成为了这个产品的第一名祭品。他必须通过不断的 PEDs 堆砌、手术整容来维持这个“神”的表型，因为在男本位的逻辑里，只有极致的物理强悍才能换取解释权。这种对表型的病态追求，本质上是对生物墙的绝望冲撞。

最讽刺的共谋在于，他在死后被后来的 "looksmaxxing" 和 Manosphere 潮流神格化。这些后继者将一个因药物相互作用而死、在私下里忍受社交焦虑和存在危机的悲剧，简化为一个“追求极致美学”的图腾。这种叙事抹去了 Aziz 的痛苦，将其转化为一种可消费的、鼓励年轻男性继续陷入“表型竞赛”的文化暴力。所谓的“成为自己故事的英雄”，如果前提是必须通过药物和手术去模拟一个虚假的强者，那这不过是另一场精心包装的 scam。

The story of Zyzz is not a mere case of bodybuilding or drug abuse, but a classic existential war. For a Kurdish immigrant youth struggling on the margins, the authentic self (Aziz) had no 'ticket' in the existing structure. Thus, he engineered a persona named "Zyzz"—an unabashedly vain god of "Aesthetics" who controlled the pricing of beauty and mocked the envy of others. This was his fake optimal expression: playing a "strongman" role validated by the masculine-centric narrative to offset the void of identity.

The cost of this game was the total surrender of subjectivity. The line "Zyzz is a person who took over who I am" reveals the brutal truth: while Aziz successfully weaponized Zyzz to seize the cognitive entrance, he became the first sacrifice of his own product. He was forced to maintain the phenotype of this "god" through PEDs and surgeries, because in masculine-centric logic, only extreme physical dominance grants the right to interpret reality. This pathological obsession with phenotype is, in essence, a desperate collision with the biological wall.

The ultimate complicity lies in how he was deified by the subsequent "looksmaxxing" and Manosphere trends. These successors reduced a tragedy—a man who died of drug interaction and suffered from social anxiety—into a totem of "extreme aesthetics." This narrative erases Aziz's suffering and transforms it into a form of cultural violence that encourages young men to stay trapped in the "phenotype race." The promise of "becoming the hero of your own story" is a complete scam if the prerequisite is simulating a fake strongman through chemicals and scalpels.
