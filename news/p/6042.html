先被裁掉的是找到病人的人The First Thing Cut Was the People Who Found the Patients

国际 结构层 · 直接层 The Intercept ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要2025年1月20日特朗普就职当天，约翰内斯堡提供HIV预防和跨性别医疗的Engage诊所突然关闭，86名员工中82人被解雇。报道称，美国2024年向南非提供5.8亿美元援助，其中78%用于HIV/AIDS；6月美国宣布将终止对南非的全部HIV资助。研究人员表示，仅约翰内斯堡和比勒陀利亚地区就有约2万名HIV感染者失联。
The newsEngage, a Johannesburg clinic offering HIV prevention and gender-affirming care, closed abruptly on January 20, 2025, the day Trump took office; 82 of its 86 workers were laid off. The U.S. sent $580 million to South Africa in 2024, 78% for HIV/AIDS, and in June announced it would end all HIV funding there. About 20,000 people with HIV are lost to care in the Johannesburg and Pretoria region.
削减的不是预算，是找到病人的那双眼睛
What was cut wasn't a budget. It was the eyes that found the patients.

特朗普政府关掉的不只是诊所，是一整套主动找人的机制。南非有近6400万人口，约800万人携带HIV，大约每8个人里一个（来源：The Intercept）。在这样的国家，公共卫生不能等病人上门，得有人出门去找。Aurum Institute的Jacqueline Pienaar算过一笔账：特朗普回到白宫之后，仅约翰内斯堡和比勒陀利亚一带，HIV防治组织就跟丢了大约两万名感染者。她的原话是，以前谁没来拿药，就去找他。现在没人去找了。病毒载量会飙升，传播会扩大，这是可以预见的，不是意外。

这不是政策失误，是政策本身。2025年1月20日，特朗普签了两份行政令：14169，重新评估并调整美国对外援助；14168，保卫女性免受性别意识形态极端主义侵害、恢复联邦政府的生物学真相。几个小时内，运营Engage诊所的OUT LGBT Well-Being通知员工第二天不用来上班，工作电脑也别再打开。86个员工里走了82个。这两份行政令放在同一天签，不是巧合：一份砍钱，一份砍人，砍的是同一批人。

Engage诊所开在约翰内斯堡Reserve街，离七所大学不远，很多学生在第一次性行为之前就来过这里。门口还挂着PEPFAR的标志、彩虹色的Simon Tseko Nkoli壁画、写着“来自美国人民的礼物”的USAID牌子。现在接手的是一个叫USpace的志愿诊所，每周开四小时，能测HIV，没有药，没有预防，没有性别肯定治疗。牌子还在，礼物没了。

钱曾经是有的。2024年美国向南非提供5.8亿美元，其中78%、4.55亿用于HIV/AIDS。这笔钱也从来不是纯粹的慈善：南非是全球HIV临床试验最多的国家，美国药企的利润依赖南非人参与试验；病毒不分国界，控制住感染人数最多的国家，全世界才更安全。所以这不是“美国慷慨解囊然后撤了”，是美国撤出了自己利益所在的阵地，顺手把当地付代价的人留在原地。

代价落在谁身上，报道写得很清楚。Gwendolyn Dube，35岁，在Orange Farm的铁皮棚屋里住了多年，没有自来水没有电。她童年被强奸时感染HIV，是南非最早一批用上抗逆转录病毒药物的儿童。她拿药从三个月一次变成两个月、一个月，然后被告知“没有你的档案”。诊所工作人员说，现在人们要等8小时、10小时、12小时，有时等完也见不到人；对赤贫的人来说，损失一天的工钱就是灾难。诊所的护士长否认缺药，但承认病人“会走掉”，也许不再回来；以前她每天派流动队下乡，现在一周一次。

还有一层代价，报道里没有直接点破，但顺着读就看见了：跨性别女性失去激素治疗后，转向黑市买药，用可能不无菌的针头注射，HIV和肝炎的风险随之上升。Shon说，她认识的两个跨性别女性，一个断了激素后变得极度虔诚、停药、出现自杀倾向；另一个在黑市买药买到肝出问题。这不是“性别肯定治疗很进步所以重要”，而是：当正规医疗入口被堵死，人不会停止需要它，只会从更危险的入口进去。

最讽刺的一段在最后。Lenacapavir，半年一针的HIV预防新药，一年定价2.8万美元；全球基金和吉利德谈成每剂40美元，限量生产，南非能拿到一点。计划是一次性运来4万剂——对一个6000多万人口、约五分之一成年人感染病毒的国家，这个数字少得可笑；其中只有5%、2000剂留给男男性行为者做检测，性工作者一剂没有。Fatima Hassan的批评是，政府又在犯同样的错误，把最受影响的人排除在外。而更根本的问题是：就算药来了，谁来发？那些能把药送到关掉的Engage诊所的男大学生手里、送到比勒陀利亚Pop Inn诊所的跨性别女性手里的人，已经被裁掉了。

马斯克在这条链子上的位置值得单独说一句。2024年他先向特朗普建议设立“政府效率委员会”，种子是他埋的；DOGE执行的削减让HIV资金在国内和全球同时跳水；他在2025年上任几个月后就辞职，据报带走了一批DOGE的数据，财富暴涨，成为世界首位万亿富翁。关于USAID削减会造成多少死亡，各种数学推算从数十万到约1000万甚至数千万不等；众议员Ro Khanna指控他“对全球约450万儿童执行了死刑”，马斯克威胁要起诉他。HIV相关的死亡通常要很多年才发生，死在南非的人不会出现在任何一个完整的数据库里。这就是这套机制最省事的地方：代价是真的，但账面上找不到。

加尔通的公式是Violence = Potential − Actual。这里的差额不是天灾，是一份行政令、一次裁员、一个被撤走的设备箱。而差额里最要命的那一块，是“找到人”的能力——它不在任何预算科目里显眼，却是整个防治网络的神经末梢。神经被剪断了，药再好也送不到该去的地方。

What the Trump administration shut down wasn't just a clinic. It was the machinery that goes out and finds people. South Africa has nearly 64 million people and some 8 million living with HIV, roughly one in eight (source: The Intercept). In a country like that, public health cannot wait for patients to walk in. Someone has to go to them. Jacqueline Pienaar of the Aurum Institute has counted it: since Trump returned to the White House, HIV prevention groups have lost track of approximately 20,000 people with HIV in the Johannesburg and Pretoria region alone. Her words: it used to be that if someone didn't show up for medication, you went to them. Now nobody goes. Viral loads spike, transmission rises. That is foreseeable, not accidental.

This is not a policy failure. It is the policy. On January 20, 2025, Trump signed two executive orders: 14169, reevaluating and realigning United States foreign aid, and 14168, defending women from gender ideology extremism and restoring biological truth to the federal government. Within hours, OUT LGBT Well-Being, which ran the Engage clinic, told staff not to come in the next day and not to open their work laptops again. 82 of 86 workers were let go. The two orders signed the same day are not a coincidence: one cuts the money, one cuts the people, and they cut the same people.

Engage sat on Reserve Street in Johannesburg, a short distance from seven universities. Many students came before they'd even had sex for the first time. The building still carries the PEPFAR logo, a rainbow mural of Simon Tseko Nkoli, and a USAID sign calling the site a gift from the American people. Its replacement, a volunteer clinic called USpace, is open four hours a week. It can do HIV tests. It has no treatment, no prevention, no gender-affirming medication. The signage stayed. The gift left.

The money used to be there. In 2024 the U.S. sent South Africa $580 million, 78 percent of it, $455 million, for HIV/AIDS. It was never pure charity: South Africa hosts more clinical trials than any other country for how medications affect people with HIV, and American drug company profits depend on South Africans undergoing those trials. Viruses don't respect borders, so controlling HIV in the most infected country makes everyone safer. The U.S. did not simply withdraw its generosity. It withdrew from a position where its own interests sat, and left the people who pay the price standing in place.

Who pays is spelled out. Gwendolyn Dube, 35, lives in a tin-roofed shack in Orange Farm with no running water or electricity. She was infected when she was raped as a child and was among the first cohort of South African children to receive antiretroviral medication. Her prescriptions went from three months to two to one, and then she was told there was no file for her. Clinic workers say people now wait 8, 10, even 12 hours and sometimes never see anyone; for people in abject poverty, losing a day's earnings is devastating. The matron denies running out of medication but concedes patients will leave and maybe not come back. Her mobile teams used to go out every day. Now it's maybe once a week.

There is a cost the story doesn't name outright but lets you read: when trans women lose hormone therapy, they turn to the contraband market, to syringes that may not be sterile, and the risk of HIV and hepatitis rises with it. Shon knew two trans women: one lost hormones, became really religious, stopped, and became suicidal; another bought hormones on the contraband market and ended up with liver problems. This isn't an argument that gender-affirming care is progressive and therefore important. It's that when the legitimate door is bricked up, people don't stop needing what was behind it. They enter through a more dangerous one.

The cruelest part is at the end. Lenacapavir, one injection every six months to prevent HIV, is priced at $28,000 a year. The Global Fund struck a deal with Gilead to produce it for $40 in limited quantities, and a small amount is headed to South Africa. The plan is a one-time shipment of 40,000 doses, a laughable number for a country of more than 60 million where about one in five adults lives with the virus. Only 5 percent, 2,000 doses, is set aside for testing on men who have sex with men. None for sex workers. Fatima Hassan's criticism is that the government is making the same mistake of excluding those most affected. But the deeper question is: even if the drug arrives, who distributes it? The people who could have carried it to the gay college students of the shuttered Engage clinic, to the trans women of the Pretoria Pop Inn clinic, have been fired.

Musk deserves a line of his own in this chain. In 2024 he first recommended a government efficiency commission to Trump, planting the seed. The DOGE cuts he pushed sent HIV funding plummeting at home and abroad. He quit a few months into 2025, reportedly took a swath of DOGE data with him, saw his wealth skyrocket, and became the world's first trillionaire. Mathematical projections of how many people the USAID cuts have killed or will kill range from hundreds of thousands to about 10 million to even tens of millions. When Rep. Ro Khanna accused him of executing a death sentence for an estimated 4.5 million children, Musk threatened to sue. HIV deaths usually take years, and South Africans who die of HIV will not be mourned in any comprehensive database. That is the most convenient thing about this machinery: the cost is real, but it never shows up on the books.

Galtung's formula is Violence = Potential − Actual. The gap here is not a natural disaster. It is an executive order, a layoff, an equipment box carried away. And the deadliest piece of that gap is the capacity to find people, which never looks prominent in any budget line but is the nerve ending of the whole prevention network. Cut the nerve, and no drug, however good, reaches where it needs to go.
