提前投票创纪录，不是民主的胜利Record Early Voting Is Not a Democratic Victory

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
投票率是症状，不是权力本身。
Turnout is a symptom, not power itself.

提前投票人数创纪录，分析人士说这是选民对共和党和特朗普不满的信号。北卡州民主党提前投票超过一百万，共和党六十七万九千；佛州之外，德州七百六十万、佐治亚两百八十万、缅因堪萨斯密歇根比 2022 年涨了三成以上（来源：The Guardian）。这些数字在报道里被读成"民意转向"。但投票率从来不是权力的分配，只是权力的计量单位——它记录的是有多少人愿意走进那个被划定的场地，而不是这场地本身是谁划的。

加尔通的公式是 Violence = Potential − Actual。一场选举能动员出创纪录的投票，说明 Potential 那头很高：人们有强烈的表达冲动。Actual 这头呢？报道给出的全部内容是哪个党多投了几张票、民调里五十二对四十四。差额没有被缩小，只是被重新记了一次账。把"更多人参与了现有程序"当成"程序变好了"，是 structural violence 最舒服的伪装——它让不满变成燃料，再把燃料倒回同一台机器。

报道里唯一被引用的具体不满是经济。Bernard Fraga 说，连坚定的共和党选民看看经济状况都提不起劲。经济是结构层的东西：谁在承担生活成本，谁在为谁的减税买单。但整篇报道没有一个字提到这些成本落在谁身上——不是抽象的"选民"，是具体的、按性别和阶级分配的负担。投票率的分母里，有大量因为照料责任、因为法律禁止某些工作、因为工时不自由而根本走不进投票站的人。他们不在 43% 和 31% 里。

Michael McDonald 把这叫"对特朗普的公投"。公投是元暴力最喜欢的句式：它把一场关于解释权的争夺压缩成两个选项，然后宣布这就是全部的可能性。谁能被投、什么议题算数、哪些不满可以被写进选票、哪些只能留在投票站外面——这些在投票开始之前就已经定好了，而定这些的人不在选票上。

民主党提前投票占 43%，共和党 31%，无党派 25%。这组数字真正说明的是：有四分之一的投票者拒绝被装进那两个格子。他们才是这篇报道里最该被追问的人，而报道把他们算成了一个百分比就翻页了。

Record numbers of people are voting early. Analysts read it as dissatisfaction with Republicans and Donald Trump. In North Carolina, more than a million Democrats have already voted against 679,000 Republicans; Texas is at 7.6 million early votes, Georgia at more than 2.8 million, and Maine, Kansas and Michigan are up more than 30% over 2022 (source: The Guardian). The report turns these numbers into a story about a shift in public opinion. But turnout is not the distribution of power. It is only the unit in which power gets counted — it records how many people walked into a space someone else drew the boundaries of.

Galtung's formula is Violence = Potential − Actual. A record turnout says the Potential side is high: people have a strong urge to express something. What about the Actual side? Everything the report offers is which party cast more ballots and a poll reading of 52 to 44. The gap has not been closed. It has been re-bookkept. Treating "more people took part in the existing procedure" as "the procedure got better" is structural violence's most comfortable disguise: it turns discontent into fuel, then pours the fuel back into the same machine.

The one concrete grievance quoted is the economy. Bernard Fraga says even steadfast Republican voters look at the state of the economy and are not enthusiastic. The economy is structural: who absorbs the cost of living, who pays for whose tax cuts. The report never says who those costs land on — not "voters" in the abstract, but specific burdens distributed by gender and class. The denominator of turnout includes a large number of people who cannot reach a polling station at all, because of care responsibilities, because the law bars them from certain work, because their hours are not their own. They are not inside the 43% and the 31%.

Michael McDonald calls it a referendum on Trump. Referendum is meta-violence's favourite sentence form: it compresses a contest over who gets to interpret into two options, then declares that this is the whole of possibility. Who can be voted for, which issues count, which grievances can be written onto a ballot and which must stay outside the polling station — all of that is settled before voting starts, and the people who settle it are not on the ballot.

Democrats 43%, Republicans 31%, no party affiliation 25%. What that last number actually says is that a quarter of voters refused to be packed into either box. They are the people this report should be interrogating, and it counts them as a percentage and turns the page.
