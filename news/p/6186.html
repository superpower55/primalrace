七不是新常态，是旧账Seven Isn't the New Normal. It's the Bill.

其他 结构层 · 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
把利率叫新常态，是把代价说成天气。
Calling 7% the new normal turns a bill into weather.

"7%是新常态"这句话不是分析，是定价。谁有资格宣布一个数字是常态，谁就替所有还不起这个数字的人把账结了。

报道里说这话的是全国房地产经纪人协会和全国住宅建筑商协会的首席经济学家，还有一家房产经纪公司的分析师。卖房的人、盖房的人、撮合交易的人，一起告诉你七是新的正常。他们不是撒谎，他们说的是自己的正常——成交量低一点，佣金薄一点，但房子还在卖，报告还在发。至于谁在这个"正常"里被挤出去，不在他们的预测模型里。

报道自己给了原因：通胀自2021年2月以来没低过2%，联邦政府在大举借债，AI 热潮在抢资本，银行因为2022到2023年那波利率飙升在按揭债券上亏怕了，监管也收紧了，美联储不再买按揭支持证券。这一串全是结构层的动作——财政、货币、监管、产业投资。加尔通的公式在这里很好用：本来可以盖得起的房子，现在盖不起了，差额就是暴力，只不过它不流血，它写在月供里。

而文化层紧接着上来把它合法化。"新常态"三个字的功能，就是把一个政策与资本共同制造出来的结果，说成天气。天气没人负责，政策有人负责。说成天气，就不用问谁在借债、谁在抢资本、谁在2022年赚了又亏了、谁在监管上收紧了口子。

代价落在谁身上，报道一个字没写。它写"潜在购房者"，写"等待是赌博"。它没写的是：高利率环境下，最先退出市场的是首付最薄、议价能力最弱的那批人；已经背着浮动利率或要续约的人，月供涨上去就下不来；而在家庭内部，谁的名字在房产证上、谁出首付、谁在生育后收入中断因而贷不到款——这些从来不是利率新闻会碰的问题。报道里没有这些人的声音，这不是疏漏，这是这类报道的默认视角：市场是主体，人是变量。

唯一说了句实话的是 Cotality 那位首席经济学家，她说不喜欢把7%叫新常态，因为这个数字没有任何永久性。她说得对，也说得很轻。数字确实不永久，但被这个数字挡在门外的几年，对一个人来说是永久的。

所以我不接受"新常态"这个说法，我接受它的反面：这是一个可以被政策改回来的状态，只是改回来对宣布常态的人没有好处。真正的问题不是利率会不会降，是谁有权力把一个可逆的结果说成不可逆，然后让所有人认命。

"Seven percent is the new normal" is not analysis. It's pricing. Whoever gets to declare a number normal has just settled the account on behalf of everyone who can't pay it.

The people saying it in this piece are the chief economists of the National Association of Realtors and the National Association of Home Builders, plus a broker-analyst at a realty firm. The people who sell houses, build houses, and take a cut on the transaction are jointly telling you seven is the new normal. They aren't lying. They're describing their normal: thinner volume, thinner commissions, but the houses still move and the forecasts still go out. Who gets pushed out of this normal doesn't appear in their models.

The article supplies the causes itself: inflation hasn't been below the Fed's 2% target since February 2021, the federal government is borrowing heavily, the AI boom is competing for capital, banks got burned on mortgage-backed securities in the 2022-23 spike and now face tighter scrutiny, and the Fed has stepped away from buying MBS. That whole chain is structural: fiscal policy, monetary policy, regulation, industrial investment. Galtung's formula fits cleanly here. The house that could have been affordable and now isn't, that gap is violence. It just doesn't bleed. It shows up in the monthly payment.

Then the cultural layer arrives to legitimize it. The function of "new normal" is to take an outcome jointly produced by policy and capital and recast it as weather. Nobody is accountable for weather. Somebody is accountable for policy. Call it weather and you never have to ask who is borrowing, who is competing for capital, who made money and then lost it in 2022, who tightened the rules.

Who pays is absent from the piece. It says "would-be homebuyers," says waiting is a gamble. It doesn't say that in a high-rate market the first people out are those with the thinnest down payments and the least bargaining power; that anyone on an adjustable rate or facing renewal sees the payment go up and stay up; that inside households, whose name is on the deed, who fronts the down payment, who can't qualify for a loan after an income gap from childbirth, are never questions a mortgage-rate story touches. Those voices aren't in the reporting. That's not an oversight. It's the default vantage point of the genre: the market is the subject, people are variables.

The one honest line comes from the Cotality chief economist, who says she doesn't like calling 7% the new normal because there's nothing permanent about that number. She's right, and she says it gently. The number isn't permanent. The years it keeps someone out of a home are.

So I don't accept "new normal." I accept its inverse: this is a reversible condition, reversible by policy, and reversing it offers no advantage to the people declaring it normal. The real question isn't whether rates fall. It's who holds the power to describe a reversible outcome as irreversible, and then get everyone to accept it.
