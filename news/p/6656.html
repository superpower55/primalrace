卖课的人，先给自己搭了神坛The People Selling Courses Built Themselves an Altar First

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把创作者供成品牌，再把品牌拆成课程卖给下一批创作者。
Turn creators into a brand, then sell the brand back to the next batch of creators as a course.

Magnet Originals 的生意说得很漂亮：帮已经成名的人扩张媒体帝国，同时孵化下一代明星。翻译一下——先签下 Justin Tranter、Boyz II Men 的 Shawn Stockman、前 NBA 球员 Byron Scott 这些已经有名字的人，用他们的名字搭一个品牌，再用这个品牌去卖 Top Creator Academy 的课和一对一咨询。Cunningham 自己说，这门课能长成七位数的生意。

这就是第三章里那个最经典的认知入口：谁有话语权，谁就能给别人的表达贴标签、定价格。Magnet Originals 卖的不是制作能力，是“你也能成为他们”的可能性。可能性是最便宜的商品，成本是零，定价权在自己手里。

Cunningham 的叙事很动人：新闻编辑室一个个关门，商业模式岌岌可危，于是一整代有创业精神的了不起的讲故事的人，只靠汗水股本（sweat equity）在搭播客、YouTube 频道和通讯。这段话是真的，问题在于她给出的解法。编辑室关门是结构层的暴力——资源被重新分配，劳动被重新定价，做内容的人拿不到稳定的回报。她的解法不是去修那个结构，而是在废墟上开一家咨询公司，把“怎么在废墟里活下去”包装成课程，卖给还站在废墟里的人。

报道里提到的那档 Everyone Watches Women's Sports，由 Sam Jay、Jordan Chiles 和 Ari Chambers 共同主持，是这家公司最值得看的一笔。女子体育的观众在涨，这是真的；把女子体育做成一个可以售卖的品牌，也是真的。但“大家看女子体育”这句话从一句被忽视的事实，变成一档节目的名字，中间隔着的正是解释权的转移——谁来决定女子体育该被怎样呈现，是运动员和长期报道它的人，还是拿着 Live Nation 联合制作合约的品牌方。报道没说 Chiles 和 Chambers 拿到什么条件，也没说这个品牌和她们之间是雇佣、分成还是别的。没写的部分，就是这门生意最需要被追问的部分。

真正被定价的，是那些还在用汗水股本搭频道的人。他们被告知，缺的不是时间、不是钱、不是让编辑室活下来的制度，缺的是一堂课。

Cunningham 说，做咨询公司一次只能服务几个客户，不让她兴奋。她兴奋的是规模。规模意味着，每一个想成为下一个 Hanif Abdurraqib 的人，都得先付一笔学费，才能被告知自己还差什么。

Magnet Originals puts it nicely: help established personalities grow their media empires, and incubate the next generation of stars. Translated: sign people who already have names — Justin Tranter, Boyz II Men's Shawn Stockman, former NBA player Byron Scott — use those names to build a brand, then use the brand to sell Top Creator Academy courses and one-on-one consulting. Cunningham herself says the program can grow into a seven-figure business.

This is the classic cognitive entry point from Chapter 3: whoever holds discourse power gets to label other people's expression and set its price. Magnet Originals isn't selling production capacity. It's selling the possibility that you, too, could become them. Possibility is the cheapest product there is — zero cost, and you own the pricing.

Cunningham's narrative is moving: newsrooms keep closing, their business model is in jeopardy, and a generation of entrepreneurial, brilliant storytellers is building podcasts, YouTube channels and newsletters on sweat equity alone. That part is true. The problem is the solution she offers. Newsrooms closing is structural violence — resources get reallocated, labor gets repriced, and the people making content can't get a stable return. Her fix isn't to repair that structure. It's to open a consultancy on the ruins and sell "how to survive the ruins" as a course to the people still standing in them.

The one show worth watching is Everyone Watches Women's Sports, co-hosted by Sam Jay, Jordan Chiles and Ari Chambers. Audiences for women's sports are growing — true. Turning women's sports into a sellable brand is also true. But the distance between "everyone watches women's sports" as a neglected fact and as the title of a show is exactly where interpretive power changes hands: who decides how women's sports gets presented — the athletes and the people who have covered it for years, or the brand holding a co-production deal with Live Nation. The report doesn't say what terms Chiles and Chambers got, or whether this is employment, revenue share, or something else. What isn't written is precisely what this business most needs to be asked.

What's really being priced is the people still building channels on sweat equity. They're being told their problem isn't time, or money, or a system that would let newsrooms survive. Their problem is that they haven't taken the course.

Cunningham says a consultancy would reach a handful of clients at a time, and that didn't excite her. Scale excites her. Scale means everyone who wants to be the next Hanif Abdurraqib has to pay tuition first, to be told what they're still missing.
