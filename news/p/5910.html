她道了歉，但没人问药去哪了She Apologized. Nobody Asked About the Medication.

文娱 结构层 · 直接层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
一场医疗事故被写成了明星失态
A medical emergency gets filed as a celebrity meltdown.

Selma Blair 道歉了。她说她对不起那些被她伤害、惊吓、惊动的人。这话说得体面，但整段视频里没有一个字提到那几剂没打的药——而报道里写得清清楚楚：她的律师告诉法官，她漏服了数剂“控制严重神经症状的强效药物”，登机前工作负荷过重，飞行中撞到了头，加上高空环境，导致她陷入“严重的意识混乱与定向障碍”。

一个 54 岁、2018 年确诊多发性硬化症的女人，在万米高空断了自己的药，然后踢了一个把她绑上担架的人。报道给了她两个罪名、两万美元现金保释、一月的庭期，一个护士胸口的淤伤，一个警察脸颈胸的肿痛。这些都是真的，受害者也是真的。但同一篇报道里，那几剂药是怎么漏的、谁负责提醒、航班上有没有人能处理，一个字没有。

这就是结构暴力最典型的形态：它不出现，它只是让某个人在错误的时间以错误的状态出现在错误的位置，然后由直接暴力来收尾。药是结构，担架是直接。法律只处理后者。

然后是道歉本身。她说“我从来没有一天想要伤害或惊吓任何人”——这是真的，也是最没用的一句话。法律要判断的是意图，而她的身体根本不在意图的管辖范围内。一个神经系统的病，在高空、在漏药、在撞头之后失控，这件事在刑法里没有对应的格子，只能被塞进“二级袭击”里。

更值得看的是叙事怎么被分配的。她道歉，她感谢支持者，她报平安说服务犬 Scout 很好，她说要照顾自己的健康和儿子。这套话术是明星危机公关的标准件：把一场医疗事件翻译成一次个人失态，再用道歉把翻译结果封存。翻译完成之后，公众记住的是“她踢了护士”，而不是“她在飞机上断药了”。

而这条新闻里真正缺席的声音，是那两个被踢的人。报道写了警察的指控、护士的指控，写在法庭文件里，用“据称”包着。她们没有对着镜头说话，没有 Instagram 视频，没有服务犬的近况。她们的身体是这场叙事里的证据，不是叙述者。

Blair 说她在“听律师的”。律师让她别说那天的事。于是那天的事就只剩法庭文件里的几行字，和一段谁都可以解读成“情绪失控”的道歉。

一月的听证会会处理那两脚。没有人会处理那几剂药——不是这次，是下次，是每一个带着慢性病登上飞机、指望自己别在途中出问题的人。

Selma Blair apologized. She said she was sorry to anyone she hurt, frightened, alarmed. It was gracious. It also never mentioned the missed doses — and the report spells them out: her lawyer told the judge she had missed several doses of "a powerful medication that controls severe neurological symptoms," boarded after a heavy workload, hit her head during the flight, and the altitude plus all of it produced "a state of severe confusion and disorientation."

A 54-year-old woman diagnosed with multiple sclerosis in 2018 ran out of her medication at cruising altitude and then kicked the person strapping her to a stretcher. The report gives her two charges, $20,000 cash bail, a January hearing, a nurse kicked in the chest, an officer kicked around the face, neck and chest with substantial swelling. All of that is real. The victims are real. But the same report says nothing about how the doses were missed, who was supposed to track them, or whether anyone on that flight could have handled it.

That is structural violence in its purest form: it doesn't show up, it just arranges for one person to be in the wrong state in the wrong place at the wrong time, and then lets direct violence close the account. The medication is structure. The stretcher is direct. The law only processes the second one.

Then the apology. "Not one day ever have I intended to hurt or frighten anyone" — true, and useless. Intent is what the law asks about, and her body was never inside intent's jurisdiction. A nervous system failing at altitude, after missed doses and a blow to the head, has no box in the criminal code. It gets filed under assault in the second degree.

What's more telling is how the narrative gets allocated. She apologizes, thanks supporters, reports that Scout the service dog is fine, says she'll care for her health and her son. That is the standard kit for celebrity crisis management: translate a medical event into a personal lapse, then seal the translation with an apology. Once the translation lands, the public remembers she kicked a nurse, not that she ran out of medication on a plane.

The voices missing from this story are the two people she kicked. Their accounts exist in charging documents, wrapped in "alleged." They don't get an Instagram video. Their bodies are evidence in this narrative, not narrators of it.

Blair says she's listening to her lawyers. Her lawyers told her not to talk about that day. So that day now survives as a few lines in a court file and an apology anyone can read as a mood swing.

January's hearing will address the two kicks. Nobody will address the missed doses — not this time, not for the next person boarding a flight with a chronic illness, hoping it holds until landing.
