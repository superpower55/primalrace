没有权力的特使是政府给家属的安慰剂The Powerless Envoy: A Placebo for the Desperate

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-14 § 链接
用一个无权决策的岗位来承接愤怒，是典型的结构性暴力 PR 化。
Creating a role without decision-making power is just PR-ing structural violence.

这个新设立的“特使”岗位从定义上就是一个 scam。Alistair Burt 在就职的第一时间就坦白自己“不制定政策”、“没有权力”，这意味着这个角色在系统中的实际功能不是 solve problems，而是 act as a buffer——一个用来缓冲受害者家属愤怒的减震器。

从加尔通的暴力三角来看，这是一种极其阴险的 structural violence。政府通过创造一个看似在“努力”但实则被剥夺了所有决策权的中间层，将家属的诉求引导至一个死胡同。当家属在向特使倾诉时，他们以为在与权力对话，但实际上是在与一个被阉割的接口对话。这种“沟通”不仅没有缩小 Potential 与 Actual 之间的差额，反而通过制造“政府在关注”的假象，掩盖了外交政策本身的无能与冷漠。

最讽刺的是，这个岗位甚至不需要政府出钱支付薪水。这种“志愿者式”的权力表演，本质上是政府在利用 Burt 的个人资历来为外交部的失职背书。Zaghari-Ratcliffe 的担忧非常精准：所谓的“安静外交” (quiet diplomacy) 正在变成政府不作为的 excuse。当一个岗位被设计成只能“踢踢轮胎” (kick the tyres) 而不能更换轮胎时，它就成了一种文化层面的 weaponized 叙事，旨在通过表演性的关怀来消解结构性的抗争。

对于那些在海外监狱中被折磨的人来说，他们需要的不是一个能陪家属聊天、能坦诚说“这很难”的特使，而是一个敢于在外交博弈中增加筹码、敢于打破共谋、敢于真正行使权力的决策者。这种没有 teeth 的任命，不过是给绝望的家属开了一剂名为“希望”的安慰剂，好让他们在等待中继续温顺。

This new 'Special Envoy' position is a scam by design. Alistair Burt’s immediate admission that he 'doesn't make policy' and 'has no authority' reveals the role's true function: he is not there to solve problems, but to act as a buffer—a shock absorber for the rage of detained citizens' families.

In terms of the Violence Triangle, this is a textbook example of structural violence. By inserting a middle layer that appears to be 'working' but is stripped of all actual power, the government funnels the grievances of families into a dead end. When families speak to the envoy, they believe they are engaging with power, but they are actually interacting with a castrated interface. This 'communication' doesn't narrow the gap between Potential and Actual; instead, it masks the incompetence and indifference of foreign policy through a performance of 'concern.'

It is peak irony that this role is not even paid for by the Foreign Office. This 'volunteer-style' performance of power is simply the government leveraging Burt's personal credentials to provide cover for institutional failure. Nazanin Zaghari-Ratcliffe’s fear is spot on: 'quiet diplomacy' is being weaponized as an excuse for inaction. When a role is designed only to 'kick the tyres' without the power to change them, it becomes a form of cultural violence—using performative empathy to neutralize structural resistance.

Those suffering in overseas prisons do not need an envoy who can chat with families and candidly admit 'it's very difficult.' They need a decision-maker who can increase leverage in diplomatic games, break the complicity of silent allies, and actually exercise power. An appointment without teeth is nothing more than a placebo named 'hope,' designed to keep the desperate quiet while they wait.
