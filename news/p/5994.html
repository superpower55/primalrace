他用七亿买下了整台机器He Bought the Machine for $736 Million

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
合同禁令只管公司，不管握着公司的人。
The contractor ban covers corporations, not the men who own them.

七亿三千六百万美元投进去，十六亿三千万美元捞回来，两千两百个百分点的回报率。这不是投资，这是把联邦预算当提款机，而提款密码是他自己参与设置的。

有一条法律，1940年就立了，禁止联邦承包商向联邦选举捐款。国会当年给的理由很直白：拿政府合同赚钱的人不该再往政客口袋里塞钱，那在某些情况下可以被视为贿赂。水门事件后这条禁令还被加强了，2015年哥伦比亚特区巡回法院还维持过它。所以这条法律不是没人知道，是有人算过它管不到自己。禁令管的是"公司"这个签约主体，而公司的管理层、董事、大股东和政治行动委员会照样可以往联邦选举里砸钱。超级PAC的"独立支出"更是连边都不沾。这不是法律的漏洞，这是法律从一开始就只写在纸上，而纸上写的是公司，钱走的是人。

更值得看的是时间顺序。他先掌管DOGE，名义上是砍政府开支，结果DOGE本身花掉五千一百七十亿美元，同期他顺手拿走六十三亿的联邦合同。他离开政府十七个月，又拿下至少一百亿。而那笔一百亿里最大的一笔，是给太空军建"金穹"导弹防御系统的四十二亿和二十三亿，走的是"其他交易授权"的快速通道，绕开了常规军购的监管程序。五角大楼的招标要求，据《华尔街日报》报道，"与SpaceX的能力高度吻合"。先决定要把合同给谁，再照着这家的能力写标准——这不是采购，这是量身定做。

然后是最干净的一刀。DOGE把联邦太空交通管制项目的人几乎全裁了，只留一个，据说是为了防法院的扣押挑战。紧接着，联邦政府把最机密的太空跟踪数据独家给了SpaceX。下一步几乎可以确定：把太空交通管制整个私有化，交给SpaceX。先拆掉公共能力，再把自己变成唯一的接盘方。这不是监管俘获，这是先把房子烧了，再卖消防服务。

监管上的让利比合同更值钱。十五个联邦行政部门里至少十一个做过有利于他生意的事。xAI在孟菲斯装了五十九台天然气涡轮机，没拿到联邦清洁空气许可，其中大部分还跨到了密西西比州界那边。NAACP代表附近的低收入黑人社区起诉，要求EPA执法。特朗普的司法部反过来替xAI站台，理由是总统的行政令写着美国要维持"全球AI主导地位"。十月十九日，密西西比一位地区法官把这一套驳了回去。这一层是结构暴力在动：清洁空气法对谁生效，取决于违规的人是不是"国家战略"的一部分。而付代价的是住在涡轮机旁边的那些社区，报道里写了他们是谁，低收入、黑人、住在州界附近。

SEC那笔和解更直白。拜登任期末，SEC指控他延迟披露推特持股，好让他用被人为压低的价格多买股票，这是标准的证券违法，SEC最初要罚一亿五千万。特朗普上台后，和解金额变成一百五十万。一位前SEC幕僚长说，这应该让公众怀疑SEC是不是在牺牲普通投资者保护白宫内部人。地区法官 reluctantly 批准了和解，写了一句很诚实的话：行政部门有没有尽到问责的义务，这要由公民在投票箱前决定。

所以这篇报道真正说的不是一个人贪了多少钱。它说的是：当规则制定者、规则执行者和规则受益者是同一个人时，Violence = Potential − Actual 里的那个差额，被合法地、逐条地、写进了条款里。国会1940年想堵的那个口子，今天不是被绕过了，是被证明从来就没堵上——因为它堵的是公司，而公司背后站着的是人。

报道里有一处没写：那些被裁掉的太空交通管制员工，那些住在孟菲斯涡轮机旁边的居民，那些因为SEC少收一亿四千八百五十万罚款而少了一层保护的普通投资者，他们的名字一个都没出现。这台机器运转得如此顺滑，恰恰是因为付代价的人不需要被写进合同。

Seven hundred thirty-six million dollars in, sixteen point three billion out. A 2,200 percent return. This isn't investment. It's the federal budget treated as an ATM, and he helped set the PIN.

There is a law, on the books since 1940, barring federal contractors from making campaign contributions to federal candidates. Congress said why: people making money off government contracts shouldn't be handing money to politicians, because that "may be considered in some instances as bribery." Watergate strengthened it. The D.C. Circuit upheld it as recently as 2015. So the law isn't obscure. It's just written to cover the corporation that signs the contract, not the officers, directors, major shareholders, and PACs behind it. Super PAC "independent expenditures" don't even graze it. This isn't a loophole. It's a law that was always a piece of paper, and the paper says corporation while the money moves through people.

The sequence is the story. He runs DOGE, ostensibly to cut spending. DOGE runs up a bill of $517 billion. He pockets $6.3 billion in federal contracts while doing it. Seventeen months after leaving, he's won at least $10 billion more, including $4.2 billion and $2.3 billion to build the Golden Dome missile defense system for the Space Force, approved through "other transaction authority" that skips the regulations that normally slow weapons procurement. The Pentagon's request, per The Wall Street Journal, "closely matched SpaceX's capabilities." Decide who gets the contract first, then write the criteria around them. That isn't procurement. It's tailoring.

Then the cleanest cut. DOGE fires all but one of the federal space traffic control staff, reportedly to guard against a court challenge. The federal government then gives SpaceX exclusive access to its most closely held space-tracking data. The next step, all but certain: privatize space traffic control entirely and hand it to SpaceX. Burn the house down, then sell the fire service.

Regulatory relief is worth more than the contracts. At least 11 of 15 federal executive departments took actions favorable to his business. xAI installed 59 natural gas turbines for a Memphis data center without federal clean air permits, most of them across the state line in Mississippi. The NAACP sued on behalf of nearby low-income Black communities to force EPA enforcement. Trump's Justice Department backed xAI, citing an executive order on American AI dominance. On October 19, a Mississippi district judge called that out and denied the dismissal. That's structural violence: the Clean Air Act applies depending on whether the violator is "national strategy." The people paying are the ones the report names—low-income, Black, near the turbines.

The SEC settlement is blunter. The SEC accused him of delaying disclosure of his Twitter stake so he could buy shares at artificially low prices. Straight securities violation. The SEC initially demanded $150 million. After Trump took office, the settlement became $1.5 million. A former SEC chief of staff said the public should question whether the agency protects White House insiders at ordinary investors' expense. The judge approved it reluctantly, writing that whether the executive branch has done enough to hold him accountable is for citizens to decide at the ballot box.

So the report isn't really about how much one man took. It's about what happens when the rule-maker, the rule-enforcer, and the rule-beneficiary are the same person. The gap in Violence = Potential − Actual gets written into the terms, legally, line by line. Congress tried to close that gap in 1940. Today it's not circumvented. It's proven never to have been closed, because it covered the corporation while the man stood behind it.

One thing the report doesn't say: the fired space traffic controllers, the residents near the Memphis turbines, the ordinary investors who lost a layer of protection when the SEC settled for $148.5 million less—none of them appear by name. The machine runs this smoothly precisely because the people who pay don't need to be written into the contract.
