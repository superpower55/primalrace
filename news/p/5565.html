所谓的“韧性”不过是银行的特权护城河The So-called 'Resilience' is Just a Moat for Bank Privileges

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-15 § 链接
将金融机构的利益定义为国家韧性，是典型的元暴力共谋。
Defining financial interests as national resilience is a classic act of meta-violence complicity.

英国财政大臣 John Healey 在曼谷的这番表演，是教科书级别的 weaponized expression。他把银行描述为英国经济皇冠上的“明珠”，称其“硬连线”在国家的经济织造中。这种叙事极其阴险：它直接将一个具体的利益集团（Big Banks）等同于国家生存的底线（Resilience）。

当我们讨论“韧性”这个词时，应该讨论的是 Potential − Actual 的差额是否在缩小。但 Healey 的韧性是结构性暴力的掩体。TUC 的数据已经揭露，银行税减免让政府损失了 60 亿英镑。这 60 亿本可以转化为公共医疗、教育或缓解能源危机，但现在它们成了银行的利润。在这种共谋下，银行的“强健”被包装成国家的“韧性”，而底层民众在能源价格压力下的“呼吸空间”则成了财政大臣施舍的 PR 词汇。

更讽刺的是，Healey 在谈论乌克兰的未来是欧洲安全的未来，却在谈论银行时使用了同样的“命运共同体”逻辑。这种逻辑的本质是：权力的持有者（银行家）不能受损，因为他们的受损被定义为整个系统的崩溃。这是一种典型的 masculine-centric narrative——由少数精英制定规则，让大众为他们的风险买单，而将他们的收益定义为全民的荣光。

所谓的“呼吸空间”只是在结构性压榨后的微小让步。真正的韧性应该是资源分配的公正，而不是让“皇冠上的明珠”继续吸血，然后告诉被吸血的人，这叫国家竞争力。

Chancellor John Healey's performance in Bangkok is a textbook case of weaponized expression. By describing banks as the "jewel in the crown" and claiming they are "hardwired" into the economic fabric, he is strategically equating the interests of a specific power group (Big Banks) with the baseline of national survival (Resilience).

When we discuss 'resilience' through the lens of the Violence Triangle, we should ask if the gap between Potential and Actual is shrinking. Instead, Healey uses resilience as a shield for structural violence. The TUC data reveals that tax cuts for big banks deprived the government of £6bn. This capital could have been diverted to healthcare or education, but it remains as bank profit. In this complicity, the 'strength' of banks is rebranded as national resilience, while the 'breathing space' for citizens struggling with energy costs is reduced to a mere PR slogan.

It is profoundly ironic that Healey uses the same 'destiny' logic for banks as he does for Ukraine's role in European security. The essence of this logic is that the holders of power must not suffer, because their loss is defined as a systemic collapse. This is a masculine-centric narrative: a few elites set the rules, the public absorbs the risk, and the elites' gains are framed as a collective victory.

The promised 'breathing space' is nothing more than a performative concession after structural extraction. True resilience is found in the just distribution of resources, not in allowing the 'jewels in the crown' to continue bleeding the system dry while calling it national competitiveness.
