谈判桌上先摆谁的尸体Who Pays Before the Strait Reopens

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要伊朗外长阿拉格齐表示，美国寻求与德黑兰对话是「积极的一步」，但目前没有与美国官员会面的计划。一个伊朗代表团已前往多哈，继续与卡塔尔和巴基斯坦调解人磋商。阿拉格齐还提出重开霍尔木兹海峡的条件，包括解除制裁、减少美军驻留、恢复石油豁免并解冻资金。
The newsIran's Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called a US effort to open dialogue "a positive step" but said there are no plans to meet US officials. An Iranian delegation has traveled to Doha to continue consultations with Qatari and Pakistani mediators. Araghchi set conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, including lifting sanctions, reducing US forces in the region, restoring oil waivers and releasing frozen funds.
霍尔木兹重开的条件里，女人的名字一次没出现。
Six conditions for Hormuz. Not one names a person.

阿拉格奇说美国的接触是「积极的一步」。这句话的代价，报道里写得很清楚：解除制裁、撤走美军、恢复石油豁免、解冻全部资金、承认伊朗与阿曼谈定的航道、关闭其他航线——六条，一条不少。霍尔木兹海峡重开的实施期，从卡塔尔提议的五天压到两天。两天。一条海峡的开合，被算到了小时。

这套语言里没有任何人的位置。不是遗漏，是这套语言的结构：制裁、豁免、资金、兵力部署，全是国家与国家之间交换的筹码。筹码不需要面孔。

第三章讲表达武器化的时候我说过，战争叙事最常见的入口是「中东女性需要被解放，所以打阿富汗」。那套叙事用了二十年，撤军之后阿富汗女性的处境比开战前更糟。现在这套叙事退场了，换上来的是更干净的一种：不谈解放，只谈航道。不谈人，只谈条件。看起来诚实多了——至少不假装为谁好。

但代价从来不会因为不被命名就消失。制裁压下去，最先垮掉的是家庭里资源分配最末端的那个人；资金冻结，被冻结的从来不是抽象的国库，是药、是粮、是医院里排不到的床位。这些在谈判文本里全部折算成「条件」，而条件的另一端坐着的是人。报道没写这些人的名字，因为谈判桌的设计里就没有他们的席位。

阿拉格奇还说「我们为所有情况做好准备」「武器已升级」「部队处于最高戒备」。同一段采访里，一边说对话是积极的一步，一边说准备好了打。这不是矛盾，这是标准配置：谈判和备战是同一件武器的两个面，谁在桌上坐得稳，取决于桌下压着多少。

真正值得盯的不是会谈有没有发生。是那六条条件里，有没有一条是关于人的。报道里没有。这不是报道的问题，这是谈判本身的问题——一场把海峡当筹码的交易，本来就不打算把人算进去。

Araghchi calls the US outreach "a positive step." The price of that step is spelled out in the same interview: lift the sanctions imposed after the Islamabad memorandum, reduce US forces in the region, restore oil waivers, release all frozen funds including those held in Qatar, recognise the shipping routes Iran discussed with Oman, close the others. Six items. And the implementation window for reopening the Strait of Hormuz has been compressed from Qatar's proposed five days to two. Two days. The opening and closing of a strait, measured in hours.

Nowhere in this language is there a person. That is not an oversight; it is the grammar of the thing. Sanctions, waivers, funds, force posture — these are counters exchanged between states. Counters do not need faces.

When I wrote about the weaponisation of expression, the most reliable entry point for war narrative was "the women of the Middle East need liberating, so we go into Afghanistan." That narrative ran for twenty years and left Afghan women worse off than before. It has now been retired and replaced with something cleaner: no liberation talk, only shipping lanes. No people, only conditions. It looks more honest — at least it doesn't pretend to be for anyone's good.

But a cost does not vanish because it goes unnamed. Sanctions land hardest at the end of the household where resources are already thinnest. Frozen funds are never an abstract treasury; they are medicine, grain, the hospital bed nobody gets. All of it gets converted into "conditions" in the negotiating text, and at the other end of those conditions are people. The report does not name them, because the table was never built with seats for them.

Araghchi also says Iran is "prepared for all scenarios," that its weaponry has been upgraded, its forces at the highest level of readiness. In the same interview: dialogue is a positive step, and we are ready to fight. This is not a contradiction. It is standard configuration. Talking and arming are two faces of one instrument, and how firmly you sit at the table depends on what is pressed underneath it.

The question worth watching is not whether the meeting happens. It is whether any of those six conditions concerns a human being. In this report, none does. That is not a failing of the reporting. It is a failing of the negotiation itself — a trade that treats a strait as a bargaining chip was never going to count people in.
