白皮肤面具与被定价的“酷”White Masks and the Pricing of 'Cool'

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
当反抗被包装成一种审美，它就成了另一种共谋。
When resistance is packaged as an aesthetic, it becomes another form of collusion.

Boots Riley 的这部新片《I Love Boosters》提供了一个极佳的样本，用来拆解“表达”是如何被武器化，以及结构性弱势者如何陷入一种假的最优解表达。电影里的那些“Boosters”——一群偷窃奢侈品并低价转卖的酷女孩，表面上在通过抢夺资源来对抗亿万富翁设计师 Christie Smith 的傲慢，但这本质上是一场关于“审美定价权”的内部博弈，而非对结构的真正颠覆。

片中最精准的隐喻是 Mariah 的超能力：通过屏息让自己变成白人女性，从而在商店里避开安检。这直接揭示了生物墙在社会权力结构中的运作方式。白皮肤在这里不是一种身份，而是一张“通行证”，一种被系统预设为“低风险”的表达。Mariah 必须通过抹除自己的原初种族特征，才能在博弈中获得暂时的最优解。这种“黑皮肤，白面具”的表达，是用主体性的暂时死亡来交换生存空间的典型案例。

然而，这些所谓的“酷女孩”在私人领域却陷入了极其低级的共谋。她们在公共空间扮演反叛者，在私人空间却被骗子 Dr Jack 的金字塔骗局和名为“魅力”实为吸血的男性 succubus 所掌控。这种对比极其讽刺：她们能看穿时尚工业的虚伪，却看不穿男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）编织的浪漫爱陷阱。她们的“酷”仅限于对商品的重新分配，而在权力关系中，她们依然在扮演那个被定义为“被诱导者”的角色。

电影真正的转折点在于中国盗贼 Jianhu 的出现。Jianhu 带来的不仅是一个传送环，而是一面镜子。她揭露了那些被抢夺的衣服背后是血汗工厂的剥削。当 Boosters 意识到她们所谓的“罗宾汉式正义”其实建立在另一个原初种族的痛苦之上时，她们才被迫面对真正的结构暴力。时尚工业的暴力不在于衣服贵不贵，而在于它如何通过定义“品味”来筛选阶级，并利用全球供应链将暴力外包给最不可见的人。

这部电影用一种极其混乱且高频的表达方式，模拟了现代消费社会的 sensory overload。但混乱本身就是一种掩体。如果这些女性仅仅满足于通过“偷窃”来获得某种亚文化圈层的认同，那么她们只是在父权和资本定义的游戏规则里，寻找一个稍微舒服一点的座位而已。

Boots Riley's 'I Love Boosters' provides a perfect specimen to dismantle how expression is weaponized and how the structurally disadvantaged fall into a fake optimal expression. The 'Boosters'—cool young women who shoplift luxury clothes to resell them—seem to be fighting the arrogance of billionaire designer Christie Smith. In reality, this is an internal gamble over the 'pricing power of aesthetics' rather than a genuine subversion of the structure.

The most precise metaphor is Mariah's superpower: holding her breath to turn into a white woman to avoid being challenged in stores. This directly reveals how the biological wall operates within social power structures. Here, white skin is not an identity, but a 'pass'—an expression preset by the system as 'low risk.' Mariah must erase her Primal Race characteristics to achieve a temporary optimal expression. This 'black skin, white masks' approach is a classic case of exchanging subjectivity for survival space.

Yet, these 'cool girls' fall into a low-level collusion in their private lives. While playing rebels in public, they are controlled by Dr Jack's pyramid scam and a male succubus masquerading as a 'charmer.' The irony is sharp: they can see through the hypocrisy of the fashion industry, but remain blind to the traps of the masculine-centric narrative. Their 'coolness' is limited to the redistribution of commodities; in power dynamics, they still play the role of the 'induced.'

The film's true turning point is the appearance of Jianhu. She brings not just a portal ring, but a mirror, revealing that the clothes they steal are products of sweatshop exploitation. When the Boosters realize their 'Robin Hood justice' is built on the suffering of another Primal Race, they are forced to confront structural violence. The violence of the fashion industry is not about the price of the clothes, but how it uses 'taste' to filter class and outsources violence to the invisible.

Riley uses a cacophonous, high-frequency style to simulate the sensory overload of modern consumerism. But chaos itself is a cover. If these women are merely satisfied with gaining subcultural recognition through theft, they are simply looking for a slightly more comfortable seat within the rules defined by patriarchy and capital.
