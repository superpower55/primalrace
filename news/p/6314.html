黑海上的死人，选票上的女人Dead Men on the Black Sea, a Woman on the Ballot

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
两打海员死了，竞选的口号是外交解决
Two dozen seafarers feared dead; the campaign slogan is diplomacy.

保加利亚这轮投票最该看的两件事：首轮投票率23%，创历史新低；黑海沿岸一个月里商船被无人机轮番打，约二十名海员恐怕已经死了，周三一艘希腊集装箱船中弹沉没。这两件事放在一起，说明的是同一件事——一个国家的表达空间已经窄到没人愿意进去。

选举是表达的一种。23%的人去投票，等于四分之三的合格选民判定：我这一票在这个博弈里不值钱。这不是冷漠，这是对最优解的计算结果。当一整个政治场域里，所有选项都被外部力量提前定价——一边被贴上“克里姆林宫友好”的标签，一边被贴上“亲西方改革派”——选民能做的表达就只剩下不表达。这是存在性战争里最安静的一种输法：不是被打败，是弃权。

然后看海。无人机打的是商船，死的是海员。这些人的名字报道里一个都没有，只有“约二十四人”这个数字。他们不是任何一方的公民投票人，他们是全球航运链条上可替换的零件。战争叙事从来这样运作：先决定谁的身体可以消耗，再决定用什么宏大词汇把消耗说成必要。总理说这些袭击的结果“有利于俄罗斯联邦”——这句话可能是对的，但它把二十几条命换算成了一个地缘政治账目上的加减项。

两位领先的候选人都谴责了袭击，都呼吁外交途径结束战争。听起来很正当。但“外交解决”这四个字，在有人正在死的时候，往往是一句让施暴结构继续运转的安慰剂：它把问题从“谁在杀海员”挪到“什么时候谈判”，而谈判桌上没有海员的位置。

伊奥托娃若胜选，会成为保加利亚第一位民选女总统。这值得记一笔——但别急着把它读成性别政治的胜利。她是以“代理总统”身份接替辞职去参选的拉德夫，而拉德夫那张票被许多分析人士视为对克里姆林宫友好。一个女人站上这个位置，不等于女性这个族群的存在性被扩大了；它只说明这个位置的继承链条恰好经过了她。第一章说过，表达决定你是谁——她在这个位置上的表达，会被她背后的政治联盟定价。

真正没被回答的问题是：那23%没去投票的人，和那二十几个死在黑海的人，谁在替他们说话。报道里一个都没有。

Two things matter in this Bulgarian vote: first-round turnout hit a record low of 23 percent, and for a month drones have been hitting merchant ships off the Black Sea coast. About two dozen seafarers are feared dead. On Wednesday a Greek container ship sank after being struck. Same story told twice: the space for expression in this country has narrowed until almost nobody wants to step into it.

An election is a form of expression. When 23 percent of eligible voters show up, three-quarters have calculated that their ballot is worth nothing in this game. That is not apathy. That is an optimal-solution reading of the board. When every option has already been priced by outside forces — one stamped "Kremlin-friendly," the other "pro-Western reformer" — the only expression left to a voter is silence. This is the quietest way to lose an existential war: not by defeat, but by abstention.

Now the sea. The drones hit merchant ships, and the dead are seafarers. Not one of their names appears in the report, only the number: about two dozen. They are not voters in anyone's election. They are replaceable parts in a global shipping chain. War narratives always run this way: first decide whose bodies are expendable, then find the grand words that make the expenditure necessary. The prime minister says the strikes' outcome "benefits the Russian Federation." He may be right. He has also converted twenty-odd lives into a line item in a geopolitical ledger.

Both leading candidates condemned the attacks and called for a diplomatic end to the war. Sounds proper. But "diplomatic solution" while people are still dying is usually a placebo that keeps the violent structure running: it moves the question from who is killing seafarers to when we negotiate, and there is no seat for seafarers at that table.

If Iotova wins, she becomes Bulgaria's first elected female president. Worth noting — but do not read it as a gender-politics victory. She became acting president after Radev resigned to run for parliament on what many analysts considered a Kremlin-friendly ticket. A woman reaching this office does not expand the existence of women as a group; it means the succession chain happened to pass through her. Expression decides who you are, and her expression in that office will be priced by the coalition behind her.

The unanswered question: who speaks for the 23 percent who stayed home, and for the two dozen dead in the Black Sea. In this report, nobody does.
