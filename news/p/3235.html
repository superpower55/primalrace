所谓的“信号”：欧盟如何通过共谋维持结构性暴力The So-called 'Signal': How the EU Maintains Structural Violence Through Complicity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-04 § 链接
将人的生存权定义为“错误信号”，是典型的结构暴力叙事。
Defining human survival as a 'bad signal' is a textbook narrative of structural violence.

欧盟移民事务专员 Magnus Brunner 对西班牙合法化 50 万无证移民计划的批评，精准地揭示了欧盟内部的共谋逻辑。在他口中，给绝望的移民一个合法身份不是人道主义，而是一个“不好的信号” (not a good signal)。

这句话是典型的 cultural violence。它将具体的、活生生的人，在 Potential（生存权）与 Actual（被驱逐/非法状态）的差额中挣扎的痛苦，抽象成了一个政治符号。在欧盟的认知入口里，移民不是主体，而是某种需要被管理、被拦截、被威慑的“流量”。当西班牙试图通过制度调整缩小这个暴力差额时，欧盟的反应不是庆祝人权的回归，而是担心这会给其他“非法”者提供某种可能性。这种对“可能性”的恐惧，本质上是对结构性暴力被拆穿的恐惧。

更讽刺的是这种共谋的伪善。Brunner 声称理解西班牙这样做是因为拉丁美洲人“说着相同的语言，拥有相同的文化”。这是一种极其恶心的 weaponized 叙事：只有在文化共谋的范围内，人的生存才具有被合法化的价值。而对于那些不符合这个文化筛选标准的移民，欧盟的共识是“无情打击”和“加强边境”。

欧盟在会议中大谈“韧性” (resilience) 和“预警系统”，实际上是在完善一套更高效的排斥机器。他们把注意力转移到所谓的“俄罗斯虚假信息”上，试图将结构性暴力包装成外部敌人的阴谋，从而掩盖一个事实：这种暴力正是由他们自己的制度、法律和对“信号”的定义权所制造的。

EU migration chief Magnus Brunner’s criticism of Spain’s plan to regularize 500,000 undocumented migrants perfectly exposes the logic of complicity within the EU. In his words, granting legal status to desperate people is not an act of humanity, but "not a good signal."

This phrasing is a prime example of cultural violence. It abstracts the agony of real human beings—struggling in the gap between their Potential (the right to exist) and their Actual (deportation/illegality)—into a mere political symbol. In the EU's cognitive entry point, migrants are not subjects; they are "flows" to be managed, intercepted, and deterred. When Spain attempts to narrow this violence gap through institutional change, the EU reacts not by celebrating human rights, but by fearing that this creates a "possibility" for others. This fear of possibility is, in essence, a fear of structural violence being dismantled.

There is a profound hypocrisy in this complicity. Brunner claims to understand Spain's move because Latin Americans "speak the same language, have the same culture." This is a weaponized narrative: human survival only gains the value of legalization when it falls within a circle of cultural complicity. For those who fail this cultural filter, the EU consensus remains "relentless fighting" and "strengthening borders."

While the EU discusses "resilience" and "early-warning systems," they are simply refining a more efficient machine of exclusion. By shifting focus to "Russian disinformation," they attempt to package structural violence as a conspiracy of external enemies, masking the fact that this violence is manufactured by their own institutions, laws, and the monopoly over the definition of a "signal."
