用皮革味掩盖无能：男用洗涤剂的性别 scamLeather-Scented Incompetence: The Gender Scam of 'Men's' Detergents

性别 文化层 · 结构层 Wirecutter: Reviews for the Real World ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
当产品试图通过‘男性化’包装来赋予家务合法性时，它在兜售一种主体性的阉割。
When a product uses 'masculinity' to legitimize housework, it is selling the castration of agency.

这不仅是一次糟糕的产品评测，而是一次典型的文化暴力 (cultural violence) 现场。所谓的‘男用洗涤剂’ (man-tergents) 试图通过烟草、皮革和木质香调，为男性进入家务领域提供一种‘心理许可’。其潜台词极其卑劣：洗衣服是女性化的、低贱的，除非你把它包装成‘硬汉’的行为，否则你的 masculinity 将会受损。

这是一种极其阴险的武器化表达 (weaponized expression)。它并不试图消弭性别分工的结构性暴力，反而通过制造‘男用’这个伪命题，将家务劳动再次性别化。它告诉男性：你可以分担家务，但你必须在气味上与‘女性’划清界限，甚至要通过‘不闻起来像个 b*tch’这种充满敌意的叙事来获得心理补偿。这种‘最优解表达’是假的，它让男性在扮演‘硬汉’的同时，心安理得地在结构上继续维持特权。

最讽刺的是，这些产品在功能上完全是 scam。它们宣称拥有专门针对‘男性气味’的酶，但实际效果却远不如中性的普通洗涤剂。这揭示了元暴力 (meta violence) 的运作逻辑：只要叙事足够‘男性中心’，消费者就愿意为低劣的性能支付更高的溢价。他们购买的不是清洁力，而是一张‘我可以做家务且依然是强者’的入场券。

当一个男人需要通过使用‘皮革味’的洗涤剂才能克服对洗衣机的恐惧时，他并没有在分担家务，他只是在进行一场关于身份的拙劣表演。真正的公正表达 (just expression) 应该是：洗衣服只是一个物理除垢过程，它不具备任何性别属性。任何试图给除垢过程加上性别标签的行为，本质上都是在加固那堵生物墙之外的文化墙。

This is more than a bad product review; it is a live demonstration of cultural violence. So-called 'man-tergents' attempt to provide 'psycho-emotional permission' for men to enter the domestic sphere through scents of tobacco, leather, and wood. The subtext is vile: laundry is feminine and debasing, and unless you wrap it in 'tough guy' branding, your masculinity is at risk.

This is a classic weaponization of expression. Rather than dismantling the structural violence of gendered labor, it reinforces it by creating a fake 'men's' category. It tells men they can share the chores, provided they distance themselves from the 'feminine'—even using hostile narratives like 'not smelling like a b*tch' to secure psychological compensation. This is a fake optimal expression; it allows men to perform 'manliness' while structurally maintaining their privilege.

The irony is that these products are a total scam. They claim specialized enzymes for 'manly odors' yet fail miserably against gender-neutral alternatives. This reveals the logic of meta violence: as long as the narrative is masculine-centric, consumers will pay a premium for inferior performance. They aren't buying cleaning power; they are buying a ticket that says 'I can do chores and still be the alpha.'

When a man needs leather-scented soap to overcome the fear of a washing machine, he isn't sharing the load; he is performing a role. A just expression would be simple: removing dirt is a physical process devoid of gender. Any attempt to gender the act of cleaning is merely another brick in the cultural wall built atop the biological wall.
