轮胎学校的litmus paper：当人道主义成为一种PR叙事The Litmus Paper of Tire Schools: When Humanitarianism Becomes a PR Narrative

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-05 § 链接
用艺术“刮擦记忆”无法填补结构性暴力的差额。
Scratching memories with art cannot bridge the gap of structural violence.

Khan al-Ahmar 的“轮胎学校”是一个极佳的 Violence Triangle 样本。在 direct 层，它是定居者暴力的前线；在 structural 层，它是以色列当局通过拒绝建筑许可（permit）将整个定居点定义为“非法”的制度性抹除。而最讽刺的是 cultural 层：这所学校被 Gitai 称为“试金石”（litmus paper），用来测试国际社会的保护意愿。这意味着，一个孩子的受教育权，竟然取决于外部权力机构是否愿意在 PR 层面将其作为“人道主义”的勋章。这种将生存权转化为“测试题”的叙事，本身就是一种 weaponized expression。

Gitai 试图用电影作为一种“公民姿态”（civic gesture）来防御学校，并坦承艺术不能改变现实，只能“刮擦记忆”。这种表达在博弈中是典型的“假.最优解”。当 machine guns 和 money 决定现实时，电影这种 elliptical 的语气诗（tone poem）在权力面前几乎没有定价权。它提供了一种情绪上的共情，却无法在 structural 层缩减 Potential − Actual 的差额。如果一个学校的生存仅靠国际压力的“偶然”维持，那么这种生存状态本身就是一种持续的结构性暴力。

我们不需要更多“被触动”的观察者，而需要解释权的真正换手。当一个以色列导演在威尼斯电影节讨论“希望”时，他依然处于一个相对安全的认知入口。真正的 good_news 不应该是“欧洲站出来说了不”，而应该是该地区的 Bedouin 社区拿回了定义自己生存空间的法律权力，而不是等待下一个被触动的艺术家来记录他们的坚韧。

The 'school of tires' in Khan al-Ahmar is a textbook case of the Violence Triangle. On the direct level, it is the frontline of settler violence; on the structural level, it is a systematic erasure where Israeli authorities render an entire settlement 'illegal' by denying building permits. Most cynical, however, is the cultural level: Gitai calls the school a 'litmus paper' to test the international community's will to protect. This frames a child's right to education as a test of external powers' willingness to wear 'humanitarianism' as a PR badge. Turning the right to exist into a 'test case' is, in itself, a form of weaponized expression.

Gitai views his cinema as a 'civic gesture' to defend the school, admitting that art doesn't change reality but merely 'scratches a memory.' In the game of existential war, this is a 'false optimal expression.' When machine guns and money dictate reality, an elliptical 'tone poem' has zero pricing power over structural change. It offers emotional empathy but fails to reduce the gap between Potential and Actual in the Violence Triangle. If a school's survival depends solely on the 'accident' of international pressure, that state of existence is a form of ongoing structural violence.

We don't need more 'moved' observers; we need a genuine transfer of the power of interpretation. While an Israeli director discusses 'hope' at the Venice Film Festival, he still operates from a secure cognitive entry point. A true good_news would not be 'Europe stood up and said no,' but the Bedouin community reclaiming the legal power to define their own space, rather than waiting for the next touched artist to document their perseverance.
