加息治通胀，代价不问人Rate Hikes Treat Inflation, Not the People Paying

其他 结构层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要澳大利亚央行11月3日将基准现金利率上调25个基点至4.85%，为2008年11月以来最高，是今年第五次加息。9月消费者物价指数同比上涨4.3%，高于8月的4%，为3月以来最高。央行行长布洛克称通胀「过高」，部分源于伊朗战争推高油价和国内支出走强。
The newsAustralia's central bank raised its benchmark cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85% on Tuesday, the highest since November 2008 and its fifth hike this year. The consumer price index rose 4.3% in September, up from 4% in August. Governor Michele Bullock called inflation "too high," driven by higher oil prices from the Iran War and stronger domestic spending.
利率是刀，握刀的手从不出现在伤口旁边
The hand holding the knife never appears beside the wound

央行把利率加到4.85%，十八年最高，今年第五次。新闻写得很干净：通胀4.3%，油价被伊朗战争推高，国内支出走强，所以要「把通胀降下来」。降下来之后谁先倒，报道没写。

加息不是抽象的数字游戏，它是把成本从一头搬到另一头。通胀是所有人都在付的账，加息是把这笔账重新分给最经不起分的人——房贷、租金、小生意、刚在还债的家庭。布洛克说「如果通胀需要，还会有更多加息」，这句话里没有主语：谁需要，谁被需要牺牲，都不出现。这就是结构暴力最标准的写法，没有人施暴，只有「政策」在动。

真正把通胀推上去的那部分——伊朗战争带来的油价——不是澳洲央行能加息加掉的。它加的是国内需求，是工资，是消费。等于是在说：外面打起来的火我灭不了，就灭你家里的灶。战争的成本从石油市场一路传导到澳洲厨房，中间的传导链条没有一个人被问责，因为链条本身就是「现状」。

报道里唯一被问到人的地方是那句「是否会出现衰退」，回答是「目前不是我们的基准情形」。基准情形是经济学家的词，不是租客的词。GDP从2.5%降到2.1%，增速在放缓，而利率在往上走——这两条线交叉的地方，是有人在还不起贷款。报道没写这些人是谁，因为这篇稿子到截稿为止还没更新到他们出场。

还有一句被轻轻放过的：通胀「由国内支出走强推动」。支出走强，翻译过来就是有人还在花钱。可在一个生活成本已经被反复提到的国家，谁在花钱、花的是谁的钱，报道没有分开。把「消费旺盛」和「生活压力」写在同一篇稿子里而不打架，靠的就是这个不分开。

The central bank lifted rates to 4.85%, an 18-year high, the fifth hike this year. The story is clean: inflation at 4.3%, oil pushed up by the Iran War, domestic spending strong, so inflation must come down. Who falls first when it does, the report doesn't say.

A rate hike is not an abstract number. It moves a cost from one pocket to another. Inflation is a bill everyone pays; the hike redistributes that bill toward those least able to carry it — mortgages, rents, small businesses, households already in debt. Bullock says more hikes will come "if that's what's needed." The sentence has no subject: who needs it, who gets sacrificed for it, both stay invisible. This is structural violence in its standard form — no one is doing harm, only "policy" is moving.

The part actually driving inflation — oil prices from the Iran War — is not something the RBA can hike away. It is hiking domestic demand, wages, spending. In effect: the fire outside I cannot put out, so I put out the stove at home. The cost of war travels from the oil market into Australian kitchens, and nobody along that chain is held to account, because the chain itself is "the situation."

The only place a person appears is the question about recession, answered with "not our base case at the moment." Base case is an economist's word, not a renter's. GDP slowed from 2.5% to 2.1% while rates climb — where those two lines cross, someone cannot make a payment. The report doesn't name them, because the story hasn't been updated to their scene yet.

One line is let through gently: inflation driven by "stronger domestic spending." Strong spending means someone is still buying. In a country whose cost-of-living pressures the same article mentions, who is spending and with whose money is never separated out. Keeping "robust consumption" and "household strain" in one piece without letting them collide depends on exactly that omission.
