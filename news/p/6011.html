算力东迁，账单西付Compute Moves East, the Bill Stays West

科技 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
海湾的AI基建是美元与解释权的外包，代价记在没被问过的人头上。
Gulf AI infrastructure is rented land: the upside is exported, the costs are absorbed by people nobody asked.

美国AI公司往海湾搬服务器，不是去冒险，是去收租。Oracle 在沙特和阿联酋建数据中心，Scale AI 在迪拜国际金融中心开门，Snowflake 承诺到 2030 年在沙特投 3 亿美元——客户里有乳企 Almarai 和 PIF 撑腰的 Red Sea Global。这三家把同一件事说得很好听：需求上升。需求是谁的，报道没写。

Bloomberg 的经济学家 Ziad Daoud 说，到目前为止，数据中心建设对海湾经济体“几乎没怎么推动指针”。也就是说，钱进去了，结构没动。伊朗战争让建这套基础设施更贵、更险，加固安保把成本抬高 5% 到 7%，而报道说，看起来有人愿意吞下这笔溢价。愿意吞的人不是签合同的人。

Snowflake 和通信部合作，给数千名沙特人做 AI 培训。这句话读起来像礼物，实际是筛选：谁被训练、训练成什么、为谁的模型标注数据，全由出钱的一方定义。Scale AI 卖的就是标注好的数据，它和 G42 的子公司合作开发 AI agent——认知入口的原材料在别处开采，成品运回美国公司账上。第三章那套“制造可能性的艺术”，在这里是字面意义上的：可能性被制造在沙漠里，解释权留在西海岸。

真正值得问的是，这笔溢价最后落在谁身上。报道只说了“有人愿意”，没说谁。数据中心要电、要水、要地，海湾的制冷负荷和淡水本来就紧；安保成本涨了，涨的是公共预算还是私人合同，报道没写。名字缺席的地方，就是代价被转移的地方。

这不是一条关于技术的新闻，是一条关于谁有权给基础设施定价的新闻。Oracle 建的不是算力，是租约；Snowflake 投的不是沙特，是进入一个由 PIF 担保的市场所需的门票。海湾出地、出电、出人，美国公司出模型、出品牌、出解释权，利润分成写在哪一页，报道没写。

最后一层：当五角大楼和硅谷同时把中东当后院，AI 基建就成了新的军事-商业复合体的地基。伊朗战争推高的是安保成本，不是撤资理由——因为这套设施从一开始就不是为当地经济建的，是为一个跨国的、不需要对当地人负责的算力网络建的。指针没动，账已经记上了。

US AI firms are not moving into the Gulf to take risks. They are moving in to collect rent. Oracle is building more data centers in Saudi Arabia and the UAE, Scale AI has opened an office at the Dubai International Financial Centre, and Snowflake projects $300 million of investment in the kingdom through 2030, with customers including the dairy company Almarai and the PIF-backed developer Red Sea Global. All three tell the same flattering story: rising demand. Whose demand, the report does not say.

Bloomberg economist Ziad Daoud says data center construction has so far “barely moved the needle for Gulf economies.” Money goes in; structure does not move. The Iran war makes building this infrastructure pricier and riskier, and hardened security adds 5% to 7% to costs. The report says there appears to be willingness to absorb the new premium. The people willing to absorb it are not the people signing the contracts.

Snowflake is working with the communications ministry to train thousands of Saudis in AI. That reads like a gift. It is a filter: who gets trained, trained for what, labeling data for whose models — all defined by the side putting up the money. Scale AI sells labeled data for model training, and it has agreed to work with a G42 unit to develop AI agents. The raw material of the cognitive entrance is mined elsewhere; the finished product lands on a US company’s books. The art of manufacturing possibilities, taken literally: possibilities manufactured in the desert, the right to interpret them kept on the West Coast.

The real question is who ends up holding the premium. The report says only that someone is willing. Data centers need power, water, and land, and the Gulf’s cooling load and freshwater are already tight. When security costs rise, does the bill land on the public budget or on private contracts? Not written. Where names are missing is where costs get shifted.

This is not a story about technology. It is a story about who gets to price infrastructure. Oracle is not building compute; it is building leases. Snowflake is not investing in Saudi Arabia; it is buying the ticket required to enter a market underwritten by PIF. The Gulf supplies land, power, and people; the US firms supply models, brands, and the right to explain. Where the profit split is written down, the report does not say.

One layer deeper: when the Pentagon and Silicon Valley both treat the Middle East as a backyard, AI infrastructure becomes the foundation of a new military-commercial complex. The Iran war raises security costs rather than prompting withdrawal, because this infrastructure was never built for the local economy. It was built for a transnational compute network that answers to no one locally. The needle has not moved. The bill is already being written.
