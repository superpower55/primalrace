死亡速度的流量博弈与男性暴力闭环Traffic-Driven Death: The Feedback Loop of Masculine Violence

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 直接层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
将危险驾驶武器化为流量筹码，是典型的男性中心叙事自毁闭环。
Weaponizing reckless driving for clout is a self-destructive loop of masculine-centric narrative.

这场惨剧不是简单的交通意外，而是一场关于“存在性”的低劣博弈。一群处于社会边缘的年轻男性，试图通过在社媒上发布危险驾驶视频来获取某种扭曲的“存在感”。在他们的认知逻辑里，通过 baiting police（挑衅警察）这种高风险行为，可以将生理上的鲁莽转化为社交货币，从而在男性主导的权力层级中通过“胆量”完成一次虚假的身份跃升。

这本质上是元暴力（meta violence）的内卷。男性中心叙事长期将“破坏”、“冒险”和“对抗权威”定义为强者的特质。当这些年轻人无法通过正常的社会路径获得权力时，他们选择将表达武器化——把 A66 公路变成战场，把警车变成背景板。他们追求的不是生存的最优解，而是某种表演性的“强悍”。

最讽刺的是，这种暴力闭环在文化层（cultural layer）得到了某种隐秘的奖赏：社交平台的算法在推波助澜，而公众的关注（即便在事后）依然在强化这种“亡命之徒”的叙事。当一个 17 岁少年认为在高速公路上逆行是某种“酷”的表达时，他已经彻底丧失了主体性，成为了父权制暴力逻辑下的一枚弃子。

至于政府和监管机构现在谈论的“采取严厉行动”，不过是 structural violence 在面对直接暴力爆发后的惯性修补。只要这种将“攻击性”等同于“力量”的男性叙事不被拆除，即便封掉了几个 App，依然会有下一群年轻人开着车，在死亡速度中寻找他们那可怜的、被定义好的身份。

This tragedy is not a mere traffic accident, but a pathetic gamble over 'existence.' A group of marginalized young men attempted to convert physical recklessness into social currency by posting dangerous driving videos, seeking a distorted sense of presence. In their logic, baiting police is a way to achieve a fake identity leap within the masculine power hierarchy through 'bravery.'

This is the involution of meta violence. The masculine-centric narrative has long defined 'destruction,' 'risk-taking,' and 'defying authority' as traits of the strong. Unable to access power through legitimate social paths, these youths weaponized their expression—turning the A66 highway into a battlefield and police cars into props. They weren't seeking an optimal solution for survival, but a performative 'toughness.'

The irony lies in the reward system at the cultural layer: social media algorithms amplify the thrill, and public attention, even in retrospect, reinforces the 'outlaw' narrative. When a 17-year-old believes driving the wrong way on a highway is a 'cool' expression, he has lost his subjectivity, becoming a disposable pawn in the logic of patriarchal violence.

Government talk of 'tough action' is merely the structural violence attempting a habitual patch after a burst of direct violence. As long as the narrative equating 'aggression' with 'power' remains intact, banning a few apps won't stop the next group of youths from seeking their pre-defined identities in the speed of death.
