没有否决权，只有接受No Veto Power, Only Acceptance

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
她的接受被写成婚姻的美德
Her acceptance is sold as the virtue of a marriage

Michael Douglas 在 CNN 上说，Catherine Zeta-Jones 对他的回忆录没有“否决权”。“但我不会把任何她不喜欢的东西放进书里。”这句话听起来像体贴，其实是同一件事的另一种说法：决定权在他手上，她只负责接受。

他还原了一段自己的时间线：拍《绿宝石》时和 Kathleen Turner 有婚外情，那时 Zeta-Jones 7 岁。他把这个数字说出口，说了不止一次，一次在 CNN，一次在 Kimmel 的节目上。7 岁这个数字在他嘴里不是忏悔，是修辞——用来衬托他的人生有多长、她的等待有多久。一个 7 岁的女孩被拉进一个成年男人的情史叙事里，充当背景板，她本人对此没有发言权，现在也没有。

Zeta-Jones 说过她没打算读这本书。她说这话时的语气被媒体当作笑料处理。一个妻子不读丈夫的书，被包装成婚姻里的可爱细节。但换个角度看：她不在场，她没有编辑权，她的名字被写进章节标题，她的形象被写进“我的爱”这个句式。她唯一能做的表态是不读。这不是幽默，这是退场。

26 年的婚姻，25 岁的年龄差，这两个数字被反复引用，好像时长本身就能证明什么。Douglas 说“她现在应该理解我了”。理解什么？理解一个男人有权把自己的过去写成书，而她只能选择要不要翻开。理解在这个结构里，他的记忆是公共财产，她的沉默是私人美德。

这不是一段婚姻的八卦，这是表达权在私人领域里的分配。谁的故事被出版，谁的名字被引用，谁被允许说“我不读”，谁被期待说“我理解”。浪漫爱叙事在这里又完成了一次工作：把不对等写成深情，把接受写成爱。

书的标题叫 One Helluva Ride。一段好旅程。坐在驾驶座上的人从来只有一个。

Michael Douglas told CNN that Catherine Zeta-Jones had no “veto power” over his memoir. “But I would not put anything in the book that she would not like,” he added. That sounds like consideration. It is the same arrangement stated twice: the decision is his, and she receives it.

He laid out his own timeline: while making Romancing the Stone and carrying on with Kathleen Turner, Zeta-Jones was 7 years old. He said the number out loud, more than once — on CNN, then again on Kimmel. The 7-year-old is not a confession; it is a rhetorical device, there to measure how long his life has been and how long her wait. A child is pulled into an adult man’s love history as scenery. She had no say then. She has no say now.

Zeta-Jones has said she has no plans to read the book. The line got filed as a joke. A wife who won’t read her husband’s memoir is a cute marital detail. Look again: she is not present, she has no editorial role, her name is on a chapter title, her image is inside the sentence “my love for her.” The one move left to her is not to read. That is not humor. That is exit.

Twenty-six years of marriage, a 25-year age gap — the numbers get repeated as if duration were proof. Douglas says she “understands me by now.” Understands what? That a man may publish his past while she chooses whether to open it. That in this structure his memory is public property and her silence is private virtue.

This is not gossip about a marriage. It is the distribution of expressive power inside the private sphere. Whose story gets published, whose name gets quoted, who is allowed to say “I won’t read it,” who is expected to say “I understand.” The romance narrative does its work again: asymmetry written as devotion, acceptance written as love.

The book is called One Helluva Ride. Only one person has ever held the wheel.
