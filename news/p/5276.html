用“indulgence”掩盖的工业化投喂Industrial Feeding Masked as 'Indulgence'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
所谓的“极致享受”不过是工业添加剂对生物本能的精准劫持。
The so-called 'ultimate indulgence' is merely a precision hijacking of biological instincts by industrial additives.

这篇评测最荒诞的地方在于，它用“full-fat indulgence”（全脂纵容/享受）这种带有某种浪漫色彩的词汇，来描述一场关于超加工食物 (UPF) 的工业竞赛。传统的柠檬芝士蛋糕最多 8 种食材，而现代超市版本平均 24 种。这多出来的 16 种东西不是为了味道，而是为了在工业流水线上维持一种“看起来像蛋糕”的稳定状态。

这是一种典型的 weaponized expression。商业团队通过定义什么是“绝妙的脂肪感”或“复杂的柠檬味”，将酸度调节剂、乳化剂和棕榈油包装成某种高级的品味体验。当你读到“Dangerously moreish”（危险地令人上瘾）时，这种“危险”并非来自美食的诱惑，而来自 salt, sugar and fat 这三位工业巨头对人类多巴胺系统的直接劫持。这根本不是在品鉴食物，而是在评测哪家公司的化学配方更能欺骗生物本能。

更讽刺的是，评测者在给分时，依然在“口感”和“比例”这种表层表达上打转，而将那些让身体受苦的添加剂视为次要的脚注。这种叙事逻辑在潜意识里完成了一次共谋：只要味道足够“诱人”，工业化对身体的侵蚀就可以被定义为一种“享受”。

在这种认知入口的操纵下，消费者被训练成只在乎“cheese-to-biscuit ratio”的评判者，而忘记了自己其实是被投喂的客体。真正的 indulgence 应该是对真实食材的掌控，而不是在 24 种添加剂的化学迷雾中，寻找一点点柠檬油的幻觉。

The most absurd part of this review is the use of the term 'full-fat indulgence' to describe an industrial race of ultra-processed foods (UPF). A traditional lemon cheesecake requires at most 8 ingredients; today's supermarket versions average 24. Those extra 16 ingredients aren't for flavor; they are there to maintain a state of 'looking like a cake' on an industrial assembly line.

This is a classic case of weaponized expression. Commercial teams define 'wonderful fattiness' or 'lemony complexity' to package acidity regulators, emulsifiers, and palm oil as a sophisticated taste experience. When the author writes 'Dangerously moreish,' the 'danger' doesn't stem from gourmet temptation, but from the ultimate trio of salt, sugar, and fat directly hijacking the human dopamine system. This isn't food tasting; it's a benchmark of which company's chemical formula can best deceive biological instincts.

More ironically, the reviewer assigns stars based on superficial expressions like 'texture' and 'ratio,' treating the additives that harm the body as mere footnotes. This narrative logic completes a complicity in the subconscious: as long as the taste is 'tempting' enough, the industrial erosion of the body can be redefined as 'indulgence.'

Under the manipulation of this cognitive entry, consumers are trained to be judges who only care about the 'cheese-to-biscuit ratio,' forgetting that they are actually the objects being fed. True indulgence is the mastery of real ingredients, not searching for a hallucination of lemon oil within a chemical fog of 24 additives.
