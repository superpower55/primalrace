偷糖果的富人与被科学化的特权The Rich Stealing Candy and the Scientization of Privilege

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-08 § 链接
道德感并非生物属性，而是权力在博弈中被豁免后的副作用。
Morality is not a biological trait, but a side effect of power being exempted from the rules.

这篇报道试图用一种“幽默”的科学视角来探讨富人的道德缺陷，但把这种现象交给 Ig Nobel 这种娱乐化奖项去定义，本身就是一种典型的 cultural violence。它把结构性的傲慢包装成了一种有趣的“生物现象”或“行为 quirk”，从而消解了其背后的权力逻辑：富人之所以更倾向于“偷走孩子的糖果”，不是因为他们的脑回路出了问题，而是因为在现实的博弈中，他们已经习惯了通过剥夺他人的 Potential 来增加自己的 Actual，且这种行为在结构层被系统性地豁免。

在存在性战争中，拥有绝对资源的人倾向于将他人客体化。当一个人在社会阶层中处于顶端，他接收到的反馈机制是：规则是为他人设计的，而我是规则的制定者或共谋者。这种元暴力的内化，让他们在面对弱小者（如孩子）时，潜意识里并不认为对方拥有平等的权利。偷糖果只是一个微小的实验样本，而在现实的 structural layer 中，这种“偷窃”体现为税收漏洞、资源垄断和对底层劳动力主体性的剥夺。

最讽刺的是，这种研究被冠以“科学”之名，让大众在“原来富人真的没素质”的快感中达成一种廉价的心理补偿，而完全忽略了那个核心问题：什么样的系统允许这种“没素质”的人掌握着定义世界真实性的权力？当你把特权者的恶行简化为一种可观察的“科学现象”时，你就在潜意识里完成了与这个系统的共谋——你接受了“富人就是这样”的设定，而放弃了对这种权力结构本身的拆解。

This report attempts to explore the moral failings of the wealthy through a "humorous" scientific lens, but relegating this phenomenon to the Ig Nobel prizes is a classic form of cultural violence. By packaging structural arrogance as an amusing "biological quirk," it erases the underlying power logic: the rich are more likely to "steal candy from a child" not because of a glitch in their brain wiring, but because they are conditioned by a system that rewards the appropriation of others' Potential to increase their own Actual, all while being structurally exempted from the consequences.

In the existential war, those with absolute resources tend to objectify others. When one stands at the apex of the social hierarchy, the internal feedback loop is simple: rules are for others; I am the rule-maker or a complicit beneficiary. This internalization of meta-violence manifests as a subconscious belief that the vulnerable—such as children—do not possess equal rights. Stealing candy is merely a micro-sample; in the structural layer of reality, this "theft" manifests as tax loopholes, resource monopolies, and the stripping of subjectivity from the working class.

The irony is that by framing this as "science," the public is offered a cheap psychological consolation—a feeling of "see, the rich really are rude"—while ignoring the central question: what kind of system allows these "rude" individuals to hold the power to define reality? When you reduce the malice of the privileged to an observable "scientific phenomenon," you become a complicit party to the system. You accept the premise that "the rich are just like that," and in doing so, you stop dismantling the structure that produces them.
