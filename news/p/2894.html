用“胶水”粘合的护理体系与被隐形的女性共谋The 'Glue' of Social Care and the Invisible Female Complicity

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
所谓的“破碎系统”，本质上是对女性无偿劳动的结构性榨取。
The 'broken system' is actually a structural extraction of unpaid female labor.

英格兰的社会护理体系被描述为用“胶水和创可贴”勉强维持，这句比喻极其精准，但它掩盖了最核心的真相：那层胶水，就是数以百万计的 unpaid carers —— 绝大多数是女性。在 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式里，这种制度设计本身就是一种 structural violence。它通过将护理定义为“家庭责任”而非“社会权利”，合法地剥夺了女性的劳动价值与生命潜能。

政客们在争论是采取 NHS 模式、苏格兰模式还是设置费用上限，讨论的焦点全在“钱”怎么出。这正是典型的 masculine-centric narrative：他们把护理视为一项需要被定价的“成本”或“开支”，而非一种基本的人权。当他们讨论如何通过税收来“修复”系统时，其实是在讨论如何用公共资金给这种长期存在的性别剥削买单，而从未触及一个核心问题——为什么护理在认知入口中被预设为女性的自然属性？

这种系统能够运行至今，依赖于一种深刻的 complicity。男性受益于低成本的家庭护理，而部分女性在“母职”或“孝道”的文化规训下，内化了这种自我牺牲，将其视为一种道德优越感。这种 cultural violence 让直接的剥夺看起来像是一种“爱”。

即便最终实现了某种形式的 universal system，如果它仅仅是把护理服务“国有化”，而没有打破“护理=女性”的元暴力，那么它不过是将女性从私人领域的无偿奴役，转移到了一个由国家管理、低薪且依然被轻视的结构性陷阱中。真正的修复不是算账，而是权力解释权的移交。

England's social care system is described as being held together by "sticking plasters and glue." This is a precise metaphor, but it masks a brutal truth: that glue consists of millions of unpaid carers, the vast majority of whom are women. In the formula Violence = Potential − Actual, this institutional design is a textbook case of structural violence. By defining care as a "family responsibility" rather than a "social right," the system legitimizes the extraction of female labor and life potential.

Politicians are currently debating NHS-style models, Scottish-style free care, or cost caps. The entire focus is on "payment." This is a classic masculine-centric narrative: treating care as a "cost" or "expense" to be budgeted, rather than a fundamental human right. When they discuss funding the "fix" through taxation, they are merely discussing how to use public funds to subsidize long-standing gender exploitation, without ever questioning why care is preset as a female attribute in the cognitive entry point.

This system persists through a profound complicity. Men benefit from low-cost domestic care, while many women, conditioned by cultural narratives of "motherhood" or "filial piety," internalize this self-sacrifice as moral superiority. This cultural violence makes direct deprivation look like "love."

Even if a universal system is achieved, if it merely "nationalizes" care without dismantling the meta-violence of "care = female," it simply shifts women from private unpaid slavery into a state-managed, low-paid, and still undervalued structural trap. Real reform is not about accounting; it is about the transfer of the power of interpretation.
