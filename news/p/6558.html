不是她写不出来，是行业不让她写She Wasn't Out of Songs. The Industry Wouldn't Let Her Write Them

文娱 元暴力 · 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把女性创作史排成榜单，就是一次解释权的回收
Ranking a woman's catalog is how the industry takes back the right to explain her.

Billboard 给 Mariah Carey 的十五张专辑排了名。这条新闻的重点不在排名先后，而在正文里那句她自己写的话：唱片公司和她当时的丈夫 Tommy Mottola 多年来一直压着她，她听到的那句“too urban”，是“too Black”的暗语。

排到第一的 Butterfly 是一张她离婚之后才做出来的专辑。她终于把 hip-hop 节奏和 R&B 放进去，Honey 和 My All 都上了 Hot 100 榜首，The Roof 把 Mobb Deep 的 Shook Ones (Part II) 翻成一曲层层叠叠的幽魂式情歌。这张专辑之所以是第一名，不是因为它比 Daydream 更流行，而是因为它第一次是她自己要的样子。榜单在这里做的，是把一次挣脱控制的行为，重新包装成“她最好的作品”。

这就是元暴力在日常文化版面上的样子：解释权不在做音乐的人手里，而在写历史的人手里。Carey 从 1990 年出道起拿了 19 首 Hot 100 冠军单曲，其中 18 首是自己写的；她是唯一一个在 90 年代每一年都有冠军单曲的艺人，97 周冠军在位时间也无人能及。这些数字是事实。但“她是谁”这件事，从来是由别人来讲述的：先是唱片公司用“too urban”来定义她不能做什么，现在是一份榜单来定义她哪一张最好。

榜单本身也有意思。它把 Merry Christmas II You 放在最后，把 Glitter 放在倒数第二，理由是“影评人和歌迷都不买账”。Glitter 是 2001 年那张配套电影的原声，12 首歌，她转向 80 年代 disco 和 hip-hop，请来 Ja Rule、Ludacris、Busta Rhymes。那张专辑发行的时候，她正处在个人崩溃期，媒体对它的围剿和音乐本身关系不大。二十多年后，同一家媒体用“尽管有失误”来带过，这个“失误”是谁定的，报道没写。

再看 Caution 那一段。作者写“Mariah、乐评人和对年龄的承认很少意见一致”，然后说 2018 年的这张专辑让三方终于对齐，证明她出道 28 年后还能跟上任何一代的 R&B。这句话里藏着一个前提：一个女歌手到了某个年纪，还能被乐评人认可，本身就算新闻。男歌手发第 15 张专辑，没有人会写“他居然还能跟上”。

我没有说这份榜单是恶意的。Billboard 的编辑们显然懂她的音乐，Hidden Gem 选得也准。问题不在恶意，在结构：一个由行业内部人写成的排名，天然会把“她对抗行业”的那部分历史，收编成“行业早就认可她”的叙事。Butterfly 是她的解放，但解放之后，解释权又回到了原来的地方。

报道里有一句话值得停一下。Carey 在回忆录里写，Butterfly 时期“the sexy-ass songs started pouring out”。歌是自己涌出来的。榜单把它排在第一，是对的；但把它排在第一的理由，和歌涌出来的理由，不是同一个。

Billboard ranked Mariah Carey's fifteen albums. The ranking is not the story. The story is a line in the middle of the piece: for years her label and her then-husband Tommy Mottola held her back, and the phrase she kept hearing, "too urban," was code for "too Black."

Butterfly, ranked first, is the album she made after the divorce. She finally put hip-hop rhythms and R&B into it; Honey and My All both went to No. 1 on the Hot 100, and The Roof flipped Mobb Deep's Shook Ones (Part II) into a lush, haunted ballad. It is No. 1 not because it was more popular than Daydream but because it was, for the first time, what she wanted it to be. The list takes an act of breaking free and repackages it as "her best work."

This is meta-violence on the culture page: the right to explain does not sit with the person who made the music, it sits with the people who write the history. Since her 1990 debut Carey has had 19 Hot 100 No. 1s, 18 of them self-penned; she is the only artist with No. 1s in every year of a decade, and her 97 weeks at No. 1 are the most of any solo artist. Those numbers are facts. But who she is has always been narrated by someone else: first the label defining what she could not do with "too urban," now a list defining which album is her best.

The list has its own tells. It puts Merry Christmas II You last and Glitter second to last, on the grounds that critics and fans panned them. Glitter was the 2001 soundtrack, twelve tracks, her turn toward '80s disco and hip-hop with Ja Rule, Ludacris and Busta Rhymes. It landed while she was in personal collapse, and the media siege around it had little to do with the music. Two decades later the same outlet waves it off as "despite her missteps." Who defined the misstep is not in the piece.

Then the Caution entry. The writer notes that "Mariah, critics and the acknowledgement of age have rarely seen eye-to-eye," and that the 2018 album finally aligned all three, proving she could still keep up with any generation's R&B 28 years in. Buried in that sentence is a premise: a woman singer reaching a certain age and still being praised by critics is itself news. No one writes that a man on his fifteenth album "can still keep up."

I am not saying the list is malicious. Billboard's editors clearly know her music, and the Hidden Gems are well chosen. The problem is not malice, it is structure: a ranking written from inside the industry will naturally absorb the part of her history where she fought the industry into a story where the industry recognized her all along. Butterfly is her emancipation, but after the emancipation the right to explain went back where it came from.

One line in the piece is worth stopping on. In her memoir Carey wrote that during Butterfly "the sexy-ass songs started pouring out." The songs poured out on their own. Ranking the album first is correct. The reason it is ranked first and the reason the songs poured out are not the same reason.
