糖果屋里没有受害者No Victims in the Candy House

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
童话把女人写成吃人的怪物，忘了是谁先把她关进炉子
Fairy tales make women the monsters, forgetting who locked them in the oven first.

一个 D&D 模组里，最邪恶的角色是女巫 Uncle Nibblecheek，她把小孩骗进糖果屋。而真正卖掉自己亲弟弟换糖果的，是开糖果店的女商人 Edith Applegarth。作者觉得她「悔改」的剧情不可信，于是把她改成不悔改、必须被逮捕或赶出镇子的经典反派。

这一步改得很诚实。原本的童话里，女巫是唯一的恶；改编之后，恶被分给了两个女人——一个骗小孩，一个卖弟弟。而救人的是男面包师 Fill，他收留了被卖的男孩 Asger，组成「奇怪的拼凑家庭」。故事的结构没有变，只是恶的份额被重新分配，仍然落在女性角色身上。

这不是在说一个桌游模组有什么罪。它是个可爱的模组，几个小时的轻松冒险，巧克力化的鸡蛇兽、能闷死人的水果软糖门垫，这些设计确实有趣。问题是：为什么「可爱的童话」这个类型，默认女人就是那个要吃人的东西？

Hansel and Gretel 的原型里，把两个孩子扔进森林的是继母，糖果屋里的女巫是第二个要吃掉他们的女人。两个女性角色，一个负责遗弃，一个负责吞食。父亲全程无辜。这套叙事被讲了几百年，现在被做进游戏，再被评论者改成「女商人卖弟弟」，恶还是女人的，救赎还是男人的。

作者说 Edith 悔改不可信——对，一个卖过弟弟的人不该被信任。但为什么 Fill 这个「前强盗、偷了龙蛋」的人，就可以被写成好人、被写成父亲？同一个模组里，男人的过去可以被原谅，女人的过去必须被清算。这不是模组的问题，这是童话类型本身的语法。

我没有说这个模组不该玩。我说的是：当你在糖果屋里打死女巫、救出小龙、把 Edith 赶出镇子的时候，你正在重演一套很旧的分配——女人承担恶，男人承担救赎，孩子承担被救。这套分配在游戏里没有代价，在游戏外有。

下次跑这个模组，可以让 Edith 不被逮捕，让她说出她为什么卖弟弟——不是悔改，是解释。让玩家自己判断。这比把她赶出镇子，更像一个冒险。

In a D&D module, the most evil character is a hag named Uncle Nibblecheek, who lures children into a candy cottage. The one who actually sells her own brother for candy is Edith Applegarth, a woman who runs a confectionery. The reviewer finds Edith's "repentance" unconvincing and rewrites her into a classic fairy-tale villain who does not repent and must be arrested or run out of town.

That rewrite is honest. In the original tale, the witch is the only evil; after the rewrite, evil is split between two women — one who tricks children, one who sells her brother. The one who rescues is Fill, the male baker, who takes in the sold boy Asger and forms an "odd found family." The structure hasn't changed. Only the share of evil has been redistributed, and it still lands on the women.

This is not a claim that a tabletop module is a crime. It's a cute module, a few hours of light adventure, chocolate-covered cockatrices and a fruit-leather doormat that tries to smother you. The designs are genuinely fun. The question is: why does the "cozy fairy tale" genre default to woman-as-thing-that-eats-people?

In the Hansel and Gretel prototype, the stepmother throws the children into the forest, and the witch in the candy house is the second woman who wants to eat them. Two female characters, one for abandonment, one for consumption. The father is innocent throughout. This narrative has been told for centuries, now it's built into a game, and now the reviewer rewrites it as "a woman sells her brother" — evil is still the woman's, redemption is still the man's.

The reviewer says Edith's repentance is unconvincing — right, someone who sold her brother shouldn't be trusted. But why can Fill, a "former brigand who stole a dragon egg," be written as a good man, as a father? In the same module, a man's past can be forgiven, a woman's past must be settled. This isn't the module's problem. It's the grammar of the fairy-tale genre itself.

I'm not saying don't play this module. I'm saying: when you kill the hag in the candy house, rescue the wyrmling, and run Edith out of town, you are reenacting a very old distribution — women carry the evil, men carry the rescue, children carry being rescued. That distribution costs nothing in the game. It costs something outside it.

Next time you run this module, let Edith not be arrested. Let her explain why she sold her brother — not repent, explain. Let the players decide. That's more of an adventure than running her out of town.
