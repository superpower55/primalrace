用“去污名化”掩盖的结构性暴力Structural Violence Masked as 'Destigmatization'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-05 § 链接
当叙事为了保护弱势群体的形象而软化真相，受害者就成了被牺牲的共谋。
When narratives soften truth to protect an image, the victims become the collateral of a complicit system.

Mind 首席执行官的这次坦白，实际上揭露了一场典型的 cultural violence。长期以来，心理健康组织为了所谓的“去污名化” (destigmatization)，在认知入口上构建了一套极其单一的叙事：精神疾病患者是纯粹的受害者，暴力是极少数的偏差。这套叙事在表面上是人道主义的，但其本质是通过“软化真相” (softening the truth) 来夺取解释权，将潜在的直接暴力风险从公共讨论中抹除。

这种操作逻辑极其危险。当慈善机构为了避免“污名”而选择性失明时，她们实际上在 structural 层面上参与了一场共谋。她们通过掩盖风险来维持一个“温情”的组织形象，而代价是让真实的潜在受害者暴露在没有预警的危险之中。在这种博弈中，Mind 等组织追求的是一个“假.最优解”：通过扮演道德高地上的保护者来获得社会声誉，而将公共安全这个变量直接从方程式中剔除了。

我们要追问的是，这种对“污名”的恐惧究竟在保护谁？如果一个系统因为害怕被贴标签而拒绝承认暴力风险，那么这个系统本身就成了暴力的共谋者。真正的公正表达 (Just Expressions) 应该是：承认精神疾病患者的权利，同时诚实地面对其可能带来的暴力风险，并以此推动医疗资源的实际落地，而不是用一套 PR 话术把风险“扫到地毯下面”。

好新闻是 Mind 开始承认失败，但真正的胜利不在于一次圆桌会议的坦白，而在于能否将这种承认转化为具体的 structural 改变——比如增加病床数和强制干预机制。否则，这次坦白不过是另一种形式的武器化叙事：通过承认小错来掩盖系统性的失职。

The admission from Mind's CEO reveals a classic case of cultural violence. For years, mental health organizations have constructed a singular narrative at the cognitive entry point: that those with mental illnesses are purely victims and violence is a negligible deviation. While framed as humanitarian, this 'softening the truth' is an attempt to monopolize the interpretation of reality, erasing the risk of direct violence from public discourse.

This logic is perilous. When charities choose selective blindness to avoid 'stigma,' they enter a state of complicity at the structural level. They maintain a 'compassionate' organizational image while leaving actual potential victims exposed to unwarned danger. In this game, these organizations pursued a 'fake optimal expression': playing the role of the moral protector to gain social capital, while simply deleting public safety from the equation.

We must ask: who is this fear of 'stigma' actually protecting? A system that refuses to acknowledge risk for fear of labels is itself a collaborator in violence. Just Expressions require acknowledging the rights of the mentally ill while honestly addressing the risk of violence to drive actual resource allocation, rather than sweeping the truth under the rug with PR rhetoric.

It is a small win that Mind admits its failure, but true victory lies not in a roundtable confession, but in translating this into structural change—such as increasing hospital beds and assertive outreach. Otherwise, this admission is just another weaponized narrative: admitting a minor lapse to mask a systemic failure.
