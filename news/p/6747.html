输掉参议院也要清理门户They'd Rather Lose Than Be Told

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要密歇根州联邦众议员海莉·史蒂文斯在参议院选举前数日发表声明，几乎指称民主党候选人阿卜杜勒·埃尔-赛义德是反犹分子，并为他周末同台助选列出条件。埃尔-赛义德此前已与一则由外部政治行动委员会制作、把国内物价与对以色列军援挂钩的广告切割。报道称，中间派民主党人视埃尔-赛义德反对以色列加沙军事行动为生存威胁，而马里斯特民调显示密歇根州95%的民主党选民支持他。
The newsDays before the election, Michigan Rep. Haley Stevens all but called Democratic Senate candidate Abdul El-Sayed an antisemite and set conditions for campaigning with him. El-Sayed had already distanced himself from an outside ad tying domestic prices to U.S. military aid to Israel. Centrist Democrats see his opposition to Israel's war on Gaza as an existential threat; a Marist poll found 95 percent of Michigan Democrats back him.
他们怕的不是输，是选民不听话。
They don't fear losing. They fear voters who won't obey.

海莉·史蒂文斯不是失控，她是在执行纪律。距离大选几天，她几乎把本党提名人称作反犹分子，还替密歇根的黑人和犹太社群发言——她是白人新教徒，没人选过她当这两个社群的发言人。这看起来像情绪失控，实际是共谋者理论的标准动作：回报不在选民那里，在捐款人和党的右翼那里。

埃尔-赛义德被围攻的罪名不是政策。报道写得很清楚，他在国内议题上和伊丽莎白·沃伦差不了多少。真正的问题是他在加沙问题上不肯闭嘴。一个候选人只要在这一点上不服从，就必须被公开处刑，好让所有后来者看见代价。这是元暴力在党内的一次演示：解释权不换手，讨论的边界就不动。

有意思的是垫脚石的位置。那则广告不是埃尔-赛义德做的，也不是他的团队发的，是外部政治行动委员会的东西。他周四已经做了和解姿态，和广告切割。对中间派来说这还不够——他们拿这个切割当杠杆，继续加码。你退一步，他们就再要一步，因为目的从来不是那则广告，是让你在投票前最后一刻跪着。

史蒂文斯的未婚夫贾森·特基宣布要投共和党人迈克·罗杰斯，还告诉TMZ，如果埃尔-赛义德不答应她那一串条件，她可能跟着一起投。这条信息比任何声明都诚实：党内纪律和击败共和党，在他们那里是两件事，而且前者优先。

然后同一批人转头指责特朗普和罗杰斯对埃尔-赛义德的攻击是伊斯兰恐惧症。丹·戈德曼这么说了，科里·布克这么说了，佩洛西在播客上把反对以色列作为种族隔离族裔国家称作「错误」之后几天，又呼吁民主党人投埃尔-赛义德。自己人先泼脏水，再谴责别人泼脏水，这不叫反歧视，这叫抢占道德位置。

马里斯特10月21日的民调：密歇根州95%的民主党选民支持埃尔-赛义德。州一级没有分裂，分裂在更高处，在那些靠以色列议题维持捐款管道的人那里。选民已经走了，捐款阶层还没走，于是党选择站在捐款阶层那一边，代价由候选人承担。

报道说，如果民主党能不带他赢下参议院，那更好。这句话把整件事说穿了：他们宁可输掉一个席位，也不肯让一个不听话的穆斯林进参议院。这不是策略失误，这是定价——他们给党的纪律定的价，高于给选民定的价。

埃尔-赛义德周五的声明只谈 affordability、击败罗杰斯、把参议院席位留在民主党手里。他把史蒂文斯晾在一边，这是聪明的。但报道没有写的是：这场围剿里，谁为那些被当作武器的「黑人社群」和「犹太社群」说过一句话？他们被拿来当理由，从头到尾没有出场。

Haley Stevens isn't melting down. She's enforcing discipline. Days before the election, she all but called her party's own Senate nominee an antisemite and appointed herself spokeswoman for Michigan's Black and Jewish communities — she is a white Protestant, and nobody elected her to speak for either. This looks like a tantrum. It's the Co-conspirators Theory in its standard form: the payoff isn't with voters, it's with donors and the party's right wing.

El-Sayed's crime isn't policy. The report is clear that on domestic issues he's not far from Elizabeth Warren. The problem is that he won't shut up about Gaza. A candidate who disobeys on this one point has to be publicly made an example of, so that everyone watching learns the price. This is meta-violence staged inside a party: until the interpretive monopoly changes hands, the boundary of debate doesn't move.

The stepping stone is the interesting part. The ad wasn't El-Sayed's — it came from an outside PAC. On Thursday the campaign made the conciliatory gesture of distancing itself. For the centrists that still wasn't enough; they used the distancing as leverage and pushed for more. You give an inch, they demand the next one, because the point was never the ad. The point was to make him kneel in the final days.

Stevens's fiancé, Jason Turkish, announced he'd vote for the Republican, Mike Rogers, and told TMZ that if El-Sayed didn't meet her list of demands, she might join him. That tells you more than any statement: party discipline and beating Republicans are two separate things to these people, and discipline comes first.

The same Democrats then turn around and call Trump's and Rogers's attacks on El-Sayed Islamophobia. Dan Goldman said it. Cory Booker said it. Pelosi, days after calling opposition to Israel as an apartheid ethnostate 「more than concerning, it's wrong」 on a podcast, urged Democrats to vote for El-Sayed. Smear your own candidate, then denounce others for smearing him — that isn't anti-bigotry, it's grabbing the moral high ground after you've already thrown the mud.

A Marist poll released October 21 found 95 percent of Michigan Democrats support El-Sayed. There is no split at the state level. The split is higher up, among people whose fundraising depends on this issue. Voters have moved; the donor class hasn't; so the party stands with the donor class and sends the candidate the bill.

The report says that if Democrats can win the Senate without him, all the better. That sentence says it all: they would rather lose a seat than seat a disobedient Muslim. This isn't a strategic error. It's a price list — party discipline valued above voters.

El-Sayed's Friday statement stuck to affordability, beating Rogers, keeping the seat blue. Leaving Stevens to flail was smart. What the report doesn't say is who in this siege ever spoke for the Black and Jewish communities it keeps invoking. They were used as the reason. They never appear.
