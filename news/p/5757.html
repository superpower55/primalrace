两个同名候选人，一场选票的魔术Two Dan Sullivans and a Ballot Trick

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
选票被同名者分流时，没人问选民为什么这么好骗。
When a name splits the vote, nobody asks why voters are so easy to fool.

阿拉斯加这场闹剧的机制简单得可笑：找一个也叫 Dan Sullivan 的退休老人进选票，让不支持现任参议员 Dan Sullivan 的人投错票。纽约时报挖出了招募过程、文件和短信，Peltola 的竞选团队否认参与，DSCC 一起被写进了报道里。民主党的操作者显然算过一笔账：选民看名字投票，不看人。这笔账如果成立，那问题就不在谁在作弊，而在于这个系统的输入端本来就设计成可以被名字糊弄的。

然后共和党这边开始表演。州长 Dunleavy 下令调查，Trump 要求把 Peltola 和那个“假 Dan Sullivan”从选票上拿掉，还说这是“最高级别的选民欺诈”，要起诉。Murkowski 的声明最有意思，她说 ballot-stacking 不该存在于选举里，然后停在那里——她没有跟着 Trump 要求取消资格或刑事调查，因为前一天她刚 endorsement 了一个民主党州长候选人。同一套规则，用在不同的人身上，松紧不一样。这不是原则，这是站位。

同一份 live 报道里还有另一条：联邦法官 David Carter 驳回了司法部要求加州隔离 2 万名“非公民”选票的请求，司法部只给了 48 小时，拒绝说明方法，洛杉矶方面初步发现名单不准确。Carter 写的是，联邦政府从未在选举前夕试图胁迫州政府放弃自己的宪法程序。这条和阿拉斯加那条放在一起看，才是完整的图景：一边是“有人在操纵选票”的指控被拿来当武器，一边是真正的选票被要求隔离时被法官挡下。哪一边在动结构层，哪一边只是在动嘴，很清楚。

这里没有人在为选民说话。Peltola 的团队被指招募同名候选人，共和党要调查要起诉，Trump 要清空选票，Murkowski 要“凭实力赢”——每一方都在用“选举诚信”这个词，每一方都在挑对自己有利的那一半事实。选民在这套叙事里只是一个被分流的数字，一个可以被同名者骗走的票仓。报道里没有出现任何一个阿拉斯加选民的名字，没有人被问过他们为什么投错。

真正被削掉的东西在这里：如果选民真的会因为两个 Dan Sullivan 而投错票，那这个选举系统的输入端就是坏的；如果选民不会，那这场调查和起诉就是一场给支持者看的戏。两种情况下，被讨论的都不是选民本身。

The mechanism in Alaska is laughably simple: put a retiree also named Dan Sullivan on the ballot so that people who don't support the incumbent senator Dan Sullivan vote for the wrong man. The New York Times dug up the recruitment effort, documents and text messages. Peltola's campaign denies involvement; the DSCC is named in the reporting alongside her officials. The Democratic operatives clearly did the math: voters read names, not people. If that math holds, the problem isn't who cheated. It's that the input end of this system was built to be fooled by a name.

Then the Republican side performs. Governor Dunleavy orders an investigation. Trump demands that Peltola and the "FAKE Dan Sullivan" be removed from the ballot, calls it "voter fraud at the highest level," and says they should be prosecuted. Murkowski's statement is the most telling: ballot-stacking has no place in elections, she says, and then stops—no disqualification, no criminal inquiry, because just yesterday she endorsed a Democratic candidate for governor. Same rule, different tightness depending on who it's applied to. That's not principle. That's position.

The same live report carries another item: federal judge David Carter refused to order California to segregate 20,000 ballots the Justice Department claimed were cast by non-citizens. The DOJ gave officials 48 hours, refused to explain its methodology, and Los Angeles found preliminary indicators the list was inaccurate. Carter wrote that never before has the federal government, on the eve of an election, tried to coerce a state into abandoning its own constitutional procedures. Put the two stories together and the picture is complete: on one side, an accusation of ballot manipulation is being weaponized; on the other, actual ballots are being ordered segregated and a judge blocks it. Which side is moving the structural layer, and which is just moving its mouth, is not hard to see.

Nobody here is speaking for voters. Peltola's team is accused of recruiting a namesake. Republicans want an investigation and a prosecution. Trump wants the ballot cleared. Murkowski wants people to "run and win on your merits." Every side uses the phrase "election integrity," and every side picks the half of the facts that suits it. The voter in this narrative is a number to be split, a pile of ballots that a shared name can peel away. Not one Alaska voter is named in the report. Nobody asked them why they might have voted wrong.

What actually gets shaved off is this: if voters really can be fooled by two Dan Sullivans, the input end of the system is broken. If they can't, then the investigation and the prosecution are a show for the base. Either way, the voters themselves are not what's being discussed.
