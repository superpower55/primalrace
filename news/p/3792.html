电影节的“健康”与叙事权的殖民The 'Health' of Cinema and the Colonization of Narrative

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-13 § 链接
所谓产业的“健康”，往往是权力中心对边缘地带的一次温情审计。
Industry 'health' is often just a benevolent audit conducted by the center upon the periphery.

爱丁堡电影节在经历崩溃与重生后，试图用“苏格兰电影产业处于健康状态”来定义自己的回归。这种叙事非常典型：将一个地区的文化产出，量化为 Sundance 的入选数量、Screen Scotland 的资金拨付以及 BFI 基金管理者的职位变动。这根本不是在讨论电影，而是在讨论一种“合格的工业化表达”。

在这种所谓的“健康”中，我们看到的是一种极其隐蔽的 structural violence。苏格兰电影人被鼓励去追求“能够在全球市场流通”的表达，这意味着他们必须在潜意识中内化伦敦乃至好莱坞的审美标准。当制片人 Ken Petrie 焦虑地询问如何让作品“travel internationally”时，他实际上在承认：真正的定价权不在爱丁堡，而是在那个他必须去“探寻”的 global marketplace。这种对认知入口的让渡，让本土表达变成了某种为了获得大厂认可而进行的“表演”。

最讽刺的是，那些被赞誉为“hyperlocal”的纪录片反而获得了好评。在元暴力的逻辑里，边缘地带的真实只有在被定义为“异域风情”或“局部样本”时，才会被权力中心接纳。这种接纳不是平等的对话，而是一种 condescending 的审视。苏格兰电影的“成功”，在很大程度上取决于它在多大程度上符合一个“充满活力且听话的地区产业”这一设定。

真正的 good_news 不应该是多少部电影入选了国际电影节，而应该是创作者是否拿回了定义“什么是好电影”的解释权。如果所谓的健康是指在伦敦定义的价值链中占据一个舒适的生态位，那么这种健康本身就是一种共谋。它用一种温情的 PR 叙事，掩盖了文化表达被资本和地理中心主义殖民的事实。

The Edinburgh International Film Festival, emerging from collapse and rebirth, attempts to frame its return through the 'rude health' of the Scottish film industry. This narrative is classic: quantifying cultural output via Sundance selections, Screen Scotland funding, and BFI appointments. It is not a discussion about cinema, but about the 'qualified industrialization of expression.'

Within this 'health' lies a subtle structural violence. Scottish filmmakers are encouraged to seek expressions that 'travel internationally,' which effectively means internalizing the aesthetic standards of London or Hollywood. When producer Ken Petrie frets over how to make work that appeals to the 'global marketplace,' he admits that the actual pricing power resides elsewhere. This surrender of cognitive entry points turns local expression into a performance designed to win approval from the hegemony.

Ironically, the most 'hyperlocal' documentaries receive the most admiration. Under the logic of meta-violence, the truth of the periphery is only accepted by the center when it is framed as 'exotic' or a 'local specimen.' This is not a peer-to-peer dialogue, but a condescending gaze. The 'success' of Scottish cinema depends on how well it fits the role of a 'vibrant yet compliant regional industry.'

True good_news would not be the number of films in a festival, but whether creators have reclaimed the power to define 'what makes a good film.' If 'health' means occupying a comfortable niche within a value chain defined by London, then this health is a form of complicity. It uses a warm PR narrative to mask the fact that cultural expression is being colonized by capital and geographic centrism.
