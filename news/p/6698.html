又一个被陷害的女人上路了Another Framed Woman on the Run

其他 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要英国演员 Keeley Hawes 将主演 Sky 六集惊悚剧《Prisoner》第二季，饰演因谋杀被定罪的心理学家 Regan Weaver。第一季中饰演监狱押运官 Amber Todd 的 Izuka Hoyle 回归，新一季里 Todd 被陷害，被迫与 Weaver 一起逃亡。该剧由 Matt Charman 创作，第二季仍由他与 Sophie Petzal 共同编剧。
The newsBritish actress Keeley Hawes will lead season two of Sky's six-part thriller Prisoner as Dr. Regan Weaver, a psychologist convicted of murder. Izuka Hoyle returns as prison transport officer Amber Todd, who is framed and forced to go on the run with Weaver. Matt Charman created the series and writes season two with Sophie Petzal.
被陷害是女性角色的入场券，不是意外
Being framed is the entry ticket for a female character, not an accident.

《Prisoner》第二季的设定是：一个女押运官被陷害，被迫和一个女囚犯一起逃亡，追她们的是罪犯、政府和 rogue police。两个女人，一个被定罪，一个被构陷，都被国家机器和暴力网络同时追捕。这个配置本身值得看一眼，因为它是少数不靠男人推动剧情的女性双主角结构。

但「被陷害」这个设定值得多想一步。Regan Weaver 是「brilliant psychologist and notorious prisoner」，聪明、危险、藏着几十年的秘密。编剧 Charman 说她「absolutely terrifying」。一个女性角色被写成让另一个女性角色恐惧的存在，这在电视剧里不算常见，但「terrifying」这个词在男性角色身上通常翻译成「复杂」，在女性角色身上往往翻译成「疯」。Weaver 的危险是被写成能力，还是被写成病理，第二季播出来才知道。

Amber Todd 的线索更清楚：第一季她「went to hell and back」，第二季她「scarred by past events」，struggling to move on and repair her fractured family。一个女性角色带着创伤、家庭破裂、被陷害，然后和一个危险女人建立「unexpected bond」。这套叙事在屏幕上出现过很多次，它的功能是让观众对女性的痛苦产生共情，同时把这种痛苦包装成戏剧张力。痛苦被消费，但不被追问来源。

真正的问题不在剧本，在产业。Sky 在第一季成为年度最 watched Sky Original dramas 之后「moved quickly to back the show with an early second-season pick-up」。决定续订的是收视数据，不是叙事质量。Hawes 是英国电视的熟面孔，The Bodyguard、Line of Duty、The Assassin，她的名字本身就是收视保障。一个女性主导的惊悚剧被快速续订，这是好事，但它说明的是市场对「女性+暴力+创伤」这个组合的接受度，不是这个组合本身被认真对待了。

报道里没有写的是：这部剧的创作者、showrunner、导演里，女性占了多少。Charman 是 creator，Petzal 是 deputy showrunner，Anthony Philipson 和 Kasia Adamik 分集执导，执行制片人里 Foz Allan、Otto Bathurst、Adrian Sturges 都是男性。一个关于两个女人逃亡的故事，解释权主要在男人手里。这不是说男人写不好女人，是说当「女性创伤」成为卖点的时候，谁在定价、谁在收账，值得问一句。

第二季会在 Sky 播出，NBCUniversal 负责国际销售，威尔士政府通过 Creative Wales 提供了支持。公共资金进入一个商业惊悚剧，这本身不新鲜，但「女性主导」的标签经常在这种时候被拿来当补贴的理由。标签是真的，钱也是真的，只是这两件事之间的关系没人核对。

Hawes 演的 Weaver 藏着「secrets she has spent decades hiding」。这句话可以是一部好剧的起点，也可以是一个女性角色被写成谜题的终点。区别在于，那些秘密最后是被揭开来理解她，还是被揭开来消费她。

The setup for Prisoner season two: a female prison transport officer is framed and forced to go on the run with a female prisoner, hunted by criminals, governments and rogue police. Two women, one convicted, one set up, both chased by the state and by violence at once. That configuration is worth a look, because it's one of the few female-led two-hander structures that doesn't run on a man.

But the framing device deserves a second thought. Regan Weaver is a "brilliant psychologist and notorious prisoner" — smart, dangerous, carrying decades of secrets. Charman calls her "absolutely terrifying." A female character written as something another female character fears is not common on TV, but "terrifying" usually translates to "complex" when the character is male, and to "unhinged" when the character is female. Whether Weaver's danger is written as capability or as pathology, we won't know until season two airs.

Amber Todd's thread is clearer: in season one she "went to hell and back," and in season two she's "scarred by past events," struggling to move on and repair her fractured family. A female character carrying trauma, a broken family, a frame-up, and then an "unexpected bond" with a dangerous woman. This narrative has appeared on screen many times. Its function is to make the audience feel for the woman's pain while packaging that pain as dramatic tension. The pain gets consumed, not interrogated.

The real question isn't the script, it's the industry. After season one became one of the year's most-watched Sky Original dramas, Sky "moved quickly to back the show with an early second-season pick-up." What decided the renewal was ratings data, not narrative quality. Hawes is a familiar face on British television — The Bodyguard, Line of Duty, The Assassin — and her name alone is a ratings guarantee. A female-led thriller getting a fast renewal is good, but what it demonstrates is the market's appetite for the "women + violence + trauma" combination, not that the combination is being taken seriously.

What the report doesn't say: how many women are among the creators, showrunners and directors. Charman is creator, Petzal is deputy showrunner, Philipson and Adamik direct episodes, and the executive producers — Foz Allan, Otto Bathurst, Adrian Sturges — are men. A story about two women on the run, with the interpretative authority mostly in men's hands. This isn't to say men can't write women. It's to say that when "female trauma" becomes the selling point, who prices it and who collects is worth asking.

Season two will air on Sky, with NBCUniversal handling international sales, and the production received support from the Welsh Government via Creative Wales. Public money going into a commercial thriller is nothing new, but "female-led" labels often get used as justification for subsidies at moments like this. The label is real, the money is real, and nobody checks the relationship between the two.

Hawes's Weaver is hiding "secrets she has spent decades hiding." That line can be the start of a good show, or the endpoint of a female character written as a puzzle. The difference is whether the secrets are finally opened to understand her, or opened to consume her.
