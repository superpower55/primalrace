医生上场，尺子没换Doctors on the Ballot, Same Ruler

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
换了穿白大褂的人，没换量人的尺子。
New white coats, same ruler measuring who counts.

一批公共卫生出身的民主党医生正在竞选国会议员，报道把这写成对特朗普的反击。可加尔通的公式里，暴力是潜在与现实之间的差额，这批人的竞选纲领里，差额被缩小了吗？报道自己给出的答案不太乐观：南卡罗来纳州的儿科医生安德鲁斯把“全民医保”称作“过时、不切实际的政策”。她反对的正是那个能把医疗从商品变回权利的入口。

这就是结构层的暴力被换上了一张更体面的脸。美国医疗体系贵得离谱，报道说它是高收入国家里最贵的之一，可支付能力被这些候选人包装成“患者与价格平台”——价格是关键词，患者是装饰。谁付不起，谁就被差额吃掉，而竞选的主轴是让这个差额看起来像是可以谈判的。

真正在动的是文化层。报道里费格尔-丁说，公共卫生不是在猪圈里弄脏手，而是“给自己打疫苗对抗政治”。这句话漂亮，也暴露了共谋的位置：他们想免疫于政治，可医疗从来不是政治之外的东西，它一直是分配。把“科学”举成旗帜，等于说解释权归我们，至于资源怎么分，那是另一回事。

更值得看的是谁在付代价，而报道没写。近千例麻疹、环孢子虫、保费上涨，这些是具体的人的身体和账单。报道写了病例数，没写谁家的孩子没打上疫苗、谁因为保费放弃了复诊。医生的白大褂在竞选广告里是可信度的符号，在诊室里才是责任。符号可以拿去换选票，责任换不了。

报道说这可能是纪录数量的一批医生进入国会，还说这会是“新一代医生立法者”。但医生进国会不是新鲜事，现在国会里多数医生是共和党人。换党籍不换职业，说明这个位置一直有人坐，坐的人一直在用“专业”给分配方式背书。

结尾那根刺在这里：如果这批人赢了，医疗的定价权会换手吗？报道里没有一个人说要把定价权从保险公司和药企手里拿走。他们说的是“让我们谈谈为什么上医学院”——谈初心，不谈账本。

A wave of public-health professionals is running for Congress, and the story frames it as a backlash to Trump. Run it through Galtung's formula: violence is the gap between potential and actual. Does this slate close that gap? The report answers itself: in South Carolina, paediatrician Annie Andrews calls Medicare for All an "outdated, non-pragmatic policy." She is running against the one entrance that would turn care from a commodity back into a right.

That is structural violence wearing a better face. US healthcare is among the costliest in the high-income world, the report says, and affordability gets packaged as a "Patients and Prices Platform" — prices are the keyword, patients the decoration. Whoever cannot pay is eaten by the gap, and the campaign's job is to make that gap look negotiable.

The cultural layer is where the real motion is. Eric Feigl-Ding says public health is not getting dirty in the pigsty but "immunising ourselves against politics." A neat line, and a confession: they want immunity from politics, but medicine was never outside politics — it is distribution. Hoisting "science" as a banner says the interpretive authority is ours; how resources get divided is another matter.

More telling is who pays, which the report leaves out. Nearly a thousand measles cases, a diarrhoea-causing parasite, rising premiums — these are bodies and bills. The report counts cases; it does not name the child who missed a vaccine or the patient who skipped a follow-up because of the premium. The white coat is a credibility symbol in an ad and a liability in a clinic. Symbols buy votes; liabilities do not.

The report says a record number of physicians could enter Congress, a "new generation of physician-lawmakers." But doctors in Congress are not new — most of them are Republicans right now. A party switch is not a profession switch. Someone has always occupied that seat, and that someone has always used "expertise" to bless the way things are divided.

Here is the thorn: if this slate wins, does the pricing power change hands? Not one person in the report says they will take it from insurers and drugmakers. They say, let's talk about why we went to medical school — about calling, not about the ledger.
