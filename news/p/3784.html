在“被霸凌”的快感中确认特权The Pleasure of Being 'Bullied' as a Privilege Check

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-13 § 链接
当强者将受虐体验作为一种表演性消费时，这并非共情，而是特权的再次确认。
When the powerful consume the experience of being oppressed as entertainment, it is not empathy, but a reaffirmation of privilege.

Andrew Rannells 在采访中提到，在电影中被 Allison Janney 这种级别的演员“推来推去”和“霸凌”是一件很有趣的事。这种“有趣”极其精准地揭示了什么叫特权者的游戏：一个在好莱坞拥有足够话语权、经济独立且处于社会优势地位的男性，可以将“被欺负”转化为一种新鲜的审美体验。对他而言，这不是真实的 structural violence，而是一场安全的、有剧本的 role-play。

真正的被霸凌者在权力结构中面对的是主体性的死亡，而 Rannells 面对的是一种“体验升级”。他能坦然地将这种权力不对等称为“fun”，是因为他深知在拍摄结束的那一刻，他依然是那个掌控自己生活、拒绝雇佣 PA 以维持控制权的成功人士。这种对“弱势位面”的短暂涉猎，本质上是对自身强大地位的一种反向确认——我强大到可以消费“弱小”的感觉。

更讽刺的是他对 Lena Dunham 的所谓“共情”。在读完 Dunham 揭露片场地狱的传记后，他的反应是“觉得自己没能成为更好的朋友”，紧接着却在开玩笑地问“为什么书里没多写写我”。这种情感转折如此之快，以至于所谓的愧疚更像是一种社交礼仪上的装饰。在男本位叙事中，即使是面对女性创作者的创伤，这种关注点依然迅速地回到了“我的存在感是否足够”这一存在性博弈上。

这种 razzle-dazzle 的表达风格掩盖了深层的 complicity。他习惯于在光鲜的身份政治中地毯式地获取利益，而将真实的暴力（如他提到的童年被神职人员侵害）转化为传记中的文学素材。当痛苦被定价并转化为成功人士的“深度”时，它就成了另一种形式的武器化表达。

Andrew Rannells mentions that being 'pushed around' and 'bullied' by an actress like Allison Janney was 'fun.' This 'fun' perfectly illustrates the game of the privileged: a successful man with significant discourse power and economic independence in Hollywood can transform the experience of being bullied into a fresh aesthetic consumption. For him, this isn't structural violence; it's a safe, scripted role-play.

Actual victims of bullying face the death of their subjectivity within a power structure, whereas Rannells experiences a 'sensory upgrade.' He can call this power imbalance 'fun' because he knows that the moment the cameras stop, he remains the successful individual who refuses to hire a PA just to maintain absolute control. This brief foray into the 'weak position' is essentially a reverse confirmation of his own dominance—he is powerful enough to consume the feeling of being powerless.

Even more ironic is his so-called empathy for Lena Dunham. After reading her memoir about the horrors of the set of 'Girls,' his reaction is a fleeting sense of guilt for not being a 'better friend,' immediately followed by a joke asking why she didn't talk about him more. This emotional pivot is so rapid that the guilt feels like a mere social ornament. Even when facing a woman's trauma, the focus quickly returns to the existential war of 'is my presence felt enough?'

This razzle-dazzle style of expression masks a deep complicity. He is adept at extracting benefits within the framework of identity politics, while transforming real violence—such as the sexual assault by a priest he mentioned—into literary material for a memoir. When pain is priced and converted into the 'depth' of a successful person, it becomes another form of weaponised expression.
