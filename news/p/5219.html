用酒精掩盖的“公平”与被遗忘的原初种族The 'Fairness' of Alcohol and the Forgotten Primal Race

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-23 § 链接
所谓的“公平”不过是男性特权在不同体育项目间的平移。
The so-called 'fairness' is merely the translation of masculine privilege across different sports.

Andy Burnham 所谓的“不公平” (unfair) 逻辑极其简单：橄榄球和板球能喝酒，为什么足球不能？这在本质上是一场典型的男性共谋。在这种叙事里，球迷被简化为一种统一的、拥有某种“权利”的身份，而这种权利的内核是男性在公共空间通过酒精释放攻击性并将其合法化的特权。

有趣的是，警察警告这种举动是“鲁莽的” (rash)，理由是足球场上的暴力和犯罪行为远超其他体育项目。这恰恰揭示了足球场作为一种 masculine-centric narrative 的极端性：它不仅是竞技，更是男性通过酒精和群体狂热确认自身力量、实施直接暴力的博弈场。此时，Burnham 试图通过“对齐”其他项目来消除所谓的“歧视”，实际上是在试图将一种结构性的暴力潜能 (Potential) 重新合法化。

而整篇新闻最讽刺的刺在最后一段：女子超级联赛 (WSL) 的球迷早已被允许喝酒，且几乎所有俱乐部都采用了这一选项，因为女性比赛中极少出现群体骚乱。这证明了禁酒令的逻辑从来不是关于“酒精”本身，而是关于“谁在喝酒”。

在元暴力的逻辑下，女性被视为天然的“安静者”和“非威胁者”，因此她们获得了这种“特权”；而男性则在通过争取这种特权来确认自己的主体性。当男性要求同样的权利时，他们追求的不是公正的表达 (Just Expressions)，而是要求系统承认他们拥有在公共空间通过酒精维持那种“狂暴表达”的权力。这根本不是什么人权，而是一场关于谁能定义“文明看球”的权力博弈。

Andy Burnham's logic of 'unfairness' is simplistic: if rugby and cricket fans can drink, why can't football fans? This is a textbook case of masculine complicity. In this narrative, supporters are reduced to a uniform identity claiming a 'right'—a right whose core is the legitimization of masculine aggression in public spaces through alcohol.

It is telling that the police warn this move is 'rash,' citing the disproportionate levels of violence and crime in football compared to other sports. This reveals the football stadium as an extreme site of masculine-centric narrative: it is not just a game, but a gaming field where men use alcohol and collective frenzy to confirm their power and exercise direct violence. Burnham's attempt to remove 'discrimination' by aligning with other sports is, in fact, an attempt to re-legitimize a structural potential for violence.

The sharpest irony lies in the final paragraph: Women’s Super League (WSL) fans have already been allowed to drink, with almost all clubs opting in because women's matches typically see far fewer incidents of crowd trouble. This proves the ban was never about the alcohol itself, but about *who* was drinking it.

Under the logic of meta-violence, women are viewed as naturally 'quiet' and 'non-threatening,' hence they are granted this 'privilege.' Meanwhile, men seek the same right to validate their own subjectivity. When they demand this 'fairness,' they are not seeking Just Expressions, but demanding the system recognize their right to maintain 'violent expression' in public. This is not about human rights; it is a power struggle over who gets to define 'civilized' spectatorship.
