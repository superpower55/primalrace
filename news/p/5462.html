猕猴桃皮的“觉醒”与被制造的厌恶The 'Awakening' of Kiwifruit Skin and Manufactured Disgust

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-08 § 链接
审美偏好往往是文化暴力的内化，所谓的“不适”是规训的结果。
Aesthetic preference is often internalized cultural violence; 'discomfort' is the result of conditioning.

一个快60岁的男人在《卫报》上惊叹猕猴桃皮可以食用，这在表面上是一篇轻快的生活随笔，但在我看来，这是一次关于“审美规训”的微型样本。作者提到的那个词很关键：prejudice（偏见）。为什么我们会对某种生物表型——比如猕猴桃那层棕色、多毛的皮肤——产生本能的厌恶？

这本质上是一种 cultural violence。在主流的审美叙事中，食物被要求是“干净”的、符合某种特定视觉逻辑的。我们被训练去接受橙子、香蕉这种光滑的表型，而将“毛茸茸”定义为不洁或不可食。这种对表型的筛选和定义，实际上是元暴力（meta violence）在日常生活中的延伸：定义什么是“正确的”，什么是“应当被剔除的”。

最讽刺的是作者提到的“easy peelers”（易剥皮）标签。商业公司通过制造“剥皮”这个动作，将一种原本自然的食用方式转化为一种“faff”（繁琐的劳作），然后通过提供“易剥皮”产品来售卖便利。这是一种典型的武器化叙事：先通过文化暗示让你觉得皮是垃圾，再通过产品设计让你为“方便丢弃垃圾”而买单。当你习惯于剔除那层皮时，你剔除的不仅是纤维，更是你对生物本能的感知力。

好在这次“发现”缩小了 Potential 和 Actual 之间的差额——人们意识到自己被某种无形的审美墙限制了。但别指望这能变成一场革命，大多数人依然会像作者的母亲那样，在面对被打破的规训时产生生理性的 gag（干呕）。因为内化的规训比物理的墙更难拆除。

A man nearing 60 marveling in The Guardian that kiwifruit skin is edible seems like a light lifestyle piece, but to me, it is a micro-sample of 'aesthetic conditioning.' The author uses a key word: prejudice. Why do we feel an instinctive disgust toward a certain phenotype—like the brown, furry skin of a kiwi?

This is essentially cultural violence. In dominant aesthetic narratives, food is required to be 'clean' and fit a specific visual logic. We are trained to accept smooth phenotypes like oranges or bananas, while defining 'furry' as unclean or inedible. This screening of phenotypes is an extension of meta violence in daily life: defining what is 'correct' and what 'should be discarded.'

The most ironic part is the 'easy peelers' label. Corporations manufacture the act of 'peeling' as a necessary chore, then sell 'convenience' by offering products that make this removal easier. This is a weaponized narrative: first, use cultural cues to make you view the skin as waste, then sell you a solution to 'dispose of waste' more efficiently. When you habituate the act of peeling, you aren't just removing fiber; you are removing your biological intuition.

Fortunately, this 'discovery' narrows the gap between Potential and Actual—people realize they've been limited by an invisible aesthetic wall. But don't expect a revolution; most will still react like the author's mother, gagging when faced with a broken rule. Internalized conditioning is always harder to dismantle than a physical wall.
