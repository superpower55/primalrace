被抹除的密西西比：一种名为“食谱”的文化暴力The Erased Mississippi: Cultural Violence Masquerading as a Recipe

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
当一个地名成为禁忌，美食就变成了掩盖结构性歧视的PR外壳。
When a place name becomes a taboo, cuisine becomes a PR shell for structural discrimination.

这是一条典型的 cultural violence 样本。表面上是一篇 NYT 的美食指南，但评论区里那句 "No one will eat anything called Mississippi" 撕开了温情脉脉的遮羞布。当一个用户决定把 Mississippi Chicken 改名为 California Chicken 才能让人们接受时，这已经不是口味问题，而是一次精准的认知操纵。

密西西比这个词在北美叙事中被 weaponized 了。它被等同于贫穷、落后、种族隔离以及那个被殖民的 Primal Race 的苦难史。在这种 masculine-centric narrative 的主导下，密西西比的文化表达被定义为“低端”的。为了获得社会认同，人们必须通过“物理迁移”——在命名上将这道菜搬迁到代表中产、进步、阳光的加州，从而完成一次阶级洗白。

这种行为是典型的共谋。评论者通过抹除原产地的身份，在潜意识里完成了对一个特定地域及其背后人群的二次放逐。他们享受着密西西比的味觉遗产，却在文化层面上拒绝承认这个遗产的来源。这是一种极其卑劣的掠夺：掠夺其成果，抹除其主体性。

最讽刺的是，NYT 这种认知入口在发布这篇食谱时，依然在扮演一个“客观”的记录者。但当结构性暴力已经让一个地名变成禁忌时，单纯的记录如果没有对这种歧视的拆穿，本身就是一种共谋。

This is a textbook sample of cultural violence. On the surface, it's a NYT cooking guide, but the comment "No one will eat anything called Mississippi" rips off the mask of warmth. When a user decides to rename Mississippi Chicken to California Chicken just to make it palatable, it's no longer about taste—it's a precise manipulation of cognition.

In the North American narrative, the word 'Mississippi' has been weaponized. It is equated with poverty, backwardness, segregation, and the suffering history of the Primal Race. Under this masculine-centric narrative, the cultural expression of Mississippi is defined as 'low-class.' To gain social acceptance, people perform a 'physical migration' in naming—moving the dish to California, a symbol of the middle class and progress—to achieve a form of class laundering.

This behavior is pure complicity. By erasing the origin, the commenter subconsciously completes a second exile of a specific region and its people. They enjoy the gustatory legacy of Mississippi while culturally refusing to acknowledge its source. It is a vile form of plunder: stealing the fruit while erasing the subject.

The irony is that NYT, as a cognitive entry point, plays the role of an 'objective' recorder. But when structural violence has turned a place name into a taboo, mere recording without dismantling the discrimination is, in itself, a form of complicity.
