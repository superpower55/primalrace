应用商店是色情业的新皮条客The App Stores Are the Porn Industry's New Pimp

其他 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
四百个伪装成宠物相册的App，把强奸做成模板上架。
Four hundred pet-portrait apps, with rape sold as a template.

一个自称「宠物肖像」的 App，付四美元买 130 枚硬币，就能把仓鼠穿上沙滩裤。这是封面。翻过去，是「bedroom rape」和「threatening knife」——一个男人把刀架在女人脖子上。封面是给审核看的，内页是给用户看的。

这不是某个变态开发者的个人作品，是四百多个域名和 App 组成的网络。语言课、AI 健身教练、宠物相册，同一个壳子，同一套代码，同一批加密密钥，互相引用。注册时间集中在 2025 年底到今年春天，一周能上三十个。这是流水线，不是作坊。

入口在哪？Meta 的广告。成千上万条露骨深伪视频广告，投放者名字是 Draftmondcreldbro Duhustepre 这种乱码，主页零粉丝。点进去，跳转 Apple 和 Google 的应用商店，用 Apple Pay 和 Google Pay 付款。三个平台，一条完整的通道，每一段都抽成，每一段都说自己禁止这类内容。

这就是第三章说的东西：武器化不是偷偷摸摸的，武器化是走正规渠道的。认知入口被买下来了，支付入口被租下来了，分发入口被平台亲手审核通过了。苹果发言人说，审核时这些 App 是合规的，通过之后开发者把内容源换成了色情。翻译一下：我们审的是封面。

技术上更值得看一眼。每次下载，App 向服务器发一个请求，带上地区、语言、唯一标识，服务器回一句：给你看宠物，还是给你看强奸。研究人员说触发条件之一，是从 Meta 的广告点进来的。也就是说，这套系统知道谁是被广告骗来的，谁是碰巧下载的。它按人分发不同的现实。

第四章的共谋者理论在这里不需要点名某个人。共谋是一个结构：苹果审核通过，Google 上架，Meta 投放，收款的通道畅通，然后每一方在被问到的时候都能拿出一份政策文本。政策文本的功能，就是让「我们不允许」这句话和「它一直在发生」这件事同时成立。

被做成模板的是谁的身体？是用户上传的照片里的女人，是广告里真实儿童的脸，报道写到其中包含一位欧洲王室成员。她们没有同意，没有被告知，没有渠道申诉。她们的存在被拆成素材，定价成「钻石」，做成一次性消费品。

报道里有一个细节我停了一下：KeLing Technology 的邮箱、地址、登录页，在 WIRED 联系之后被抹掉了。不是否认，是擦除。这说明操作者清楚自己在做什么，也清楚哪一条线连着哪一块。

还有一句得单独拎出来。Meta 说，在 WIRED 提供域名之后，它处理了三千多条广告，其中三分之二此前已经删除；后来又追加处理了一万多个广告账户、四千多个商业主页、五千多个用户、一万五千个页面，禁止 209 个 App 投放。数字很大，姿态很足。但报道的最后一句是：WIRED 写完这篇稿子的时候，类似的广告和 App 还在继续发布。

所以这些数字不是战果，是账单。它告诉你这套系统处理违规的产能有多大，同时告诉你，产能再大也追不上注册速度。一个运营者，几百个一次性 App，被删了就再注册一个。封禁的成本落在平台账上，注册的成本落在运营者账上，而两边的成本根本不在一个量级。

真正被定价的，是那些照片里的女人。她们的脸被做成「bedroom rape」的模板，下载量三十五万次，很多 App 标注为适合所有年龄段。这个数字背后有多少未成年人用过，报道没写。没写，不等于没有。

An app that calls itself a "pet portrait" generator charges four dollars for 130 coins, and a hamster gets beach shorts. That is the cover. Turn the page and you find "bedroom rape" and "threatening knife"—a man holding a blade to a woman's neck. The cover is for the reviewers. The inside is for the users.

This is not one deviant developer. It is a network of more than 400 domains and apps. Language tuition, an AI workout coach, a pet portrait app: same shell, same code, same encryption keys, all referencing one another. Registrations cluster from late 2025 into this spring, around thirty a week at the peak. This is an assembly line, not a workshop.

The entrance is Meta's ads. Tens of thousands of explicit deepfake video ads, run under payer names like Draftmondcreldbro Duhustepre, on pages with zero followers. Click through and you land in Apple's and Google's app stores, paying with Apple Pay and Google Pay. Three platforms, one continuous channel, each taking its cut, each saying it prohibits this content.

This is what weaponization looks like in practice: not furtive, but fully licensed. The cognitive entrance is bought, the payment entrance is rented, the distribution entrance is personally approved by platform review. Apple's spokesperson says the apps complied when reviewed, and that after approval the developers swapped the content source for pornography. Translated: we reviewed the cover.

The engineering deserves a look. On every download the app calls its server with region, language, and a unique identifier, and the server answers: pet portraits or rape. One trigger, researchers say, is arriving from a Meta ad. The system knows who was lured and who stumbled in. It distributes a different reality to each.

The co-conspirator theory doesn't need a named villain here. The conspiracy is structural: Apple approves, Google lists, Meta advertises, the payment rails stay open, and every party can produce a policy document when asked. The function of the policy document is to let "we don't allow this" and "it keeps happening" be true at the same time.

Whose bodies become the templates? The women in the uploaded photos, the real children in the ads—WIRED reports one was a member of a European royal family. No consent, no notification, no channel to appeal. Their existence is broken into material, priced in diamonds, sold as a disposable product.

One detail made me stop: after WIRED reached out, KeLing Technology's email addresses, its address, its admin login page were scrubbed. Not denied. Erased. The operators know exactly what they are doing and exactly which thread connects to which block.

And one line needs pulling out. Meta says that after WIRED shared the domains it acted on more than 3,000 associated ads, two-thirds already removed; later it added 11,600 ad accounts, 4,700 business pages, 5,200 users, 15,000 pages, and blocked 209 apps from advertising. Big numbers, strong posture. The last line of the story: as WIRED finished reporting, similar ads and apps kept being published.

So those numbers are not a scoreboard. They are an invoice. They show how much enforcement capacity the system has, and they show that no amount of capacity outruns the registration rate. One operator, hundreds of disposable apps, delete one and register another. The cost of banning lands on the platforms; the cost of registering lands on the operator. The two are not in the same order of magnitude.

What is actually being priced is the women in those photos. Their faces become "bedroom rape" templates, downloaded more than 350,000 times, many of the apps rated suitable for all ages. How many minors used them, the report does not say. Not saying is not the same as not happening.
