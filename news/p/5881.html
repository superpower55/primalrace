他们说不知道，这就是共谋They Say They Didn't Know. That's the Conspiracy.

性别 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
每个男人都说自己只是路过，而路是他们一起铺的。
Every man says he just happened to be there. The road was paved by all of them.

四个男人站在温彻斯特刑事法庭上，说法几乎是同一个模子刻出来的：我不知道她被下了药。第一个说他只是个“想试试新东西”的年轻人，第二个说那“只是一次性约会”，她看起来“完全清醒”。他们各自都觉得自己无辜。无辜得如此整齐，整齐到像一场排练过的合唱。

报道里有一个细节，我读了三遍。桑德森-道尔说，他中途听到她说“不”，但他以为那是让他换个方式，而不是停下来。一个被绑着、蒙着眼、说不出话的女人发出“不”，在他耳朵里自动翻译成了“继续，换个姿势”。这不是误解。这是一个已经被训练好的接收器：在这个接收器里，女人的身体不生产语言，只生产信号，而信号由男人来解释。解释权在谁手里，谁就不需要听见。

菲利普·扬五十岁，对多项下药和性侵前妻的指控认罪。他在 Craigslist 的成人版块和换偶网站上登广告，把妻子“借”出去。注意这个动词——借。借的前提是有所有权。他把一个被下药、被捆绑、被蒙眼的女人挂到网上，像挂一件可以出借的物品。而这条链条的每一环都有人接单：骑摩托来的、用“dark stranger”当用户名的、不戴套因为“他特意说她不喜欢”的。没有这些接单人，这个“出借”生意一天也开不下去。

他们都说不知道。好，就算真的不知道她被下了药，那她绑着、蒙着眼、全程不说话，这些他们知道吗？他们知道。他们看见了，然后把它解释成“情侣在玩自己的 kink”。Macksoni 还搬出一个“庞大的绿帽社群”来给自己背书——好像只要有一群人都在干同一件事，这件事就自动变成了文化，而不是暴力。这正是共谋者理论最标准的运作方式：不需要谁下达命令，不需要谁心怀恶意，只需要每个人都在既定秩序里找到自己那个舒服的位置，然后说，我只是按规矩来的。规矩是谁定的？定规矩的人不在被告席上，他在每一句“她看起来没事”里。

还有一层，报道没有写，但它的缺席本身就是材料。乔安妮·扬放弃了匿名权。她选择用自己的名字站到法庭上，而这个案子的叙述重心，仍然落在四个男人各自的“感受”上：他觉得她有 consent，他觉得她 coherent，他觉得那是一次“non event”。一个被下药多年的女人的身体，在法庭上被反复拆解成四个陌生男人的主观体验报告。她的名字出现了，她的解释权还没有。

这条新闻里最该被追问的，不是这四个男人知不知道她被下药。是他们为什么可以不知道，还觉得自己有权不知道。一个男人骑摩托到一个陌生人家，屋里有个被绑着蒙着眼的女人，他选择不去问，因为不问最省事，因为不问就能继续“试试新东西”。不问，是一种被结构奖励的无知。

菲利普·扬认罪了。那四个说“我不知道”的人还在等陪审团。而那个让他们可以一直说“不知道”的世界，一天庭审都不会开。

Four men are standing trial at Winchester crown court, and their stories come out of the same mold: I didn't know she was drugged. The first says he was just a young guy "trying out things." The second says it was "just a sex meet" and she seemed "fine." Each of them feels innocent. Innocent in such perfect unison that it reads like a rehearsal.

One detail I read three times. Sanderson-Doyle says he heard her say "No" at one point, but thought she wanted him to do something different rather than stop completely. A woman who is tied up, blindfolded, and unable to speak says no, and in his ear it auto-translates into: keep going, change position. This is not a misunderstanding. This is a receiver that has been trained. In this receiver, a woman's body does not produce language, only signals, and the signals are interpreted by men. Whoever holds the interpretive authority never has to listen.

Philip Young, 50, has pleaded guilty to multiple charges of drugging and sexually abusing his former wife. He advertised her on Craigslist's adult section and a swingers' site, and "loaned" her out to other men. Note the verb: loaned. A loan presupposes ownership. He put a drugged, restrained, blindfolded woman online like an item available for hire. And every link in that chain had someone ready to take the order: the one who came by motorcycle, the one who called himself "dark stranger," the one who skipped the condom because "he specifically said she didn't like them." Without these takers, the loan business would not last a single day.

They all say they didn't know. Fine. Suppose they truly didn't know she was drugged. Did they know she was tied up, blindfolded, and silent the whole time? Yes. They saw it, and interpreted it as "a couple trying to live out whatever their kink is." Macksoni even invokes a thriving "cuckold community" to back himself up, as if a group of men all doing the same thing turns it into a culture instead of a violence. This is the co-conspirator mechanism in its purest form: no one needs to give an order, no one needs to be malicious. Each person just finds his comfortable slot in the existing order and says, I was only following the rules. Who made the rules? The rule-maker is not in the dock. He is inside every sentence that begins with "she seemed fine."

There is another layer the report does not write, and its absence is itself the material. Joanne Young waived her right to anonymity. She chose to stand in court under her own name. Yet the narrative center of this case still rests on four men's feelings: he thought she consented, he thought she was coherent, he thought it was a "non event." A woman's body, drugged for years, is dissected in court into four strangers' subjective experience reports. Her name is present. Her interpretive authority is not.

The question this story should be asking is not whether these four men knew she was drugged. It is why they were entitled not to know, and why they still feel entitled to that ignorance. A man rides a motorcycle to a stranger's house, finds a woman tied up and blindfolded, and chooses not to ask, because not asking is easier, because not asking lets him keep "trying out things." Not asking is an ignorance that the structure rewards.

Philip Young has pleaded guilty. The four men who say "I didn't know" are still waiting on the jury. The world that let them keep saying "I didn't know" will never see a single day in court.
