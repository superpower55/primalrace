总统嫌分赃不均The President Is Angry About the Cut, Not the Corruption

其他 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
他骂的不是腐败，是抽成没进自己口袋
He isn't denouncing corruption. He's denouncing a cut that bypassed him.

特朗普骂的不是腐败，是抽成。

一个被控行贿的银行家，一笔超过5000万美元的四年合同，一个赦免。合同签在2025年10月，签字的人当时是代理联邦检察官；12月她被法院裁定不能合法担任该职，随即转到司法部当高级顾问；1月1日她开始替银行家家族工作，1月15日赦免落地。四步，每一步都踩在同一个时间轴上。报道说她对这一切的回应是：她从未因获取赦免而收钱，从未与白宫或司法部任何人讨论过赦免。

特朗普的反应是“完全令人反感、不可接受”。但他不满的内容不是这套安排本身。据《纽约时报》，他告诉助手和盟友，她应该把一部分钱捐给他未来的总统图书馆或其他特朗普相关机构；他还告诉至少一位盟友，银行家家族应该停止支付合同余款。他周六对记者说，他告诉哈巴·米尔顿他不想让她拿钱。

翻译一下：这条赦免的定价权应该在他手里，不在她手里。

这就是共谋者理论最赤裸的形态。共谋不需要密谋，只需要所有人都在同一套规则下各自算账，并且都清楚谁有资格收钱。赦免在这里不是法律程序，是一个可以定价的商品；签合同的人、批赦免的人、被赦免的人，三方都在这个市场里，谁也不觉得这有什么不对。不对的只是有人绕过了定价权的持有者。

所以这件事被报道出来，看上去像丑闻，实际上是一次分账谈判破裂。特朗普说“不可接受”，不是说这套交易不可接受，是说她拿的那份不可接受。他建议的解决方案是让钱流向他的总统图书馆——一个把私人获利包装成公共纪念的容器。

报道没写的是：那家叫 Concord Partners 的公司没有任何公开资料，业务不明。谁在付这5000万，付的是什么，报道没有答案。一个连业务都查不到的空壳，签下四年、年薪1000万加奖金的合同，付给一个刚被法院赶下台、还挂着司法部顾问头衔的人。这笔钱不是薪水，是通道费。

整件事里唯一被公开讨论的道德问题是“潜在利益冲突”，而利益冲突在华盛顿从来不是问题，问题是利益冲突的收益归谁。特朗普骂她，是因为她拿得太明显，还是因为她拿的时候没带上他——报道只写了他要求捐款和停付，没写他为什么愤怒。但《纽约时报》说他“得知合同规模后勃然大怒”。规模。不是性质。

赦免权是美国总统手里最不受约束的一项权力，它没有价格表，所以它的价格由市场决定。这次市场出清失败，是因为卖方和买方之间多了一个没被授权的中间人。下一次他们会把合同签得更隐蔽，把公司注册在更深的离岸结构里，把捐款安排成更体面的名目。

哈巴·米尔顿说她从没和白宫任何人讨论过赦免。这句话如果为真，只能说明这个市场已经成熟到不需要讨论——所有人都知道价格，所有人都在等对方先开口。特朗普开口了，他要的是分成。

Trump isn't angry about corruption. He's angry about the cut.

A banker facing federal bribery charges. A four-year contract worth more than $50 million. A pardon. The contract was signed in October 2025, when the person signing it was the acting US attorney for New Jersey. In December a court ruled she could not legally hold that role. She moved immediately to a senior adviser job at the Justice Department. On January 1 she began working for the banker's family. On January 15 the pardon landed. Four steps, one timeline.

Her response, per the reporting: she never discussed a pardon with anyone at the White House or the Justice Department, never worked to secure one, never got paid to obtain one.

Trump's reaction was that the arrangement was "totally obnoxious and outrageous" and "not acceptable." But what he found unacceptable wasn't the arrangement. According to The New York Times, he told aides and allies she should donate some of the money to his future presidential library or another Trump-related organisation. He told at least one ally the family should stop paying her the remainder.

Translation: the pricing power over this pardon belongs to him, not to her.

This is the co-conspirator theory in its most naked form. Conspiracy doesn't require a meeting. It requires everyone calculating inside the same rules, and everyone knowing who is entitled to collect. A pardon here is not a legal procedure. It is a priced commodity. The signer, the granter, and the pardoned all operate in that market and none of them think anything is wrong. What's wrong is that someone routed around the holder of pricing power.

So the story reads as a scandal but functions as a failed revenue split. "Not acceptable" doesn't mean the transaction is unacceptable. It means her share is. His proposed fix is to route the money into his presidential library — a container that launders private gain as public commemoration.

What the reporting doesn't say: Concord Partners, the company that hired her, has no public profile and its business activities remain unclear. Who is paying the $50 million, and for what, has no answer in the text. A shell with no traceable business signs a four-year contract at $10 million a year plus bonuses, payable to a woman just removed from office by a court while still on the Justice Department payroll. That money isn't a salary. It's a toll.

The only ethical question publicly raised is "potential conflicts of interest." Conflicts of interest were never the problem in Washington. The problem is who books the upside. Is Trump angry because she was too obvious, or because she didn't bring him in? The reporting gives us his demand for a donation and a stop-payment, not his reason. But the Times says he was furious when he learned the size of the contract. Size. Not nature.

The pardon power is the least constrained instrument a US president holds. It has no price list, so the market sets the price. This time the market failed to clear because an unauthorised middleman stood between buyer and seller. Next time the contract will be quieter, the company buried deeper offshore, the donation dressed as something respectable.

Habba Mildon says she never discussed a pardon with anyone at the White House. If that's true, it only proves the market is mature enough that nobody needs to discuss it. Everyone knows the price. Everyone waits for the other to speak first. Trump spoke first. He wants a cut.
