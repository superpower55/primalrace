不肯算的账，就是暴力本身The Uncounted Ledger Is the Violence

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
拒绝公布影响评估，是把无家可归者从统计里删掉
Refusing to publish the impact assessment deletes the homeless from the statistics.

内政大臣拒绝说出自己的政策会让多少人睡到街上，这不是信息管理问题，这是暴力。加尔通的公式很简单：Violence = Potential − Actual。一个本可以住进安置房、本可以不被推到街头的人，因为一项政策的还款机制设计，落到了露宿的境地——差额就是暴力，而拒绝测量这个差额，是让暴力免于被计数。

注意这件事发生在哪一层。直接暴力没有出现，没有人被打。动的是 structural 层：政府取消了原本为会陷入赤贫的人提供支持的义务，改成让他们「还款」。还款挤压可支配收入，评估文件自己写了，「一些人可能经历经济困难或赤贫」，可能推高无家可归服务与露宿支持的需求。写下来了，然后说机制还没设计好，所以无法评估风险规模。机制还没设计好，政策已经要推了。这个顺序本身就是答案。

然后是 cultural 层，而且这一层才是关键。住房大臣雷纳担心的不是露宿人数，是「重新点燃反移民抗议」。同一批要被赶出酒店、塞进多人合租房的人，在这套叙事里同时是财政负担和治安风险。他们先被定义成问题，再被处理成问题，最后处理的结果被拿来证明问题真实存在。这就是武器化的标准流程：夺取「什么是事实」的制造权，然后让事实自己长成你预言的样子。

共谋在这里的样子很具体。盟友的说法是「细节尚未敲定，所以无法量化」，同时又保证新制度会「尽量减少对赤贫和无家可归的影响」。两句话不能同时成立：如果连规模都算不出来，你凭什么保证影响被最小化？这不是谨慎，这是把不可测量当成免责条款。而那位发言人说的「结束寻求庇护者酒店不会让人无家可归」，和本部门自己发布的评估文件正面对撞。同一份文件，对外是「不会」，对内是「有风险」。

还有一个更安静的共谋：麦克戈文和法恩布拉本来准备联名写信正式索要数据，信没发出去，因为法恩布拉辞职了，而那场辞职促成了斯塔默下台。一件关于无家可归者会不会死在大街上的追问，被更高层的权力更替吞掉了。这不是谁的恶意，这是元暴力的日常运作方式——重要的东西被更重要的事情覆盖，而「更重要」的定义权从来不在被覆盖的人手上。

首相承诺圣诞节前给每个露宿者一条离开街头的路，为此砸下数亿英镑。同一届政府，一边花钱把人从街上捞起来，一边拒绝说明自己的另一项政策会把多少人推下去。这不是精神分裂，这是两笔账分别记在不同的科目里：捞人的钱是可见的政绩，推人的数字是不可见的负债。

真正该问的不是马哈茂德为什么不说话，而是：一份承认「可能导致赤贫」的评估，为什么可以作为政策继续推进的依据。评估的作用本该是拦住政策，现在它的作用是给政策盖章，顺便把责任分散到「机制尚未设计」这句空话里。等机制设计好了，街上的人已经在了。

The home secretary will not say how many people her policy will put on the street. That is not information management. That is violence. Galtung's formula is simple: Violence = Potential − Actual. A person who could have been housed, who could have stayed off the pavement, is pushed into destitution by a repayment mechanism — the gap is the violence, and refusing to measure the gap is how violence escapes the count.

Look at which layer is moving. No one is being beaten. The structural layer is where this happens: the government ends its duty to support people who would otherwise become destitute, and replaces it with a debt. Repayments squeeze disposable income; the department's own assessment admits some individuals could experience financial hardship or destitution, that demand for homelessness services and rough sleeping support may rise. It is written down. Then: the mechanism has not yet been designed, so the scale of risk cannot be assessed. The mechanism is not designed. The policy is moving anyway. That order is the answer.

The cultural layer is where it gets ugly. What worries the housing secretary is not the number of people sleeping outside — it is the prospect of reigniting anti-immigration protests. The same people being moved out of hotels and into houses of multiple occupancy are, in this narrative, simultaneously a fiscal burden and a public order risk. They are defined as a problem, then processed as a problem, and the result of the processing is used to prove the problem was real. That is the standard weaponisation sequence: seize the power to manufacture fact, then let the fact grow into your prophecy.

The co-conspiracy here is specific. The allies' line is that the detail is not finalised, so nothing can be quantified — while insisting the new system will be designed to minimise destitution. Both cannot be true. If you cannot compute the scale, you cannot promise the minimisation. This is not caution; it is unmeasurability used as an exemption clause. And the spokesperson's claim that ending asylum hotels will not leave people homeless collides head-on with the department's own published assessment. Same document, two readings: no impact for the public, real risk for the file.

There is a quieter complicity too. McGovern and Fahnbulleh were preparing a formal letter demanding the data. The letter was never sent, because Fahnbulleh resigned, and that resignation helped bring down Starmer. A question about whether people will die on the street was swallowed by a higher-level power transition. No malice required. This is how meta-violence operates day to day: the important thing gets covered by the more important thing, and the power to define more important never sits with the covered.

The prime minister promises every rough sleeper a route off the streets by Christmas, hundreds of millions of pounds behind it. The same government spends to lift people off the pavement while refusing to say how many its other policy will push down onto it. Not schizophrenia — two ledgers in different columns. The money that lifts is visible achievement. The number that pushes is invisible liability.

The question is not why Mahmood stays silent. It is why an assessment that admits possible destitution can serve as grounds to proceed. An impact assessment is supposed to stop a policy. Here it rubber-stamps one, then spreads the blame into the phrase not yet designed. By the time the mechanism is designed, the people will already be outside.
