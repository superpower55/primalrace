救灾秀背后的暴力差额与民主DNA的伪装Disaster Theater: The Gap of Violence and the Masquerade of Democratic DNA

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
制度的效率不代表公正，快速响应往往是权力合法性的PR表演。
Administrative efficiency is not justice; rapid response is often a PR performance for political legitimacy.

面对地震这种 direct violence，人们习惯于在“高效”与“低效”之间做对比。新闻在赞美哥伦比亚新总统 De la Espriella 的快速反应，将其与委内瑞拉的瘫痪对比，并将其归功于所谓的“民主 DNA”。但这是一种典型的认知陷阱。所谓的“高效”，在此时更多是一种权力入场券的抢夺——一个毫无政治经验的极右翼民粹主义者，在就职 72 小时内通过一次高调的救灾秀，迅速完成了从“争议人物”到“国家救星”的叙事转换。这就是表达的武器化：利用灾难作为认知入口，将行政效率等同于治理能力。

我们得用加尔通的公式来看：Violence = Potential − Actual。地震是自然灾害，但死伤人数的差额是由 structural violence 决定的。建筑坍塌、医疗资源匮乏、贫民窟的脆弱性，这些才是真正的暴力。一个总统在镜头前宣布“我们将共同度过难关”，并派遣 1000 名军队以“防止骚乱”为名进入城市，这本质上是在用 structural violence 的掩护下，提前部署对潜在异见者的控制。军队的快速部署不是为了救人，而是为了确保在混乱中权力依然稳固。

委内瑞拉的瘫痪是 authoritarian rule 导致的系统性崩塌，而哥伦比亚的“高效”则是民主制度在危机时刻的惯性反应。但这两种反应的底层逻辑是一致的：权力者在衡量如何通过对灾难的定义权，来巩固自身的合法性。当保守派网红开始在社交媒体上对比两国反应时，这场救灾已经变成了某种政治营销。真正的 good_news 应该是结构性暴力（如建筑标准、资源分配）的削减，而不是一个新总统在镜头前表现得像个勤勉的CEO。

When facing the direct violence of an earthquake, the public tends to contrast 'efficiency' with 'inefficiency.' The news praises Colombia's new president, De la Espriella, for his rapid response compared to Venezuela's paralysis, attributing it to a 'democratic DNA.' This is a classic cognitive trap. This 'efficiency' is, in fact, a strategic seizure of the entry point of power—a far-right populist with zero political experience using a high-profile rescue show to pivot his narrative from 'controversial figure' to 'national savior' within 72 hours of taking office. This is the weaponisation of expression: using disaster as a cognitive entry point to equate administrative speed with governing capacity.

Applying Galtung's formula: Violence = Potential − Actual. While the quake is a natural event, the gap in casualties is dictated by structural violence. Collapsed buildings, scarce medical resources, and the vulnerability of slums are the real violence. A president declaring 'we will get through this together' while deploying 1,000 troops to 'prevent looting' is essentially deploying control over potential dissidents under the guise of disaster relief. The military's rapid deployment is less about saving lives and more about ensuring power remains stable amidst chaos.

Venezuela's paralysis is a systemic collapse caused by authoritarian rule, while Colombia's 'efficiency' is the inertial response of a democratic system in crisis. Yet, the underlying logic is identical: the powerful calculate how to consolidate legitimacy by controlling the definition of the disaster. As conservative influencers push the comparison on social media, the rescue operation transforms into political marketing. Actual good_news would be the reduction of structural violence—such as improved building codes and resource distribution—not a new president performing as a diligent CEO in front of the cameras.
