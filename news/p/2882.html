被许可的尖叫：一场关于情绪排泄的商业共谋Permitted Screams: A Commercial Complicity of Emotional Venting

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
当表达被转化为可量化的“资源”，释放就变成了另一种规训。
When expression is converted into a quantifiable 'resource,' release becomes another form of discipline.

在芝加哥湖边对着水面尖叫，听起来像是一种对现代压抑生活的反叛。但请注意这个关键的词：Permission（许可）。创始人 Hernandez 认为人们因为得到了他人的“许可”才敢尖叫。这种逻辑极其荒诞——一个成年人释放生物性情绪竟然需要一份由“男性教练”颁发的准入证。这根本不是在打破禁忌，而是在建立一套新的认知入口：将原始的、失控的表达，转化为一种被引导的、有步骤的（从安静到大声）、且带有仪式感（写在可降解纸上）的商品化行为。

这是一场典型的文化层共谋。人们在 structural violence 造成的压力下感到窒息，于是他们通过加入一个“俱乐部”来寻求慰藉。然而，这种慰藉并没有指向对造成压力的结构性根源的质询，而是被导向了“let go”这种典型的中产阶级心理按摩。当一个新婚妻子带着闺蜜来这里“庆祝”婚礼时，这种荒诞感达到了顶峰——婚姻这种利男的结构性契约，竟然需要通过一次集体的、被许可的尖叫来完成某种心理补偿。

最讽刺的是，这个俱乐部的组织者恰恰是一个“男性教练”和一个“女性品牌顾问”。他们精准地捕捉到了人们对主体性丧失的焦虑，然后将其包装成一种“资源”进行分发。在这种叙事里，尖叫不再是痛苦的真实表达，而成了一个被定价的、可复制的快闪活动。它让人们产生了一种“我已经处理了压力”的错觉，从而心安理得地回到那个继续剥削他们的结构中去。

尖叫本身没有问题，但当尖叫需要一个“俱乐部”来背书时，它就成了另一种伪装成解放的规训。

Screaming into the lake in Chicago sounds like a rebellion against modern repression. But note the keyword: Permission. The founder, Hernandez, claims people scream because they have 'permission' from others. This logic is absurd—an adult requiring a permit from a 'men's coach' to release a biological emotion. This isn't breaking a taboo; it's establishing a new cognitive entry point, transforming raw, uncontrolled expression into a guided, stepped, and ritualized (biodegradable paper) commercial act.

This is a textbook case of cultural complicity. People, suffocated by structural violence, seek solace in a 'club.' Yet, this solace doesn't lead to a questioning of the structural roots of their stress; instead, it's channeled into 'letting go'—a typical middle-class psychological massage. The absurdity peaks when a bride celebrates her wedding here; the structural contract of marriage, which predominantly benefits the masculine center, now requires a collective, permitted scream for psychological compensation.

Ironically, the organizers are a 'men's coach' and a 'branding consultant.' They've precisely captured the anxiety of lost subjectivity and repackaged it as a 'resource.' In this narrative, screaming is no longer a real expression of pain, but a priced, replicable pop-up event. It creates the illusion that stress has been 'processed,' allowing participants to comfortably return to the very structures that exploit them.

Screaming is fine. But when screaming requires a 'club' for validation, it becomes another form of discipline masquerading as liberation.
