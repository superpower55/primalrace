欧洲被叫醒，女人照旧睡着Europe Wakes Up, Women Stay Asleep

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
防御靠军备，不靠把谁排除在'人'之外
Defense is built with weapons, not by deciding who counts as human

Starmer 这篇讲话里最诚实的一句是：美国不再替欧洲买单，这不是 Trump 的偏差，是永久性的变化。这句话在国防预算的语境里是清醒的。但一个把安全重新定义成军备和核威慑的欧洲，和它要保护的那一半人口之间的关系，他一个字都没提。

报道里出现的所有位置——前首相、现任首相 Burnham、防长 Streeting、爱尔兰总理 Martin、商界领袖——全是男性。谈的是战斗机项目、核武库、共同安全框架、共同市场。加尔通的暴力三角里，这是 structural 层在重新洗牌：谁出钱、谁驻军、谁签协议。而 cultural 层的那句话——什么样的欧洲值得保卫、保卫的是谁的安全——被完全跳过了。

"Europe has twice been caught asleep" 这个比喻值得停一下。睡着了，是被动，是无辜，是醒来就好。可欧洲在防务上省下的钱，不是凭空消失的，它变成了别的东西：更低的税、更松的福利义务、更便宜的劳动力。这些成本的分配从来不是中性的。一个把军费当作唯一安全指标的政治共同体，会把照护、生育、家暴庇护所、女性就业的保障继续排在后排，然后说这是现实主义的必然。

Starmer 说 Brexit 对英国经济造成了 deep damage，说要 closer economic cooperation。这句话是对的，也是他任内最不敢落地的一句。经济合作意味着人员流动、监管对齐、食品标准——这些恰恰是普通人日常生活被政治直接定价的地方。报道里说下个月的峰会重点是食品标准、能源、青年流动。青年流动是唯一一个沾到身体和生活的议题，而它被列在最后。

真正的问题不是英国该不该靠近欧洲。是靠近之后，这个欧洲的安全叙事里，女性是主体还是被保护的对象。报道没写，因为没人问。一个把安全全部押在战斗机和核弹头上的联盟，最终保护的是一种秩序，而不是秩序里的每一个人。

欧洲醒过来了。醒过来之后第一件事，是继续假装另一半人还在睡。

The most honest line in Starmer's speech is that the US will no longer bankroll European security, and that this is not an aberration of the Trump presidency but a permanent change. In the language of defense budgets, that is clarity. What he never mentions is the relationship between a Europe redefined as military capability and the half of its population that capability is supposed to protect.

Every position named in the report is held by a man: the former prime minister, the current prime minister Burnham, the defense secretary Streeting, the taoiseach Martin, the business leaders. The subjects are fighter jet programs, nuclear arsenals, a common security framework, a common market. In Galtung's triangle, this is the structural layer being reshuffled: who pays, who stations troops, who signs. The cultural layer—what kind of Europe is worth defending, whose security is being secured—is skipped entirely.

"Europe has twice been caught asleep" deserves a pause. Asleep is passive, innocent, fixed by waking up. But the money Europe saved on defense did not vanish; it became something else: lower taxes, looser welfare obligations, cheaper labor. The distribution of those costs was never neutral. A political community that measures security only in military spending will keep care work, birth, domestic violence shelters, and women's employment at the back of the queue, and call it realism.

Starmer says Brexit did "deep damage" to the UK economy and calls for closer economic cooperation. That is correct, and it is also the thing he least dared to deliver in office. Economic cooperation means mobility, regulatory alignment, food standards—the places where politics directly prices ordinary life. Next month's summit will focus on food standards, energy, and youth mobility. Youth mobility is the only item that touches bodies and daily life, and it is listed last.

The real question is not whether Britain should move closer to Europe. It is whether, once closer, women are subjects of that security narrative or objects of it. The report does not say, because nobody asked. An alliance that stakes everything on fighter jets and warheads ultimately protects an order, not everyone inside it.

Europe has woken up. The first thing it did was keep pretending the other half is still asleep.
