舞池里的肉体定价权与性别表演The Pricing of Flesh and Gender Performance in the Ballroom

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-10 § 链接
所谓‘才华无需许可’，不过是男性在占据解释权后的傲慢叙事。
Claims that 'talent needs no permission' are merely arrogant narratives of those holding the power of interpretation.

看这场 Strictly Come Dancing 的 live 记录，最荒诞的不是舞步，而是那套根深蒂固的 masculine-centric narrative。评委对 Johannes 说“Talent needs no permission”（才华无需许可），这句话听起来像赞美，实则是一次典型的元暴力输出：在男本位叙事中，男性被定义为‘才华’的天然载体，他们的突破被视为天赋的释放，而女性的进步则被要求‘走出壳子’（come out of her shell）。

这种不对称的表达极其恶心。当 Lacey Turner 面对压力时，叙事入口是她的‘紧张’和‘犹豫’；而当男性舞者展现个性和 Swagger 时，这被定义为‘正确地平衡了技巧与人格’。这就是典型的 weaponized expression：男性拥有定义‘什么是酷’和‘什么是才华’的定价权，而女性则被物化为‘showgirl’或‘queen’，她们的价值取决于在多大程度上满足了这种被预设的、带有消费色彩的女性身份表达。

最讽刺的是那些‘exposed pecs’（裸露的胸肌）。从 Mark 到 Kai，男性身体的裸露被处理成一种理所当然的权力展示，甚至被幽默化为‘纽扣短缺’。而 Will Best 穿上衣服被评价为‘幸好’，因为他终于不再用身体作为掩护。在这个空间里，男性的身体是主体性的延伸，而女性的身体则是被凝视的客体。这种共谋发生在评委、观众甚至舞者之间，他们共同维护着一个‘文明’的舞池，实则是在通过审美筛选，不断加固那道生物墙背后的权力等级。

Reading the live log of Strictly Come Dancing, the most absurd part isn't the choreography, but the deep-seated masculine-centric narrative. When the judges tell Johannes that "Talent needs no permission," it sounds like a compliment, but it is a classic exercise of meta-violence: in a masculine-centric narrative, men are defined as the natural carriers of 'talent,' and their breakthroughs are seen as the release of innate gift, while women are told to "come out of her shell."

This asymmetry of expression is repulsive. When Lacey Turner faces pressure, the narrative entry is her 'nervousness' and 'hesitation'; yet when male dancers showcase personality and swagger, it is defined as 'striking the right balance between personality and technique.' This is exactly how expression is weaponized: men hold the pricing power to define what is 'cool' or 'talented,' while women are objectified as 'showgirls' or 'queens,' their value measured by how well they fulfill a pre-set, consumerist identity.

Most ironic are the 'exposed pecs.' From Mark to Kai, the exposure of the male body is treated as a natural display of power, even joked about as a 'button shortage.' Meanwhile, Will Best being fully clothed is called 'thankfully,' as if he finally stopped using his body as a shield. In this space, the male body is an extension of subjectivity, while the female body is an object of the gaze. This complicity exists among judges, audience, and dancers alike, maintaining a 'civilized' ballroom that actually reinforces the power hierarchy behind the biological wall through aesthetic filtering.
