浪漫爱的账单与主体性的廉价出卖The Bill for Romance and the Cheap Sale of Subjectivity

性别 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
浪漫爱叙事是掩盖资源掠夺与主体性让渡的商业 scam。
Romantic love narratives are a commercial scam masking resource predation and the surrender of subjectivity.

这篇文章用一种典型的幽默主义掩盖了一个残酷的结构性事实：所谓的“约会太贵”，本质上是浪漫爱叙事在认知入口上的 weaponized 结果。当约会被定义为必须包含“两份主菜、两杯饮料和开胃菜”时，它就不再是两个主体的兼容性测试，而是一场关于阶级筛选和资源交换的表演。这种由商业消费定义的“浪漫”，实际上是元暴力的延伸——它规定了进入亲密关系的入场券必须通过消费主义的仪式来兑现。

作者建议用“廉价约会”来破局，这在表面上是提供了最优解，但在深层逻辑上依然在共谋。他将约会比作 gambling，暗示爱是一种概率性的奖赏，而消费只是筹码。这种叙事将亲密关系客体化为一种“投资”，而忽略了在绝大多数约会博弈中，谁在支付账单往往决定了谁在定义这段关系的权力结构。在父权结构的惯性下，男性支付账单不仅是礼貌，更是一种通过经济支配权确立主体地位的潜意识表达。

最讽刺的是作者对“贫穷者”的自我解构。他将经济弱势描述为一种个人窘迫，而非结构性暴力。当 Gen Z 因为 150 美元的账单而放弃约会时，他们实际上是在潜意识中拒绝参与一场注定会让他们在主体性上处于劣势的博弈。真正的最优解不是寻找更便宜的餐厅，而是拆穿这套将“爱”与“消费仪式”绑定的 scam。如果一段关系的开始必须依赖于对某种特定生活方式的扮演，那么这种爱从一开始就是对主体性的死亡邀请。

This piece uses typical humorism to mask a brutal structural fact: the notion that "dating is too expensive" is the result of the weaponisation of romantic love narratives at the cognitive entry point. When a date is defined as requiring "two entrées, two drinks, and an appetizer," it ceases to be a compatibility test between two subjects and becomes a performance of class screening and resource exchange. This "romance" defined by commercial consumption is an extension of meta-violence—it mandates that the ticket to intimacy must be redeemed through the rituals of consumerism.

The author suggests "cheap dates" as a solution, which seems like an optimal expression on the surface but is actually a form of complicity in the deeper logic. He likens dating to gambling, implying love is a probabilistic reward and consumption is merely the chip. This narrative objectifies intimacy as an "investment," ignoring that in most dating games, whoever pays the bill often determines the power structure of the relationship. Under the inertia of the masculine-centric narrative, a man paying the bill is not just politeness; it is a subconscious expression of establishing subjectivity through economic dominance.

The most ironic part is the author's self-deconstruction of the "broke." He describes economic disadvantage as personal embarrassment rather than structural violence. When Gen Z avoids dating over a $150 bill, they are subconsciously refusing to participate in a game where they are destined to be subjectively disadvantaged. The true optimal expression is not finding a cheaper restaurant, but exposing the scam that binds "love" to "consumption rituals." If the start of a relationship requires performing a specific lifestyle, then such love is an invitation to the death of subjectivity from the very beginning.
