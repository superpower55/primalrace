禁售一次性烧烤架：一场关于“韧性”的PR表演Banning Disposable BBQs: A PR Performance of 'Resilience'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
用禁售消费品掩盖结构性气候渎职，是典型的叙事武器化。
Using consumer bans to mask structural climate negligence is a classic weaponisation of narrative.

这是一场标准的 PR scam。当英国变成一个“火药桶” (tinderbox)，政府的第一反应是禁售一次性烧烤架，并调动军队。在 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式里，这看起来像是在减少 direct violence（火灾），但实际上它在 structural 层面上通过转移矛盾来掩盖更大的暴力。

禁售这种 disposable product 是一种极其廉价的“可见性”治理。它把气候危机这个结构性问题，简化成了个体消费者的“不负责任”。通过定义烧烤架为“不安全产品”，政府成功地将公众的注意力从 North Sea 的石油钻探（Jackdaw 和 Rosebank 字段）转移到了周末的野餐习惯上。这是一种典型的认知入口争夺：用个体的“风险”来置换体制的“罪责”。

最讽刺的共谋在于，首相一边在 Stourbridge 谈论“面对气候变化”，一边用“务实” (pragmatic) 这个词为继续钻探油气背书。所谓的“务实”其实就是元暴力的伪装——它定义了什么是“事实”，并宣布在这个事实面前，环保主义者的警告是无效的。在这种叙事下，政府成了那个“在危机中建立韧性”的救世主，而真正的 structural violence（对生态的持续掠夺）则被定义成了不可避免的“事实”。

禁售烧烤架不会让英国停止燃烧，它只是让政府在面对大火时，手里有一张可以交给选民的“禁令”清单。这场表演的代价是，我们被诱导去相信，只要不再买一次性烧烤架，我们就参与了气候治理。

This is a textbook PR scam. As Britain becomes a 'tinderbox,' the government's first instinct is to ban disposable barbecues and deploy the military. In the formula Violence = Potential − Actual, this appears to reduce direct violence (wildfires), but structurally, it masks a far greater violence by shifting blame.

Banning a disposable product is a cheap form of 'visibility' governance. It reduces the structural crisis of climate change to the 'irresponsibility' of individual consumers. By labeling the BBQ grill as an 'unsafe product,' the government successfully diverts attention from North Sea oil drilling—specifically the Jackdaw and Rosebank fields—toward weekend picnic habits. This is a strategic capture of the cognitive entry point: replacing systemic guilt with individual risk.

The most cynical complicity lies in the Prime Minister's rhetoric. He speaks of 'facing the changing climate' while using the word 'pragmatic' to justify continued oil and gas extraction. This 'pragmatism' is simply a mask for meta-violence—it defines what 'fact' is and declares that, in the face of this fact, environmental warnings are irrelevant. In this narrative, the government casts itself as the savior 'building resilience,' while the structural violence of ecological plunder is rebranded as an inevitable reality.

Banning disposable BBQs won't stop Britain from burning; it just gives the government a 'ban list' to hand to voters while the world ignites. The cost of this performance is the illusion that we are contributing to climate action simply by avoiding a piece of disposable charcoal.
