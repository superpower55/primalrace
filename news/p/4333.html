财政纪律：一种名为“稳定”的结构性暴力Fiscal Discipline: A Structural Violence Masked as 'Stability'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-21 § 链接
所谓的财政纪律，本质上是优先保障债权人利益的共谋叙事。
So-called fiscal discipline is a complicit narrative prioritizing creditors over human existence.

18亿英镑的意外赤字，在金融分析师眼里是“挑战”，在财政大臣 John Healey 口中是需要用“财政纪律” (Fiscal discipline) 来修补的漏洞。但按照加尔通的暴力三角，任何现状低于本可达到的状态，差额就是暴力。当政府在公共债务高达 2.98 万亿英镑、GDP 占比 94% 的时候，依然把“财政纪律”视为稳定基石，这意味着什么？

这意味着系统在进行一次典型的共谋 (complicity)。财政大臣口中的“稳定”，其实是债券市场和金融资本的稳定，而非底层民众生活成本 (cost of living) 的稳定。在这种叙事里，欠债给资本市场的钱是必须死守的“纪律”，而由于通胀、增长缓慢导致的公共资源匮乏，则被包装成不可抗力的“全球不确定性”。

这是一种典型的结构性暴力 (structural violence)。政府通过定义什么是“财政责任”，将资源分配的优先级设定为：先偿还债务利息，再考虑基础设施，最后才在所谓的“呼吸空间”里给年轻人一点点施舍。这种顺序不是经济定律，而是一种武器化的认知入口，它让人们相信：削减公共开支是唯一的救赎，而这种救赎的代价是由那些在结构中处于弱势地位的人来承担的。

Healey 宣称英国削减赤字速度快于 G7 其他国家，这不过是在用一套新的尺子来定义胜利。如果这种“快速削减”是通过牺牲公共服务、加剧社会不平等来实现的，那么这种胜利本身就是一种元暴力 (meta violence) 的体现——它垄断了对“经济健康”的解释权，将资本的盈余定义为国家的繁荣。

An unexpected £1.8bn deficit is viewed as a 'challenge' by analysts and a 'leak' to be patched by 'fiscal discipline' by John Healey. But applying Galtung's Violence Triangle, any gap between the actual state and the potential state is violence. When public debt hits £2.98tn (94% of GDP) and the government still treats 'fiscal discipline' as the bedrock of stability, we must ask: whose stability?

This is a textbook case of complicity. The 'stability' Healey refers to is the stability of bond markets and financial capital, not the stability of the cost of living for the masses. In this narrative, debts owed to capital markets are a 'discipline' to be upheld, while the erosion of public resources due to inflation is dismissed as 'global uncertainty'.

This is structural violence. By defining 'fiscal responsibility', the government sets a priority hierarchy: first, service the debt; second, invest in infrastructure; and finally, offer a sliver of 'breathing space' to the youth. This sequence is not an economic law, but a weaponized cognitive entry point. It tricks the public into believing that austerity is the only salvation, while the cost of this salvation is borne by those structurally marginalized.

Healey boasts that the UK is cutting the deficit faster than any other G7 economy. This is merely redefining victory with a different ruler. If this 'speed' is achieved by gutting public services and deepening inequality, the victory itself is an expression of meta-violence—monopolizing the interpretation of 'economic health' to equate capital surplus with national prosperity.
