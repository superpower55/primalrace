十万块石头，一个国家的焦虑A Hundred and Eight Stones, One Country's Anxiety

国际 文化层 · 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
用宗教仪式包装经济焦虑，代价由年轻人付。
A religious spectacle wrapping economic panic, paid for by the young.

四万人半夜起来搬石头，二十万人到场，接近全国人口的四分之一。报道说这是“以石与土固定下来的祈祷”。但一个把国民幸福感写进国策的国家，为什么要靠一场宗教奇观来证明自己还有未来？

答案在报道自己的经济段落里：失业率上升、出生率下降、技术工人成批外流。不丹不到八十万人，年轻人正在用脚投票。国王的应对是造一座“正念之城”，请丹麦建筑师设计，让印度亿万富翁阿达尼当创始成员，接上印度的铁路网，2029年开国际机场，还挖比特币。这套组合里，“正念”是包装，招商引资是内容。

这不是宗教，这是第三章说的那种东西：把表达武器化。宗教作为认知入口，最擅长的就是把一个具体的、有代价的经济决策，翻译成一件不可质疑的神圣事业。108 座佛塔在一天内立起来，被写进“子孙后代的历史”——从此谁要问这座城市到底能不能吸引到投资、钱从哪来、年轻人回来做什么，问的人就先显得不虔诚。分析师已经在质疑投资够不够，但质疑声被二十万人的仪式盖过去了。

真正被消音的是谁？是那些已经走掉的年轻人。报道提到他们外流，却没写他们为什么走、走去了哪里、还回不回来。整篇新闻里，年轻人是统计数字，是“希望留住”的宾语，是这场宏大叙事要解决的问题，而不是有自己判断的人。国王说“我担心一天之内立起 108 座佛塔会失败”，他担心的是石头，不是人。

65 岁的 Nima Gyeltshen 说这一刻会被记入历史，56 岁的 Bishnu Maya Moktang 说她希望自己活得够久，能看到这座城市。两位老人在为一个他们多半看不到的未来作证。仪式是他们的，代价是年轻人的。

一座城市可以靠皇家特许状在 2024 年凭空设立，但留不住人的地方，通常不是因为缺一座城。

Forty thousand people got up after midnight to move stones. Two hundred thousand showed up to watch — nearly a quarter of the country. The report calls it a prayer made permanent in stone and earth. But why does a kingdom that wrote Gross National Happiness into policy need a religious spectacle to prove it still has a future?

The answer is in the report's own economics paragraph: rising unemployment, falling birth rates, skilled workers leaving en masse. Bhutan has fewer than 800,000 people, and its young are voting with their feet. The king's response is a Mindfulness City, designed by Danish architects, with Indian billionaire Gautam Adani as a founding member, plugged into India's rail network, an international airport slated for 2029, and Bitcoin mining on the side. Mindfulness is the wrapping. Investment recruitment is the content.

This is not religion. This is religion as a cognitive entry point — the most reliable way to translate a concrete economic decision with real costs into a sacred undertaking nobody can question. A hundred and eight stupas raised in a single day, recorded in history for generations to come. From that point on, anyone asking whether the city can actually attract investment, where the money comes from, what the young would return to do, first has to look impious. Analysts are already questioning whether Bhutan can attract sufficient investment. The ceremony drowned them out.

And who gets silenced? The young people who already left. The report notes the exodus and stops there — no reasons, no destinations, no word on whether they are coming back. In this story they are statistics, the object of the verb to retain, a problem the grand narrative is meant to solve, never people with their own judgment. The king said he worried that raising 108 stupas in one day might not succeed. He was worried about the stones.

Nima Gyeltshen, 65, says the occasion will be recorded in history. Bishnu Maya Moktang, 56, hopes she lives long enough to see the city. Two old people are bearing witness to a future they will most likely never inhabit. The ritual is theirs. The cost belongs to the young.

A city can be conjured into existence by royal charter in 2024. But places that cannot hold their people are rarely short of a city.
