他卖的不是技术，是解释权He Wasn't Selling Tech. He Was Selling the Story.

其他 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 ProPublica ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
勒索者被起诉了，帮忙赎人的那位也被起诉了。
The ransom was paid. So was the man who said he never paid it.

Pinhasi 的生意经很简单：客户以为自己在买技术，其实买的是一个说法。他说 MonsterCloud 从不付赎金，付没付是 trade secret，客户只管“sit back, relax and enjoy the ride”。联邦调查发现，他替客户付了八百多万美元赎金，向客户收了超过一千九百万美元；有一单，他自己付了约八千二百美元，向客户收了约十五万美元。

这不是诈骗案的边角料，这是整个案子的核心。勒索者锁住文件，制造一个“没有别的路”的假象；Pinhasi 接手这个假象，把它包装成“我们有别的路”，再卖一遍。第一次暴力来自黑客，第二次来自他自己。客户付的第二次钱，买的不是解密，是那个让他们安心的叙事。

叙事能卖钱，是因为它踩在一条真实的裂缝上。地方执法机构不愿跟罪犯打交道，不愿付赎金，于是需要一个能替他们保持体面的中间人。MonsterCloud 的客户里有很多正是这些机构。他们要的不是真相，是一个可以写进报告的版本：我们没有向黑客低头。Pinhasi 卖的就是这个版本。认知入口一旦被垄断，定价权就跟着走——八千二进，十五万出，差价买的是沉默。

更有意思的是背书。FBI 前副局长 Pistole 出现在 MonsterCloud 首页的宣传视频里，说警察局、政府机构、医院、财富五百强都信任这家公司。他自己后来承认，他清楚大多数情况下——包括 MonsterCloud 的——唯一能解密的路就是付钱，“这就是我理解的商业模式”。他通过演讲经纪公司间接拿钱，台词由 Pinhasi 写。起诉书里没有点他的名字。司法部对 ProPublica 当年这条发现不予置评。

一个执法体系的前高层，为一家靠替人向罪犯付钱赚钱的公司站台，而客户正是那些最不想碰罪犯的执法机构。这不叫巧合，这叫共谋：他提供合法性外观，公司提供体面，客户提供钱，黑客提供需求。每一方都在自己的位置上获利，账单落在被锁了文件、又被锁了一次解释权的人身上。

Pinhasi 不认罪，最高可判二十年。这是 direct 层的一次具体推进：一个把受害者危机变成自己利润中心的人被推上了被告席。但别忘了那个没有被写进起诉书的名字，和那句“这就是我理解的商业模式”——它说明这套叙事从来不是一个人的发明，而是一个行业心照不宣的共识。被起诉的只是那个把共识标了价、还标得太狠的人。

Pinhasi's business model was simple: clients thought they were buying technology. They were buying a story. He said MonsterCloud never paid ransoms, that how the firm recovered data was a trade secret, that clients should sit back, relax and enjoy the ride. Federal investigators found he paid more than $8 million in ransoms while charging clients more than $19 million. In one case he paid about $8,200 and billed the client about $150,000.

That is not a detail of the fraud. It is the fraud. The hackers locked the files and manufactured the appearance that there was no other way. Pinhasi took that appearance, repackaged it as "we have another way," and sold it again. The first act of violence came from the attackers; the second came from him. What clients paid the second time wasn't for decryption. It was for a narrative that let them keep their dignity.

The narrative sold because it sat on a real crack. Local law enforcement agencies hate dealing with criminals and hate paying ransoms, so they need a middleman who can keep their hands clean. Many of MonsterCloud's clients were exactly those agencies. They didn't want the truth. They wanted a version they could put in a report: we did not bow to the hackers. Pinhasi sold them that version. Once you control the cognitive entry point, you control the price. Buy at $8,200, sell at $150,000. The margin is the silence.

Then there is the endorsement. John Pistole, former deputy director of the FBI, appeared in a promotional video on MonsterCloud's homepage saying police departments, government agencies, hospitals and Fortune 500 firms trusted the company. He later acknowledged he knew that in most cases — including MonsterCloud's — the only way to decrypt locked data is to pay the hackers. "That's the business model as I understood it," he said. He was paid indirectly through a speakers' bureau, and Pinhasi scripted his testimonial. The indictment does not name him. The Justice Department declined to comment on the connection.

A former top official of the enforcement system vouching for a company that made its money paying criminals on behalf of clients who were themselves enforcement agencies. That is not a coincidence. That is collusion: he supplied the appearance of legitimacy, the company supplied deniability, the clients supplied money, the hackers supplied demand. Everyone profited in their assigned position. The bill landed on the people whose files were locked, and then whose explanation of what happened was locked too.

Pinhasi has pleaded not guilty and faces up to 20 years. This is a concrete advance at the direct level: a man who turned victims' crises into his own profit center is now a defendant. But don't forget the name that isn't in the indictment, or the line "that's the business model as I understood it." It tells you this narrative was never one man's invention. It was an industry's open secret. The only one indicted is the man who put a price on the secret — and priced it too high.
