恐怖片里的殖民幽灵与被收编的“正确光学”Colonial Ghosts and the Co-opted 'Optics' of Race-Horror

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-24 § 链接
当历史暴力被转化为娱乐消费的“惊悚感”，补偿的正义就成了叙事装饰。
When historical violence is converted into horror entertainment, justice for reparations becomes mere narrative decoration.

Gugu Mbatha-Raw 在新剧《Possession》中扮演一个被派往牙买加的黑人律师，理由是她的身份能提供更好的“optics”（光学/观感）。这个设定本身就是一次精准的 meta violence 揭露：在结构性暴力（structural violence）中，弱势者的身份不再是主体性，而是一件被权力中心用来刷掉“种族主义”标签的工具。这种“正确的光学”是典型的武器化表达，它让既得利益者在维持权力结构的同时，通过扮演一个“包容”的角色来获得道德豁免权。

剧集将殖民主义的血债转化为 race-horror（种族恐怖），用超自然力量让奴隶主与被奴役者的幽灵在现代交汇。但这正是文化暴力（cultural violence）的高明之处：将真实的、关于赔偿（reparations）和财富掠夺的政治诉求，包装成一种“被历史纠缠”的惊悚快感。当观众在屏幕前为“幽灵复仇”而战栗时，现实中直到 2015 年仍在由纳税人承担的奴隶主补偿金，被稀释成了一个有趣的剧情点。

Mbatha-Raw 提到她通过《Belle》拓宽了黑人在英国时代剧中的存在空间。但这是一种什么样的胜利？是在原有的男本位、白本位叙事框架内，争取一个“被允许出现”的席位。如果这种可见性（visibility）仅仅是为了让主流观众感到“多样性已达成”，而没有触碰权力分配的底层逻辑，那么这种进步只是表演性的让步。真正的存在性战争，不应该是争取进入白人客厅的入场券，而应该是拆掉那座客厅本身。

Gugu Mbatha-Raw plays a Black lawyer in 'Possession' dispatched to Jamaica because her identity provides 'better optics.' This setup is a precise exposure of meta violence: in a system of structural violence, the identity of the marginalized is no longer a site of subjectivity, but a tool used by power centers to scrub away 'racist' labels. This 'correct optics' is a classic weaponisation of expression, allowing the privileged to maintain power structures while gaining moral absolution by performing 'inclusivity.'

By transforming the blood-debts of colonialism into race-horror, the show lets the ghosts of enslavers and the enslaved collide. This is exactly how cultural violence operates: it packages real political demands for reparations and the recovery of plundered wealth into a 'haunting' thrill. While audiences shiver at 'ghostly revenge,' the fact that British taxpayers funded compensations for slave owners until 2015 is diluted into a mere plot point.

Mbatha-Raw notes that 'Belle' widened the space for Black actors in British period dramas. But what kind of victory is this? It is a struggle for a 'permitted seat' within an existing masculine-centric and white-centric narrative framework. If this visibility only serves to make mainstream audiences feel that 'diversity has been achieved' without touching the underlying logic of power distribution, it is merely a performative concession. The real existential war is not about winning a ticket into the white drawing room, but about tearing down the room itself.
