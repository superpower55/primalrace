讽刺的特权与被修剪的“文明”The Privilege of Satire and the Pruning of 'Civilization'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-27 § 链接
所谓的“文化战争”只是在讨论修剪枝叶，而忽略了谁在定义土壤。
The 'culture war' is merely a debate over pruning branches while ignoring who owns the soil.

BBC 删掉 Mitchell and Webb 那些涉及 blackface 的片段，在评论者看来是一场关于“冒犯”与“自由”的文化战争。但这种讨论本身就是一种典型的 cultural violence。双方都在争论一个点：这些片段是在“讽刺”种族主义，还是在“实施”种族主义？

事实上，这种争论是无效的。无论其意图是讽刺还是冒犯，blackface 这种表达方式本身就是元暴力 (meta violence) 的产物——它建立在一种权力不对等的基础上，即白人拥有定义、扮演和嘲弄他者肤色的解释权。当 Mitchell and Webb 在 sketch 里问“这是否是种族主义”时，他们实际上是在行使一种“讽刺者的特权”：我意识到我在冒犯，但我通过“意识到自己在冒犯”这件事，把自己置于了道德高地，从而获得了继续冒犯的许可。

BBC 的删除行为不是什么“政治正确”的审查，而是一次简单的 PR 维护。这不过是 structural violence 的一种温和表现：系统通过剔除个别片段，让整体叙事显得“文明”且“无害”，从而掩盖了这种权力结构依然稳固的事实。这种“修剪”让观众产生一种错觉，认为价值观在进步，而实际上，解释权依然掌握在那些能够决定哪些片段该留、哪些该删的共谋者手中。

最讽刺的是，评论者在呼吁 BBC 投资新喜剧去“探索时代的问题”。但只要这种“探索”依然建立在男性中心叙事和西方中心主义的认知入口之上，它就永远只是在旧房子的墙上刷了一层新漆。真正的最优解表达不是删除几个冒犯的片段，而是承认这种“定义他人”的权力本身就是一种暴力。

The BBC's decision to scrub blackface sketches from Mitchell and Webb's catalogue is framed as a clash between 'offensiveness' and 'freedom.' But this entire discourse is a manifestation of cultural violence. The debate centers on whether the sketches were 'satirizing' or 'practicing' racism.

This is a moot point. Regardless of intent, blackface is a tool of meta violence. It relies on a power imbalance where the white subject holds the explanatory power to define, perform, and mock the skin of the Other. When the comedians ask in their sketch, 'Is this racist?', they are exercising the 'privilege of the satirist': by acknowledging the offense, they claim a moral high ground that grants them the license to continue the offense.

BBC's editing is not 'woke' censorship; it is simple PR maintenance. It is a sanitized version of structural violence. By pruning a few branches, the system makes the overall narrative appear 'civilized' and 'harmless,' masking the fact that the underlying power structure remains untouched. This 'bowdlerism' creates an illusion of progress, while the power to decide what stays and what goes remains with the complicity of the institution.

Most ironic is the call for the BBC to invest in new comedy to 'explore the issues of our time.' As long as this 'exploration' is rooted in a masculine-centric narrative and Western-centric cognitive entries, it is merely a fresh coat of paint on an old house. The true optimal expression is not about deleting offensive clips, but acknowledging that the power to define the Other is, in itself, a form of violence.
