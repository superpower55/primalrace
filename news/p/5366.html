FIFA的“试运行”：女性身体作为基础设施的压力测试FIFA's 'Dry Run': Testing Infrastructure on Female Bodies

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-02 § 链接
女性赛事被当作男性主导市场的低成本Dry Run，是典型的结构性暴力。
Using women's tournaments as a low-cost dry run for male-dominated markets is textbook structural violence.

FIFA把首届女子俱乐部世界杯放在美国，名义上是为2031年女足世界杯做“Dry Run”（试运行）。这在叙事上被包装成对女足的重视，但剥开来看，这不过是一次低成本的压力测试。FIFA在评估场馆、定价和需求时，实际上是在利用女性运动员的身体作为基础设施的测试样本，为之后更大规模的商业收割铺路。

最讽刺的是，这次赛程的排布完全是围绕男性赛事的“避让”逻辑。为了不与男性的美洲杯冲突，女足被塞进了1月。这意味着顶级女球员将在2027年女足世界杯、2028年俱乐部世界杯和2028年奥运会之间进行毫无缓冲的极限冲刺。在FIFA的权力计算中，女性运动员的生理极限和职业寿命是可忽略的变量，她们被简化为填充赛程的资源。

而这次主办权的移交更是典型的“共谋”结果。没有正式竞标，直接跳过卡塔尔选择美国，这背后是FIFA领导层在应对欧洲俱乐部威胁和内部权力危机时的权宜之计。女足赛事在这里成了一个方便的筹码，被用来在复杂的商业谈判和政治博弈中作为润滑剂。在这种 masculine-centric narrative 下，女足的成功与否并不重要，重要的是它能否在不干扰男性权力版图的前提下，完成资本的预演。

FIFA is positioning the first Women’s Club World Cup in the US as a "dry run" for the 2031 Women’s World Cup. While framed as support for the women's game, it is actually a low-cost stress test. FIFA is using female athletes' bodies as infrastructure samples to assess venues, pricing, and demand, essentially paving the way for future commercial extraction.

The scheduling is the most telling part: it is designed entirely around the "avoidance" logic of men's sports. To avoid clashing with the men’s Copa América, the women are shoved into January. This forces top players into a brutal sprint between the 2027 World Cup, the 2028 Club World Cup, and the 2028 Olympics. In FIFA's power calculus, the physiological limits and professional longevity of female athletes are negligible variables; they are merely resources to fill a calendar.

The hosting shift is a result of pure complicity. By bypassing a formal bidding process and skipping Qatar for the US, FIFA is simply using the women's event as a lubricant in complex commercial negotiations and internal power struggles. In this masculine-centric narrative, the success of the women's game is secondary to its utility as a tool for capital rehearsal, provided it doesn't disturb the existing male power structure.
