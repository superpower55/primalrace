他们把尺子砸了，人还在量They Broke the Ruler, People Still Measure

其他 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
法律被拆掉之后，投票成了唯一的表达
With the law dismantled, voting becomes the only expression left

最高法院把《选举权法》拆了，Alabama 的共和党人拿笔重划选区，把 Shomari Figures 的老家 Mobile 和几个黑人带县划出去，把白人占压倒多数的 Wiregrass 划进来，黑人投票年龄人口从 48% 降到 40%。然后 Dothan 一个旧厂房里挤进四五百人，站着，没有座位。Figures 上台说，他们给我 Wiregrass 的时候，不知道 Wiregrass 长这样。

这是结构暴力被拆掉一层之后，直接暴力还没来得及落地的那段空隙。加尔通的公式里，暴力是潜在和实际之间的差额。Callais 判决把这个差额往下压了一大截——本来可以有两个黑人民选议员，现在被设计成一个都不该有。但差额被压下去，不等于人就认了。Alabama 初选，Montgomery 投票率比 2022 年高 10 个点，比全州平均高 7 个点；Louisiana 提前投票黑人占比涨 9 个点，South Carolina 8 个点，Georgia 6 个点（来源：Mother Jones）。投票率是差额的回弹。

回弹这件事本身值得看清楚。它不是制度给的，是人在制度不给的时候自己凑出来的。Alabama 没有提前投票，邮寄选票要有批准的理由，投票率全国垫底，法律一条条都在往低处压。压着压着，八十多岁的民权运动遗产、EJI 在 Montgomery 立的那些雕像、Figures 父亲 Michael Figures 让三K党破产的那场官司，全部变成了动员材料。历史在这里不是纪念品，是燃料。

但燃料烧的是人。Figures 41 岁，四个不到八岁的孩子，妻子坐在第一排抱着一个三岁一个一岁，旁边是他妈 Vivian——州参议员，同时是奶奶和政治军师。这个画面很好看，也很贵。一个人要带着全家上阵，才在一个被设计成让他输的选区里打成 coin flip。报道没写的是，这种动员能撑几次。

真正被暴露的是共谋者的算盘打错了。共和党两年重划 15 个席位，只有 5 个被 Cook 评为稳或倾向共和党，Trump 的保院计划反过来给民主党送了燃料。这不是正义赢了，是算计失准。算计失准和正义之间的距离，就是这篇报道里所有那些站着的人。

最刺的一句在报道里：民调显示黑人选民把最高法院列为社区最大威胁，排在 Trump 和经济之前；87% 的黑人女性说 Callais 让她们更想投票。而民主党顾问还在担心，讲投票权会吓跑喜欢经济议题的白人。Belcher 说得很准，我们没在选民的焦虑所在处和他们碰面。这就是元暴力最日常的样子：连反抗的一方都在替对方筛选什么话能说。

Figures 就算赢了，选后大概率还要再划一次。Alabama 总检察长 Steve Marshall 已经在喊 7-0 的地图。他们砸了尺子，人还在量——量出来的数字，下一次会被再划一遍。

The Supreme Court gutted the Voting Rights Act, and Alabama Republicans took a pen to the map: they carved out Shomari Figures' hometown of Mobile and several Black Belt counties, carved in the overwhelmingly white Wiregrass, and dropped the district's Black voting-age population from 48 percent to 40. Then four to five hundred people packed a former industrial building in Dothan and stood, because there were no seats. Figures took the stage and said that when they gave him the Wiregrass, they did not know the Wiregrass looked like this.

This is the gap between structural violence being dismantled and direct violence not yet landed. In Galtung's formula, violence is the difference between potential and actual. Callais pushed that difference down hard—two Black congressmen became a district designed to produce none. But pushing the difference down is not the same as people accepting it. In the Alabama primaries, turnout in Montgomery ran 10 points above 2022 and 7 points above the statewide average; the Black share of the early vote rose 9 points in Louisiana, 8 in South Carolina, 6 in Georgia (source: Mother Jones). Turnout is the difference bouncing back.

Look closely at what the bounce is made of. It is not the institution giving anything. Alabama has no early voting, mail ballots require an approved excuse, turnout ranks near the bottom nationally—every rule presses downward. And still the civil rights legacy, the EJI statues in Montgomery, the lawsuit in which Figures' father Michael bankrupted the United Klans of America, all of it gets converted into fuel. History here is not a memorial. It is combustible.

But fuel burns people. Figures is 41 with four kids under eight; his wife sits in the front row with a three-year-old and a one-year-old, next to his mother Vivian, state senator and grandmother and political consigliere at once. It is a good picture and an expensive one. A man has to bring his whole family onstage to turn a district designed to defeat him into a coin flip. What the report does not say is how many times this can be done.

What it does expose is that the co-conspirators miscalculated. Republicans redrew 15 seats in two years; only five are rated solid or likely Republican by Cook, and Trump's plan to hold the House ended up feeding Democrats. This is not justice winning. This is arithmetic failing. The distance between arithmetic failing and justice is every person standing in that room.

The sharpest line is buried in the polling: Black voters named the Supreme Court the biggest threat to their community, ahead of Trump and the economy; 87 percent of Black women said Callais made them more likely to vote. Meanwhile Democratic consultants worry that talking about voting rights will scare off white voters who like the economic message. Belcher puts it exactly—we are not meeting voters where they are on their angst. That is meta-violence in its most ordinary form: even the resisting side pre-screens what may be said, on the opponent's behalf.

Even if Figures wins, the map will likely be redrawn after the election. Alabama Attorney General Steve Marshall is already calling for a 7-0 map. They broke the ruler. People are still measuring—and the number they get will be carved up again.
