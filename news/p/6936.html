十八个点的仁慈Eighteen Percent of Mercy

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要堪萨斯州共和党参议员罗杰·马歇尔正竞选连任，其前产科病人 Meischa Zimmerman 于周五在州法院提起集体诉讼，指控他在行医期间向患者收取 18% 的年利率，超过堪萨斯州 15% 的法定上限。Zimmerman 称，2009 年她因 3596 美元的接生账单被起诉，追债过程中三次被捕。上月《纽约时报》曾报道马歇尔在巴顿县起诉数百名贫困患者追讨医疗债务。
The newsRepublican Senator Roger Marshall of Kansas, mid-reelection campaign, was hit Friday with a class-action suit in state court by former obstetrics patient Meischa Zimmerman, who says his practice charged 18 percent annual interest—above Kansas's 15 percent cap. She was sued over a $3,596 delivery bill and arrested three times during collection. Last month the New York Times reported Marshall sued hundreds of poor patients for medical debt.
他不制造暴力，他只是给暴力算了个利率
He didn't invent the violence. He just put an interest rate on it.

一个产科医生给接生账单定 18% 的年利率，州法写的是 15%。多出来的三个点不是贪婪的溢出，是定价：他算准了这些病人付不起、告不动、也搬不走。

看这条新闻先别看参议员，看那个位置。巴顿县，农村，穷人，没有保险。马歇尔在那里既是唯一的产科医生，又是债主，又是能把人告上法庭的一方。这三重身份叠在一起的时候，病人签的那张纸叫什么合同？加尔通说暴力是潜在与现实之间的差额，这里差额被写进了利率条款：你本可以不被追债、不被起诉、不在怀孕生子的那一年被拖进法院，现实是你被追了，被诉了，还因为这笔债三次被捕。中间那三个百分点，就是这道差额的收据。

更值得看的是他后来干的事。当了参议员，他在 Health, Education, Labor and Pensions 委员会里坐着，本周又被曝出八年前在委员会任上有一笔时间可疑的制药股票买入。从向付不起钱的人收 18%，到在决定医药规则的位子上买药企股票，这是同一条线，不是两件事。他对医疗的定价权，从来没有离开过他的手，只是换了个柜台。

现在他竞选连任，特朗普和万斯飞到堪萨斯给他站台，民调还是落后。对手 Adam Hamilton 在辩论里说，圣经里有 2000 节经文讲照顾穷人。这句话本身也是武器化的表达——用宗教叙事打宗教选民，我不觉得这比马歇尔干净多少，只是这次枪口对着的方向不同。但即便是一句竞选修辞，它至少点破了一件事：一个靠起诉穷人赚钱的医生，和一个自称代表“生命”“家庭价值”的政客，是同一个人。文化层那套叙事和结构层那套收费，一直是配套卖的。

真正被削掉的那一层，是结构层。诉讼如果成立，18% 会被认定违法，那三个点会被拿掉——但拿掉的是利率，不是机制。让一个农村产科医生可以同时当唯一供给方、债主和起诉人的那套东西，还站在原地。下一次换个人，换个 15%，照样成立。

至于 Zimmerman，报道里只有她的名字、2009 年、3596 美元、三次被捕。她为什么三次被捕、每次关了多久、孩子现在多大，一个字都没有。这条新闻里最该有声音的人，只有一行履历。

An obstetrician sets 18 percent annual interest on a delivery bill. Kansas law says 15. The extra three points aren't greed spilling over—they're pricing. He was counting on patients who couldn't pay, couldn't sue, and couldn't leave.

Don't look at the senator first. Look at the position. Barton County: rural, poor, uninsured. There Marshall was simultaneously the only OB, the creditor, and the party who could drag you into court. When those three roles sit in one chair, what exactly is the paper the patient signed? Galtung's formula: violence is the gap between the potential and the actual. Here the gap is written into the interest clause. You could have not been dunned, not been sued, not been put through court in the year you gave birth. You were. And the debt got you arrested three times. Those three percentage points are the receipt.

What he did afterward matters more. As a senator he sat on Health, Education, Labor and Pensions—and this week brought word of a suspiciously timed pharmaceutical stock purchase eight years ago while on that committee. Charging 18 percent to people who can't pay, then buying drug-company stock from the seat that writes drug rules: one line, not two. He never let go of the pricing power over medicine. He just moved counters.

Now he's running, Trump and Vance fly in, and he's still behind. His opponent, Pastor Adam Hamilton, says the Bible has 2,000 verses on caring for the poor. That line is itself a weaponized expression—religious narrative aimed at religious voters—and I don't find it cleaner than Marshall, only pointed elsewhere. But even as campaign rhetoric it names something real: the doctor who sues the poor and the politician who sells "life" and "family values" are the same man. The cultural-layer story and the structural-layer billing were always sold as a set.

The layer actually being shaved here is structural. If the suit holds, 18 percent is illegal and those three points come off. What comes off is the rate, not the machinery. The arrangement that lets one rural OB be the sole supplier, the creditor, and the plaintiff all at once is still standing. Next time it's someone else at 15 percent, and it works just the same.

As for Zimmerman, the report gives us a name, 2009, $3,596, three arrests. Why she was arrested, how long she was held, how old the child is now—not a word. The person in this story who most needs a voice gets one line of résumé.
