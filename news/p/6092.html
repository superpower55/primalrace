没人合影的G20，没人买单的战争No Group Photo, No One Paying for the Wars

国际 结构层 · 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要二十国集团外长会议在亚特兰大结束，因在俄罗斯对乌克兰战争等问题上的分歧，会议未能达成共识，未发表联合公报，改由主席声明总结会谈。美国国务卿卢比奥表示，各方尚未接近找到普遍接受的解决方案，但部分国家正就供应链和关键矿产合作。传统合影被取消，欧洲外交官反对与俄外长拉夫罗夫同框。
The newsG20 foreign ministers ended two days of talks in Atlanta without consensus on key global security issues, notably over Russia's war in Ukraine, and issued no joint communique, relying instead on a chairman's statement. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the group was not close to universally accepted solutions but some countries were cooperating on supply chains and critical minerals. The traditional group photo was canceled after European diplomats objected to posing with Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov.
联合公报缺席，不是外交失败，是共谋者懒得再演。
The missing communique isn't a diplomatic failure. It's the co-conspirators no longer bothering to perform.

G20外长会谈了两天，没发联合公报，改由主席发一份声明。鲁比奥自己说得挺诚实：不假装接近找到各方普遍接受的方案。这话倒是真的，只是他说的"各方"里，坐着一个正在打仗的外长。

波兰外长西科尔斯基把话说白了：桌上有正在打仗的人，就很难谈出共同立场。拉夫罗夫坐在那儿，欧洲外交官不愿意跟他站在一起拍合照，于是传统的集体照取消了。这张照片没拍成，比任何公报都更能说明问题。一个以"协调"为名的场子，连让人并排站两秒都做不到。

真正值得看的不是没通过什么，是通过了什么。鲁比奥第一天说会谈"非常富有成效"，第二天跟澳大利亚、印度、日本的外长开了小会，谈的是霍尔木兹海峡、红海、黑海、南海的自由航行，是关键矿产供应链。他在X上写得很清楚：供应链安全对增长至关重要，关键贸易通道必须保持安全和开放。

这就是这场会谈的实际产出：不是共识，是通道。谁能保证航道、谁能锁住矿产，这几家谈得很来；谁在打仗、谁该被邀请、谁不被邀请，谈不拢。前者是利益，后者是叙事。利益可以私下分，叙事必须公开吵。

俄罗斯那边，拉夫罗夫说莫斯科不会允许乌克兰对俄罗斯联邦的"合法安全利益"构成军事威胁。同一周，美国国务院批准了向乌克兰出售价值21亿美元的爱国者防空拦截弹。一边是"合法安全利益"的措辞，一边是军售合同，两件事并行不悖。战争叙事负责给军售定价，军售负责给战争续命。

而这场会谈上空还悬着另一场战争。美联社报道，美国官员已告知多个中东国家和地区盟友，为美国大选后可能重启对伊朗的军事打击做准备，也要准备伊朗报复。鲁比奥周五见了沙特和土耳其外长。沙特是G20成员，土耳其是关键的斡旋方。这两个国家在会谈里的位置，不是来讨论公报措辞的，是来听通知的。

还有一件事没进公报，甚至没进正式议程：特朗普政府决定不邀请G20成员南非，尽管其他国家反对。谁有资格坐上这张桌子，由邀请方决定；而被排除的人，连"未达成共识"这句话里都不会出现。

所以这场会谈没有失败。它按设计运转了：能分的利益在小房间里分，分不了的分歧留给主席声明，战争继续，军售继续，航道和矿产继续谈。缺席的那张合照，恰好是唯一诚实的东西——他们本来就不打算站在一起。

真正没被写进任何声明的是：燃料成本上涨、贸易不确定性，这些词后面站着的人。战争推高油价，油价推高运费，运费推高货架上每一样东西的价格。外长们谈的是航道安全，付账的是航道两边的人。

The G20 foreign ministers talked for two days and produced no joint communique, only a chairman's statement. Rubio was honest about it: he wouldn't pretend they were close to universally accepted solutions. True enough — except the "solutions" table had a foreign minister sitting at it whose country is at war.

Poland's Sikorski said it plainly: there are wars going on, and participants in those wars are around the table. Lavrov was there; European diplomats refused to stand beside him for the traditional group photo, so the photo was canceled. That canceled photo explains more than any communique would. A body whose entire purpose is coordination couldn't get people to stand side by side for two seconds.

What matters isn't what failed to pass. It's what did. Rubio called day one "very productive," then held sideline talks with Australia, India and Japan on freedom of navigation in the Strait of Hormuz and the Red, Black and South China seas, and on critical mineral supply chains. On X he wrote it out: secure supply chains are essential for growth, critical trade routes must remain secure and open to commerce.

That's the actual output: not consensus, but corridors. Who guarantees the shipping lanes, who locks down the minerals — those countries got along fine. Who is at war, who gets invited, who doesn't — no agreement. The first is interest. The second is narrative. Interest gets divided in private; narrative has to be fought over in public.

On Russia: Lavrov said Moscow would not allow Ukraine to pose a military threat to the Russian Federation's legitimate security interests. The same week, the State Department approved a $2.1 billion sale of Patriot interceptors to Ukraine. "Legitimate security interests" on one side, an arms contract on the other, running in parallel without contradiction. War narrative prices the weapons; weapons keep the war running.

And another war hung over the meeting. The AP reported that U.S. officials told Middle East countries and regional allies to prepare for potential renewed strikes on Iran after Tuesday's elections, and for possible retaliation. Rubio met the Saudi and Turkish foreign ministers on Friday. Saudi Arabia is a G20 member; Turkey is a key mediator. They weren't there to negotiate communique language. They were there to be briefed.

One more thing never made it into any statement, or even the formal agenda: the Trump administration's decision not to invite G20 member South Africa, despite objections from others. Who gets a seat is decided by the host. Those excluded don't even appear in the sentence "no consensus was reached."

So the meeting didn't fail. It ran exactly as designed: divisible interests split in small rooms, indivisible divisions left to a chairman's statement, wars continuing, arms sales continuing, shipping lanes and minerals still under discussion. The missing photo was the only honest thing there — they never intended to stand together.

What no statement mentions is the people standing behind the words "spikes in fuel costs" and "uncertainty about trade." War pushes up oil, oil pushes up freight, freight pushes up the price of everything on the shelf. The ministers discussed the safety of shipping lanes. The people on either side of those lanes pay the bill.
