母职的记忆损耗与中产阶级的焦虑共谋Maternal Memory Loss and the Complicity of Middle-Class Anxiety

母职的疲惫不是因为遗忘，而是结构性剥削的生理化
Maternal exhaustion is not about forgetting, but the physiological manifestation of structural exploitation.

这篇文章表面在聊一个母亲关于二胎养育的记忆缺失，实则揭露了一个残酷的生物学事实：母职在结构层面上就是一场对女性主体性的持续消磨。作者提到的那些“遗忘”——洗澡水温、出牙顺序、排便间隔——根本不是记忆力问题，而是当一个女性被强制推入“养育者”这个单一角色时，她的认知带宽被琐碎的生存维持任务完全占满。这种认知损耗是 structural violence 的一种体现：社会默认女性应承担这些琐碎，因此这些信息在女性大脑中被标记为“背景噪音”而非“核心知识”，最终在主体性被稀释的过程中自然流失。

更讽刺的是作者对“无聊”的怀旧。她发现自己童年的无聊反而成了韧性的来源，而现在她的中产阶级孩子却陷入了“计划性丰富” (scheduled enrichment) 的陷阱。这其实是另一种 weaponized 叙事：中产阶级父母通过将“闲暇”定义为“大脑腐烂” (brain rot)，将养育变成了一场关于文化资本的军备竞赛。这种焦虑是共谋的结果——父母通过扮演“负责任的塑造者”来缓解自身的阶级焦虑，而代价是孩子失去了在自我探索中确立身份的机会。

从“没钱所以无聊”到“有钱所以焦虑”，这并不是进步，而是评价方式的转移。作者最后轻飘飘的一句“总有心理医生 (there’s always therapy)”，恰恰揭示了现代母职的闭环：结构性地制造压力 $ ightarrow$ 导致精神内耗 $ ightarrow$ 通过商业化的心理咨询进行修补 $ ightarrow$ 继续在结构中循环。这种循环中，女性的生物墙被利用到了极致，而主体性则在一次次“忘记”中被悄悄没收。

This piece appears to be a reflection on a mother's memory lapses during her second pregnancy, but it actually exposes a brutal biological fact: motherhood, at a structural level, is a continuous erosion of female subjectivity. The "forgetting" mentioned—bath temperatures, teething orders, bowel movements—is not a cognitive failure, but a result of a woman's cognitive bandwidth being entirely consumed by the trivial tasks of survival maintenance. This cognitive attrition is a form of structural violence: society assumes women should handle these minutiae, thus this information is tagged as "background noise" rather than "core knowledge" in the female brain, eventually dissipating as subjectivity is diluted.

More ironic is the author's nostalgia for "boredom." She realizes her childhood boredom was a source of resilience, while her middle-class children are now trapped in "scheduled enrichment." This is another weaponized narrative: middle-class parents define "idleness" as "brain rot," turning parenting into an arms race for cultural capital. This anxiety is a result of complicity—parents alleviate their own class anxiety by performing the role of "responsible shapers," at the cost of their children's opportunity to establish identity through self-discovery.

Moving from "boredom due to poverty" to "anxiety due to wealth" is not progress, but a shift in the metric of evaluation. The author's casual closing remark, "there’s always therapy," perfectly illustrates the loop of modern motherhood: structural pressure $ ightarrow$ mental exhaustion $ ightarrow$ commercialized psychological repair $ ightarrow$ return to the structure. In this cycle, the biological wall of women is exploited to the limit, while their subjectivity is quietly confiscated in every single "forgetting."
