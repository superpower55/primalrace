拒绝浪漫爱 scam 的正确姿势How to Properly Reject the Romantic Love Scam

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-05 § 链接
真正的爱情不是消除矛盾，而是承认彼此都是不正确的个体。
True love isn't about erasing conflict, but acknowledging that both parties are fundamentally 'incorrect' individuals.

绝大多数 Romcom 都是一种文化暴力 (cultural violence)。它们通过制造一种“灵魂伴侣”的幻象，将复杂的权力博弈简化为命中注定，诱导人们掉入浪漫爱叙事陷阱，最终在婚姻这个利男的经济单位里完成自我规训。在这种叙事中，女性往往被要求扮演某种特定的角色来换取所谓的“安全感”。

但《Colin from Accounts》有趣的地方在于它对 schmaltz（滥情）的过敏。它不提供一个经过美化的认知入口，而是直接把关系的 messiness 摊在桌面上：金钱不平等、生育恐慌、性探索以及永无止境的摩擦。这其实是一场关于“存在性战争”的诚实记录——两个主体在博弈中承认对方的“不正确”，而不是试图将对方修剪成符合某种期待的客体。

最精彩的讽刺在于剧中的 WAWAM 组织。这种“女性反对女性反对男性”的逻辑，精准地捕捉到了共谋者 (complicity) 的生态：一部分女性为了在父权结构中求生，选择通过维护男权秩序来换取微小的生存优势。这种表演性的“赋权”本质上是元暴力的延伸，将女性的主体性彻底让渡给所谓的“男性能量”。

当一部剧敢于把爱情定义为“拥抱那些让你心烦的东西”时，它实际上是在解构浪漫爱作为武器的效力。它告诉我们，真正的 Just Expression 不是寻找一个完美契合的拼图，而是在两个破碎且真实的个体之间，达成一种互不掠夺的共识。

Most Romcoms are a form of cultural violence. By manufacturing the illusion of 'soulmates,' they simplify complex power dynamics into destiny, luring people into the romantic love scam, and eventually completing their self-discipline within the male-centric economic unit of marriage. In such narratives, women are often required to perform specific roles in exchange for a perceived 'sense of security.'

What makes *Colin from Accounts* interesting is its allergy to schmaltz. It refuses to provide a sanitized cognitive entry point, instead laying bare the messiness of relationships: money imbalances, fertility panics, sexual exploration, and endless friction. This is, in essence, an honest record of an existential war—two subjects acknowledging each other's 'incorrectness' in a game of stakes, rather than attempting to prune the other into an object that fits a certain expectation.

The most brilliant satire lies in the WAWAM organization. The logic of 'Women Against Women Against Men' precisely captures the ecology of complicity: some women, to survive within the patriarchal structure, choose to maintain the male-centric order in exchange for marginal survival advantages. This performative 'empowerment' is essentially an extension of meta-violence, completely surrendering female subjectivity to so-called 'male energy.'

When a show dares to define love as 'embracing what annoys you,' it is effectively dismantling the efficacy of romantic love as a weapon. It tells us that a Just Expression is not about finding a perfectly fitting puzzle piece, but about reaching a non-predatory consensus between two broken and authentic individuals.
