名人的“真实”：一场关于客体化表达的精密骗局The 'Authenticity' of Celebrity: A Precise Scam of Objectified Expression

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-21 § 链接
所谓的“真实”只是更高阶的表演，是权力对认知入口的再次垄断。
So-called 'authenticity' is merely a higher-order performance, a re-monopolization of cognitive entries by power.

Rachel Cusk 在新书里讨论了一个极其典型的存在性战争命题：一个顶级女星 M 如何在被全世界凝视的 lifetime 中“获得真实”。这在本质上是一个 scam。在元暴力的结构中，像 M 这样的女性从童年起就被生产为“被凝视的客体”，她的表达被时尚品牌、电影剧本和公众期待彻底武器化。当她试图通过一个叙述者来“重建真实”时，她其实是在请求对方帮她设计一套更高级的扮演方案——“你会编造吗？” (Would you just make it up?)。这就是假.最优解表达：试图通过扮演一个“真实的人”来换取主体性，但这种请求本身就证明了主体性的死亡。

小说中那个无性别、无名的叙述者扮演了一个 mirrored surface。这种镜像关系揭示了名人工业的共谋机制：名人提供一个被美化的空壳，而大众（那个“我们”）则在其中投射自己的欲望与窥视欲。Cusk 敏锐地捕捉到了这种 frictionless 的生活状态——一种因为被权力场完全包裹而导致生命力丧失的真空。M 表现得比叙述者更“真实”，是因为她作为职业演员，已经将“扮演真实”内化成了她的生物墙，她能精准地在认知入口处投放公众想要看到的“真实”碎片。

最讽刺的是，这种对“真实”的追求依然在男本位叙事的阴影下运行。无论是控制欲强的前任，还是被当作“旅游纪念碑”的自拍请求，M 的存在性始终被定义为他者的附属品或消费品。Cusk 用冷峻的笔触拆穿了这种 illusion：在后社交媒体时代，外观 (appearance) 就是唯一的确定性。当一个人必须通过“表演”才能抵达“真实”时，这本身就是一种 structural violence——一个人的主体性被彻底剥夺，只能在被定义的角色中寻找生存的缝隙。

Rachel Cusk’s new novel tackles a classic theme of existential war: how a top film star, M, attempts to 'acquire reality' after a lifetime of being gazed upon. This is essentially a scam. Within the structure of meta-violence, women like M are produced from childhood as 'objectified entities'; her expression is weaponized by fashion brands, scripts, and public expectations. When she asks a narrator to 'make it up' for her autobiography, she is seeking a more sophisticated acting blueprint. This is the fake optimal expression: attempting to regain subjectivity by playing the role of a 'real person,' a request that confirms the death of the subject.

The nameless, genderless narrator serves as a mirrored surface, exposing the complicity of the celebrity industry. The celebrity provides a beautified shell, while the public (the 'we') projects their desires and voyeurism into it. Cusk captures this frictionless existence—a vacuum where vitality is lost because the individual is entirely encased in forcefields of power. M appears more 'authentic' than the narrator only because, as a professional actor, she has internalized 'performing authenticity' as her biological wall, precisely delivering the fragments of 'truth' the public craves at the cognitive entry.

Most ironically, this quest for authenticity still operates under the shadow of masculine-centric narratives. Whether it is a controlling ex or a fan treating her as a 'tourist monument,' M’s existence is defined as an appendage or a commodity for others. Cusk strips away the illusion: in the post-saturation era, appearance is the only certainty. When one must 'act' to reach 'reality,' it is a form of structural violence—the subject is entirely stripped, left only to find cracks of survival within a predefined role.
