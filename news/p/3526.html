禁绝“站立饮酒”：一场关于中产阶级审美霸权的微观战争Banning 'Vertical Drinking': A Micro-War of Middle-Class Aesthetic Hegemony

好消息 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
所谓的“公共秩序”往往是少数特权阶层用来清除异己表达的武器。
What is called 'public order' is often a weapon used by a privileged few to erase dissident expressions.

Westminster council 试图禁止“vertical drinking”（站立饮酒），要求酒吧实行桌边服务，并限制深夜营业。这在表面上是关于噪音和治安的 licensing policy，但在本质上是一次典型的 cultural violence。站立饮酒、在吧台喧哗、深夜出没，这些行为构成了伦敦 Soho 和 West End 的底层文化表达。而保守派议员试图通过制度手段，将这些具有“流动性”和“嘈杂感”的表达，替换成符合中产阶级审美的、静止的、可控的“优雅”消费。

这是一种极其傲慢的认知入口争夺战。council 将整个区域定义为 “cumulative impact zone”（累积影响区），实际上是在制造一个事实：这里的夜生活已经“过载”了。这种叙事将公共空间的活力污名化为 “nuisance behaviours”（骚扰行为）。谁在定义什么是“骚扰”？是那些住在昂贵公寓里、希望窗外绝对安静的少数 vocal residents。他们通过共谋，利用行政权力将自己的审美偏好转化为法律限制，试图在结构层（structural layer）抹除那些不符合他们阶级品味的生存状态。

Sadiq Khan 的介入是一次及时的 structural 修正。他通过数据揭露了申请执照的极低通过率，拆穿了 council 制造的“过载”假象。当他指出决策被“少数大嗓门居民”劫持时，他实际上是在指出这场共谋的利益链条。好新闻在于，这种基于特权审美的行政暴力被更高层级的权力拦截了，Actual 重新向 Potential 靠近了一步。但我们要警惕的是，这种胜利依然在权力框架内完成，而那些被定义为“骚扰”的底层表达，在面对下一次“文明化”改造时，依然缺乏真正的解释权。

Westminster council's attempt to ban 'vertical drinking' and mandate table service is framed as a licensing policy, but it is essentially an act of cultural violence. Standing, shouting at the bar, and staying out late are the core expressions of the subcultures in Soho and the West End. Conservative councillors sought to use institutional means to replace these fluid, noisy expressions with a 'refined' consumption model that fits middle-class aesthetics.

This is an arrogant struggle for the cognitive entry point. By labeling the area a 'cumulative impact zone,' the council manufactured a 'fact' that nightlife had become 'overloaded.' This narrative stigmatizes the vitality of public space as 'nuisance behaviours.' Who defines 'nuisance'? A handful of vocal residents in expensive apartments who demand absolute silence. Through complicity, they attempted to translate their aesthetic preferences into structural violence, erasing any existence that doesn't align with their class taste.

Sadiq Khan's intervention is a timely structural correction. By using data to expose the abysmal approval rates for licenses, he debunked the manufactured 'overload' narrative. When he noted that decisions were skewed toward 'highly vocal residents,' he pointed directly at the chain of complicity. The good news is that this aesthetic-driven administrative violence was intercepted by a higher power, moving the Actual closer to the Potential. However, we must remain vigilant: this victory happened within the power hierarchy. Those whose expressions are labeled as 'nuisance' still lack the actual power of interpretation when the next 'civilizing' mission begins.
