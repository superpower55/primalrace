爱泼斯坦成了竞选广告的弹药Epstein Is Campaign Ammunition Now

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
受害者被做成广告素材，加害结构原地不动
Victims become ad material; the structure that enabled him stays put.

四千万美元买来的不是正义，是情绪。民主党候选人和他们的外围团体在六个关键参议院选区砸下超过四千万美元做爱泼斯坦相关广告（来源：Washington Post），CNN 统计的全国数字是三千五百万以上。这些钱买到的效果是让选民在投票前想起一个死去的恋童癖者，而不是让任何一个活着的共谋者付代价。

广告里最锋利的一句是弗吉尼亚·朱弗雷的家人说 Crank“掩盖了爱泼斯坦的罪行以保护唐纳德·特朗普”。朱弗雷是爱泼斯坦手下幸存的受害者，去年自杀身亡。她的家人被请进镜头，她的死亡被剪进三十秒。Crank 的竞选团队说这是“利用一个悲伤的家庭”，这话由 Crank 的团队说出口当然滑稽——但机制描述得没错：受害者的身体和死亡在这里是素材，是情绪入口，不是需要被救济的人。

更值得拆的是这些广告省略了什么。几乎所有众议院共和党人都投票支持了《爱泼斯坦文件透明法案》，只有一个人没投。广告指的是他们早先反对强制表决的程序性动议。这两件事在制度上不是一回事，在广告里被压成同一句话：“他保护恋童癖”。Crank 团队的解释是，他要先确认受害者信息被妥善隐去才肯投票——这个理由可以是真的，也可以是借口，报道没有给出答案。但广告也不需要答案，它需要的是“保护”这个词在选民脑子里炸开。

“保护”是这套叙事里最顺手的武器化词汇。保护儿童、保护受害者、保护正义——谁反对我，谁就是在保护加害者。这个句式的结构和“中东女性需要被解放所以打阿富汗”是同一个：把一个具体的人道诉求抽出来，做成道德通行证，然后拿去换选票。换到的东西和受害者的处境没有关系。

共和党的反击同样在这个场域里打转。Collins 在辩论里说 Ossoff 收了 Reid Hoffman 的钱，Hoffman 承认和爱泼斯坦有募款往来并表示后悔；新墨西哥州的外围团体说 Haaland 坐了爱泼斯坦的私人飞机，报道写明她没有坐那架飞机，她坐的是爱泼斯坦旗下公司预订的包机，她说不清楚这架飞机的归属。两边都在做同一件事：把“和爱泼斯坦有过任何接触”做成一个可以贴到任何人身上的标签，然后比谁的标签更脏。

没有人问那些被贩运的十四岁女孩后来怎么样了。报道里她们只出现一次，作为 Collins 广告里的一句修饰。也没有人问文件公开之后谁被追责——法案通过了，文件是否真的公开、公开到什么程度、有没有人被起诉，这篇报道一个字没写。四千万美元买的是投票日的情绪，不是文件柜里的内容。

爱泼斯坦的共谋网络里，那些真正安排航班、招募女孩、提供场地的人，大部分从未出现在任何一则广告里。他们不够有名，做成广告没人看。这才是这场竞选最诚实的部分：被攻击的从来不是共谋者，是共谋者的政治对手。

Forty million dollars bought emotion, not justice. Democratic candidates and allied groups have spent more than $40m on Epstein-related ads across six key Senate races (source: Washington Post); CNN puts the national figure above $35m. What that money buys is a voter remembering a dead pedophile before walking into the booth — not a single living co-conspirator paying a price.

The sharpest line in these ads has Virginia Giuffre's family saying Crank "covered up Epstein's crimes to protect Donald Trump." Giuffre survived Epstein's abuse and died by suicide last year. Her family is put on camera; her death is cut into thirty seconds. Crank's campaign calls this "preying on a grieving family" — rich coming from them, but the mechanism they describe is accurate: the victim's body and death are raw material here, an emotional entry point, not a person owed relief.

What the ads leave out matters more. Nearly every House Republican voted for the Epstein Files Transparency Act — all but one. The ads refer to earlier votes against a discharge petition, a procedural move to force the bill to the floor. Those are not the same act. In the ad they collapse into one sentence: he protects pedophiles. Crank's team says he wanted victim information properly redacted first. That could be true or it could be a cover; the reporting doesn't settle it. The ad doesn't need it settled. It needs the word "protect" to detonate.

"Protect" is the most serviceable weapon in this vocabulary. Protect children, protect victims, protect justice — whoever opposes me protects predators. The grammar is identical to "Middle Eastern women need liberating, therefore bomb Afghanistan": lift a real humanitarian claim out of context, turn it into a moral passport, spend it on votes. What it purchases has nothing to do with the people it invokes.

The Republican counterattack stays inside the same arena. Collins accused Ossoff of taking money from Reid Hoffman, who has acknowledged fundraising ties to Epstein and says he regrets them. An outside group in New Mexico said Haaland took a ride on Epstein's jet and money from his businesses; the reporting states she did not fly on his jet — she took a charter booked by one of his companies in 2014 and says she didn't know who owned it. Both sides are doing one thing: turning "any contact with Epstein" into a label you slap on an opponent and then compare stains.

Nobody asks what happened to the fourteen-year-old girls who were trafficked. They appear once in this story, as a modifier in a Collins ad. Nobody asks who gets held accountable now that the files are supposed to come out — whether they were actually released, how much was redacted, whether anyone was charged. The article doesn't say. Forty million dollars buys election-day feeling, not the contents of the filing cabinet.

The people who booked the flights, recruited the girls, provided the venues — most of them never appear in an ad. They aren't famous enough to sell one. That is the most honest thing about this campaign: the target was never the co-conspirators. It was their political opponents.
