三十周年，先数一数坟头Thirty Years, Count the Graves First

国际 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
解释权的换手，是用记者的命换来的。
The handover of interpretive power was paid for in journalists' lives.

半岛电视台三十周年，先别看它拿了多少奖，看它死了多少人。二十多名记者被杀，2022年希琳·阿布·阿克莱在杰宁被以色列士兵开枪打死，此后三十个月里又有十二人被杀，全部死于以色列在加沙的战争。2025年8月，阿纳斯·沙里夫和四名同事在希法医院外的记者帐篷里被炸死。这不是意外，这是定价：你从谁手里抢走了解释权，谁就用炸弹给你开账单。

它当年做的事，本质上是把阿拉伯世界的认知入口从王室和军方手里撬开一条缝。1996年之前，地区电视上不太公开讨论的东西，它拿上台面；"意见与异见"这句口号，是它给自己立的规矩。这是文化层的操作，但直接后果落在元层：谁有资格定义中东发生了什么，第一次不完全由华盛顿和地区强人说了算。2001年美军打阿富汗，它是喀布尔唯一的外国电视台；2011年阿拉伯之春，几百万人是跟着它的直播看自己国家的。

代价随之而来。2003年美军炸了它的巴格达办公室，记者塔里克·阿尤布死亡。至少九国政府关过它的办事处、吊销过它的执照。2024年以色列封禁它并突袭拉马拉办公室，几个月后巴勒斯坦权力机构也把它停了。注意这个名单：封它的不只有敌人，还有它长期报道其事业的权力机构。这就是共谋者的分布图——没有谁真的想要一个不受控的解释权，无论他站在哪一边。

三十周年这天，它同时宣布把阿拉伯语新闻业务拆成三条频道：滚动突发、直播事件、深度报道。总经理说这是为了"扩大报道范围、给更多观点更大位置"。这话可以是真的，也可以是重组带来的自我表述。报道没写这次拆分是谁推动的、预算怎么变、哪些岗位消失。一个靠"异见"立身的机构，在三十岁时把自己切成三个更专业、更可管理的模块，这件事至少值得问一句：效率的提升，和解释权的集中，是不是同一件事。

同一天它还发布了 The Core，与谷歌云合作的AI计划，用来管理信息、协调报道、调用资料库。把新闻编辑室的记忆交给一家美国云服务商，等于把档案的钥匙复制了一份给第三方。半岛电视台的整个历史就是关于谁能拿到原始素材、谁能在别人封锁时还在现场。现在它把素材的处理权外包出去，理由是效率。这是它自己的选择，但选择的代价通常不在签约那天显现。

三十年前它撬开的那条缝，今天还在，只是缝的两边都站满了人。死了的记者名字被念出来，被称作"半岛电视台承诺的一部分"——这句话很体面。体面的话通常用来盖住一个不太体面的事实：承诺是别人用命兑现的，而兑现承诺的机构现在正在重新安排自己的结构，好让下一个三十年更安全一点。安全一点，往往也意味着少撬开一点。

Before you count Al Jazeera's awards on its thirtieth anniversary, count its dead. More than two dozen of its journalists have been killed. In 2022 an Israeli soldier shot Shireen Abu Akleh in Jenin; in the thirty months that followed, twelve more were killed, every one of them by Israel during its war in Gaza. In August 2025, Anas al-Sharif and four colleagues were killed when a strike hit the press tent outside al-Shifa Hospital. This is not accident. This is pricing: take the power to explain away from someone, and they will send you the bill in bombs.

What it did in 1996 was pry open a crack in the Arab world's cognitive entry points, previously held by royals and militaries. Things rarely discussed openly on regional television were put on the table. "Opinion and the other opinion" was a rule it set for itself. That is cultural-layer work, but the consequence landed on the meta layer: who gets to define what happens in the Middle East stopped being entirely Washington's and the region's strongmen's call. In 2001, when the US attacked Afghanistan, it was the only foreign broadcaster in Kabul. In 2011, millions watched the Arab Spring live on its air.

The bill came due. In 2003 a US strike hit its Baghdad office and killed correspondent Tareq Ayyoub. At least nine governments have closed its bureaus or pulled its licences. In 2024 Israel banned it and raided its Ramallah office; months later the Palestinian Authority suspended it too. Look at that list: it isn't only enemies. The bodies that suppress it include authorities whose cause it has long covered. That is the map of co-conspirators — nobody actually wants an uncontrolled interpretive power, whichever side he stands on.

On the anniversary it also announced splitting its Arabic news operation into three channels: rolling breaking news, live events, in-depth reporting. The director says this broadens coverage and gives more viewpoints more prominence. That could be true, or it could be the self-description of a restructuring. The report doesn't say who pushed the split, how the budget changed, which jobs disappeared. An institution built on dissent, at thirty, slicing itself into three more professional, more manageable modules — at minimum worth asking whether the efficiency gain and the concentration of interpretive power are the same thing.

Same day, it launched The Core, an AI initiative with Google Cloud, to manage information, coordinate coverage, and use its archives. Handing a newsroom's memory to an American cloud provider means copying the key to the archive for a third party. Al Jazeera's whole history is about who gets the raw material, who is still on the ground when everyone else is locked out. Now it outsources the processing of that material, in the name of efficiency. Its own choice — but the cost of a choice rarely shows up on signing day.

The crack it opened thirty years ago is still there, only now both sides of it are crowded. The dead journalists' names were read out and called part of Al Jazeera's promise. That is a dignified sentence. Dignified sentences usually cover an undignified fact: the promise was cashed by other people's lives, and the institution cashing it is now rearranging itself to make the next thirty years safer. Safer usually means prying less.
