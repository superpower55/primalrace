球场上的“英雄主义”与男本位叙事的共谋Heroism on the Pitch: The Complicity of Masculine Narratives

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-16 § 链接
体育新闻的浪漫化叙事，是元暴力在文化层面的完美伪装。
The romanticization of sports news is a perfect camouflage for meta-violence at the cultural level.

读这条欧联杯战报，最让我不适的不是比分，而是那种极其典型的、充满男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）的表达方式。从“不可能的梦想实现”到“英雄般的反击”，再到对球员“个性”和“决定力”的歌颂，整篇报道在潜意识里完成了一次对元暴力的加固。

注意那些词汇：determination, glorious, personality, punished。在体育这个典型的 masculine 场域里，输赢被简化为一种关于“意志”和“纯粹力量”的博弈。这种叙事将复杂的结构性因素——俱乐部资本的运作、球员的生物学差异、甚至战术层面的资源分配——全部抹除，将其包装成一场关于“男人如何面对失败并重新站起来”的个人英雄主义史诗。这就是典型的 cultural violence：它让人们相信，只要拥有足够的“决心”，就能在系统中获胜，从而掩盖了系统本身的不公正。

最讽刺的是，这种叙事在公共空间被奉为至宝。球迷在看台上挥舞旗帜，庆祝一个俱乐部的“阶级跃迁”，而这种跃迁的逻辑与父权制下的成功学如出一辙：通过竞争、征服和对弱者的“惩罚”（punished）来确立自身的存在感。这不仅是足球，这是一场关于权力、等级和支配权的共谋。在这种共谋中，体育成为了男性确认自身主体性、练习支配欲的低成本模拟器。

所谓的“体育精神”，在很多时候不过是元暴力的 PR 版本。它教导人们接受竞争的残酷，并将其浪漫化为一种“荣耀”。当这种叙事渗透到生活的所有领域，人们就会习惯于用“强者生存”来解释一切结构性暴力，从而在潜意识里完成了对既定秩序的投名状。

Reading this Europa League report, what bothers me most isn't the score, but the quintessential masculine-centric narrative permeating the text. From "impossible dreams realised" to "glorious" recoveries and the celebration of "personality," the entire report unconsciously reinforces meta-violence.

Notice the lexicon: determination, glorious, personality, punished. In the masculine domain of sports, victory and defeat are reduced to a game of "will" and "raw power." This narrative erases structural factors—capital operations, biological differences, and resource allocation—packaging them instead as an epic of "men facing failure and standing up again." This is classic cultural violence: it convinces us that with enough "determination," one can win within the system, effectively masking the system's inherent injustice.

The irony is that this narrative is cherished in the public sphere. Fans wave flags, celebrating a club's "class leap," but the logic of this leap is identical to the success stories of patriarchy: establishing existence through competition, conquest, and the "punishment" of the weak. This isn't just football; it's a complicity of power, hierarchy, and dominance. Sports here serve as a low-cost simulator for men to confirm their subjectivity and practice the urge to dominate.

So-called "sportsmanship" is often just the PR version of meta-violence. It teaches us to accept the cruelty of competition and romanticizes it as "glory." When this narrative leaks into every aspect of life, people begin to use "survival of the fittest" to explain all structural violence, unconsciously signing a pledge of allegiance to the established order.
