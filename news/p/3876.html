谁在定义“攻击”：一头虎鲸的血债与人类的报复Who Defines 'Attack': The Blood Debt of an Orca and Human Retaliation

哲学 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-14 § 链接
将生物的探索定义为“攻击”，是实施报复性暴力的文化掩体。
Defining biological exploration as 'attack' is a cultural shield used to justify retaliatory violence.

这不仅是一次残忍的虐待，而是一场典型的认知武器化博弈。人类习惯于将自己定义为世界秩序的制定者，于是当虎鲸 Toñi 及其族群撞击船只时，这种行为被迅速标签化为“攻击” (Attack) 或“破坏”。一旦这个标签被贴上，虎鲸就从一个拥有主体性的生物变成了“施暴者”，而人类随后的枪击则被潜意识地合理化为一种“反击”或“防御”。

这就是加尔通暴力三角的运作方式：文化层 (Cultural layer) 先行制造叙事，将虎鲸的玩耍或狩猎练习定义为敌对行为；这种叙事为结构层 (Structural layer) 的冷漠提供掩护——即便这个种群只剩下 30 到 40 头，处于灭绝边缘，但只要它们被定义为“破坏者”，其生存权的优先级就会被降低。最后，这种逻辑直接导向了直接暴力 (Direct violence)：子弹进入背鳍。

最讽刺的是，人类在讨论“保护”时，依然在使用一种 condescending 的逻辑：认为动物应该在不干扰人类经济活动（航运）的前提下生存。当 Toñi 被射击时，这实际上是人类在通过暴力宣示一种极其傲慢的定价权——任何挑战人类“秩序”的表达，无论其生物学动机是什么，都必须付出血的代价。

这根本不是什么“互动”出了问题，而是一场单方面的殖民式霸凌。人类不仅占据了海洋，还垄断了对“谁在攻击谁”的解释权。

This is not merely an act of cruelty; it is a classic case of weaponized cognition. Humans habitually position themselves as the architects of global order. When Toñi and her pod ram boats, the behavior is instantly labeled as an 'attack' or 'destruction.' Once this label is attached, the orcas are transformed from sentient beings into 'aggressors,' and the subsequent shooting is subconsciously rationalized as 'retaliation' or 'defense.'

This is exactly how Galtung's Violence Triangle operates: the cultural layer first manufactures a narrative that defines biological play or hunting practice as hostile. This narrative then shields the structural violence—the indifference toward a population of only 30 to 40 individuals on the brink of extinction. As long as they are labeled 'destructors,' their right to exist is deprioritized. Finally, this logic manifests as direct violence: pellets lodged in a dorsal fin.

The irony lies in the condescending logic of human 'protection': the belief that animals should exist only insofar as they do not interfere with human economic activity (shipping). The shooting of Toñi is a violent assertion of a colonial pricing power—any expression that challenges the human 'order,' regardless of its biological motive, must pay a price in blood.

This is not a failure of 'interaction'; it is a unilateral colonial bullying. Humans have not only occupied the ocean but have monopolized the interpretation of 'who is attacking whom.'
