免费的同人站倒下时，付费的替代品立刻来收尸。
When the free archive falls, the paid substitutes come to collect.

AO3 停摆，最先扑上来的不是黑客，是生意。

OTW 周五说检测到"security incident"（安全事件），站点从最初估计的 12 小时，改成"大约两周"，可能"长达四周"。读者在 X 上的哀嚎有 15000 条回复。这些都不意外。意外的是 Wattpad 和 Quinn 的反应速度——一个老牌同人站出来刷存在感，底下最高赞回复是"像凌晨两点那个有毒前任发来的短信"；一个音频色情 App 直接甩出"AO3OUTAGE"折扣码。

这就是认知入口的争夺，只不过这次争夺发生在一个网站宕机的空档里。Quinn 那招尤其难看：AO3 的整个伦理是免费内容、免费站点、全志愿者运营、只靠捐款活着。一个靠订阅收费的色情音频 App，趁这个空档给"免费"标了个折扣价。它卖的不是内容，是把"同人"这个表达重新定价的权力——原来你们可以不花钱写、不花钱读，现在趁你们没得读，我来告诉你们这东西值多少钱。

读者那句"你永远、永远不可能是它们"，说的其实是这件事。AO3 的免费不是商业模式，是政治立场：表达不该被标价，写的人不该被平台抽成。所以当它短暂消失，第一个动作不是"我们去别处读"，而是有人立刻把"别处"包装成商品递过来。

至于那个安全事件本身，报道没写是什么、范围多大，只说"no AO3 data has been deleted"，用户数据库据信没被偷，但建议在别处用过同一密码的人改密码。一个靠捐款和志愿者撑起来的站点，出事之后能给出的最硬承诺是"数据没删"——这不是它的失败，是它没有预算请一整支安全团队。免费的东西，代价总是落在看不见的地方。

报道末尾说，站点可能以"降级状态"回来，只读，不能传新章节。到那时候，Quinn 的折扣码大概已经过期了。但那个把免费表达换算成订阅价的念头不会过期——它只是在等下一次宕机。

AO3 went down, and the first thing to rush in wasn't a hacker. It was business.

OTW said Friday it had "detected evidence of a security incident." The timeline went from 12 hours to "around 2 weeks," possibly "up to 4 weeks." The X post has 15,000 replies of grief. None of that is surprising. What is surprising is how fast Wattpad and Quinn moved—one aging fic site reminding everyone it exists, top reply: "like a text from that toxic ex at 2 am." An audio-erotica app offering an "AO3OUTAGE" discount code.

This is a fight over the cognitive entrance, except the fight is happening in the gap left by a dead website. Quinn's move is the ugliest: AO3's entire ethic is free content, a free site, all volunteers, funded by donations. A subscription audio-porn app used the outage to put a discount price on "free." It isn't selling content. It's selling the power to re-price fan expression—you used to write and read this for nothing, now that you can't read it, let me tell you what it's worth.

The reader who wrote "you'll never EVER be them" was saying exactly this. AO3's free isn't a business model, it's a political position: expression shouldn't be priced, writers shouldn't be skimmed by a platform. So when it vanishes briefly, the first move isn't "let's read elsewhere," it's someone packaging "elsewhere" and handing it over as a product.

As for the incident itself, the report doesn't say what happened or how big it was—only that "no AO3 data has been deleted," the user database is believed safe, and anyone reusing their AO3 password elsewhere should change it. A site run on donations and volunteers, after a breach, can offer "data not deleted" as its hardest promise. That's not its failure. It's what happens when there's no budget for a security team. Free things always cost somewhere you can't see.

The report says the site may come back "in a degraded state," read-only, no new chapters. By then Quinn's discount code will have expired. The idea of converting free expression into a subscription price won't. It's just waiting for the next outage.
