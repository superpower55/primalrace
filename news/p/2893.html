Roster Dating：一场关于客体化与风险对冲的伪进步博弈Roster Dating: A Pseudo-Progressive Game of Objectification and Risk Hedging

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-27 § 链接
将人分解为功能组件的“名单约会”，是主体性死亡后的风险对冲。
Roster dating is not discovery; it is the risk-hedging of a dead subjectivity that treats humans as functional components.

所谓的 Roster Dating（名单约会），本质上是将潜在伴侣“零件化”的客体化操作。一个人负责提供情绪价值，一个人负责艺术审美，一个人负责经济支撑。这种把人拆解为具体 function 需求的行为，不是什么“个人探索之旅”，而是一场典型的存在性战争中的风险对冲策略。当个体不再追求与另一个完整人格建立 connection，而是通过建立一个“供应名单”来确保自己的需求被全覆盖时，这种表达已经彻底异化成了对他人主体性的抹杀。

有趣的是，这种叙事被包装成 Gen Z 的“刻意选择”和“成长路径”。这正是典型的表达武器化：将由于不安全感和缺乏承诺能力导致的碎片化关系，重新定义为一种“高效”的资源管理。在父权结构的余波中，这种博弈逻辑其实是男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）的内化——将情感对象视为可替换的资源池，通过增加样本量来降低单次投入失败的成本。这不是在寻找爱，而是在经营一个情感资产组合。

而最讽刺的共谋在于，这种模式在执行时往往依赖于一种心照不宣的沉默，或者说是一种对“对方也同样在操作”的傲慢假设。在这种共谋场域里，诚实被替换成了“不撒谎”，而真正的公正表达（Just Expressions）——即双方在对等、透明的状态下确认彼此的需求——被视为低效且破坏快感的。当一个人在名单里把对方定义为“那个富有的”或“那个忠诚的”时，对方在这一关系中的存在性就被剥夺了，只剩下一个标签。

这场博弈的最终赢家是谁？是那些能够维持名单且无需支付约会成本的人。在这种结构性不对等中，Roster Dating 成了强者用来筛选和消耗弱者的工具。所谓的“放下预期”，其实是强者在单方面宣布不再承担承诺的责任，而弱者则在等待被选中的焦虑中，心甘情愿地成为了对方名单上的一个功能组件。

Roster Dating is essentially an objectification operation that 'componentizes' potential partners. One person provides emotional value, another offers artistic taste, and another handles the financial burden. This act of dismantling a human being into a set of specific functional needs is not a 'journey of personal discovery,' but a classic risk-hedging strategy within an existential war. When an individual stops seeking a connection with another complete personality and instead builds a 'supply list' to ensure all needs are covered, this expression has completely mutated into the erasure of the other's subjectivity.

It is amusing how this narrative is packaged as a 'conscious choice' and a 'growth path' for Gen Z. This is a textbook example of the weaponisation of expression: recasting fragmented relationships—born of insecurity and an inability to commit—as 'efficient' resource management. In the wake of the masculine-centric narrative, this logic is an internalisation of the patriarchy—treating emotional objects as a replaceable resource pool and reducing the cost of a single failure by increasing the sample size. This is not searching for love; it is managing an emotional asset portfolio.

The most ironic part is the complicity involved. This mode of dating often relies on a tacit silence, or an arrogant assumption that 'the other person must be doing the same.' In this field of complicity, honesty is replaced by 'not lying,' while Just Expressions—where both parties transparently confirm their needs as equals—are dismissed as inefficient or mood-killing. When someone labels a partner as 'the rich one' or 'the devoted one,' the other person's existence in that relationship is stripped away, leaving only a tag.

Who is the ultimate winner in this game? Those who can maintain the roster without paying the dating costs. Within this structural inequality, Roster Dating becomes a tool for the powerful to screen and consume the vulnerable. The so-called 'letting go of expectations' is actually the powerful unilaterally declaring they will no longer bear the responsibility of commitment, while the vulnerable, in their anxiety of being chosen, willingly become a functional component on someone else's list.
