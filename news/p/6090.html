一顶帽子戴了三十年A Hat Worn for Thirty Years

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要美国空军发言人表示，内华达州共和党国会候选人戴维·弗利波在沙漠风暴行动结束九个月后的1991年12月10日才进入现役。弗利波多次声称自己参加了1991年的沙漠风暴行动，还常戴一顶标明该战争老兵身份的帽子。其竞选团队则称相关质疑「完全不是新闻」。
The newsA US Air Force spokesperson said David Flippo, the Republican nominee for Congress in a competitive Nevada district, did not enter active duty until December 10, 1991, nine months after Operation Desert Storm ended. Flippo has repeatedly claimed he served in Desert Storm and often wears a hat identifying him as a veteran of that war. His campaign called the questions a "total non story."
军功是表达，冒充军功是把别人的死拿来定价
Stolen valor is pricing other people's deaths into your own résumé.

David Flippo 从1985年到2009年在空军服役，这是真的。他1991年1月31日当上蒙大拿州立大学空军 ROTC 的学员指挥官，这也是真的，校报二月八日那期写着。

不真的是沙漠风暴。空军发言人说，他作为军官进入现役是1991年12月10日，战争在2月28日就结束了。中间那九个月，他在蒙大拿读书。

表达决定你是谁。军装、帽子、勋章、你在募款餐会上说“我在沙漠里待过”的那个语气——这些都是表达，而表达是存在的确证。Flippo 不是在撒谎说自己去过某个地方，他是在用别人的表达给自己定价。沙漠风暴有65万人服役、383人死亡（来源：Mother Jones 引2017年新闻稿），那383条命是这套表达的硬通货，他借来用了。

他借得很系统。帽子上的 V 形徽章，竞选团队说他拿了带 V 的铜星勋章，实际上他拿的是普通铜星。照片出来之后，他怪孙子孙女把徽章别上去的——那顶帽子他戴了好几年，帽子本身还写着他是沙漠风暴老兵。

这里有个共谋的细节值得看：他的顾问 Rory McShane 被问到这件事，说这是“total non story”，理由是国会和空军“说他是沙漠风暴老兵”。然后他搬出1991年国会法案里对“沙漠风暴行动”的定义，那个定义把 Desert Shield、Desert Storm、Provide Comfort 全包进去了。

换尺子。能力没变，评价方式变了。做不了小芯片就宣布一条新定律，没赶上战争就找一个把战后也算进去的法条。这是同一套操作，只不过这次尺子是国会1991年为了发福利写的。

McShane 还说 Flippo 拿到了 Southwest Asia Service Medal。空军官网写着这个奖发给1990年8月2日到1995年11月30日之间支援 Desert Shield 或 Desert Storm 的人。Flippo 1992年4月二儿子出生后去的沙特，那时候是停火行动。他的服役记录里没有科威特解放勋章——那是沙特发给真正参加解放科威特的联军部队的。奖章是真的，时间不对。

这不是一个关于虚荣的故事。Flippo 竞选投入超过250万美元，没有明显财富来源；从2023年第一次竞选起就没交过法定的财务披露；竞选团队声称收到过两个死人的捐款；他还牵涉一个亚利桑那州的土地项目，声称能连续20年给出20%的回报。

一个人如果连“我在哪一年穿了什么制服”都要重新定义，你猜他会怎么定义“20%的回报”和“这笔钱是谁捐的”。

退役空军军士长 Daniel Casentini 说，真正在沙漠风暴服役的老兵不会认他是同袍，除非他当时真的在战区。“Integrity First”是空军三条核心价值观的第一条，他说这话很讽刺。退役陆军中校 Scott Maryott 说得更直接：对服役最谦虚的人往往做得最多，夸大战绩的人暴露的是品格缺陷。

这两个人是这条新闻里唯一在做减法的人。他们没有增加什么叙事，只是把尺子放回原位。

那顶帽子他戴了好几年。帽子上的 V 是假的，帽子上“Desert Storm Veteran”那行字是假的，戴着帽子的人是真的——一个从1985年服役到2009年、2008年被解除中队长职务、第二年离开空军的人。他有一份真实的履历可以拿出来，他选择了另一份。

问题不在于他为什么选。问题在于，那383个人没得选，而他们的死被做成了帽子上的一行字，还可以被解释成 clerical error。

David Flippo served in the Air Force from 1985 to 2009. That part is true. He became the cadet commander of Montana State's Air Force ROTC on January 31, 1991. That part is also true; the student paper ran it on February 8.

Desert Storm is the part that isn't. An Air Force spokesperson says Flippo entered active duty as an officer on December 10, 1991. The war ended February 28. For those nine months, he was in Montana, in school.

Expression decides who you are. The uniform, the hat, the medals, the particular tone in which you say "I was in the desert" at a fundraiser — these are expressions, and expression is the confirmation of existence. Flippo wasn't lying about having visited a place. He was using other people's expressions to price himself. Desert Storm had 650,000 service members and 383 dead (source: Mother Jones, citing a 2017 press release). Those 383 lives are the hard currency of this expression. He borrowed them.

He borrowed systematically. The V devices on his hat. His campaign said he had a Bronze Star with valor; he has the standard Bronze Star. When photos surfaced, he blamed his grandkids for attaching the emblems — a hat he wore for years, a hat that also identified him as a Desert Storm veteran.

There is a co-conspiracy detail worth watching. His consultant, Rory McShane, called the questions a "total non story," on the grounds that Congress and the Air Force "say he's a Desert Storm Veteran." Then he produced a 1991 act of Congress whose definition of Operation Desert Storm folds in Desert Shield, Desert Storm, and Provide Comfort.

Change the ruler. The capability stays the same; the evaluation method changes. Can't build the small chip, so declare a new law. Missed the war, so find a statute that counts the aftermath. Same move, except this time the ruler was written in 1991 to hand out benefits.

McShane also pointed to the Southwest Asia Service Medal. An Air Force website says it went to those who served in support of Desert Shield or Desert Storm between August 2, 1990 and November 30, 1995. Flippo deployed to Saudi Arabia in 1992, after his second son was born in April — by then it was a ceasefire campaign. His records don't list the Kuwait Liberation Medal, which Saudi Arabia gave to coalition forces who served in Desert Storm. The medal is real. The timing isn't.

This is not a story about vanity. Flippo has put more than $2.5 million into his campaigns with no obvious source of wealth. He hasn't filed legally required financial disclosures since launching his first bid in 2023. His campaign claimed donations from two dead people. He has ties to an Arizona land venture promising "constant" 20 percent returns for 20 years.

A man who redefines which year he wore which uniform — ask yourself how he defines a 20 percent return, and who donated that money.

Retired Air Force master sergeant Daniel Casentini said no veteran who actually served in Desert Storm would see him as one unless he was in theater during the operation. "Integrity First" is the first of the Air Force's three core values, he noted, and the irony is the point. Retired Army lieutenant colonel Scott Maryott put it plainly: the people most humble about their service probably did the most, and the ones who exaggerate show a character flaw.

Those two men are the only ones in this story doing subtraction. They added no narrative. They just put the ruler back where it was.

He wore that hat for years. The V was fake. The words "Desert Storm Veteran" were fake. The man wearing it was real — 1985 to 2009, relieved of his squadron command in 2008, out the following year. He had a real record to run on. He chose a different one.

The question isn't why he chose it. The question is that those 383 people had no choice, and their deaths got made into a line on a hat — a line that can still be explained away as a clerical error.
