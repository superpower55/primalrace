把防卫权当筹码：一场关于‘保护’的顶级 scamDefense as a Bargaining Chip: The Ultimate 'Protection' Scam

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-28 § 链接
所有将他者生存权作为谈判筹码的行为，本质都是元暴力的商业化
Turning the survival rights of others into negotiating chips is the commercialization of meta-violence.

把 140 亿美元的台湾军售方案定义为“很好的谈判筹码” (negotiating chip)，这句话精准地揭露了 Trump 这种男性中心叙事者的权力逻辑：在他们的认知入口里，世界不是由权利和义务组成的，而是由资产和筹码组成的。台湾的生存权，在 Trump 眼里不是一个关于人权或民主的 structural 议题，而是一个可以用来换取贸易让步或政治面子的 commodity。

更荒诞的是，他竟然试探性地询问 Xi 是否愿意买美国武器。这不仅是外交上的 anachronism，更是一次极具讽刺意味的博弈。在 Trump 的逻辑里，只要价格合适，无论是保护者还是潜在的威胁者，都可以被转化为客户。这种将暴力工具（weapons）商品化的姿态，正是典型的 masculine-centric narrative：通过垄断暴力手段的定义权和分配权，将所有参与者拉入一场关于“谁能给谁提供保护”的交易博弈中。

在这种叙事下，所谓的“战略模糊” (strategic ambiguity) 成了掩盖这种 opportunistic 行为的绝佳掩体。当他把军售放在“暂缓” (abeyance) 状态时，他实际上是在对台湾进行一种 structural violence——通过制造不安全感来榨取最大价值。而这种行为在男性权力圈层中被视为“高明的谈判艺术”，但在人权视角下，这不过是两个强权者在共谋场域中，通过物化一个第三方实体的生存状态来完成的一次权力互换。

Defining a $14bn Taiwan arms package as a “very good negotiating chip” precisely exposes the power logic of a masculine-centric narrative like Trump's: in his cognitive entry, the world is not composed of rights and obligations, but of assets and chips. Taiwan's right to exist is not a structural issue of human rights or democracy; it is a commodity to be traded for trade concessions or political prestige.

Even more absurd is his tentative inquiry into whether Xi would like to buy US weapons. This is not just diplomatic anachronism; it is a deeply ironic game. In Trump's logic, as long as the price is right, both the protector and the potential threat can be converted into customers. This posture of commodifying weapons is a textbook example of masculine-centric narrative: monopolizing the definition and distribution of violence to drag all players into a transaction game of "who can protect whom."

Under this narrative, "strategic ambiguity" serves as the perfect cover for such opportunistic behavior. By keeping arms sales in "abeyance," he is effectively exerting structural violence on Taiwan—extracting maximum value by manufacturing insecurity. In the male power circle, this is lauded as "the art of the deal," but from a human rights perspective, it is merely two hegemons in a field of complicity, exchanging power by objectifying the survival state of a third party.
