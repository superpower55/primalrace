挂毯上的红裙与真正的入侵Red Petticoats on the Wall

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
女人把裙子挂上墙，历史就把她们写成风景。
Women hung their skirts on a wall, and history hung them as scenery.

菲什加德那幅挂毯讲的是一场被红衬裙吓退的入侵。哈里斯家的女人们把红衬裙晾出来，法国人以为是穿红衣的士兵，退了。故事被织进挂毯，挂在墙上，供人参观。

这里有一件事值得停一下：这支吓退敌军的队伍，在叙事里留下的身份是“士兵”，而她们实际的身份是女人。红衬裙之所以有效，是因为它被误读成了军装。也就是说，她们赢的那一仗，靠的是让自己看起来像男人。这不是她们的最优解，这是当时唯一可用的表达。表达即身份的确立，而她们的身份在这场胜利里被抹掉了，只剩下一个视觉上的误会。

更值得看的是挂毯本身。挂毯是叙事机器，和新闻、宗教、教科书是同一类东西。它把一场具体的、可能混乱的、由几个女人临时起意的行动，织成一个干净的画面：红裙子，法国人，退兵。谁下的命令，谁害怕了，谁后来被记住，谁没有——画面不回答这些。它只负责让故事好看，让游客拍照。

然后这封信的版面还排着另一条：有人在抱怨风暴报道里总拍人在海堤上走。这条抱怨是对的，但它和红衬裙放在一起，恰好说明报纸的版面逻辑：女人的身体要么是风景，要么是危险行为的背景板，要么是吓退敌军的道具。三种用法，同一种功能——她们不是叙事的主体，是叙事的材料。

挂毯挂在菲什加德，游客去看红裙子。没人问那几个哈里斯家的女人当时怎么想的，她们有没有害怕，她们知不知道自己在赌什么。挂毯不会问，因为它织的是胜利，不是人。

The Fishguard tapestry tells of an invasion scared off by red petticoats. The Harries ladies hung them out; the French took them for soldiers in red and withdrew. The story got woven into a tapestry, hung on a wall, put on display.

One thing is worth pausing on: the troop that routed an army left the narrative with the identity of "soldiers," while their actual identity was women. The red petticoat worked because it was misread as a uniform. In other words, the battle they won depended on making themselves look like men. That was not their optimal expression; it was the only expression available. Expression establishes identity, and their identity was erased from this victory, leaving only a visual misunderstanding.

The tapestry itself deserves more attention. A tapestry is a narrative machine, the same species as news, religion, textbooks. It takes a specific, probably chaotic, improvised action by a few women and weaves it into a clean image: red skirts, Frenchmen, retreat. Who gave the order, who was afraid, who was remembered afterward, who was not—the image does not answer. It only makes the story look good, so tourists can photograph it.

The same letters page carries another item: someone complaining that storm coverage keeps photographing people walking along sea walls. The complaint is fair, but placed beside the red petticoats it exposes the page's logic: a woman's body is either scenery, or the backdrop to reckless behavior, or a prop that scares off an army. Three uses, one function—they are not the subjects of the narrative, they are its material.

The tapestry hangs in Fishguard; visitors come for the red skirts. Nobody asks what those Harries women were thinking, whether they were afraid, whether they knew what they were betting. The tapestry will not ask, because it weaves victory, not people.
