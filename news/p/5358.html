在真人秀的‘背叛’中，元暴力从未离场Meta-Violence Never Leaves the Set of 'The Traitors'

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-01 § 链接
当‘领导力’被预设为男性特权，背叛就成了男性的游戏。
When 'leadership' is preset as a male privilege, betrayal becomes a masculine playground.

看这种所谓的‘名流版’真人秀，最讽刺的不是那些明星在演戏，而是节目组在潜意识里完成了一次精准的元暴力 (meta violence) 投放。第一段就直接点破了：所谓的‘自封领导者’ (self-appointed leaders) 是前军人 James Blunt 和爱大吼大叫的 Ross Kemp。这种对‘领导力’的定义权被天然地交给了具备‘强男性特质’的人，而这种特质本身就是一种 weaponized 的表达——它告诉观众，掌控局面的能力与肌肉、嗓门和军事背景正相关。

在这个被设计好的‘背叛游戏’中，男性参与者在进行一场关于存在性战争的模拟。Richard E Grant 的古怪表演、Amol Rajan 对‘精神病态’ (psychopath) 的自我标榜，本质上都是在 masculine-centric narrative 下的权力博弈。他们可以扮演疯子、天才或背叛者，因为他们拥有最基础的、无需证明的‘主体性’。而女性参与者如 Maya Jama，在被选为 Traitor 后，被描述为‘瞬间变成迪士尼反派’。注意这个描述：女性的权力表达被简化为一种‘表演性的邪恶’，而男性的权力表达则被视为‘领导力’或‘博弈策略’。

这不仅仅是一场娱乐节目，这是一次关于共谋 (complicity) 的大规模演示。观众在消费这种‘紧张感’时，实际上在共谋维护一套旧的叙事：男性负责定义规则、争夺领导权并进行权力游戏；女性则在其中扮演被选中的棋子，或被贴上‘反派’标签的客体。在这种结构性暴力面前，所谓的‘游戏规则’只是掩体，它让这种根深蒂固的性别权力分配看起来像是一种‘综艺效果’。

Watching this 'Celebrity' reality show, the real irony isn't the acting, but the precise deployment of meta-violence by the producers. The opening immediately exposes it: the 'self-appointed leaders' are a former soldier and a man known for shouting. This definition of 'leadership' is naturally handed to those with 'strong masculine traits'—a weaponized expression telling the audience that the ability to control a situation is tied to muscles, volume, and military backgrounds.

In this choreographed game of betrayal, the male participants are engaging in a simulation of existential war. Richard E Grant’s eccentricity and Amol Rajan’s bragging about being a 'psychopath' are essentially power plays within a masculine-centric narrative. They can afford to play the fool, the genius, or the traitor because they possess a baseline 'subjectivity' that requires no proof. In contrast, Maya Jama, upon becoming a Traitor, is described as 'turning into a Disney villain.' Note the framing: female power is reduced to a 'performative evil,' while male power is viewed as 'leadership' or 'strategic gaming.'

This is more than entertainment; it is a mass demonstration of complicity. As the audience consumes this 'tension,' they are complicit in maintaining an old narrative: men define the rules, seize leadership, and play the power game, while women are cast as selected pawns or objects labeled as 'villains.' Faced with this structural violence, the 'rules of the game' are merely a cover, making a deep-seated gendered power distribution look like a 'variety show effect.'
