他只剩厌女这一件工具He Has Only Misogyny Left

性别 直接层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
当一个男人只剩厌女可打，他打的就是年轻女人的脸
When a man has only misogyny left, he aims it at a young woman's face.

特朗普对 NBC 记者 Monica Alba 发飙的那段录音，值得逐字看一遍。她问的是：这些性侵指控对你毫无影响吗？他先问她是哪家媒体的，听说是 NBC，说那是“最烂的电视台之一”，然后说：“安静。她是不是个新人？你是个糟糕的小记者，对吧？”

这不是失控。这是定价。一个年轻女人在公共场合要求他对性侵指控表态，他做的第一件事是确认她属于哪个阵营、有没有资格开口，第二件事是把她重新标价成“新人”“小记者”。表达在这里不是沟通，是把她从提问者的位置上拽下来，放回一个不需要被回答的位置。

Hemmer 在节目里说得很准：他并不是被问题难住，他是被三个开关同时按到——Epstein、他自己的指控者名单、以及曾被判负有性侵责任这件事。一个年轻女人胆敢追问，等于同时踩中这三处。所以他的反应不是回答，是把她赶出对话。

再往上一层看，那条 Ramaswamy 在俄亥俄州长选战里投放的广告才是真正的现场：Amy Acton 的母亲对着镜头说女儿是骗子，而报道说，Acton 的继父、也就是这位母亲的丈夫，当年强奸了她，她十二岁那年因此被从母亲身边带走。特朗普、Vance、Ramaswamy 三个人一起押注这件事能动员选民。

Sargent 在节目里说这是一场赌局：赌厌女在选举最后阶段还能赢。Hemmer 的回答更狠——这不是新策略，2016 年那场大会就是一场厌女狂欢，对希拉里的攻击从来不是政策，是对她作为一个女人的攻击；他赢的两次，对手都是女人。这套东西一直是那笔交易的一部分。

问题在于，这次连 Fox 都皱眉了。Mary Katharine Ham 说她不同意总统和 Vance，普通妈妈看到这条广告只会想：一个母亲对着镜头撕自己的女儿，最好手里真有货，而看起来她没有。另一位共和党顾问说，用家人做政治广告不新鲜，但你最好知道整个故事，而这次知道的方式是“最糟糕的那种”。Lawrence Jones 只说了一句：这很蠢。

Hemmer 点出了最要紧的一层：这些共和党女性本来并不买女权主义的账，对 #MeToo 也长期怀疑，Kavanaugh、特朗普、乃至最近的 Cornell 7，她们的默认姿势是不信。但这次是一个孩子被继父强奸、母亲选择了继父。这条线踩过去，就不是立场问题了，是本能反应，是“你没法靠 MAGA 绕过去”的那种反应。

所以真正的机制在这里：厌女不是他们的信仰，是他们的工具。Stephen Miller 在 2024 年选举夜发的那条推文大意是“男人们，去投票”——他们清楚女人投票他们就完了，所以要靠年轻男人。可中期选举不是总统选举，那批低投票率的年轻男性不会来，来的是一大群女人。《纽约时报》的全国民调里，民主党在女性选民上以 56 比 37 领先，十九个百分点。哈佛青年民调说三十岁以下男性近七成不认可他第二任期的表现。

工具钝了，他还在用。因为工具箱里就剩这一件，另一件是种族主义。

Vance 的算盘更露骨：一边放风说自己对战争有疑虑，一边全力拥抱这条广告，摆出“硬汉政治”的架势，要“把女性候选人碾进土里”。Sargent 说得直白，这就是他说的。但 Hemmer 的判断是，他卖不动——他不是特朗普，他更像 Ramaswamy，一开口就让人不适；而且他娶了印度裔妻子，在特朗普联盟的种族主义基本盘那里先天有账。

这一切的底色，是解释权的问题。一条把强奸受害者说成骗子的广告，之所以能成为选战中心，不是因为事实不清楚，而是因为有一整套叙事机器在决定“什么算事实”：母亲的眼泪算事实，女儿的创伤不算；提问的记者算“糟糕的小记者”，回答不回答由被问的人决定。这就是元暴力在运作的样子——它不需要否认强奸发生过，它只需要让追问强奸的人失去资格。

而这条广告里真正被反复使用的，是那个十二岁女孩的身体。她的创伤被拿来当政治素材，她的母亲被摆到镜头前，她的继父从头到尾没有出现在这场辩论里。谁在为这条广告付代价，报道写得很清楚；谁从这条广告里拿票，也写得很清楚。

Fox 上那几声“cringe”不是良心发现，是市场调研。他们闻到的是中期选举里女人投票的味道，不是正义的味道。等这条广告的收益算完，那些皱眉的人会重新排队——报道里 Hemmer 说的那句“他们最后总会归队”，才是这次风波里最不该被忘记的一句。

Listen to the tape of Trump going off on NBC reporter Monica Alba. She asks whether the sexual abuse allegations make no difference to him. He first asks who she's with, hears NBC, calls it one of the worst networks, then says: "Quiet. Isn't she a fresh person? You're a horrible little reporter, aren't you?"

This is not a loss of control. It is pricing. A young woman publicly demands that he answer for sexual abuse allegations, and his first move is to establish which camp she belongs to and whether she has standing to speak; his second is to re-price her as a "fresh person," a "little reporter." Expression here is not communication. It is dragging her out of the position of questioner and putting her back where she doesn't need to be answered.

Hemmer nails it on the podcast: he isn't stumped by the question, he's hit on three triggers at once—Epstein, his own list of accusers, and having already been found liable for sexual assault. A young woman daring to press him trips all three. So his response isn't an answer. It's removing her from the conversation.

One level up, the real scene is the ad Vivek Ramaswamy is running in the Ohio governor's race: Amy Acton's mother telling the camera her daughter is a liar, when, per the reporting, Acton's stepfather—her mother's husband—raped her, and she was taken from her mother at twelve because of those accusations. Trump, Vance, and Ramaswamy are all in on it.

Sargent calls it a bet: that misogyny can still win in the closing stretch. Hemmer's answer is sharper—this isn't new. The 2016 convention was a misogyny fest; the attacks on Hillary Clinton were never about policy but about her as a woman; the two elections he won, he won against women. This has been part of the bargain from the start.

What's new is that even Fox is cringing. Mary Katharine Ham says she disagrees with the president and Vance: normal moms watching a mother trash her daughter straight to camera will think, they'd better have the goods—and it doesn't appear they do. A Republican consultant says using family members in ads isn't new, but you'd better know the whole story, and this one came out "in the absolute worst possible way." Lawrence Jones: "I thought this was just stupid."

Hemmer points to the crucial layer: these Republican women were never on board with feminism, were skeptical of #MeToo, skeptical of claims against Kavanaugh and Trump and in the Cornell 7 case. But this is the rape of a child by a stepfather, and a mother choosing the stepfather over the child. That crosses a line that isn't about ideology. It's gut-level. You can't MAGA your way out of it.

So the actual mechanism: misogyny isn't their faith, it's their tool. Stephen Miller's election-night tweet in 2024 was essentially "men, get out to the polls"—they know that if women vote, they're screwed, so they need young men. But a midterm is not a presidential election. The low-propensity young men won't show. A whole lot of women will. The New York Times national poll has Democrats winning women 56 to 37, a nineteen-point edge. The Harvard Youth Poll finds nearly 70 percent of men under thirty disapprove of his second term.

The tool is dull and he's still swinging it, because it's one of the only things in the belt, along with racism.

Vance's calculation is more naked: leak that he was a war skeptic, then go full misogyny on the ad, play the tough guy grinding the female candidate into the dirt. Sargent says it plainly—that's what he's saying. Hemmer's read is that he can't sell it. He's not Trump; he's closer to Ramaswamy, off-putting every time he opens his mouth. And he's married to an Indian woman, which is a problem with the racist base of the coalition.

The底色 here is interpretive power. An ad calling a rape victim a liar can become the center of a campaign not because the facts are unclear, but because a narrative machine decides what counts as a fact: the mother's tears count, the daughter's trauma doesn't; the reporter asking is a "horrible little reporter," and whether to answer is up to the man being asked. That is meta-violence at work—it doesn't need to deny the rape happened. It only needs to disqualify the person asking about it.

And what the ad keeps using is a twelve-year-old girl's body. Her trauma becomes political material, her mother is put before the camera, her stepfather never appears in the debate at all. Who pays for this ad is in the reporting. Who profits from it is too.

The cringing on Fox isn't a conscience waking up. It's market research. They smell women voting in a midterm, not justice. Once the ad's returns are tallied, the cringers will fall back in line—Hemmer's line about everyone eventually coalescing is the one thing from this whole mess that shouldn't be forgotten.
