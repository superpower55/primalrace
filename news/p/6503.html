首位女总统，和一场27%的投票A First Female President, and a 27% Turnout

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把女性推上最高位置，不等于把女性从结构里解放出来。
Putting a woman at the top is not the same as taking women out of the structure.

保加利亚要出第一位民选女国家元首了。这条新闻最值得看的不是这个，是27%到29%的投票率——上一轮23%，创下历史最低。

一个国家的总统选举，七成多的人没去。报道里两位选民说得很清楚：一位64岁的教师说她两个都不喜欢，还是去投了；一位67岁的退休工程师上轮没去，因为他早就料到会有第二轮，这轮投的是"两害相权取其轻"，没有一个候选人符合他"对民主的想象"。这不是冷漠，这是精确的估价——在一个总统职位"largely ceremonial"的国家里，选票的边际价值被选民自己算过了。

所以"首位女总统"这个头衔，要先放在这个背景里看。仪礼性职位上的女性面孔，是structural层最便宜的一种改变：它不重新分配任何资源，不改变任何决策权，只改变谁站在台上。这叫代表性，不叫权力。把它当成女权的胜利，等于把橱窗当成仓库。

真正的暴力在这条新闻的另一头。上月黑海沿岸商船遭一系列无人机袭击，21人仍然失踪。报道说这疑似是俄罗斯对乌克兰战争的溢出，总理说这些袭击看起来符合莫斯科的利益，召见了俄罗斯大使，同时政府又说没有证据直接指向莫斯科。21个人失踪，没有名字，没有国籍，没有年龄，在报道里是一句"21 people remain missing"。

然后这21个人变成了选战里的一个攻防点。Gyurov在电视辩论里批评Iotova没有明确指责俄罗斯，"俄罗斯必须停止侵略"；Iotova说"和平只能通过谈判实现"。分析人士Slavev说，选民是在对Iotova"在俄罗斯黑海威胁上缺乏说服力的回应"作出判断。

看这个机制：21条人命先被转译成"俄罗斯威胁"这个议题，再被转译成候选人的表态得分，最后被转译成选票。人在这个链条上出现了三次，每一次都不是作为人。这是文化层的操作——用"安全""主权""和平"这些词，把具体的死亡抽象成可竞标的立场。Iotova说她要"让保加利亚在一个强大的欧洲里强大"，"在自己的决定上主权"。这两句话可以同时成立，也正因如此，它们什么也没说。

还有一个细节值得记下：Iotova是接替Radev当代理总统的，Radev辞职去参加议会选举并赢了。她的位置来自一个男性盟友的让渡，不是来自一场独立的政治积累。这不否定她赢下的58%，但它说明这个"第一"是怎么被安排出来的。

结尾我想说的是那21个人。他们的失踪被写进了选战，却没有被写进任何人的承诺。周一官方结果出来，Iotova会成为第一位民选女总统，黑海上还有21个家庭在等一个他们可能永远等不到的消息。差额就在这里：一个国家可以把女性送上最高礼仪位置，同时让21个具体的人在新闻里保持无名。

Bulgaria is about to get its first elected female head of state. That is not the most interesting thing in this story. The 27 to 29 percent turnout is — up from 23 percent last week, which was a record low.

More than seven in ten people did not vote in a presidential election. Two voters in the report say it plainly: a 64-year-old teacher in Sofia said she liked neither candidate and voted anyway; a 67-year-old retired engineer skipped the first round because he expected a run-off, and this time voted for "the lesser evil," since neither matched his "vision of democracy." This is not apathy. It is an accurate valuation. In a country where the presidency is "largely ceremonial," the voters have done the marginal-value math themselves.

So read "first female head of state" against that backdrop. A female face in a ceremonial office is the cheapest possible structural change: it redistributes no resources, shifts no decision-making power, and changes only who stands on the stage. That is representation, not power. Calling it a feminist win is mistaking the display window for the warehouse.

The real violence sits at the other end of the piece. Last month a series of drone attacks hit commercial vessels off the Black Sea coast; 21 people remain missing. The report says this is suspected spillover from Russia's war on Ukraine, that the prime minister said the attacks appear to serve Moscow's interests and summoned the Russian ambassador, while his government said there was no evidence directly linking them to Moscow. Twenty-one people missing — no names, no nationalities, no ages, one clause: "21 people remain missing."

Then those 21 become a point of attack in the campaign. Gyurov criticised Iotova in a TV debate for not explicitly blaming Russia: "Russia must stop its aggression so that the next steps can be taken." Iotova said "peace can come only through negotiations." Analyst Teodor Slavev said voters were passing judgement on "Iotova's unconvincing response regarding Russian threats in the Black Sea."

Watch the mechanism: 21 lives get translated into the issue of "the Russian threat," then into a candidate's rhetorical score, then into votes. The people appear three times in that chain, and never once as people. This is cultural-layer work — using "security," "sovereignty," "peace" to abstract specific deaths into biddable positions. Iotova said she wants "Bulgaria to be strong in a strong Europe" and "sovereign in its decisions." Both sentences can be true at once, which is exactly why they say nothing.

One more detail worth noting: Iotova became acting president when Radev, her longtime ally, resigned to run in and win a parliamentary election. Her position came from a male ally's handover, not from an independent political accumulation. That does not negate the 58 percent she won, but it tells you how the "first" was arranged.

I end with the 21. Their disappearance was written into the campaign and into no one's promise. On Monday the official results come in, Iotova becomes the first elected female president, and 21 families on the Black Sea wait for news they may never get. The gap is right there: a country can lift a woman to its highest ceremonial office while keeping 21 specific people nameless in the news.
