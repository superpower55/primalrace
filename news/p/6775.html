承诺选举，不等于交出解释权A Promise of Elections Is Not a Handover of the Power to Define Them

国际 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要委内瑞拉临时总统德尔西·罗德里格斯上周罕见发表讲话，承诺举行选举。但报道指出，选举日期尚未确定，反对派领袖马查多随后也强调了这一点。罗德里格斯已任命前反对派助手奥利弗·布兰科为外长，显示其与华盛顿合作的意愿。
The newsVenezuela's interim president, Delcy Rodríguez, vowed in a rare address last week to hold elections, but no date has been set, opposition leader María Corina Machado noted afterward. Rodríguez has named former opposition aide Oliver Blanco as foreign minister, signaling interest in working with Washington.
没有日期的选举，是一张不写金额的支票
An election without a date is a cheque with no amount written on it.

「承诺举行选举」这句话，在委内瑞拉现在的语境里，先要问的不是民不民主，而是谁在给「选举」这个词定价。

罗德里格斯是临时总统，这个「临时」来自马杜罗在一月被美国逮捕。她的权力不是选出来的，是那场逮捕之后留下来的空位。她现在承诺选举，等于是在说：这个空位将来可以由票来填。但报道写得很清楚——没有日期。没有日期的承诺不是承诺，是一张不写金额的支票，随时可以兑现，也随时可以宣布作废。

马查多随后强调的正是这一点。反对派领袖要的不是「我们会选」，是「哪一天」。这个区别看起来是技术性的，其实是全部：日期一旦定下，权力交接就有了倒计时，既得利益者就失去了拖延的空间。不定日期，选举就永远停在「即将」里，而「即将」可以维持很多年。

更值得看的是布兰科这条线。罗德里格斯把前反对派助手放到外长位置上，同时公开 rhetoric 上还保持着某种姿态。这是一个很典型的动作：不改变权力结构，只改变权力结构对外说话的人。华盛顿要的是「合作信号」，这个任命就是信号本身，成本极低，不需要定日期，不需要放权，只需要换一张脸出现在谈判桌对面。

美国中期选举刚过，报道说华盛顿会重新对这件事产生兴趣。这句话才是整篇里最冷的一句。委内瑞拉的选举时间表，取决于华盛顿的政治日历，而不是委内瑞拉人的。一个国家的过渡被另一个国家的选举周期牵着走，这本身就是结构暴力的样子——不是谁打了谁，是有些人的时间不属于自己。

所以现在的问题不是「委内瑞拉能不能民主过渡」，而是：这场过渡里，谁有资格定日期，谁只能等日期。罗德里格斯在等华盛顿，马查多在等罗德里格斯，而委内瑞拉人在这条链的末端，手里什么都没有。

报道里没有出现任何一个普通委内瑞拉人的名字。这不是记者的疏忽，是这条新闻的结构：它从头到尾是三个权力节点之间的对话，人民是背景。等到日期真的定下来那天，值得再问一次：那个日期是为谁定的。

The first question about「promising elections」in Venezuela is not whether it is democratic. It is who gets to set the price of the word「election.」

Rodríguez is an interim president, and the「interim」comes from Maduro's arrest by the US in January. Her power was not voted in; it is the vacancy left behind by that arrest. Promising elections is her way of saying that vacancy can someday be filled by ballots. But the report is clear: no date. A promise without a date is not a promise. It is a cheque with no amount written on it — cashable anytime, voidable anytime.

Machado's remark afterward is exactly this point. What the opposition leader wants is not「we will hold elections」but「on which day.」The difference looks technical; it is everything. Once a date is fixed, the handover has a countdown, and incumbents lose the room to stall. Without a date, elections stay in「soon,」and「soon」can last for years.

The Blanco appointment is the more telling move. Putting a former opposition aide into the foreign ministry while keeping some of the public rhetoric intact is a classic operation: don't change the power structure, change the person who speaks for it. Washington wants a signal of cooperation; the appointment is the signal itself, at minimal cost — no date, no handover, just a new face across the table.

The coldest line in the piece is that with the US midterms over, Washington will soon take renewed interest. Venezuela's election timetable depends on Washington's political calendar, not on Venezuelans'. A country's transition being paced by another country's election cycle is what structural violence looks like — not someone hitting someone, but some people's time not belonging to them.

So the question is not whether Venezuela can transition to democracy. It is who gets to set the date, and who can only wait for it. Rodríguez waits on Washington, Machado waits on Rodríguez, and Venezuelans sit at the end of that chain holding nothing.

Not a single ordinary Venezuelan appears in the report. That is not the reporter's oversight; it is the structure of the story — a conversation among three power nodes, with the people as backdrop. When the date finally comes, ask again: who was that date for.
