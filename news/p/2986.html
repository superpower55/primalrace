Badass 叙事的温情陷阱与主体性死亡The Sentimental Trap of the 'Badass' Narrative and the Death of Subjectivity

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
将结构性痛苦浪漫化为“成长神话”，是文化暴力最隐蔽的 weaponization。
Romanticizing structural suffering as a 'coming-of-age myth' is the most covert weaponization of cultural violence.

这部电影试图把 Anthony Bourdain 早年的厨房生活包装成一个 sentimental 的 coming-of-age story。但请注意，在这个叙事里，年轻的 Tony 学习的是如何成为一个 "tough guy" 和 "player"。所谓的“成长”，本质上是进入一套由男性中心叙事定义的权力等级制——在酒精、可卡因和对女性的物化中，通过扮演一个 an arrogant, smug 但又脆弱的男性角色，来换取在男性社群中的 entry ticket。

这种叙事最危险的地方在于它将 structural violence 转化为一种审美。厨房里的高压、殴打和精神崩溃被定义为“锻造”一个 legendary badass 的必要过程。当电影将这些危机处理成“需要被克服的障碍”时，它实际上是在抹除 Bourdain 真实生命中那些无法被克服的 depression 和 instability。这种 feel-good trajectory 是一种典型的 cultural violence：它告诉观众，只要你足够“硬”，所有的精神内耗都可以被转化为一种迷人的、带有忧郁色彩的男性特权标签。

最讽刺的是，电影试图通过引入 Vatel 自杀的典故来暗示 Bourdain 的结局，但这恰恰证明了这种叙事的共谋性。它试图将一个真实的悲剧转化为一种“极致完美主义”的浪漫注脚。当一个人的痛苦被定义为“艺术性的追求”或“英雄式的陨落”时，他的主体性就彻底死亡了。他不再是一个在结构性压迫中挣扎的人，而变成了一个供后人消费的、关于“坏男孩”的 origin myth。

This film attempts to package Anthony Bourdain's early kitchen life as a sentimental coming-of-age story. But notice that in this narrative, the young Tony is learning how to become a "tough guy" and a "player." This so-called "growth" is essentially an initiation into a power hierarchy defined by a masculine-centric narrative—where one trades in alcohol, cocaine, and the objectification of women to earn an entry ticket into the male collective by performing the role of an arrogant, smug, yet vulnerable man.

The most dangerous part of this narrative is the transformation of structural violence into an aesthetic. The high pressure, the punch-ups, and the mental breakdowns in the kitchen are defined as necessary processes to forge a "legendary badass." When the film treats these crises as "obstacles to be surmounted," it effectively erases the real, insurmountable depression and instability of Bourdain's actual life. This feel-good trajectory is a classic form of cultural violence: it tells the audience that as long as you are "tough" enough, all mental attrition can be converted into a glamorous, melancholic label of masculine privilege.

Most ironically, the film's attempt to foreshadow Bourdain's suicide via the Vatel anecdote only proves its complicity. It seeks to transform a genuine tragedy into a romantic footnote of "extreme perfectionism." When a person's suffering is redefined as an "artistic pursuit" or a "heroic fall," their subjectivity is completely dead. They are no longer a human struggling within structural oppression, but a consumable origin myth of the "bad boy."
