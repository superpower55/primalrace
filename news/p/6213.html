选票算数，人却不算The Votes Count, the Voters Don't

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
19个席位进了账本，投票的人被叫作恐怖分子
Nineteen seats in the ledger; the people who cast them, called terrorists.

以色列的阿拉伯政党拿了19席，将近84万人投了票，比2022年多了64%（来源：Al Jazeera）。数字进了账本，人没有。右翼圈子把这次投票称作「选举恐怖主义」，还把它和10月7日的袭击相提并论——一个政党在选举里多拿了席位，被说成和一场杀死一千二百多人的袭击同构。这就是武器化叙事最标准的动作：把对手合法的表达重新命名成暴力，命名完了，就不必再和它谈条件。

加尔通的三角在这里三层同时转动。直接层是10月7日，那场袭击被反复调用，成了万能背书。文化层是「恐怖分子」这个词，它一旦贴上，19席就从民意变成威胁。结构层是组阁的算术：内塔尼亚胡阵营53席，艾森科特48席，谁都要那19席才能过半数，可艾森科特此前已经公开排除与阿拉伯政党联合组阁。选票在制度里是硬的，在文化里是软的——阿拉伯选民把票投进了议会，议会外的舆论却还在争论他们配不配算数。

特拉维夫街头那几千人喊的是「最后一次真正改变的机会」，他们要艾森科特听选民的话。这句话本身没错，但它绕开了一个更硬的问题：为什么和阿拉伯政党合作在以色列政治里是「政治上难以下咽」的？不是算术难，67席的多数很舒服。难的是元层——谁被默认算作政治共同体的一员，谁被默认是外来的、可疑的、需要被审查的。这个默认不写在法律里，写在每一次把阿拉伯公民的投票叫作「偷来的」的时候。

内塔尼亚胡指控阿拉伯政党「偷走」选举、接受卡塔尔资助，卡塔尔否认，称之为虚假信息 campaign。这套指控的功能不在于真假，在于把选举结果本身变成需要重新验证的东西。一旦结果需要验证，验证权就在指控者手里。这是解释权的争夺，不是席位的争夺。

反示威者喊的是「不能有一个和所谓恐怖分子在一起的政府」。注意「所谓」两个字——它承认这个标签是可以被质疑的，却照样拿来用。共谋不需要每个人都真心相信，只需要每个人都照着说。艾森科特至今没有松口，说明这套共谋在他那里也生效了：19席可以帮他上台，但拿了这19席，他就要为「和阿拉伯人合作」付政治代价。代价由谁承担，账本上没写。

真正被削掉的差额在这里：84万张票已经投出，制度却还没有准备好把它们当成同等分量的表达来接收。这不是组阁的技术问题，是一个共同体在决定谁算人的问题。那19席最后会不会被用上，取决于有人愿不愿意为它付代价——而付代价的从来不是喊「最后一次机会」的那几千人。

Israel's Arab-led parties won 19 seats. Nearly 840,000 people voted for Ra'am and the Joint List, a 64 percent increase over 2022 (source: Al Jazeera). The number entered the ledger. The people did not. Right-wing circles described the historic turnout of Palestinian Israelis as "electoral terrorism" and equated it with the October 7 attack — a party gaining seats in an election, framed as structurally identical to an assault that killed more than 1,200 people. This is the standard move of weaponized narrative: rename the opponent's legitimate expression as violence, and once renamed, you no longer have to negotiate with it.

All three layers of Galtung's triangle turn at once here. The direct layer is October 7, invoked as universal authorization. The cultural layer is the word "terrorist": once applied, 19 seats stop being public opinion and become a threat. The structural layer is coalition arithmetic — Netanyahu's bloc at 53, Eisenkot's at 48, neither able to govern without those 19, while Eisenkot had already publicly ruled out a coalition with the Arab parties. Votes are hard inside the institution and soft outside it: Arab voters put their ballots into the Knesset, and the discourse outside keeps debating whether they should count.

The thousands in Tel Aviv chanted that this may be the "last chance for real change" and demanded Eisenkot listen to the voters. True enough, and it sidesteps the harder question: why is partnering with Arab parties politically unpalatable in Israel? The arithmetic isn't hard — 67 seats is a comfortable majority. What's hard is the meta layer: who is默认 counted as a member of the political community, and who is默认 foreign, suspect, subject to verification. That default isn't written in law. It's written every time an Arab citizen's ballot is called stolen.

Netanyahu accused the Palestinian-Israeli parties of "stealing" the polls and taking Qatari funding; Qatar denied it and called the allegations a disinformation campaign. The function of the accusation isn't truth-value. It's to turn the election result itself into something requiring re-verification — and once verification is required, the verifier holds the power. This is a fight over interpretive authority, not over seats.

The counter-protesters shouted that Israel should not have a government with "so-called terrorists." Note the "so-called": it concedes the label is contestable and uses it anyway. Complicity doesn't require belief. It requires repetition. Eisenkot still hasn't budged, which means the complicity holds for him too: those 19 seats could put him in office, but taking them means paying the political price of "cooperating with Arabs." Who pays that price is not on the ledger.

The gap being narrowed is this: 840,000 ballots have been cast, and the system is not yet prepared to receive them as expressions of equal weight. This is not a technical coalition problem. It is a community deciding who counts as a person. Whether those 19 seats get used depends on someone willing to pay the price — and the price is never paid by the few thousand chanting "last chance."
