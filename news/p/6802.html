把居留权按工资定价Residency Priced by Payslip

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要英国内政大臣马哈茂德（Shabana Mahmood）在内阁就部分外籍工人定居等待期延长至15年的提议失势后作出部分让步，但保留将标准定居资格期从5年翻倍至10年的方案，并坚持以纳税额、英语水平和申请人「品行」作为定居条件，获首相「全力支持」。报道称，2022至2025年间入境、年薪2.5万英镑以上的护工及其家属约14.6万人和13.6万名受养人仍可按5年申请永居，不达标者须再申请一次5年「健康与社会照护」签证；庇护申请者中经小船「非法入境」者即使被认定需要保护，等待期可长达30年。
The newsUK home secretary Shabana Mahmood has partially retreated on her plan to make some foreign workers wait 15 years for settlement, but kept the core: the standard qualifying period doubles from five to 10 years, and settlement now hinges on tax paid, English proficiency and an applicant's "character", with the prime minister's "full support". Care workers who arrived between 2022 and 2025 earning £25,000 or more can still apply for indefinite leave to remain after five years—up to 146,000 workers and 136,000 dependants—while those who fall short need another five-year visa. Asylum seekers who arrived by small boat face waits of up to 30 years even after protection needs are recognised.

定居权被拆成工资单上的数字，谁付得起谁才算扎根。马哈茂德在内阁里输掉的只是「15年」这个数字，赢下的是整张尺子：住多久不算数，交了多少税、英语考到几分、品行由谁认定才算数。首相给了「全力支持」，说明这不是失手，是设计。

内政部自己的数字就拆了这套逻辑。年薪£41,700的人终身财政贡献与年薪£50,271的人相当，前者却要多等五年；年薪约£125,000、进入收入前2%的人只需三年。同一份贡献，不同的等待。这不是在筛选谁更有益于英国，是在给不同价位的身体标注不同的入籍速度——钱多的先上岸，钱少的在岸上排队。

护工这一段最能看清机制。疫情后英国招人来填照护岗位的空缺，这是对的；然后政府拿这份工作报酬低当理由，推迟他们成为社会成员的时间。马哈茂德让了一步：2022到2025年间入境、时薪条件达标、年薪£25,000以上的护工及家属，约14.6万人和13.6万名受养人，仍可按五年申请永居。不达标的，再签一次五年「健康与社会照护」签证。

问题在于，谁来判断「不达标」是工人自己的选择，还是雇主故意压低的？报道写得很清楚：照护行业一直有指控称，不良雇主利用员工脆弱的签证身份进行剥削。政府说不清怎么分辨工资是不是被无良公司刻意压低。说不清，就等于把这个判断权交给了压低工资的那一方。工人被剥削一次，还要为被剥削的结果再等五年。

庇护那条线更直接。经「安全合法」途径入境的人五年可定居；乘小船「非法入境」的人，即使已被认定需要保护，等待期可长达30年。联合国说，难民无法直接向英国申请重新安置。那么政府需要解释：因为没能使用一条基本不对他们开放的途径而惩罚他们，怎么算公平。这不是在管理边境，是在用一个人到达的方式给他的余生定价。

报道用了「客工」（Gastarbeiter）这个词，指向战后西德：让外国人填补低薪岗位的劳动力缺口，同时把他们的社会成员资格保持为有条件。这叫融合很勉强。定居下来、自由换工作、规划家庭生活，这些才是扎根的动作；马哈茂德的方案恰恰在阻挠她自己声称想要的融合。

让步是真的，但让步只发生在最难看的那一刀上。尺子还在，握尺子的手还在，被量的人还在排队。

Settlement is now a number on a payslip: pay enough and you belong. What Mahmood lost in cabinet was the number 15. What she kept was the ruler itself—how long you have lived here no longer counts; how much tax you paid, what score you got in English, and who certifies your "character" do. The prime minister's "full support" tells you this is not a slip. It is the design.

The Home Office's own figures dismantle the logic. Someone earning £41,700 makes a lifetime fiscal contribution similar to someone on £50,271, yet waits five years longer. Someone on about £125,000, the top 2% of earners, waits three years. Same contribution, different wait. This is not selecting who benefits Britain. It is stamping different speeds of naturalisation onto differently priced bodies—the rich disembark first, the rest queue on the shore.

The care worker passage shows the mechanism plainest. Britain recruited people after the pandemic to fill care shortages, which was right. Then the government used the low pay of that work as grounds for delaying their membership in society. Mahmood conceded a step: those who arrived between 2022 and 2025, met their visa conditions and earn £25,000 or more—up to 146,000 workers and 136,000 dependants—can still apply for indefinite leave to remain after five years. Those who fall short need another five-year health and social care visa.

So who decides whether falling short was the worker's choice or the employer's? The report is clear: the care sector has been beset by allegations that unscrupulous employers exploit migrant staff through their vulnerable visa status. The government cannot say how it will tell whether earnings were deliberately kept low by unprincipled firms. Not being able to tell means handing that judgment to the side that suppresses the wages. The worker is exploited once, then made to wait five more years for the result of the exploitation.

Asylum is more direct still. Arrive through a "safe and legal" route and you can settle in five years; arrive by small boat and, even once your need for protection is recognised, you may wait up to 30. The UN says refugees cannot apply directly for UK resettlement. So ministers must explain how penalising people for not using a route largely unavailable to them is fair play. This is not border management. It is pricing the rest of a life by the manner of arrival.

The report reaches for Gastarbeiter, postwar West Germany: admit foreigners to fill labour shortages in low-earning work while keeping their membership conditional. Calling that integration is a stretch. Settling down, changing jobs at will, planning a family—those are the acts of putting down roots. Mahmood's plans frustrate the very integration she says she wants.

The retreat is real, but it happened only on the ugliest cut. The ruler is still there, the hand holding it is still there, and the people being measured are still in line.
