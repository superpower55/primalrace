所谓的“激励”，不过是结构性暴力的另一种翻译The So-called 'Incentive' is Just a Translation of Structural Violence

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
当权力试图用“激励”来掩盖“剥夺”时，这本质上是一场生存权的博弈。
When power masks 'deprivation' as 'incentive', it is fundamentally a gamble with the right to exist.

Alan Milburn 的逻辑非常典型：他把一个人的生存底线（disability benefits）描述成了阻碍其进步的“陷阱”。这种叙事最阴险的地方在于，它将结构性的资源匮乏伪装成了个体的“缺乏动力”。在权力者的算盘里，1:25 的支出比例被定义为“激励失衡”，但他们刻意忽略了，对于一个身患复杂生理或心理疾病的年轻人来说，那笔钱不是用来“躺平”的，而是用来在生物墙面前维持最起码的生存尊严的。

这其实是一次标准的 structural violence 升级尝试。政府试图通过削减 health element 来强行将 disabled young people 驱赶进劳动力市场。但这根本不是在创造 opportunity，而是在进行一场残酷的生存筛选。正如 Scope 的数据所示，一旦支持被移除，九成以上的家庭将陷入贫困。这种所谓的“鼓励就业”，本质上是通过制造极端的生存恐惧，强迫弱势群体接受任何形式的、哪怕是剥削性的低端岗位。这不是在帮他们找工作，而是在通过剥夺生存权来降低劳动力成本。

而那些被提及的“实习计划”或“教育改革”，不过是 cultural violence 的掩体。通过在话语中加入“turbocharge”或“potential”这样的积极词汇，政府试图让这次削减看起来像是一场“为了年轻人好”的现代化改革。在这种叙事下，拒绝被驱赶的人被贴上“不积极”的标签，而执行剥夺的官员则成了“救世主”。

这场博弈的本质是：国家不愿支付维持一个原初种族/弱势群体生存的成本，于是决定通过重新定义“激励”来合法化这种抛弃。当生存底线被当作筹码，所谓的“就业率”不过是权力者在报表上刷出的数字，而真实的代价则由那些被推入贫困深渊的年轻人承担。

Alan Milburn's logic is a textbook example: he describes a person's survival baseline—disability benefits—as a 'trap' hindering progress. The most insidious part of this narrative is how it disguises structural resource deprivation as a lack of individual 'motivation'. In the eyes of power, a 1:25 spending ratio is defined as an 'incentive imbalance', deliberately ignoring that for a young person facing complex biological walls, that money isn't for 'slacking'—it is for maintaining minimal human dignity.

This is a standard attempt to escalate structural violence. The government seeks to forcibly drive disabled young people into the labor market by cutting the health element. This isn't about creating opportunity; it is a brutal survival screening. As Scope's data suggests, removing this support would plunge over 90% of these households into poverty. This 'encouragement to work' is actually the use of extreme survival terror to force vulnerable groups into any form of exploitative, low-end employment. It is not about finding jobs; it is about lowering labor costs by stripping away the right to survive.

Meanwhile, the mentioned 'internship programs' or 'education reforms' serve as shields of cultural violence. By injecting positive terms like 'turbocharge' or 'potential' into the discourse, the government attempts to frame this deprivation as a 'modernization reform' for the benefit of the youth. In this narrative, those who resist being driven out are labeled 'inactive', while the officials executing the cuts are cast as 'saviors'.

The essence of this game is simple: the state is unwilling to pay the cost of maintaining the survival of a marginalized group, so it decides to legitimize this abandonment by redefining 'incentives'. When the baseline of survival is treated as a bargaining chip, the 'employment rate' is merely a number on a report for the powerful, while the actual cost is borne by the youth pushed into the abyss of poverty.
