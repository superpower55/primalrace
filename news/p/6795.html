Meta 买了饥饿游戏的痛感Meta Buys the Hunger Games' Pain

文娱 文化层 IndieWire ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要狮门影业与 Meta 合作，将《饥饿游戏》前传《Sunrise on the Reaping》转制为 3D，影片 11 月 20 日在影院上映，之后将上线 Meta 计划于 2027 年春季推出的 VR 眼镜。这是 Meta 首次与好莱坞片厂合作做电影 3D 转制，由导演 Francis Lawrence 参与适配。
The newsLionsgate has partnered with Meta to convert its Hunger Games prequel Sunrise on the Reaping into 3D. The film opens in theaters November 20 and will later be available on Meta's VR Glasses, due in spring 2027. It is Meta's first such 3D conversion deal with a Hollywood studio.

A dystopia becomes a gimmick; the pain becomes the selling point.

《饥饿游戏》讲的是权力把人的身体做成景观，现在这部电影自己成了景观的供应商。Meta 和狮门合作，把前传转成 3D，再搬上 2027 年春季开卖的 VR 眼镜。导演 Francis Lawrence 说，3D 是让观众「更接近 Haymitch 的视角」的创作工具。把一个人被丢进竞技场、被观看、被消费的视角，做成沉浸式体验——这句话本身就是原著最想讽刺的东西。

这是第三章的教科书案例：认知入口的争夺。Meta 要进娱乐业，和 YouTube、Google 抢注意力，它缺的不是技术，是「premium content」，一部自带全球粉丝的 IP。狮门要的是票房和渠道。双方各取所需，新闻稿里叫「first-of-its-kind collaboration」。真正被定价的是观众的注意力，以及观众愿意为「更沉浸」再付一次钱。

注意这个时间差。影院 11 月 20 日上映，VR 眼镜 2027 年春季才上架。也就是说，一部电影被切成两次售卖：先在影院卖一次 3D 票，再在两年后卖一次「entirely new way」。同一部片，同一批人，付两遍。这套模式不新，3D 重映、导演剪辑版、流媒体独家窗口，都是同一个逻辑：把已经生产出来的内容，用新的容器再卖一次。VR 眼镜只是最新的容器。

值得说的是，这篇报道里没有一个人在为这件事付代价。没有演员谈片场，没有观众谈票价，没有 VR 用户谈眩晕和眼睛。Sarah Malkin 说这是「premium entertainment the way it's meant to be felt」——被感受的方式，由卖设备的人定义。这句话里的「meant」，是谁的意思？报道没写。

《饥饿游戏》的原著里，竞技场的设计者从不觉得自己在施暴，他们觉得自己在「做节目」。Meta 和狮门现在做的，是同一件事的温和版本：不杀人，只卖沉浸感。观众不会死，只会多付一次钱，多戴一副眼镜，多让一家公司知道你的眼球在什么时候转向哪里。

Francis Lawrence 说想让观众「pull deep into Haymitch's experience」。Haymitch 的经历是被迫观看、被迫参与、被迫成为景观。把这种经历做成 3D，卖给坐在沙发上的人，这件事本身比电影好看。

The Hunger Games is about power turning bodies into spectacle. Now the film is supplying the spectacle itself. Meta is partnering with Lionsgate to convert the prequel to 3D and later put it on VR Glasses due in spring 2027. Director Francis Lawrence says 3D is a creative tool to bring audiences closer to Haymitch's perspective. Turning the perspective of a person thrown into an arena, watched and consumed, into an immersive experience — that sentence is the thing the original books were satirizing.

This is the Chapter 3 textbook case: the fight for the cognitive entry point. Meta wants into entertainment, to compete with YouTube and Google, and what it lacks isn't technology but premium content — an IP with a global fanbase. Lionsgate wants box office and a channel. Each gets what it needs; the press release calls it a first-of-its-kind collaboration. What actually gets priced is the audience's attention, and the audience's willingness to pay again for more immersion.

Note the gap. The film opens November 20; the glasses arrive spring 2027. One movie sold twice: a 3D ticket now, an entirely new way two years later. Same film, same people, paying twice. The model isn't new — 3D re-releases, director's cuts, streaming exclusivity windows all run on the same logic: take content already produced and sell it again in a new container. VR glasses are just the latest container.

What's notable is that no one in this report pays a price. No actor talks about the set, no viewer about ticket prices, no VR user about nausea or eye strain. Sarah Malkin says this is premium entertainment the way it's meant to be felt — the way it's meant to be felt, defined by the company selling the device. Whose meant is that? The report doesn't say.

In the original Hunger Games, the arena designers never thought they were committing violence. They thought they were making a show. Meta and Lionsgate are doing the same thing in a gentler version: no one dies, you just pay again, wear a pair of glasses, and let one company know where your eyes turn and when.

Lawrence says he wants to pull audiences deep into Haymitch's experience. Haymitch's experience is being forced to watch, forced to participate, forced to become spectacle. Turning that into 3D and selling it to people on their couches is a better story than the movie.
