谁在给选举结果定价Who Gets to Price an Election

国际 元暴力 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
选举结果的合法性，由解释权决定，不由选票决定。
The legitimacy of a result is set by who interprets it, not by the votes.

特朗普这套操作的重点不在赢，而在让结果本身变成一个待定项。阿拉斯加州长邓利维被问到会不会认证民主党人佩尔托拉胜选时，说的是“如果查出不该发生的事、违法的事，我们会采取行动，这可能影响某人能否进入参议院”。注意这句话的结构：认证不是义务，是一个可以“采取行动”影响的条件。选举结果从“计票产物”变成“州长的裁量对象”，这一步不需要成功，只需要说出来。

报道自己承认，民主党那边也有把戏：纽约时报披露民主党操作者包括佩尔托拉竞选团队的人，鼓动一批人出来竞选参议员，分流共和党在任者沙利文的票。作者说这很下作，但属于常规的 ratfucking。我同意前半句。问题在于双方都这么干的时候，谁有权力把对方的把戏定义成“犯罪”，谁就有权力把自己的把戏定义成“政治”。邓利维已经下令刑事调查，特朗普的司法部已经起诉要求阿拉斯加不得认证。同一套动作，一边叫调查，一边叫干预。

法律专家在这篇报道里说得很清楚：认证是州长的法定义务，不签就会被起诉，最后还是得签；排名选择投票让“两个丹·沙利文”混淆选民的可能性更小，因为被混淆的选民几乎肯定会把另一个沙利文当第二选择；参议院理论上可以投票推迟宣誓，但会很丑。所有这些细节都指向一个结论：这套操作大概率失败。而失败恰恰是它的设计。特朗普要的不是让佩尔托拉坐不进去，是让足够多的人相信她坐进去本身就有问题。他最近被问到佩尔托拉该不该就任，说的是“很难相信她甚至能被允许参选”。这句话不针对结果，针对资格。资格一旦被质疑，结果就永远只是暂定。

这就是元暴力在选举现场的样子：不是改票数，是改“什么算数”的定义权。谁掌握了解释权，谁就不需要赢下计票。报道里反复出现“unlikely to succeed”“could get ugly”，这两个词放在一起才是全貌——法律上赢不了，气氛上可以赢。气氛是公共资源，被制造出来的困惑就是武器，而制造困惑的人同时是那个指着困惑说“你看，有问题吧”的人。

这篇报道有一个名字始终没有出现：佩尔托拉本人对这场分流选票的操作知道多少，报道说“不清楚”。她的名字出现在标题里，出现在“如果她赢了”的假设里，出现在特朗普那句“很难相信她甚至能被允许参选”里，但她在这整篇报道里没有一句话。围绕一个女人能不能就任的讨论，从头到尾没有她的声音。这不是报道的疏漏，这是这套操作的语法：把一个人变成程序上的变量，变量不需要说话。

真正值得盯的不是邓利维会不会签，是他签之前的那几周里，有多少人已经被说服这件事本来就不该算数。

The point of Trump's maneuver isn't to win. It's to make the result itself provisional.

Asked whether he'd certify a Democratic win, Alaska Governor Mike Dunleavy said that if the state finds things that shouldn't have happened, things that are illegal, they'll act on that, and it may affect whether somebody is seated. Read the grammar: certification is not a duty, it's a condition that can be acted upon. The count stops being the product of counting and becomes the governor's discretion. This step doesn't need to succeed. It only needs to be said out loud.

The article admits the other side runs plays too: New York Times reporting says Democratic operatives, including people on Peltola's campaign, encouraged candidates to run and siphon votes from Senator Dan Sullivan. The author calls it scummy but routine ratfucking. Fine. The question is who gets to define whose ratfucking as a crime. Dunleavy has ordered a criminal probe. Trump's Justice Department is suing to block certification. Same move, two names: investigation on one side, interference on the other.

The election law experts in this piece are clear. Certification is the governor's legal obligation; refuse and you get sued and sign anyway. Ranked-choice voting makes the two-Sullivan confusion even less plausible, since a confused voter would almost certainly rank the other Sullivan second. The Senate could theoretically vote to delay a swearing-in, and it could get ugly. Every detail points one way: this probably fails. Failure is the design.

Trump doesn't need Peltola kept out. He needs enough people to believe her being seated is itself suspect. Asked recently whether she should be seated, he said it's hard to believe she can even be allowed to run. That sentence doesn't target the outcome. It targets eligibility. Once eligibility is in question, the result is permanently pending.

This is meta-violence at the ballot box: not changing the count, changing what counts. Whoever holds the interpretive monopoly doesn't need to win the tally. The article keeps saying unlikely to succeed and could get ugly. Put together, that's the whole picture. Loses in court, wins in atmosphere. Atmosphere is a public resource. Manufactured confusion is the weapon, and the person manufacturing it is also the one pointing at the confusion and saying, see, something's wrong.

One name never appears: Peltola's own. How much she knew about the vote-siphoning scheme is, per the report, unclear. Her name is in the headline, in the if-she-wins hypothetical, in Trump's line about not being allowed to run. She has no voice anywhere in the piece. A debate about whether a woman may take her seat, conducted entirely without her. That's not an oversight. That's the syntax: turn a person into a procedural variable. Variables don't speak.

What matters isn't whether Dunleavy signs. It's how many people, in the weeks before he does, have already been persuaded this shouldn't have counted.
