享受呼吸，先得有呼吸的余裕Resting the Nervous System Requires a Nervous System That Can Rest

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要南天大学高级讲师Nadine Levy在文中讲述佛陀忆起儿时在玫瑰苹果树下体验宁静喜悦的故事，指出极端自我克制并非通往自由之路，某些细微的愉悦反而能引向心灵自由。她谈到压力会削弱人对愉悦的感受力，并提醒人们主动接纳自然生起的愉悦，让神经系统获得休息。
The newsNadine Levy, a senior lecturer at the Nan Tien Institute, recounts the Buddha's childhood memory of ease under a rose apple tree, suggesting extreme self-denial is not the path to freedom. She notes that stress dulls sensitivity to pleasure and urges receiving pleasure when it naturally arises, giving the nervous system a chance to rest.
把休息说成个人修行，是结构暴力的老把戏
Selling rest as a personal practice is structural violence's oldest trick

这篇文章讲的是如何在苦里接住快乐：佛陀想起儿时坐在玫瑰苹果树下，忽然明白极端的自我剥夺不是自由之路；作者教正念学生做“快乐检查”，问他们空闲时在干什么、能不能读本小说、晒着太阳散个步。道理不坏。但它把一件需要时间、安全、空间才能做到的事，写成了一个人的姿势问题。

作者自己其实点到了这一层：能否接触快乐，受社会与物质处境影响。然后这句话就滑过去了，接下来全是呼吸、身体、接受。暴力三角里，这一滑就是关键——潜在的状态是“人能休息”，实际的状态是“很多人不能”，差额是暴力。差额被写成个人修行的不足，结构层就成功隐身了。

更值得拆的是那个“快乐检查”。问一个精疲力竭的人“你空闲时在干什么”，前提是她有空闲。全球多少女性因为照料责任被挡在有薪工作之外，这个数字书里摆着；剩下的人里，又有多少人的“空闲”是通勤路上、孩子睡着之后、第二份工之间。把休息包装成一项可以练习的技能，等于把没有余裕说成没有悟性。

这不是说正念无用。作者写母亲临终前几周，医院里仍有笑声、有腿部按摩、有母女相伴的熟悉感——这一段是真的，因为它没有假装痛苦不存在，只是说快乐和痛苦可以编在一起。精确的地方在这里：她没有说“所以你要乐观”，她说的是“接受是关键”，而接受的前提是有人在场、有时间在场。

问题在于，这篇文章的服务对象是那些已经坐在冥想垫上的人。它给他们的东西是好的：允许自己休息，不必把苦修当虔诚。但它没有问，谁连垫子都没有。

“我还是爱床。”作者这样收尾，很可爱。可爱之外，还有一层没被说破：能安心躺下、注意到床单、感到枕头是特权的人，和躺下就得想着明天房租的人，睡的是同一张床吗？

The piece is about catching pleasure inside suffering: the Buddha remembers sitting under a rose apple tree as a child and realizes that extreme self-denial is not the path to freedom; the author runs a "pleasure check" with her mindfulness students, asking what they do in their free time, whether they can read a novel, take a walk in the sun. The advice is not wrong. But it turns something that requires time, safety and space into a matter of personal posture.

She names the problem herself: access to pleasure is not enjoyed equally, and the time, safety and space to notice it depend on social and material circumstances. Then the sentence slides past, and the rest is breath, body, receptivity. In the Violence Triangle, that slide is the whole trick—the potential is "a person can rest," the actual is "many cannot," and the gap is violence. Write the gap as a failure of practice, and the structural layer disappears from view.

The pleasure check deserves a closer look. Asking an exhausted person what she does in her free time assumes she has free time. The book's own figures put millions of working-age women outside the labor market because of care responsibilities; of those still in it, how many have "free time" that is a commute, an hour after the kids sleep, the gap between two jobs. Packaging rest as a skill to be practiced converts the absence of slack into a lack of aptitude.

None of this makes mindfulness useless. The passage about her mother's final weeks—laughter, a leg massage, the familiarity of being with her mum, pleasure braided through despair—is true, because it does not pretend the pain away. It says receptivity is key, and receptivity requires someone present, with time to be present.

The trouble is the audience. The essay serves people already on the cushion, and what it gives them is good: permission to rest, permission not to treat austerity as devotion. It never asks who has no cushion at all.

"I still love bed," she ends, charmingly. Charming, and also the place where the argument stops: the person who can lie down and notice the sheets, the relief, the privilege of a pillow, and the person who lies down already calculating tomorrow's rent are not lying in the same bed.
