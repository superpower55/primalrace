高塔上的嘲笑与被定价的“Z级”人生Laughter from the Tower and the Pricing of 'Z-List' Lives

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
权力不在于游戏本身，而在于谁拥有在塔顶发笑的解释权。
Power lies not in the game, but in who holds the right to laugh from above.

Gary Lineker 的新节目《The Box》提供了一个极其精准的 meta violence 样本：一个处于权力中心（塔顶）的男性，俯瞰一群被定义为“Z-listers”的边缘人，在废弃停车场里玩鸡蛋接力这种极其 mundane 的游戏。这里的快感并不来自游戏，而来自这种空间上的垂直等级制。Lineker 扮演的是那个定义“谁是失败者”的裁判，而那些嘉宾通过扮演“笨拙的边缘人”来换取一点点屏幕曝光率——这就是典型的假.最优解表达：通过出卖主体性，扮演他者认可的滑稽角色，来换取在结构性边缘生存的微小利益。

有趣的是，这份电视指南将这种“权力游戏”与迪士尼的浪漫叙事、医疗剧的政治根源、以及 Ryan Reynolds 的商业魅力打包在一起。这本质上是一场认知入口的集体共谋。从 Lineker 的高塔到迪士尼的魔法，再到《曼达洛人》中被简化为“可爱”的生物，所有的叙事都在强化一种逻辑：权力的持有者定义什么是“有趣”，而客体则在被定义的框架内竞争。这种对“Z级”身份的消费，实际上是在给社会阶级打标签并定价。

最讽刺的是，在同一份名单的末尾，Women’s Super League 的比赛被像附录一样地列在 Premier League 之后。这种排版本身就是一种 structural violence：即便女性足球在 actual 层面取得了进展，但在 cultural 层的认知入口里，她们依然是那个“次要的”补充项。在这种共谋的叙事环境下，女性球员的胜利往往被解读为“进步”，而男足的统治则被视为“自然”。

Gary Lineker’s new show, *The Box*, provides a precise specimen of meta-violence: a man at the center of power (the tower) looks down on 'Z-listers' stumbling through a disused car park playing mundane games like egg-and-spoon races. The pleasure here isn't the game; it's the vertical hierarchy of space. Lineker acts as the judge who defines who is a 'loser,' while the guests trade their subjectivity for a sliver of screen time by playing the role of the clumsy marginal—a classic fake optimal expression: performing a caricature approved by others to survive on the structural periphery.

It is telling that this TV guide packages this power play alongside Disney's romantic narratives and the commercial charm of Ryan Reynolds. This is a collective complicity of cognitive entry points. From Lineker’s tower to Disney’s magic, and the 'cute' Baby Yoda in *The Mandalorian*, the narrative reinforces one logic: those in power define what is 'fun,' and objects compete within that defined frame. The consumption of 'Z-list' identities is, in essence, the labeling and pricing of social class.

Most ironic is the placement of the Women’s Super League at the very bottom, following the Premier League like an appendix. This layout is structural violence in its purest form: while women's football has made actual progress, in the cultural entry point, it remains the 'secondary' supplement. In this complicit narrative, female victory is framed as 'progress,' while male dominance is framed as 'natural.'
