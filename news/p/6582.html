疏散三万人的名单里没有谁Who Isn't on the List of Thirty Thousand

其他 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
灾难不分配暴力，它只是把既有的分配照抄一遍
A disaster doesn't distribute violence; it copies the existing distribution.

这条新闻里真正值得看的不是风速，是那句“我们计划疏散三万人”，而截至周一只走了两千。差额一万五千倍的风力会过去，差额两万八千人的执行力不会自己长出来。加尔通说暴力是潜在与实际之间的差，这里最直观的一层暴力不在天上，在公路上——谁有车、谁被告知、谁被算进那三万。

报道给了很多名字：灾害管理局的常务董事、首席部长、副首席部长、内洛尔县的县长，还有六支国家救援队、七支邦救援队，三支还是从泰米尔纳德邦调来的。名单很齐整，齐整得像一份免责声明。上一场气旋 Arnab 周三刚扫过同一段海岸，死了六个人，毁了几十间房子和庄稼，一周之后同一片地方又要再来一次。灾害的节奏已经快过重建的节奏，这不是天灾的频率问题，是结构层在拿旧账本应对新账目。

首席部长提到的是电线杆——六千到八千根可能受损，“对供电构成严重威胁”。电线杆是能被统计、能被报数、能被写进简报的东西。渔民只是被劝告不要出海，学校停课，官员留守岗位。劝告不是资源，停课不是补偿，留守不是保障。低洼地带的人被疏散，但他们被疏散到哪里、住多久、房子泡了之后回来还剩什么，报道一个字没写。没写的地方，往往就是代价落地的地方。

这类新闻最容易滑进“天灾无情人有情”的叙事，救援队提前部署、官员靠前指挥、学校及时关闭，一切都显得有序。有序是真的，但有序只说明机器在转，不说明机器转得公平。同一个邦，上周刚被砸过一次的人，这周又被要求撤离，他们的恢复能力已经被上一次透支了。灾难不会创造不平等，它只是把已有的不平等按原样放大一遍，然后盖上“不可抗力”的章。

两千和三万之间那两万八千人，现在还在低洼地带等着。他们不是数字没到位，是安排没到位。等风过去，新闻会报伤亡、报损失、报恢复供电用了几天，不会报这两万八千人里有多少人根本没有收到撤离通知，有多少人收到了也走不了——因为家里有老人，因为渔船是全部家当，因为走了就没人看着那间屋子。这些不在报道里，也不在简报里。

风会停，电线杆会重新立起来，下一场气旋还会来。真正没被疏散的，是那套永远把最脆弱的人留在最后一排的安排。

The number worth looking at in this story isn't the wind speed. It's "we are planning to evacuate some 30,000 people" against the 2,000 who had actually left by Monday. The wind will pass in a night. The gap between the plan and the road won't close by itself. Galtung's formula says violence is the distance between potential and actual, and here the most visible layer of violence isn't in the sky — it's on the highway. Who has a vehicle, who got told, who counts inside that thirty thousand.

The report is generous with names: the managing director of the disaster management authority, the chief minister, the deputy chief minister, the collector of Nellore district, six NDRF teams and seven state teams, three of them borrowed from neighbouring Tamil Nadu. A tidy list. Tidy like a liability waiver. Cyclone Arnab hit the same coastline on Wednesday, killed six people, destroyed dozens of homes and crops, and a week later the same stretch is bracing again. The rhythm of disaster has outrun the rhythm of rebuilding. That isn't a weather problem. That's the structural layer settling old accounts with new bills.

The chief minister's concern is electrical poles — six to eight thousand possibly damaged, a "severe threat to power supply." Poles can be counted, reported, written into a briefing. Fishermen get urged not to go to sea. Schools close. Officials stay at their posts. An advisory is not a resource. A school closure is not compensation. Staying at your post is not protection. People in low-lying areas are being evacuated, but to where, for how long, and what's left of their houses when the water recedes — the report says nothing. Where nothing is said is usually where the cost lands.

This kind of story slides easily into the "natural disaster, human warmth" frame: teams pre-deployed, officials on the ground, schools shut in time, everything orderly. The order is real. But order only proves the machine is running, not that it's running fairly. The same district that was hit last week is being asked to evacuate this week, and its capacity to recover was already spent the first time. Disaster doesn't invent inequality. It reproduces the existing distribution at scale and stamps it "act of God."

The twenty-eight thousand people between 2,000 and 30,000 are still down there waiting. They're not an unfulfilled statistic. They're an unfulfilled arrangement. After the wind passes, the coverage will report deaths, damage, and how many days it took to restore power. It won't report how many of those twenty-eight thousand never got the evacuation notice, or got it and couldn't leave — because of an elderly parent, because the boat is the entire household, because someone has to watch the house. None of that is in the report. None of it will be in the briefing.

The wind will stop. The poles will go back up. The next cyclone will come. What never gets evacuated is the arrangement that keeps the most fragile people in the last row.
