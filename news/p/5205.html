权力下放的幻觉与审计制度的共谋The Illusion of Devolution and the Complicity of Audit

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-23 § 链接
所谓的权力下放，不过是将元暴力的执行端从中心移向局部。
Devolution is merely shifting the execution of meta-violence from the center to the periphery.

这是一场典型的关于“权力”与“监督”的叙事博弈。Burnham 政府试图通过 devolution（权力下放）将数十亿英镑的资金控制权交给地区市长，美其名曰“扭转过度集权”，但本质上这只是在重新分配权力在地理空间上的分布，而非改变权力的运作逻辑。当资金从 Whitehall 转移到地方，改变的是谁在签字，而不是谁在被剥削。

有趣的是，IPPR North 作为一个与政府紧密合作的智库，在赞美这种“大胆计划”的同时，迅速抛出了“加强审计”和“问责委员会”的药方。这是一种极其熟练的共谋：一方制造权力扩张的快感，另一方提供监督的合法性外壳。通过建立所谓的“区域审计办公室”和“高级会计官员”，系统在权力被滥用之前，先预设了一套能够被管理、被量化的“问责”机制。这种机制的作用不是为了防止 corruption 或 elite capture，而是为了在出事时，能够迅速通过审计报告将责任个体化，从而保护结构本身的稳定性。

在这种叙事中，最被忽略的是那些在权力移交过程中依然处于结构性弱势的群体。无论资金是在伦敦还是在曼彻斯特被决定如何使用，决定权依然掌握在那些能够定义“增长”和“基础设施”的男性精英手中。这种权力下放并没有触及元暴力的核心——即谁拥有解释权。如果评价标准依然是男本位的经济指标，那么所谓的“转型”不过是将同样的结构性暴力在更小的尺度上重复一遍。

真正的问责不应该是增加几个审计员，而应该是让那些被权力决定命运的人进入决策层。但显然，这不在 IPPR North 的报告里，也不在任何一个政治家的 agenda 中。

This is a classic narrative game between 'power' and 'scrutiny.' The Burnham government attempts to hand over control of billions to regional mayors under the banner of devolution, claiming to reverse 'over-centralisation.' In reality, this is merely a redistribution of power across geographic space, not a change in the logic of power. When funding moves from Whitehall to the regions, the only thing that changes is who signs the check, not who is being exploited.

It is telling that IPPR North, a thinktank working closely with the government, praises this 'bold' plan while simultaneously prescribing 'tougher oversight' and 'accountability committees.' This is a sophisticated form of complicity: one party creates the thrill of expanding power, while the other provides the legitimizing shell of supervision. By establishing 'regional audit offices' and 'senior accounting officers,' the system pre-installs a manageable, quantifiable mechanism of accountability. The purpose of such a mechanism is not to prevent corruption or elite capture, but to ensure that when failures occur, responsibility can be individualized through an audit report, thereby shielding the structural stability of the system.

What remains invisible in this narrative are the structurally disadvantaged groups who remain powerless regardless of where the money is managed. Whether funds are allocated in London or Manchester, the decision-making power remains with the male elites who define 'growth' and 'infrastructure.' This devolution does not touch the core of meta-violence—the monopoly over the right to interpret. If the metrics of success remain masculine-centric economic indicators, then this 'transformation' is nothing more than repeating the same structural violence on a smaller scale.

Real accountability is not about adding more auditors; it is about integrating those whose lives are decided by power into the decision-making process. Obviously, this is not in the IPPR North report, nor is it on any politician's agenda.
