怀旧是最高级的共谋：被抹除的底层设计者Nostalgia as the Ultimate Complicity: The Erased Laborers

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
当垃圾被包装成艺术，消失的劳动者才是真正的成本。
When trash is rebranded as art, the erased laborer is the true cost.

这篇文章读起来像是一场温情的怀旧派对，但剥开那层 wax paper，里面全是 structural violence 的气味。一个收藏家通过一个 hoarding 者的遗物，把 70 年代的冰棍包装纸变成了所谓的“艺术品”和“圣杯”，而这件事最讽刺的地方在于：这些设计者是谁？没人知道。

这就是典型的元暴力（meta violence）运作方式。在那个 era，这些包装纸被定义为“throwaway”，意味着创造它们的人——那些 in-house designers——在生产关系中同样是被当作 throwaway 的消耗品。他们的姓名、信用和主体性在产品被投向市场的那一刻起就被抹除了。这种抹除不是偶然，而是一种制度性的共谋：公司不需要给“低端”设计署名，因为这些劳动被定义为次要的、无价值的。

现在，这些垃圾变成了收藏品，价格飙升至 150 英镑，收藏家在享受“设计之美”和“童年快乐”的 nostalgia，但这种快乐是建立在对原作者主体性彻底剥夺的基础之上的。当 Trunk 遗憾地说“这太可惜了”时，他其实是在一种特权者的视角下俯瞰缺失的档案。对于那些被抹除的设计者来说，这种“可惜”正是他们当年被结构性压榨的证明。

最典型的共谋在于：我们习惯于赞美一个结果的“稀有”，却习惯于忽略这个结果产生的过程中，谁的权利被牺牲了。这种把“无名之作”神圣化的叙事，本质上是在掩盖一个事实——在那个被美化的 70 年代夏天，底层劳动者的表达权被定价为零。

This piece reads like a cozy nostalgia party, but peel back the wax paper and you'll smell structural violence. A collector turns a hoarder's debris into 'art' and 'Holy Grails,' while the most ironic part remains: who were the designers? Nobody knows.

This is meta violence in action. In that era, these wrappers were defined as 'throwaway,' meaning the in-house designers who created them were treated as throwaway consumables in the production chain. Their names, credits, and subjectivity were erased the moment the product hit the market. This wasn't an accident; it was a systemic complicity. Companies didn't credit 'low-end' design because that labor was defined as secondary and worthless.

Now, this trash has become a collectible, with prices hitting £150. The collector enjoys the 'beauty of design' and the nostalgia of 'childhood joy,' but this pleasure is built on the total deprivation of the original creators' subjectivity. When Trunk says it's 'such a shame' that archives are missing, he is viewing this from a position of privilege. For the erased designers, this 'shame' is the evidence of their structural exploitation.

The ultimate complicity lies here: we are trained to praise the 'rarity' of a result while ignoring whose rights were sacrificed to produce it. The narrative of sanctifying 'anonymous works' effectively masks the fact that in that glorified 1970s summer, the expression rights of bottom-tier laborers were priced at zero.
