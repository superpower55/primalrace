票房榜上女人在排队Women Are Queuing at the Box Office

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
女性观众不是票房变量，是被反复定价的入口
Female audiences aren't a variable. They're an entry point, priced and repriced.

《Wicker》上映第二周，PostTrak 五颗星，74% 的人明确推荐，73% 的观众是女性，35 岁以上占 52%。同一周，索尼那部请了 Jenna Ortega 和 Amy Adams、明摆着冲女性观众去的《Klara and the Sun》，两星，明确推荐只有 40%，第二周索尼干脆不报票房了。这不是巧合，这是一次定价权的当场演示。

一部片子里有 Olivia Colman 和 Alexander Sargaard，从 1,471 家影院扩到 2,374 家，靠的是口碑自己长出来。另一部片子有明星、有大制片厂、有营销预算，把"女性向"三个字写在脸上，然后扑了。区别在哪？前者的女性角色是主体，后者的女性角色是卖点。卖点不需要被说服，主体需要被说服——而被当成主体对待的观众，会用五颗星回报你。

DC 那部《Clayface》的观众 63% 是男性，68% 明确推荐。这个数字不难看，但它说明的是另一件事：一部关于脸在融化的蝙蝠侠反派的电影，默认观众是男的，默认不需要解释。这就是元暴力的日常形态——不是谁在恶意排挤女性，而是整个产业在决定什么是"默认"、什么是"特殊"。女性向是特殊，男性向是通用。

再看《You Can See Everything》。一部三小时的纪录片，讲 Elizabeth Holmes 和 Nathan Fielder，第三周还能拿 1,080 万美元，累计 6,400 万，把《华氏 9/11》之外的一堆纪录片甩在身后。Deadline 说它靠的是社媒上没完没了的梗，以及 Holmes 和 Fielder 成了今年万圣节的"it"装扮。一个靠欺诈融资的女人，和一个专门拍尴尬的喜剧人，被消费成了 costume。Holmes 的罪是真的，受害者的钱是真的，但到了票房这里，她成了一个可穿戴的符号。这不是在替她翻案，这是在说：连骗局都能被重新包装成审美，只要它够好笑。

《Wicker》赢的那部分是真的。女性观众用钱包投票，一部把她们当人看的电影赢了，一部把她们当人群标签的电影输了。这是 cultural 层的一次小规模换手。但别急着庆祝——Black Bear 上一部《Conclave》也是这么滚起来的，行业学到的是"女性向也能赚钱"，不是"女性是主体"。下一个季度，会有十部学《Wicker》的片子，其中九部会把"女性向"重新做成标签。

真正没被回答的问题是：为什么一部 73% 女性观众的电影，需要靠第二周扩馆才能被看见？为什么一部明摆着冲女性去的片子，扑了之后连票房都不报了？前者说明口碑有效，后者说明失败可以被藏起来。藏起来的失败不会变成教训，只会变成沉默。而沉默，是这个行业最擅长处理女性观众的方式。

Wicker's second weekend: five stars on PostTrak, 74% definite recommend, 73% female, 52% over 35. The same week, Sony's Klara and the Sun—starring Jenna Ortega and Amy Adams, aimed squarely at women—got two stars and a 40% definite recommend, and by its second weekend Sony had stopped reporting the grosses. That's not a coincidence. That's a demonstration of who gets to set the price.

One film has Olivia Colman and Alexander Sargaard, expands from 1,471 theaters to 2,374, and grows on word of mouth. The other has stars, a major studio, a marketing budget, and "for women" written across its face—and it dies. The difference: in the first, women are subjects. In the second, women are a selling point. A selling point doesn't need to be persuaded. A subject does—and an audience treated as subjects pays back in five stars.

Clayface's audience is 63% male, 68% definite recommend. Those numbers aren't bad, but they say something else: a movie about a Batman villain whose face is melting defaults to male, and needs no explanation. That's meta-violence in its everyday form—not someone maliciously excluding women, but an industry deciding what counts as default and what counts as special. Female is special. Male is general.

Then there's You Can See Everything. A three-hour documentary about Elizabeth Holmes and Nathan Fielder, still pulling $10.8M in its third weekend, $64M total, already past a stack of documentaries. Deadline credits the endless memes and the fact that Holmes and Fielder became this year's "it" Halloween costumes. A woman who defrauded investors, and a comedian who specializes in discomfort, consumed as costume. Holmes's crime is real. The victims' money is real. But at the box office she's a wearable symbol. This isn't rehabilitating her. It's saying that even a scam can be repackaged as aesthetics, as long as it's funny enough.

The part Wicker won is real. Women voted with their wallets, and a film that treats them as people beat a film that treats them as a demographic. That's a small transfer of power at the cultural layer. Don't celebrate too fast—Black Bear rolled out Conclave the same way two years ago, and what the industry learned was "female-driven can make money," not "women are subjects." Next quarter there will be ten films imitating Wicker, and nine of them will turn "female-driven" back into a label.

The question nobody answered: why does a film with a 73% female audience need a second-week expansion to be seen? Why does a film aimed at women, once it flops, stop having its grosses reported? The first says word of mouth works. The second says failure can be hidden. A hidden failure doesn't become a lesson. It becomes silence. And silence is what this industry handles women with best.
