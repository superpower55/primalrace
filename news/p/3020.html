从“疯女人”到播客：一场关于解释权的夺回战From 'Crazy Ex' to Podcasters: A War for the Right of Interpretation

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-30 § 链接
当女性停止扮演受害者并开始定义事实，元暴力的闭环就被撕开了。
When women stop performing the victim and start defining facts, the loop of meta-violence is broken.

这则关于《Don’t Date Brandon》的简讯，本质上是一场关于“认知入口”的夺回战。在典型的男性中心叙事中，Brandon 这种男人最强大的武器不是暴力，而是定义权：他将前任定义为“crazy ex”，通过在此时的伴侣面前制造一个“疯女人”的虚构叙事，成功地将女性的合理反应污名化，从而为自己的操纵行为建立防火墙。

这种“crazy ex”的标签是极具杀伤力的 cultural violence。它让女性陷入一种绝望的博弈：如果你反抗，你就验证了“疯”的标签；如果你沉默，你就在主体性死亡中被吞噬。Brandon 试图通过操纵两个女性的认知，让她们在互不相识的情况下，分别成为他最优解表达中的“客体”和“反面教材”。

但这件事最 an interesting 的转折在于：两个受害者不再尝试在男性的评价体系里证明自己“不疯”，而是直接跳出博弈，通过建立播客（podcast）这个新的表达空间，将碎片化的谎言拼接成完整的结构性真相。这不再是简单的“告密”，而是一次解释权的移交。当她们联手定义什么是“Brandon 的谎言”时，她们实际上是在削弱那个由男性定义的“疯女人”叙事，将 Potential（真相）与 Actual（被操纵的现状）之间的差额强行缩小。

这种团结是身份政治在现实中的高效运用。她们意识到，在这个由男性共谋的“浪漫爱陷阱”中，个体单打独斗只能被定义为个案，而只有通过共创叙事，才能将个体的痛苦转化为结构性的武器。这场胜利在于：那个曾经掌控一切的“full package”男人，现在成了被定义、被消费的客体。

The snippet about 'Don’t Date Brandon' is essentially a war over the 'cognitive entry point.' In a typical masculine-centric narrative, a man like Brandon doesn't rely on direct violence, but on the power of definition: by labeling his former partner as the 'crazy ex,' he manufactures a fictional narrative to demonize any reasonable reaction, creating a firewall for his own manipulation.

This 'crazy ex' label is a lethal form of cultural violence. It traps women in a losing game: resist, and you validate the 'crazy' tag; stay silent, and your subjectivity dies in the shadows. Brandon attempted to manipulate the perceptions of two women, turning them into 'objects' and 'cautionary tales' to serve his own fake optimal expression.

The most interesting turn here is that these women stopped trying to prove they weren't 'crazy' within the male-defined system. Instead, they bypassed the game entirely by creating a podcast—a new expression space—to piece together fragmented lies into a structural truth. This is not mere 'whistleblowing'; it is a transfer of the right of interpretation. By jointly defining 'Brandon's lies,' they dismantle the narrative of the 'crazy woman,' forcibly closing the gap between Potential and Actual.

This is the efficient use of identity politics. They realized that in a 'romantic love scam' bolstered by male complicity, an individual is easily dismissed as an isolated case. Only through co-creating a narrative can individual pain be weaponized into a structural critique. The victory here is that the man who once controlled the script is now the object being defined and consumed.
