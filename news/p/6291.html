五千万张票，换不来一个能选的选项Fifty Million Votes, Zero Real Choices

国际 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要美国中期选举提前投票人数激增，截至周六上午已有超过5050万人投票，比2022年中期选举的提前投票总数多出近500万。登记民主党选民投票1070万，共和党选民780万。此次选举将决定国会控制权，多州选情胶着。
The newsMore than 50.5 million Americans had cast early votes by Saturday morning, nearly 5 million more than the total early votes in the 2022 midterms. Registered Democrats have cast 10.7 million votes so far, compared with 7.8 million Republicans. The election will decide control of Congress.
投票率上升不等于民主在修复，只说明有人在给两个烂选项里较不烂的那个付代价。
Higher turnout isn't democracy healing. It's people paying the cost of choosing the less rotten of two rotten options.

五千万人提前投票，比 2022 年中期选举的全部提前票还多出近五百万。报道把这读成"热情"。热情是有的，但热情不是凭空长出来的——它是被一个不受欢迎的总统、涨上去的食品和汽油价格、一场伊朗战争和一堆腐败指控逼出来的。人不为喜欢的选项投票，人为不能忍受的现状投票。

民主党的提前票比共和党多出近三百万（1070 万对 780 万）。报道自己补了一句：党籍登记不等于实际投票。这句话是对的，但它漏掉的是更重要的东西——党籍登记也不等于人。一个登记为民主党的人投这一票，可能不是为了哪个候选人，而是为了把另一个人挡在门外。这在博弈里叫防守，不叫支持。

得州七百万人提前投票，哈里斯县 862,700 人，比 2022 年同期多出近三十万。报道说这个县近几次都稳定投民主党，2024 年哈里斯在这里赢了近 6%，参议员候选人 Allred 赢了克鲁兹约 11%。这些数字被摆出来是为了证明"得州要变天"。但得州三十多年没有选出过一个全州民主党人，几亿美元的竞选资金砸进去，砸的是同一个州。钱能买注意力，买不到结构——结构是三十年的选区划分、投票规则和登记门槛。

南卡罗来纳提前票涨了 83%，堪萨斯涨 34%，缅因涨 44%，密歇根涨 32%。这些州里，特朗普 2024 年在南卡赢了近 18%。票数上涨说明的是同一件事：原本不投票的人开始投票了。但投票率上升本身不是任何一方的胜利，它只是把博弈的盘子做大了一点。盘子做大，规则没变，谁在规则里占便宜还是谁。

特朗普在佛罗里达的集会上说："我以为就这样了，我以为我可以放松了。连续二十八天这种破事，太多了。"这句话是整篇报道里最诚实的一句。一个总统把为自己党候选人站台叫作"破事"，说明他清楚这些票不是冲他来的。他还在反复评论候选人的外貌，把汽油涨价说成不如化石燃料公司的利润重要。这就是共谋的日常形态：利益站队一致的人互相抬轿子，轿子里的人抱怨抬轿子太累。

报道说这场选举将决定国会两院的构成，背景是食品和汽油涨价、伊朗战争、政府腐败。这些是结构层的东西。但整篇报道从头到尾没有一个人说清楚：投了这一票，这些结构会怎么变。票数、党籍、百分比、涨幅——全是过程指标。过程指标好看，不等于差额被缩小了。

五十万、五百万、五千万，这些数字被当成新闻，是因为它们可以被计数。真正不能被计数的是：一个在哈里斯县排了四十分钟队的人，投完票回家，食品价格没有降，伊朗战争没有停，他下次还得再来一遍。投票率上升是暴力的反面吗？只有当它真的改变了资源分配才算。报道没写这个，因为没人知道，也可能因为没人问。

Fifty million early votes, nearly five million more than the entire early vote in 2022. The report reads this as "enthusiasm." Enthusiasm exists, but it doesn't grow from nothing. It's squeezed out by an unpopular president, rising food and gasoline prices, an Iran war, and a pile of corruption concerns. People don't vote for what they want. They vote against what they can't stand.

Democrats have cast nearly three million more early ballots than Republicans (10.7 million to 7.8 million). The report itself adds that party registration doesn't equal actual votes. True. But what it leaves out is bigger: registration doesn't equal people either. Someone registered as a Democrat may be voting not for a candidate but to keep another person out. In game theory that's called defense, not support.

Seven million early votes in Texas. In Harris County, 862,700 people had voted by Wednesday, roughly 300,000 more than the same point in 2022. The report notes the county has reliably voted Democratic, that Harris won it by nearly 6% in 2024, and that Senate candidate Allred carried it by about 11% over Cruz. These numbers are laid out to prove Texas is turning. But Texas hasn't elected a statewide Democrat in over three decades. Hundreds of millions of dollars are pouring into the same state. Money buys attention. It doesn't buy structure. Structure is thirty years of district lines, voting rules, and registration thresholds.

Early voting is up 83% in South Carolina, 34% in Kansas, 44% in Maine, 32% in Michigan. Trump won South Carolina by nearly 18% in 2024. The surge means one thing: people who didn't vote before are voting now. But higher turnout isn't a victory for anyone. It just makes the game board bigger. The board gets bigger, the rules stay the same, and whoever benefits from the rules still benefits.

Trump said at a Florida rally: "I thought this was it. I thought I could relax. Twenty-eight days of this crap in a row is a lot." That's the most honest line in the whole report. A president calling his own campaign appearances "crap" knows these votes aren't for him. He's still commenting on candidates' physical appearance and dismissing gasoline prices as secondary to fossil fuel profits. This is what collusion looks like day to day: people whose interests align carry each other's palanquin, and the one inside complains the carrying is exhausting.

The report says this election decides the composition of Congress, against a backdrop of food and gasoline prices, the Iran war, and government corruption. These are structural things. But nowhere in the report does anyone explain how casting this ballot changes any of them. Vote counts, party affiliations, percentages, increases—all process metrics. Good process metrics don't mean the gap has been closed.

Fifty million, five million, five hundred thousand—these numbers make news because they can be counted. What can't be counted is the person who waited forty minutes in Harris County, voted, went home, and found food prices unchanged, the Iran war ongoing, and the whole thing waiting to be done again. Is higher turnout the opposite of violence? Only if it actually redistributes resources. The report doesn't say that. Nobody knows it. And maybe nobody asked.
