宠物不是明星的配件Pets Are Not Celebrity Accessories

文娱 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把宠物列成权力榜，是在给爱标价
Ranking pets by their owners' fame puts a price on love

Billboard 这份"Power Pets"名单里，动物被排成了一张权力榜。谁养的猫、谁养的狗、谁家的狗 Instagram 粉丝七百万。这不是在写动物，是在写主人的身价，动物只是挂在名字后面的一个配件。

看它怎么给动物定价：Jung Kook 的 Doberman Bam 有七百多万粉丝，Megan Thee Stallion 的法国斗牛犬"基本上是个网红"，粉丝五十多万。数字是给狗算的，账是记在主人头上的。狗不知道自己有粉丝，主人知道。这就是整份名单的机制——把"我养了一只可爱的动物"翻译成"我有能力让一只动物成为资产"。

再看它怎么给"爱"写文案。Ariana Grande 说狗是"世界上最无害、最甜的小东西"，"它们只给出无条件的爱，所以它们也该得到同样的回报"。这句话本身没错，错的是它出现在一份把动物按主人咖位排序的榜单里。无条件的爱被拿来当排名的注脚，就变成了有条件的：你得先够红，你的狗才配上这个名单。

名单里唯一有点分量的细节，是 Lady Gaga 的法国斗牛犬 Gustav 和 Koji——它们是在一次暴力抢劫中被抢走、又奇迹般被送回主人身边的。这条被轻描淡写地塞在一长串名字里，和"谁家的猫叫什么"并列。动物真的遭遇了暴力，这件事在榜单里只值一个从句。

整份名单没有一处写到代价。没有一处写到这些动物被怎么养、被谁照顾、在巡演大巴上待了多少小时。Chase Rice 的黑色拉布拉多 Jack 被带上台唱一首歌，"他就坐在我旁边，等我的许可"——这句话可以读成温馨，也可以读成一个动物被训练成在噪音和灯光里保持安静。报道选了前一种读法，因为后一种读法会让"Power Pets"这个标题不成立。

这不是什么大恶。它是一篇软文，轻的题材，我也不会把它写成重大议题。但它示范了一个很小的动作：把爱变成可排名的东西，把陪伴变成可展示的资本，把动物变成主人表达的一部分。这个动作在明星身上无伤大雅，在普通人身上就是另一回事了——宠物被当成身份标签、被当成社交货币、被当成"我很善良"的证明。

名单最后一条是 Travis Kelce 和 Taylor Swift 新领养的萨摩耶 Wendy，报道说"她点亮了他们的日子"。这句话可能是真的。但同一篇报道里，Wendy 被介绍的方式是"新领养的家庭成员"加上"Tommy Hilfiger 的新广告"。一只狗第一次出现在公众面前，是因为一支广告。

Billboard's "Power Pets" list arranges animals into a power ranking. Whose cat, whose dog, whose dog has seven million Instagram followers. This isn't writing about animals. It's writing about the owners' market value, with the animal hung off the name as an accessory.

Look at how it prices them: Jung Kook's Doberman Bam has over seven million followers; Megan Thee Stallion's French bulldog is "basically an influencer" with more than half a million. The numbers are counted for the dogs, but the ledger is kept for the owners. The dog doesn't know it has followers. The owner does. That's the mechanism of the whole list — translating "I own a cute animal" into "I have the power to turn an animal into an asset."

Then look at how it writes "love." Ariana Grande says dogs are "the most harmless, sweetest babes in the world," that "they show nothing but unconditional love, so they deserve that in return." The sentence itself is fine. What's wrong is that it appears inside a list that ranks animals by their owners' status. Unconditional love used as a footnote to a ranking becomes conditional: you have to be famous enough first, and then your dog qualifies.

The one detail in the list with any weight is Lady Gaga's French bulldogs Gustav and Koji — taken in a violent robbery and miraculously returned to her. This is slipped in among a long string of names, sitting next to "whose cat is called what." Animals actually experienced violence, and in this list it's worth one subordinate clause.

Nowhere does the list mention cost. Nowhere does it say how these animals are kept, by whom, how many hours on a tour bus. Chase Rice's black Lab Jack is brought onstage for one song, "he just sits next to me and looks for my approval" — that can be read as heartwarming, or as an animal trained to stay quiet under noise and lights. The piece chooses the first reading, because the second would make the title "Power Pets" collapse.

This isn't a great evil. It's a soft piece, a light subject, and I won't inflate it into a major issue. But it demonstrates a small move: turning love into something rankable, companionship into displayable capital, animals into part of the owner's expression. On celebrities it's harmless. On ordinary people it's something else — pets as identity tags, as social currency, as proof that "I'm kind."

The last entry is Travis Kelce and Taylor Swift's newly adopted Samoyed, Wendy, who "lights up" their days. That might be true. But in the same piece, Wendy is introduced as a "newly adopted family member" plus "a new advertisement for Tommy Hilfiger." A dog's first public appearance happens because of an ad.
