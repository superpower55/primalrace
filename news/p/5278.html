BART 的死亡螺旋：一场关于“自给自足”的结构性诈骗The BART Death Spiral: A Structural Scam of 'Self-Sufficiency'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
将公共基础设施定义为“自给自足”，本身就是一种结构性暴力。
Defining public infrastructure as 'self-sufficient' is, in itself, a form of structural violence.

旧金山湾区 BART 的所谓“死亡螺旋” (death spiral)，本质上是一场持续了五十年的认知 scam。新闻里把 BART 描述为“自给自足资金模式的典范” (model of self-sufficient funding)，这简直是今年最大的笑话。在公共交通领域，追求“自给自足”就是一种 structural violence。它把一项保障生存权的公共服务，强行扭曲成了需要通过票价维持利润的商业产品。

当 BART 将 60%-70% 的运营收入押注在票价上时，它就完成了一次危险的共谋：与那些认为“使用者付费”才是公正的 neoliberal 叙事站队。这种模式在经济繁荣期被掩盖，但在 pandemic 这种系统性冲击面前，它直接变成了勒在通勤者脖子上的绳索。现在，当系统崩溃在即，他们试图通过向选民征收销售税来补窟窿，这不过是在 structural 层面对之前的错误定价进行一次迟到的、且充满风险的修正。

最讽刺的是，那些在湾区赚得盆满钵满的 tech giants 长期享受着 BART 提供的劳动力流动性，却在资金链断裂时选择做沉默的共谋者。正如那位外科医生 David 所言，这些公司受益最多，却在补贴面前消失。这正是元暴力的体现：公共资源的成本由底层劳动力（如在麦当劳工作的 70 岁 Alvina）通过时间成本和生活质量来承担，而其产生的经济红利则被资本端垄断。

如果这次税收投票失败，BART 的崩溃将直接导致 Potential − Actual 的差额剧增。对于低收入者和残障人士来说，这不再是“交通拥堵”的问题，而是一次直接的生存空间剥夺。所谓的“死亡螺旋”不是因为乘客少了，而是因为这个系统从设计之初，就试图用商业逻辑去衡量一个人的基本移动权。

The so-called 'death spiral' of the SF Bay Area's BART is essentially a cognitive scam that has lasted five decades. The narrative describing BART as a 'model of self-sufficient funding' is the biggest joke of the year. In public transit, the pursuit of 'self-sufficiency' is a form of structural violence. It forcibly distorts a public service—a fundamental right to survival—into a commercial product that must maintain profits through fares.

By betting 60%-70% of its operating revenue on passenger fares, BART entered into a dangerous complicity with the neoliberal narrative that 'user-pays' is the only just model. This model was masked during economic booms, but in the face of a systemic shock like the pandemic, it became a noose around the necks of commuters. Now, as the system teeters on collapse, the attempt to patch the hole via sales tax is merely a belated and risky correction of a structural mispricing.

It is profoundly ironic that the tech giants of the Bay Area, who have long reaped the rewards of labor mobility provided by BART, remain silent complicitors as the funding chain snaps. As the surgeon David pointed out, these companies benefit most but vanish when it comes to subsidies. This is the manifestation of meta-violence: the costs of public resources are borne by low-income workers (like 70-year-old Alvina at McDonald's) through time and quality of life, while the economic dividends are monopolized by capital.

If the ballot measure fails, the collapse of BART will cause the gap between Potential and Actual to explode. For low-income workers and people with disabilities, this is not about 'traffic congestion,' but a direct deprivation of their existential space. The 'death spiral' isn't caused by a lack of riders, but by a system that, from its inception, attempted to measure the basic right to mobility with a commercial ledger.
