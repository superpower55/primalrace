谎言不是失手，是产品The Lie Is the Product

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要特朗普在共和党竞选集会上称华盛顿特区曾犯罪猖獗、餐馆被抢，并称学校未经家长同意为儿童做性别转换手术。文章指出这些均为不实说法，并称其正把共和党中期选举竞选变成一场虚假信息行动。
The newsTrump claimed at Republican rallies that Washington, DC was once overrun by crime and that schools perform gender transitions on children without parental consent. The article says both claims are false and that he is turning the GOP midterm campaign into a disinformation operation.
他不制造事实，他制造接收事实的人。
He doesn't manufacture facts. He manufactures the people who receive them.

特朗普在集会上说，华盛顿的餐厅以前会被整间打劫，“就像狂野西部”。这不是失手，这是产品。

《琼斯母亲》的记者是华盛顿居民，他直接说这是假话。可这条报道最值得看的地方不是特朗普又撒了一个谎，而是它顺带交代了这套谎言的产能：第一个任期里，《华盛顿邮报》统计过他说的三万零五百七十三条假话；如今《华盛顿邮报》的老板贝索斯拥抱了特朗普，就不再系统追踪了。追踪消失，不等于谎言消失。

把表达当武器的人，争的从来不是“事实是怎样的”，而是“事实如何被描绘”。描绘得足够密集，接收端的人就没有别的入口。特朗普说索马里移民“98%不工作”，说通胀降到1.8%，说拜登放进来“一万一千八百八十八个杀人犯”。这些数字不需要真，它们需要的是被反复说，说到听众的脑子里只剩这个版本。

最狠的一条是关于学校的：孩子早上出门叫蒂姆，三四天后回来叫塔米，学校偷偷做变性手术，家长不知情。报道说得很干脆，学校连一片阿司匹林都要家长同意，也没有任何证据支持这套说法。但这条谎言的用途根本不在真假，而在于它把跨性别者做成一个可以随时调用的恐惧符号。共和党团体在全国投放了约二十一万七千条拿跨性别议题做文章的广告，其中约四分之一在得克萨斯。马斯克为击败塔拉里科、扶上帕克斯顿，已经花了接近两亿美元，大部分用来垫这类攻击广告。

这就是武器化的完整链条：先制造一个不存在的手术室，再用它给一个具体的候选人定价。塔拉里科“会带走你的孩子、给他们做转变”——这句话是假的，但它能投票。

报道里没有一个字提到谁在为此付代价，因为代价不在报道里，代价在那些跨性别青少年身上，在那些被当成道具的家长身上。他们没有被采访，没有名字，只有作为恐惧的素材被反复播放。

克鲁兹说埃尔-赛义德是“穆斯林兄弟会”，阿博特说民主党对手支持伊斯兰教法。报道说这些都是胡说，放在过去足以毁掉散布者的竞选。现在它是共和党的标准操作。

所以这篇文章最后问：这次选举能不能击退一个以撒谎为前提的政治运动。这个问题问反了。该问的是，当追踪谎言的机构被收买、当散布谎言的人不受任何惩罚、当谎言已经变成竞选的基础设施，还有什么机制能把它挡回去。报道没有答案。它只证明了差额还在扩大。

Trump told a rally that Washington restaurants used to be held up wholesale, "like the Wild West." That isn't a slip. That's the product.

The Mother Jones writer lives in the DC area and says flatly that it's a lie. But the most revealing thing in the piece isn't the lie—it's the production capacity disclosed along the way: The Washington Post catalogued 30,573 false statements during his first term. Now the Post's owner, Jeff Bezos, has embraced Trump, and the paper no longer bothers to track them. The tracking stopped. The lying didn't.

When expression becomes a weapon, the fight is never over what the facts are. It's over how the facts get depicted. Depict it densely enough and the receiving end has no other entrance. Trump says 98 percent of Somali immigrants don't work, that inflation is down to 1.8 percent, that Biden let in 11,888 murderers. The numbers don't need to be true. They need to be repeated until the only version left in the listener's head is his.

The cruelest one is about schools: a child leaves as Tim and comes back as Tammy three or four days later, the school performs the transition in secret, the parents never consent. The piece is blunt—schools can't dispense an aspirin without a parent's permission, and there's no evidence for any of it. But the lie's function was never truth-value. It turns trans people into a fear symbol available on demand. Republican groups have aired about 217,000 ads nationwide exploiting the trans issue, roughly a quarter of them in Texas. Elon Musk has spent close to $200 million to defeat Talarico and elect Paxton, much of it underwriting those attack ads.

That's the full weaponization chain: manufacture a surgery room that doesn't exist, then use it to price a specific candidate. Talarico "will vote to take away your children and transition them"—false, but it votes.

Not one word in the piece names who pays. The cost isn't in the reporting; it's on the trans teenagers, on the parents used as props. They get no interview, no name—only playback as fear material.

Cruz says El-Sayed is "Muslim Brotherhood." Abbott says his opponent supports Shariah law. The piece says it's all bunk—the sort of thing that once would have derailed the candidate spewing it. Now it's GOP standard operating procedure.

The article ends by asking whether this election can beat back a political movement premised on lying. That's the wrong question. The question is what mechanism remains when the outfit that tracked the lies gets bought, when the liars pay no price, when lying has become the campaign's infrastructure. The piece has no answer. It only shows the gap still widening.
