浪漫爱叙事的温床与‘真实’的 scamThe Bed of Romance and the Scam of 'Realness'

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-07 § 链接
所谓的‘情绪真实’，不过是给浪漫爱陷阱披上的高级糖衣。
So-called 'emotional authenticity' is just a high-end glaze for the romantic love trap.

看到《卫报》给这部澳洲 romcom 的评价，我简直想笑。评论员用‘wonderfully messy and real’（美妙地混乱且真实）来赞美一对男女在经历失败关系后再次‘命中注定’地相遇。这就是典型的 romantic love 叙事陷阱：它把生活中的随机性、情绪的波动和关系的反复，包装成一种名为‘真实’的审美体验，从而让观众在潜意识里认同——只要感情足够‘深’，所有的 baggage 都可以被丢弃，最终回归到一对一的永恒绑定中。

这种叙事最阴险的地方在于它通过‘messy’（混乱）来伪装公正。它让你觉得它在探讨复杂的人性，但实际上，它依然在加固那个最古老的 meta-violence：女性和男性的存在价值必须通过一段‘完美’的浪漫关系来确证。在这种叙事里，Ashley 的主体性并不在于她如何处理生活，而在于她是否最终与 Gordon 重新达成某种‘化学反应’。这根本不是真实，而是一种经过工业化处理的、旨在诱导人们进入婚姻/伴侣绑定关系的 cultural violence。

有趣的是，同一份电视指南里还出现了一部 1959 年的电影《模仿人生》，讨论种族和阶级。这恰恰形成了讽刺：我们能意识到肤色和阶级的殖民，却在面对‘浪漫爱’这种最彻底的意识形态殖民时，依然在为那种‘甜美’（sweetest thing）而欢呼。当人们在电视前为这种‘命中注定’地心动时，他们其实是在为自己的主体性死亡而鼓掌。

Reading The Guardian's take on this Aussie romcom makes me laugh. The critic uses 'wonderfully messy and real' to praise a couple who, after failed relationships, 'fatefully' encounter each other again. This is the classic romantic love narrative scam: it packages randomness and emotional volatility as an aesthetic experience of 'realness,' conditioning the audience to believe that as long as feelings are 'strong,' all baggage can be discarded in favor of a one-to-one eternal bond.

The most insidious part of this narrative is how it uses 'messiness' to fake justice. It pretends to explore the complexity of human nature, but in reality, it only reinforces the meta-violence: that the existential value of masculine and feminine identities must be validated through a 'perfect' romantic relationship. In this script, Ashley's subjectivity isn't about her own life, but whether she eventually achieves 'chemistry' with Gordon. This isn't real; it's a processed form of cultural violence designed to lure people into the trap of partnership binding.

It's ironic that the same TV guide mentions 'Imitation of Life' (1959), which discusses race and class. This is the punchline: we can recognize the colonization of skin color and class, yet we still cheer for the 'sweetness' of romantic love—the most thorough ideological colonization of all. When people swoon over this 'fate' on screen, they are effectively applauding the death of their own subjectivity.
