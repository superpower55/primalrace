没有起诉书，先关人No Indictment, Just a Signature

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国左翼富豪费吉·钱伯斯（Fergie Chambers）自七月起被关押在西班牙，美方正寻求将其引渡，依据是一份由一名FBI探员签署的刑事控告书而非大陪审团起诉书。两名曾参加2023年新罕布什尔州抗议的证人表示将拒绝向大陪审团作证。钱伯斯在西班牙申请庇护，引渡程序须待庇护案结束。
The newsLeft-wing donor Fergie Chambers has been held in Spain since July as the U.S. seeks his extradition based on a criminal complaint signed by a single FBI agent, not an indictment. Two witnesses from a 2023 New Hampshire protest say they will refuse to testify before a grand jury. Chambers has applied for asylum in Spain.
引渡不需要起诉书，只需要一个探员的签名。
Extradition doesn't need an indictment. It needs one agent's signature.

引渡一个人，不需要起诉书。一份由一名FBI探员签署的刑事控告书就够了。控告书至今封存，钱伯斯看不到，西班牙法官看到的是摘要。他的律师马丁内斯说，二十年执业没见过这样的美国引渡请求。这不是程序瑕疵，这是程序本身在说话：先把人关起来，再去找证据。

控告书里三条罪，两条跟2023年新罕布什尔州那场针对以色列军火商Elbit Systems工厂的抗议有关，说他出钱买了烟雾弹、付了抗议者的律师费。第三条是国际洗钱，涉及他2024年转去突尼斯的七百多万美元，美方说这钱给了哈马斯。但引渡令里没写他因“实质支持恐怖主义”被起诉。突尼斯俱乐部方面和他家人说，那笔钱是还俱乐部的债、修训练场。哪个版本是真的，报道没给答案；可一个连起诉书都还没有的案子，已经让一个人在马德里关了四个月。

真正刺眼的是那两个女人。Shergalis和Ross为同一场抗议被查了一年，被威胁重罪、几十年刑期，拒绝了几次交易，最后判了轻罪、六十天。现在大陪审团传她们，她们说不去，准备援引第五修正案。律师Kelley说，最多可能因藐视法庭关十八个月。马丁内斯说这像多年后的切尔西·曼宁。确实像：拒绝为别人的案子作证，代价是自己进去。

这套机器不需要证明你有罪，只需要让你付得起代价。控告书封着，起诉书可以等引渡之后再“寻求”，证人不合作就关到合作。马丁内斯那句话说得准：干这些事的人拿了六十天，这个他们不告诉西班牙。因为西班牙大概不会为六十天的事交人。

钱伯斯是Cox家族的继承人，2023年把约两亿五千万美元的股票卖回给家族，此后在二十个国家资助约一百个人道和左翼项目，包括加沙的援助。他的伴侣说这些是援助项目，美方说其中一笔去了哈马斯。一个人有多少钱，决定了他能撑多久；可这套程序对没钱的人是一样的，只是更快。

NSPM-7把左翼行动主义写成恐怖主义，FBI照着去查。Shergalis和Ross签了那个被告联盟的承诺：不合作、集体策略、公开。她们公开了，于是大陪审团的保密第一次被反过来用。这不是英雄主义，这是被逼到只剩这一招。

马丁内斯说，真正的原因是钱伯斯反对加沙的种族灭绝。这话是推断，但和控告书的形状对得上：抗议的轻罪、洗钱的重罪、封存的签名。一个国家要惩罚一种立场，不必先证明那个人犯了罪，只要让他付不起辩护的价钱。那两个女人周三去法庭，准备不说话。她们不说话，是因为这个案子还没准备好让她们说话。

You don't need an indictment to extradite a man. A criminal complaint signed by a single FBI agent will do. The complaint is sealed; Chambers can't see it, and the Spanish judge sees a summary. His lawyer, Martínez, says he has never in twenty years seen a U.S. extradition request built on a complaint. This isn't a procedural hiccup. It's the procedure speaking: lock the man up first, look for evidence later.

Two of the three charges concern a late-2023 protest in New Hampshire against a factory run by Elbit Systems, Israel's largest arms manufacturer. The summary says Chambers funded smoke bombs and protesters' legal fees. The third is international money laundering, over $7 million sent to Tunisian accounts in 2024. The U.S. says the money went to Hamas; the order does not charge him with material support for terrorism. His family and Club Africain say it paid the club's debts and training grounds. The report doesn't settle which version is true. What it does show is a man held in Madrid for months on a case that has no indictment yet.

The sharper edge is the two women. Shergalis and Ross were investigated for a year over the same protest, threatened with felonies and decades in prison, turned down deals, and ended up with misdemeanors and 60-day sentences. Now a grand jury wants them. They say they won't testify, and will invoke the Fifth. Their lawyer says civil contempt could mean up to 18 months. Martínez compares it to Chelsea Manning. It is: refusing to testify against someone else's case, at the price of your own freedom.

The machine doesn't need to prove you guilty. It needs to make you pay. The complaint stays sealed, the indictment can be sought after extradition, and a witness who won't cooperate gets held until she does. Martínez puts it plainly: the people who carried out the acts got 60 days, and that detail is left out. Spain probably won't hand someone over for conduct that earned 60 days.

Chambers is heir to the Cox family; in 2023 he sold back stock worth about $250 million and has since funded roughly 100 humanitarian and left-wing projects in 20 countries, including aid in Gaza. His partner says aid. The U.S. says one transfer went to Hamas. Money decides how long you can hold out. The procedure is the same for people without it, only faster.

NSPM-7 paints left-wing activism as terrorism, and the FBI follows. Shergalis and Ross signed the defendants union's pledge: no cooperation, collective strategy, go public. They went public, and for once grand jury secrecy got turned around. Not heroism. The only move left.

Martínez says the real reason is Chambers's opposition to genocide in Gaza. That's an inference, but it fits the shape of the complaint: misdemeanor protest, felony money laundering, a sealed signature. A state that wants to punish a position doesn't have to prove the man committed a crime. It only has to make the defense unaffordable. On Wednesday the two women go to court and plan to say nothing. They say nothing because the case isn't ready for them to speak.
