不让你就座，也是一种暴力Not Seating You Is Also Violence

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国智库「美国复兴中心」（CRA）上月发文，主张即使部分民主党候选人赢得选举，也可因其所谓「红绿联盟」立场——即「新马克思主义进步派与反西方穆斯林」的联合——而被拒绝就任国会议员。CRA由「2025计划」主要设计者、现任白宫管理和预算办公室主任拉塞尔·沃特创立。法律学者认为该主张难获法院支持，但指出其目的在于试探并制造恐惧。
The newsThe Center for Renewing America, a right-wing think tank founded by Russell Vought, published a post arguing that certain Democrats—branded part of a "Red-Green Alliance" of "neo-Marxist progressives and anti-Western Muslims"—could be denied their elected seats even if they win. Legal scholars say the theory is unlikely to survive court challenge, but warn it is being floated to test what sticks and to create fear.
选举不是权力来源，计票和就座才是。
Elections aren't the source of power. Counting and seating are.

这篇东西最值得看的不是它多离谱，而是它把「谁算合法当选」这件事，从计票结果挪到了资格审查。CRA的奥斯本写得很清楚：一个候选人如果「拥抱了摧毁宪政秩序」的立场，就不能诚实地宣誓支持那个秩序——所以不能让他就座。翻译一下：赢不赢是选民的事，坐不坐得下是另一回事。

这是元暴力最标准的一次操作。它不跟你争政策，不跟你争选票，它直接跳到解释权那一层，重新定义「什么算一个合法的议员」。被点名的是谁？「小队」四名有色人种女性议员、曼达尼、易卜拉欣。理由不是他们做了什么违法的事，是智库替他们认定的「意识形态」。报道写得很干净：这些被点名的政客里，没有一个自称马克思主义者。

CRA的创始人沃特，一边自称「激进宪政主义者」，一边扣着国会批的钱不发，去年冻了50亿对外援助、今年又收回8亿多，连共和党参议员柯林斯都说这可能违反《扣款控制法》。一个拒绝执行国会拨款决定的人，现在来论证谁不配进国会。这不是讽刺，这是共谋者理论的标准样本：规则对他有利时他讲规则，规则对他不利时他重新解释规则，而解释权一直在他那一边。

宗教那条线更直接。布雷特施奈德说，用穆斯林的宗教信仰来取消资格，比针对某个政治群体还难，因为宪法明文禁止宗教审查。也就是说，这套方案连自己援引的那部宪法都过不去。但过不去不是重点。布雷特施奈德那句话才是重点：「他们显然是在试探，看能不能站住。」

试探本身就是收益。格罗斯讲得很实在：如果民主党只赢一两个参议院席位，共和党就可能去争结果、去拖、去让法官发禁制令，最后变成国会和法院的对峙。如果赢四席，这套就玩不转。所以这事的真正变量不是法律，是差距有多大——差距越小，拒绝就座的成本越低，收益越高。1967年众议院拒绝让鲍威尔就座，先例在那儿摆着。

皮尔德斯说「这不会发生」。也许。但布雷特施奈德提醒的那件事更该被记住：政府已经多次试图给科米安罪名，哪怕检察官大概率定不了罪。定不了罪，恐惧已经生产出来了。「他们下一个可能来找我。」这句话不需要法院支持，它自己会走路。

所以真正被削掉的不是哪个席次，是「赢了就能坐上」这个默认前提。这个前提一旦变成可以谈判的东西，每一次选举的差额就都多了一个不由选民决定的变量。差额越大越安全，这个逻辑本身，就是对每一个险胜的选区里的选民说：你们的票，得赢到让他们不好意思赖，才算数。

The interesting thing here isn't how absurd the argument is. It's that it moves "who is legitimately elected" from the vote count to a qualification review. Osborne at CRA writes it plainly: a candidate who has "embraced the destruction of the constitutional order" cannot truthfully swear to support that order—so he or she cannot be seated. Translation: winning is the voters' business; sitting down is something else.

This is meta-violence in its purest form. It doesn't argue policy, it doesn't argue votes. It jumps straight to the layer of interpretation and redefines what counts as a legitimate member of Congress. Who gets named? The four women of color in the Squad, Mamdani, El-Sayed. Not for anything they did illegally—for an ideology the think tank has assigned to them. The reporting is clean on this: none of the politicians named have self-identified as Marxists.

CRA's founder, Russell Vought, calls himself a "radical constitutionalist" while impounding money Congress approved—$5 billion in foreign aid frozen last year, more than $800 million clawed back this year, a move Senator Collins says may violate the Impoundment Control Act. A man who refuses to execute Congress's spending decisions is now arguing about who doesn't deserve to enter Congress. That's not irony. That's the co-conspirator model in its natural habitat: rules when they help, reinterpretation when they don't, and the interpretive authority staying on his side the whole time.

The religion line is even more direct. Brettschneider says using a Muslim politician's faith to disqualify them is harder than targeting a political group, because the Constitution explicitly forbids a religious test for office. The scheme can't even clear the document it claims to be defending. But clearing it was never the point. His other sentence is the point: "Clearly what they're doing is floating it to see if it will stick."

Floating it is itself the payoff. Grose lays out the real variable: if Democrats win one or two Senate seats, Republicans can dispute results, stall, get an injunction, and turn it into a Congress-versus-courts showdown. Win four, and the strategy collapses. So the variable isn't law, it's margin—the smaller the margin, the cheaper the refusal to seat, the higher the return. The 1967 refusal to seat Adam Clayton Powell Jr. is the precedent sitting right there.

Pildes says, "This isn't going to happen." Maybe. But Brettschneider's reminder matters more: the administration has repeatedly tried to charge Comey with crimes even when conviction is unlikely. No conviction, and the fear is already manufactured. "They might come after me next." That sentence doesn't need a court to back it. It walks on its own.

So what's actually being削ed away isn't a seat. It's the default premise that winning means sitting. Once that premise becomes negotiable, every election margin gains a variable that voters don't control. The bigger the margin, the safer—and that logic itself tells every voter in every close district: your vote only counts if you win by enough that they're embarrassed to deny it.
