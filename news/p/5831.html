免税额涨了，谁在付这笔钱The Allowance Went Up. Who Pays for It?

其他 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把税免掉，不等于把压力免掉
A tax break is not a pressure break.

把副业的免税额度从 £1,000 提到 £3,000，报道说最多 140 万人受益，其中约 40 万人税单会下降，平均省 £380，另有约 10 万人不用再向 HMRC 申报这笔收入（来源：The Guardian）。这是一笔真实的差额被缩小了——在 structural 层，行政负担和税负同时往下走了一格。但先别急着鼓掌，看清楚这笔钱是从哪里挪出来的。

报道自己给了背景：家庭财务承压，副业人数爆炸。2025 年有 260 万人通过 eBay、Vinted、Etsy 卖东西，2024 年这个数字是 45 万（来源：The Guardian）。一年之内翻了快六倍。这不是英国人突然爱上了整理衣柜，这是主业的收入不够用了。副业从“爱好变现”变成“第二份工”，免税额度跟着涨，等于国家承认了这件事，并且选择用减税的方式承认，而不是用涨工资或者降房租的方式。

所以这条政策的真正内容是：你的收入缺口，请你自己在业余时间补上，我少收一点，算是支持。听起来慷慨，实际上是把一个 structural 问题翻译成了一份税务便利。一个需要靠遛狗和卖旧衣服补家用的人，省下 £380 当然好，但 £380 不会让房租变便宜，也不会让主业的小时工资变高。

报道里两位受访者的说法值得并排看。AJ Bell 的 Sarah Coles 说，这“不只是省税，还把行政的麻烦从你手上拿走了”，你可以花更多时间为自己工作。Lady Janey 的 Jane Hawkes 说这是把爱好和技能变成额外收入的好办法，同时提醒人们搞清楚“卖闲置”和“经营获利”的区别，别不小心踩了 HMRC 的规则。一个在说省事，一个在说别犯错。两句都对，两句都没有提到那个让 260 万人上线卖东西的原因。

还有一层要指出：平台从去年起必须把卖家信息报给 HMRC，一年内任何平台上卖了 30 笔或 €2,000 的人都在数据里（来源：The Guardian）。也就是说，监管的入口已经建好了，免税额度是在这个入口建好之后才调高的。先能看见你，再决定少收你一点。这不是阴谋，但顺序值得记住——国家先拿到了名单，然后才展示了慷慨。

£3,000 这个数字本身也说明问题：它被设定在“小副业”的尺度上。够你卖几件衣服、接几个家教单子，不够你把副业做成主业。政策精准地鼓励了一种状态——你有一份不够用的主职，外加一份不能太大的补贴。这个形状，就是它想让你待着的地方。

省下的 £380 是真的。但一个社会需要给 260 万人发一张副业免税券，这件事本身就是一张账单，只是它没有写在预算文件的那一页上。

Raising the tax-free side-hustle allowance from £1,000 to £3,000 will benefit up to 1.4 million people, cut the tax bill of roughly 400,000 of them by an average of £380, and free about 100,000 from having to report the income to HMRC at all (source: The Guardian). That is a real gap being closed — at the structural layer, both the tax and the paperwork move down a notch. But before the applause, look at where the money is being moved from.

The article supplies its own context: household finances under pressure, side hustles exploding. 2.6 million people sold goods through eBay, Vinted and Etsy last year; in 2024 the number was 450,000 (source: The Guardian). Nearly six times in a year. That is not a nation suddenly discovering the joy of decluttering. That is the main job no longer covering the bills. The side hustle has shifted from hobby-monetising to second shift, and the allowance is rising because the state has quietly conceded the point — conceding it with a tax cut rather than with higher wages or lower rent.

So the actual content of the policy is this: your income shortfall is yours to close in your spare time, and I will take a little less, as a gesture of support. It sounds generous. It is a structural problem translated into a tax convenience. £380 is £380, and it is welcome, but it does not make rent cheaper and it does not raise the hourly wage of the main job.

The two quoted voices are worth reading side by side. Sarah Coles of AJ Bell says the more generous allowance “doesn’t just save tax, it also takes the hassle of admin off their plate” — you can spend more time working for yourself. Jane Hawkes of Lady Janey calls side hustles a great way to turn hobbies into cash, and warns people to understand the difference between selling unwanted belongings and trading for profit so they don’t fall foul of HMRC. One talks about convenience, the other about not making a mistake. Both are correct. Neither mentions the reason 2.6 million people are online selling things.

One more layer: since last year, platforms must report sellers to HMRC, and the department now receives data on anyone who made 30 sales or €2,000 on a platform in a year (source: The Guardian). The surveillance gate was built first. The allowance was raised after. Being seen came before being spared. Not a conspiracy — but the order is worth remembering.

The £3,000 figure is itself a statement. It is calibrated to the scale of a small side hustle: enough for a few resold coats and a handful of tutoring sessions, not enough to turn the hustle into the main thing. The policy precisely rewards one arrangement — an insufficient main job plus a supplement that must stay small. That shape is where it wants you to stay.

The £380 saved is real. But a society that needs to hand 2.6 million people a side-hustle tax voucher is itself a bill. It just isn’t printed on that page of the budget.
