砍掉热泵补贴，先砍的是谁的家Cutting Heat Pump Subsidies Cuts Someone's Home First

其他 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把账单转给纳税人，先挨冻的不是财相
Shifting the bill to taxpayers: the chancellor stays warm.

这份预算案真正的动作，不是"削减开支"，而是把成本从一个口袋挪到另一个口袋，然后宣布目标还在。

报道写得很清楚：Healey 本周砍了能源部超过 30 亿英镑，大部分拿去填国防预算。其中 7.41 亿来自锅炉升级计划——那个给普通家庭 7500 英镑换热泵的补贴。政府原本要把安装量从每年 6 万抬到 2030 年的 25 万，补贴额度从今年的 4 亿涨到 2029-30 年的 7 亿多。现在改成"只随通胀上涨"。Nesta 的 Andrew Sissons 说，这样很难看到怎么达标，甚至可能出现第一个错过自己签下的碳预算的工党政府。

"只随通胀上涨"这五个字是整条新闻的核心。它不是取消，是冻结；不是说不做，是让做的速度停在原地。目标写在纸上，钱没跟上，于是目标变成了一个修辞。这是结构暴力最标准的做法：不动声色地把资源抽走，然后继续用原来的数字说话。

更值得看的是钱去了哪里。国防投资计划需要钱，能源部的资本预算成了提款机。前 Starmer 顾问 Paul Ovenden 说得直白：这是把 Miliband 项目的骨头捡干净。他还补了一句关键的：这些讨论以前也有过，但 Miliband 每次都赢下那场争论；现在他不在那个部门了，事情就发生了。

这句话把机制点破了。政策方向不是被辩论推翻的，是被人事变动腾出来的。Miliband 帮人赶走了 Starmer 和 Reeves，自己也失去了那个能赢下预算争论的位置。能源部消息人士坚持说长期改革会激励人们转向清洁能源、整体有利于绿色议程——把绿色成本从账单挪到纳税人身上，把电价和天然气价格脱钩。这些话可能是真的，但它们回答的是另一个问题：方向还在不在。方向当然还在，只是速度被调慢了，而气候目标只认速度。

这里要说清楚代价落在谁身上。热泵补贴的受益者是住在旧房子里、要换掉燃气锅炉的普通家庭。7500 英镑不是小数目，没有它，换不换就是一道算术题，而算术题的答案通常是"再等等"。等的人越多，安装量越停在 6 万，2030 年的 25 万就越像一个没人打算兑现的承诺。与此同时，被砍的还有 2.5 亿的暖房计划、5 亿的氢能、2 亿的碳捕集——全是当年 Miliband 力推的项目。暖房计划砍的是最直接的一层：冬天付不起取暖费的人。

一个政府消息源说得很坦白：这是本届政府上台以来能源政策最重要的转向，现在的重点是成本，而不只是建设足够容量去达标。"重点是成本"这句话，在预算表上意味着省钱，在住户的账单上意味着另一回事。

所谓"优先考虑可负担性"，听起来像是在替消费者说话。但把补贴砍掉、让安装量停滞，省下的是财政部的钱，付出的是那些继续烧燃气、继续付波动气价的人。可负担性被当成了一个词，用在了和它本意相反的地方。

报道里没有出现任何一个具体住户的名字，没有谁站出来说自己换不起热泵了。这不是报道的疏漏，是这类预算新闻的常态：数字被反复引用，受影响的人始终缺席。缺席本身就是材料——一项政策的代价，往往要等到下一个冬天才有人替它说话。

The real move in this budget isn't "spending cuts." It's moving a cost from one pocket to another, then declaring the target intact.

The report is clear: Healey cut more than £3bn from the energy department this week, much of it to pay for higher defence spending. One casualty is £741m from the boiler upgrade scheme — the £7,500 grant that helps ordinary households swap a gas boiler for a heat pump. The government had planned to raise installations from 60,000 a year to 250,000 by 2030, and to grow the scheme's funding from £400m this year to just over £700m by 2029-30. Now funding rises only with inflation. Andrew Sissons of Nesta says it's very difficult to see how the clean heating targets get hit, and that a Labour government could be the first to miss the carbon budget it signed up to.

"Only with inflation" is the whole story in five words. It isn't cancellation, it's a freeze. It isn't saying no, it's letting the speed stay where it is. The target stays on paper, the money doesn't follow, and the target becomes a figure of speech. This is structural violence in its standard form: quietly withdraw the resources, keep quoting the original number.

Where the money went matters more. Defence investment needs funding, and the energy department's capital budget became the ATM. Paul Ovenden, a former Starmer adviser, puts it plainly: the bones of Ed Miliband's project being picked clean. Then the line that names the mechanism — this had been discussed in government before, but Miliband always won that argument; now he's not in that department, it happened.

Policy direction wasn't argued down. It was vacated. Miliband helped oust Starmer and Reeves and lost the seat from which he used to win the budget fight. Department sources insist the longer-term changes will incentivise cleaner power and benefit the green agenda overall — shifting green costs from bills to taxpayers, decoupling electricity prices from gas. Those may be true. They answer a different question: whether the direction survives. Of course it survives. The speed was slowed, and climate targets only recognise speed.

Now the cost, and who pays it. The grant's beneficiaries are ordinary households in old houses with gas boilers to replace. £7,500 is not a rounding error; without it, swapping becomes an arithmetic problem, and the answer to that problem is usually "later." The more people wait, the more installations sit at 60,000, and the further 250,000 by 2030 looks like a promise nobody intends to keep. Also cut: £250m from the warm homes plan, £500m from hydrogen, £200m from carbon capture and storage — all projects Miliband once championed. The warm homes cut lands on the most direct layer: people who can't afford to heat their homes in winter.

One government source is refreshingly honest: probably the most significant shift in energy policy since taking office; the emphasis now is on cost, not just building enough capacity to hit green targets. "Emphasis on cost" means savings in the budget table and something else entirely on a household bill.

"Prioritising affordability" sounds like it's speaking for consumers. But cutting the subsidy and letting installations stagnate saves the Treasury money and costs the people still burning gas and still paying volatile prices. Affordability gets used as a word against its own meaning.

No household is named in the report. Nobody stands up to say they can no longer afford a heat pump. That isn't an oversight; it's how budget journalism works. Numbers get quoted, the affected stay absent. The absence is itself the material — a policy's cost usually waits until the next winter to find someone to speak for it.
