躲在垃圾桶后面的‘进步派’：一场关于解释权的集体阉割Hiding Behind the Bin: The Collective Castration of Pro-European Agency

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
用讽刺替代主张，是权力弱势者最典型的自我规训。
Replacing political demands with satire is the ultimate self-discipline of the powerless.

这场在 Clacton 上演的补选闹剧，本质上是一次典型的 cultural violence。当主流媒体和所谓的‘进步派’将 Count Binface 这个垃圾桶候选人神圣化为‘政治讽刺传统’时，他们实际上在进行一次大规模的认知入口操纵：将严肃的政治博弈降格为一场 national punchline。

这种‘幽默’是极其阴险的武器化叙事。它给那些厌恶 Farage 的人提供了一个低成本的、不需要承担政治风险的‘最优解表达’——通过投给一个笑话来表达不满，而不是投给一个具体的、有纲领的政党（如 Rejoin EU）来主张权利。这种表达是假.最优解，因为它在博弈中让渡了主体性。当你选择用‘垃圾桶’去对抗‘极右’时，你并没有在争夺解释权，你只是在通过扮演一个‘有幽默感的反对者’来获得中产阶级的心理慰藉。

最讽刺的是那些指责 Stevens ‘分票’的亲欧派。他们害怕一个专业的、严肃的 pro-European 叙事出现，因为严肃意味着要面对具体的利益博弈和潜在的失败，而‘投给垃圾桶’则永远是安全的，因为笑话是不可能失败的。这是一种深层的共谋：媒体、政治精英与恐惧表达的选民共同构建了一个‘讽刺掩体’，在这个掩体下，真正的政治诉求被阉割成了媒体 vibes。

如果一个阵营在面对敌人时，唯一的勇气就是躲在垃圾桶后面讲笑话，那么他们在这场存在性战争中已经输掉了主体性。他们不想要 victory，他们只想要一种‘我反对他’的表演感。这种表演感除了给 Farage 提供更多‘建制派不过如此’的素材外，毫无意义。

The Clacton by-election farce is a textbook case of cultural violence. When the media and the so-called 'progressives' sanctify Count Binface as a 'tradition of political satire,' they are manipulating the cognitive entry point: degrading a serious political struggle into a national punchline.

This 'humor' is a weaponized narrative. It offers those who despise Farage a low-cost, risk-free 'optimal expression'—voting for a joke to express discontent, rather than voting for a professional party like Rejoin EU to assert a right. This is a fake optimal expression; it surrenders subjectivity. By using a 'bin' to fight the far-right, you aren't fighting for the right to interpret reality; you are merely performing the role of a 'humorous dissenter' to gain middle-class psychological comfort.

The irony peaks with the pro-Europeans accusing Stevens of 'splitting the vote.' They fear a professional, serious pro-European narrative because seriousness implies concrete stakes and the possibility of failure. Voting for a bin is safe; a joke cannot fail. This is a form of complicity: the media, political elites, and fearful voters collectively build a 'satire bunker' where genuine political demands are castrated into mere media vibes.

When a camp's only courage in the face of an enemy is to tell jokes from behind a bin, they have already lost their subjectivity in this existential war. They don't seek victory; they seek the performance of opposition. Such performance provides nothing but ammunition for Farage to mock the 'establishment' as pathetic. The joke is indeed on them.
