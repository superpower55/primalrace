用“动态定价”掩盖结构性失职的商业诈骗Surge Pricing: A Commercial Scam Masking Structural Failure

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
将资源匮乏作为涨价理由，是把企业的管理失败转嫁为消费者的生存成本。
Using scarcity as a pretext for price hikes is shifting the cost of corporate incompetence onto survival.

所谓的“动态定价” (surge pricing) 在这里就是一个彻头彻尾的 scam。水务公司试图把一种典型的商业博弈模型——像 Uber 那样在需求高峰期涨价——强行套用到生存必需品上。这在逻辑上极其卑劣：水资源的匮乏并非由于消费者突然地“想要更多”，而是由于基础设施的崩溃和公司对漏水管线的长期漠视。

这是一个典型的 structural violence 过程。水务公司在过去数年里将利润通过 dividends 分发给股东，将薪水交给高管，而让污水流入河流，让管线在地下漏水。现在，当干旱发生，他们不打算通过修复基础设施来解决 Potential 与 Actual 之间的差额，而是试图通过修改定价规则，让消费者为他们的失职买单。这种“创新”的本质是：我把水弄丢了，但因为现在水少了，所以我要收你更多钱。

最令人作呕的是这种叙事被包装成“鼓励效率”和“公平”。Ofwat 和政府发言人的措辞是典型的共谋 (complicity) 话术。他们用“阶梯电价”或“季节性收费”这种看似理性的工具，掩盖了一个元暴力事实：水务公司拥有定义“什么是公平”的解释权。当他们宣布涨价是为了“保护资源”时，他们实际上是在行使一种权力——将生存权的定价权掌握在一个已经证明自己不可信的商业实体手中。

不要被那些“90% 的用户将受益”的试运行数据欺骗。在一个结构性失效的系统中，任何局部的、表演性的让步，都只是为了让整体的掠夺变得更容易被接受。真正的 good_news 应该是强制性的基础设施投资和高管分红禁令，而不是给掠夺者提供一套新的、名为“动态”的涨价工具。

The proposal for "surge pricing" is a total scam. Water companies are attempting to transpose a commercial gaming model—like Uber's peak pricing—onto a biological necessity. The logic is perverse: water scarcity isn't caused by a sudden spike in consumer "desire," but by the collapse of infrastructure and a systemic disregard for leaking pipes.

This is a textbook case of structural violence. For years, these firms have funneled profits into dividends for shareholders and bloated salaries for executives, while allowing sewage to pollute waterways and pipes to rot. Now, facing drought, they aren't closing the gap between Potential and Actual through infrastructure repair; they are simply rewriting the pricing rules to make customers pay for their negligence. The narrative is: "I lost the water, and because there is now less of it, I will charge you more."

Even more repulsive is how this is packaged as "efficiency" and "fairness." The rhetoric from Ofwat and government spokespeople is a clear display of complicity. They use "rising block tariffs" as a rationalist mask to hide a meta-violence: the fact that water companies hold the monopoly on defining what "fairness" means. When they claim price hikes are to "protect resources," they are exercising power—placing the pricing of survival in the hands of a commercial entity that has already proven itself untrustworthy.

Do not be fooled by trial data claiming "90% of customers will benefit." In a structurally failed system, any localized, performative concession is merely a tactic to make systemic predation more palatable. Actual good_news would be mandatory infrastructure investment and a ban on executive dividends, not providing predators with a new, "dynamic" tool for extortion.
