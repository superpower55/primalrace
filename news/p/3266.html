被抹除的地理与被献祭的牧民Erasure of Geography and the Sacrificed Pastoralists

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-05 § 链接
气候灾难不是自然随机，而是结构性剥削的物理显现。
Climate disasters are not natural randoms, but physical manifestations of structural exploitation.

把克什米尔山区的暴雨称为“自然灾害”是一场典型的文化暴力。当人们在讨论“气候变化”这个宏大且中立的词汇时，他们实际上在掩盖一个 structural violence 的事实：那些在泥石流中被吞噬的 Gujjar 牧民，本身就是被制度性边缘化的“原初种族”分支。

在这篇报道中，最令人不安的细节不是房屋的坍塌，而是基础设施的极度匮乏——救援人员需要步行 90 分钟才能进入村庄。这种“地理上的隔绝”并非天生，而是权力分配的结果。在一个被军事化管理、资源被高度垄断的地区，边缘社区的生存权被定价为最低。他们被允许在山上生存，但并不被赋予生存所需的 early warning systems 或坚固的基础设施。这种状态就是 Violence = Potential − Actual：他们本可以拥有预警系统，但实际状态是赤手空拳地在河床里翻找尸体。差额部分，就是暴力。

最讽刺的是，这种暴力在叙事中被包装成了“不可预测的极端天气”。当 Aamir Asghar Khan 说“没有什么事是可预测的”时，他其实在描述一种生存状态的崩塌。这种崩塌不仅是生态的，更是主体性的。当一个族群的 ancestral livelihoods 被抹除，他们不仅失去了房子，更失去了定义自己是谁的表达空间。

在这种共谋下，国际社会关注气候变暖，政府关注边境安全，而最底层的牧民在一个没有路、没有电、没有信号的黑盒里，等待着被泥石流随机地选中。

Labeling the cloudbursts in Kashmir as a "natural disaster" is a classic form of cultural violence. While the world discusses "climate change"—a grand, neutral term—they are effectively masking a structural violence: the Gujjar pastoralists swallowed by mudslides are, in fact, a branch of the Primal Race, systematically marginalized by the state.

The most disturbing detail in this report is not the collapsed houses, but the utter lack of infrastructure—rescue teams having to trek for 90 minutes to reach the village. This "geographical isolation" is not an accident of nature; it is a product of power distribution. In a region under heavy militarization and resource monopoly, the right to exist for marginalized communities is priced at the lowest. They are permitted to live on the mountains, but are denied the early warning systems or resilient infrastructure necessary for survival. This is the Violence Triangle in action: Violence = Potential − Actual. The gap between the possibility of a warning system and the reality of searching for bodies by hand is the violence.

Ironically, this violence is weaponized as "unpredictable extreme weather." When Aamir Asghar Khan says "nothing seems predictable anymore," he is describing the collapse of an existential state. This collapse is not just ecological, but an erasure of identity. When a community's ancestral livelihoods are wiped out, they lose not only their homes but the very space of expression that defines who they are.

Under this complicity, the international community worries about global warming, governments focus on border security, and the bottom-tier pastoralists remain in a black box—without roads, electricity, or signals—waiting to be randomly selected by the next landslide.
