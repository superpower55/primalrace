反数据中心的人，正靠数据中心赚钱They Protest Data Centers, They Own Them

科技 结构层 · 文化层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
反AI数据中心的政客，自己就是股东。
The politicians attacking AI data centers are shareholders in them.

柯林斯在辩论台上被一个喊"AI数据中心在毁掉我们的气候、毒害我们的水"的人打断，她的第一反应是：这是特洛伊的人。杰克逊回了一句：我根本不认识这孩子。两个人合力把这个人的话处理成了一次党派噪音，然后他被带走了。

他没有说错什么。报道写得很清楚：柯林斯的丈夫托马斯·达夫隆持有大约30万到80万美元与AI和数据中心相关的投资，这让他们家在参议院排第五。柯林斯本人反对全面禁令——她的选民里有76%反对继续建数据中心。她还用拨款委员会主席的位置，为缅因州中部一个"高性能计算中心数据中心"拿到了620万美元联邦资金。

这就是共谋的样子，而且它不需要秘密。它发生在公开场合：一个人喊出了真话，两个候选人同时判定这句话不算数。解释权在这一刻完成了交接——什么问题可以被提出，由台上那两个人决定。

国会两院加起来持有1亿到300万美元之间的数据中心相关投资（来源：Mother Jones）——等等，是1亿到3亿美元。五分之一议员报告自己或家人持有AI开发商、芯片厂、云服务商、数据中心公司或AI初创的股份。前五大投资家庭全是民主党人，包括康纳和佩洛西。这几位同时也是国会最活跃的股票交易者。

所以别把它读成两党互相攻击的素材。它是一份名单。

真正让我停下来的是亨里科县那一段。那里今年热浪期间，官员让学校关灯"省电"，同时县里37个数据中心满负荷运转。谁先断电，谁后断电，这不是市场决定的，是结构决定的：能付得起电费、能雇得起游说者、能把议员变成股东的一方，继续亮着；学校的孩子关灯。加尔通那个公式在这里不需要修辞——本可以让孩子开着灯，实际没有，差额就是暴力，只不过它不流血，它叫"能源管理"。

维特曼在国防授权法案里塞进条款，给军事基地建数据中心和小型核反应堆。他的披露文件显示他持有10万到30万美元相关股票。他的竞选网站说，投资由财务经理打理，"不咨询他"。这句话的功能不是澄清，是把责任外包给一个没有名字的人。

卡纳提出《数据中心权利法案》，禁止数据中心建在住宅区、以及距离住宅、学校、托儿所、医院和养老院2500英尺以内。他的妻子和受抚养子女持有340万到840万美元的AI基础设施公司股票，包括英伟达。他称这个行业"掠夺性"。这两件事可以同时是真的，也可以同时是策略：批评是用来占据话语位置的，持股是用来收租的。

特朗普倒是诚实，他说美国人应该让数据中心"统治"，同时他持有290万到950万美元相关股票，一年内增长600%。诚实不等于无害，它只是把共谋从桌下搬到了桌上，然后要求你为这份坦诚鼓掌。

这里没有人在撒谎，这才是最坏的部分。所有人都在同一套激励里做最优解表达：对外反对、对内持股、对选民说"我不是反对发展，我是反对不受约束的发展"。这套话术的成本由谁付？由那个在辩论现场被请出去的人付，由亨里科县关灯上课的孩子付，由水被抽走、电价被推高的社区付。

报道没有写这些社区的名字，也没有写那个喊话的人后来怎么样了。它只写了谁持有多少股。这个信息结构本身就是答案：在这场博弈里，被定价的是股票，不被定价的是那个孩子头顶的灯。

At the October 15 debate, a heckler shouted that AI data centers are destroying the climate and poisoning the water. Susan Collins said he was obviously a Troy Jackson supporter. Jackson said he didn't even know the kid. The two of them jointly processed his sentence into partisan noise, and he was escorted out.

He wasn't wrong about anything. Collins's husband, Thomas Daffron, holds an estimated $300,000 to $800,000 in AI- and data center-linked investments, making their household the Senate's fifth largest such investor. Collins opposes a blanket ban; 76 percent of her constituents oppose further construction. She also used her position as Appropriations Committee chair to secure $6.2 million in federal funding for a "high-performance computing hub data center" in central Maine.

This is what complicity looks like, and it doesn't require secrecy. It happens in public: one person says something true, and both candidates agree it doesn't count. The interpretive authority changes hands in that instant. What questions may be asked is decided by the two people on stage.

Congress collectively holds between $100 million and $300 million in data-center-related investments, according to their financial disclosures (source: Mother Jones). One in five members reports that they or someone in their household is invested in AI developers, chipmakers, cloud providers, data center companies, or private AI startups. The top five investing households are all Democrats, including Ro Khanna and Nancy Pelosi. They are also among Congress's most active stock traders.

So don't read this as ammunition for one party against the other. Read it as a list.

The Henrico County detail is where I stop. During a heat wave this year, officials told schools to turn off their lights to "save energy," while the county's 37 data centers kept operating at full capacity. Who loses power first and who keeps it is not decided by the market. It's decided by structure: the side that can pay the bills, hire the lobbyists, and turn legislators into shareholders keeps the lights on. The schoolchildren turn theirs off. Galtung's formula needs no rhetoric here — the children could have had light, they didn't, and the gap is violence. It just doesn't bleed. It's called "energy management."

Rob Wittman reportedly inserted a clause into this year's NDAA to fund data centers and small nuclear reactors on military bases. His disclosures show $100,000 to $300,000 in related stocks. His campaign website says his investments are overseen by a financial manager who makes decisions "without consulting him." That sentence doesn't clarify anything. It outsources responsibility to a person with no name.

Khanna introduced a Data Center Bill of Rights banning construction in residential areas and within 2,500 feet of homes, schools, childcare facilities, hospitals, and nursing homes. His wife and dependent children reportedly own between $3.4 million and $8.4 million in AI infrastructure stocks, including Nvidia. He calls the industry "extractive." Both things can be true at once, and both can be strategy: criticism occupies the discursive position, shareholding collects the rent.

Trump is at least honest. He says Americans should let data centers "reign," and he holds between $2.9 million and $9.5 million in companies that own, operate, or provide infrastructure for them, a 600 percent increase in one year. Honesty is not harmlessness. It just moves the complicity from under the table to on top of it and asks you to applaud the candor.

Nobody here is lying, which is the worst part. Everyone is executing an optimal expression inside the same incentive: oppose in public, hold in private, tell voters you're not against development, only against unconstrained development. Who pays for that line? The person escorted out of the debate hall. The children in Henrico County doing homework with the lights off. The communities whose water gets drawn down and whose electricity bills go up.

The report doesn't name those communities, and it doesn't say what happened to the heckler afterward. It only says who holds how much stock. That information structure is itself the answer: in this game, the stocks get priced. The light above that child's head does not.
