输掉四百万票，飞往德州Four Million Votes Down, Headed for Texas

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
他们把竞选当提款，把败选当登机牌。
They treat the campaign as an ATM and the loss as a boarding pass.

弗拉维奥·博索纳罗输掉大选第二天对一千六百万粉丝说，他从没想过离开这个国家。几个小时后，他离开巴西利亚的宅邸，去美国申请庇护。这不是他一个人的事，这是博索纳罗家传的操作手册：先煽动，再逃跑，让追随者留在原地承担后果。

这套剧本的结构值得看清楚。它不生产权力，它生产"被偷走的胜利"。特朗普说"看起来又是场被操纵的选举"，而弗拉维奥自己的党当天晚些时候发声明承认了结果。叙事和事实在这里分道扬镳，分道扬镳本身就是目的：只要有一半人相信选举被偷，输掉的人就仍然是受害者，而不是失败者。这是文化暴力最标准的一道工序——把"我输了"改写成"他们偷了"，于是暴力被追认成正义。

代价由谁付，报道写得很清楚。雅伊尔·博索纳罗拒绝出席就职典礼、飞往美国、住在奥兰多一间小黄人主题的儿童卧室里；一周后，他的盟友围攻巴西利亚，打砸国会、总统府和最高法院。近四十年的巴西民主靠几位军方高层在最后一刻犹豫才保住。雅伊尔后来被判二十七年，罪名包括策划刺杀卢拉、阿尔克明和最高法院大法官莫赖斯。他如今在家软禁，据普遍认为，他把获释的希望押在弗拉维奥的总统任期上——而那个任期没有来。

所以这条新闻真正刺人的地方不在"逃"，在"谁留下"。冲进三权广场的人被判刑了，高层将领被判刑了，而发号施令的那家人，一个在德州住一百二十万美元的豪宅，一个刚拿到绿卡，弗拉维奥正准备在德州郊区的沙发上收到自己的判决书。埃杜阿多甚至是被缺席判了四年两个月——因为他去游说美国政府干预他父亲的审判。游说本身是罪，判决本身可以不到场。这就是共谋者理论最赤裸的样子：同一套规则，对底层是刑期，对顶层是航班。

弗拉维奥的竞选资金也遵循同一逻辑。他和被罢黜的银行家丹尼尔·沃尔卡罗谈成两千四百万美元、已到账一千万，钱打进美国账户，名义上是给一部由吉姆·卡维泽主演的雅伊尔传记片融资。巴西联邦警察已经戳穿了这个说法，正在查这笔钱是否实际用于资助埃杜阿多游说特朗普政府制裁巴西、帮他哥哥的选情。他还因与威勒·托马兹运营的在线博彩公司相关的十亿美元洗钱案被调查，而托马兹八月离境，现在住在迈阿密。警察以他有潜逃风险为由请最高法院收缴护照，法官批准了，期限一过就注销了他的旅行证件——那时他已经在去美国的路上了。

特朗普政府说要给他和家人签证。这句话是整个故事里最诚实的部分：两个家族互相认出了对方。爱国主义的修辞、道德正义的外衣、把公职当个人发财捷径，这些是他们的共同语言。而真正的共同语言是最后一句——当谎言追上来的时候，先跳船，把追随者留下。

报道没有写那些在巴西利亚被定罪的人现在怎么想。这才是这份操作手册最稳定的收益结构：它需要有人相信，需要有人上街，需要有人坐牢，然后需要有人买机票。前三件事由别人做，最后一件由自己人做。

弗拉维奥说他是"工人党摧毁国家计划的最后一道障碍"。他现在在德州。障碍物挪走了，国家还在，而他自己成了那个被留在原地的国家的逃犯。

The day after Flávio Bolsonaro lost the presidential election, he told his 16 million Instagram followers he had never thought about leaving the country. A few hours later he left his mansion in Brasília and sought asylum in the U.S. This is not one man's story. It is the Bolsonaro family playbook: rile them up, run, and leave the followers behind to pay.

The structure is worth naming. This playbook does not produce power; it produces the stolen victory. Trump told reporters it looked like another rigged election, while Flávio's own party put out a statement that same day acknowledging the results. Narrative and fact part ways here, and the parting is the point: as long as half the country believes the election was stolen, the loser remains a victim rather than a loser. This is cultural violence at its most standard — rewriting "I lost" as "they stole it," so that violence gets retroactively certified as justice.

Who pays is spelled out. Jair Bolsonaro skipped the January 2023 inauguration, fled to the U.S., and stayed in a Minions-themed children's bedroom in Orlando. A week later his allies besieged Brasília, vandalizing Congress, the presidential palace, and the Supreme Court. Nearly four decades of Brazilian democracy survived only because key military leaders blinked at the last moment. Jair was later sentenced to 27 years for leading the plot, which included a plan to assassinate Lula, Geraldo Alckmin, and Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes. He is under house arrest now, widely thought to be pinning his hopes for freedom on a Flávio presidency that wasn't to be.

So the sharp edge of this story isn't the flight. It's who stays. The people who stormed the plaza were convicted. The top military brass were convicted. And the family that gave the orders? One son lives in a $1.2 million McMansion in Southlake, Texas, with fresh green cards. Flávio is poised to receive news of his own prison sentence while sitting on a couch in a Texas suburb. Eduardo was sentenced in absentia to four years and two months — for lobbying the U.S. government to interfere in his father's trial. The lobbying is the crime; the sentencing can happen without you. This is the co-conspirator theory in its rawest form: same rules, but for the bottom it's a prison term and for the top it's a flight number.

The campaign money follows the same logic. Flávio negotiated $24 million — and was wired $10 million — from disgraced banker Daniel Vorcaro, into a U.S. account, supposedly to finance a Jair biopic starring Jim Caviezel. Brazilian Federal Police have already poked holes in that story and are investigating whether the money actually bankrolled Eduardo's lobbying of the Trump administration to sanction Brazil and help his brother's electoral chances. Flávio is also under investigation over a billion-dollar money-laundering scheme tied to an online betting company run by Willer Tomaz, who left Brazil in August and now lives in Miami. Police asked the Supreme Court to order Flávio to surrender his passport, citing flight risk. A top justice granted it and canceled his travel documents when the deadline passed. By then he was already on his way to the U.S.

The Trump State Department says it will grant him and his family visas. That line is the most honest part of the whole story: two families recognizing each other. Patriotism, moral righteousness, political office as a personal get-rich-quick scheme — that's their shared language. The real shared language is the last sentence: when the lies catch up, jump ship first and abandon the followers.

The report doesn't say what the people convicted in Brasília are thinking now. That is the playbook's most stable profit structure: it needs someone to believe, someone to take to the streets, someone to go to prison — and then it needs someone to buy a ticket. The first three jobs go to other people. The last one goes to family.

Flávio said he was "the last obstacle to the Workers' Party's plan to destroy the country." He is in Texas now. The obstacle moved. The country is still there. And he is the fugitive from the country he left behind.
