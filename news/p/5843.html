军队不是稀缺品，服役才是The Army Is Not Scarce. Serving Is.

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
他们嘲笑的不是孙兴慜没当兵，是有人可以不当兵。
They aren't mocking Son for skipping service. They're mocking that anyone can.

孙兴慜这次被队友叫“7号”或者“那个人”，韩国国会开会问，足协代理主席不否认。整件事看起来像更衣室矛盾，其实不是。这是一场关于谁有资格豁免的公开分裂。

报道写得很清楚：世界杯小组赛出局，起因之一是“未具名记者”嘲讽孙兴慜没有服兵役，随后球队抵制媒体。请注意这个顺序：不是球员先闹，是有人先把“没当兵”拿出来当武器，然后才有人反击。孙兴慜16岁离开韩国，在德国体系里长大，拿到亚运会金牌免役——在韩国的制度里，这是合法豁免，不是逃兵。但合法和体面是两回事。当整个国家把服役当成男性成年礼的硬通货，一个被豁免的人站在队伍最前面，他身上的每一分钱、每一个代言、每一次首发，都会被换算成“你凭什么”。

这是表达的定价问题。孙兴慜的表达——英超队长、欧联杯、国家队59球——在球场上是最优解，但在更衣室里，它被另一套算法重新定价：资历、兵役、年龄。1996年那批人（金玟哉、黄仁范、黄喜灿）现在也是资深球员了，他们要新队长、新教练，报道说他们一度准备退出国家队，被足协官员劝住。这不是年轻人不服老将，这是同一套等级秩序内部在争夺谁有资格定义“我们”。韩国队里大一天的前辈要用敬语，希丁克2001年就发现这个问题，逼着球员吃饭坐一起，好让后辈敢在场上喊前辈。二十五年过去，还是这件事。

所以真正的暴力在结构层：一个把男性身体征用为nation-building材料的兵役制度，加上一套把年龄和服从编码成“团队精神”的更衣室文化。这两样东西共同制造了一个悖论——最优秀的球员因为太优秀而豁免，豁免又让他失去在这个体系里的合法性。他被要求代表国家，却不被允许拥有国家要求每个男人付出的那部分代价。

然后是共谋。足协主席和教练在世界杯后辞职、被国会质询、办公室被警察搜查，现在没有主席，临时教练干到11月底，亚洲杯1月就开打。所有人都在说“需要改变”，黄仁范说“九年了，该变的都没变”，金玟哉说“有人不出力”。没有人说清楚要改什么。这种含糊本身就是共谋：只要不点名，等级秩序就不会被真正触碰，责任就停留在“更衣室气氛”这个安全词上。国会那场质询也一样——问的是“他们是不是不叫他名字”，不是“为什么一个免役球员必须靠赢球来赎买自己的合法性”。

孙兴慜34岁，不再是顶级前锋，这是事实。但报道里最冷的一句是：讨论已经从“他该踢边路还是中路”变成“他该不该首发”。一个为国家队进了59球的人，最后要证明的不是自己还能踢，是自己还配。

亚洲杯如果因为也门胡塞武装袭击沙特而推迟到2028年，报道说这可能是“因祸得福”，让分裂的球队重新聚拢。它也可能是另一种延期：把“谁有资格豁免”这个问题再往后推两年，推到孙兴慜退役，推到没有人再需要回答它。

Son Heung-min is now called "No 7" or "that person" inside his own national squad. A politician raised it in a parliamentary committee; the acting KFA president did not deny it. This looks like dressing-room friction. It isn't. It's a public split over who gets to be exempt.

The article is precise about the sequence: at the World Cup, unnamed reporters mocked Son's lack of military service, and the team boycotted the media. The jab came first. Son left Korea at 16, came up through the German system, won an Asian Games gold and with it a legal exemption. Legal is not the same as respectable. When a country treats conscription as the hard currency of male adulthood, an exempt man standing at the front of the line gets every won, every endorsement, every start converted into: what gives you the right.

This is a pricing problem. Son's expression — Premier League captain, Europa League, 59 international goals — is an optimal expression on the pitch. Inside the dressing room it gets repriced by a different ledger: seniority, service, age. The 1996 cohort — Kim Min-jae, Hwang In-beom, Hwang Hee-chan — are senior players now, and they wanted a new captain and a new coach; reports say they were ready to quit the national team before an KFA official talked them down. This is not youth rebelling against age. It's one hierarchy fighting over who gets to define "we."

The violence sits in the structural layer: a conscription system that requisitions male bodies as nation-building material, wrapped in a dressing-room culture that codes age and deference as team spirit. Together they produce a paradox — the best player is exempted for being the best, and the exemption strips him of legitimacy inside the very system that produced him. He is asked to represent the nation while being denied the price the nation demands of every man.

Then there is the complicity. The KFA president and the coach resigned, were grilled by parliament, and the KFA offices were raided. There is no president now, an interim coach until the end of November, and the Asian Cup in January. Everyone says change is needed. Hwang In-beom says nine years have passed and nothing has changed; Kim Min-jae says some players aren't pulling their weight. Nobody names what should change. That vagueness is the complicity: as long as no one names it, the hierarchy stays untouched and responsibility parks itself in the safe word "atmosphere." The parliamentary question was whether they call him by his name — not why an exempt player has to buy back his legitimacy by winning.

Son is 34 and no longer an elite forward. True. The coldest line in the piece is that the debate has moved from where he should play to whether he should start. A man with 59 goals for his country now has to prove not that he can still play, but that he still deserves to.

If the Asian Cup is pushed to 2028 because of Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia, the article calls it a possible blessing — time for a divided team to regroup. It could also be a postponement of the only question that matters: who gets to be exempt. Pushed back two years, until Son retires, until nobody has to answer it.
