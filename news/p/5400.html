亚伯拉罕·林肯号：被当作燃料的“战士精神”USS Abraham Lincoln: The 'Warrior Ethos' as Fuel for the Machine

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-04 § 链接
所谓“战士精神”是掩盖结构性暴力最廉价的文化掩体。
The 'warrior ethos' is the cheapest cultural cover for structural violence.

当特朗普说林肯号部署的时间“还远远不够”时，他完成了一次典型的元暴力（meta violence）操作：将具体的生理极限与精神崩溃，在叙事层面直接抹除，并将其替换为一种宏大的、关于“胜利”和“准备就绪”的男性中心叙事。

在加尔通的暴力三角中，这艘航母是一个微缩的暴力实验室。直接层（direct）是那 8 起自杀企图和跳海行为；结构层（structural）是由于战争规划缺失导致的 250 天连续在海漂泊、食物短缺和医疗匮乏；而文化层（cultural）则是国防部长 Hegseth 试图恢复的所谓“战士精神”（warrior ethos）。这里的逻辑极其阴险：如果你在糟糕的条件下崩溃了，那不是因为制度在剥削你，而是因为你的“战士精神”不足。这种叙事将结构性的资源匮乏转化为个体的心理缺陷，从而让施暴的体制在道德上获得豁免。

最令人作呕的共谋（complicity）发生在权力顶端。行政当局一方面在公共领域将士兵塑造为“英勇的战争机器”，另一方面在实际操作中将他们视为可消耗的燃料。当士兵在社交媒体上控诉父亲被移民局拘留，而自己却在 12 小时的高强度工作中无法分心时，这种个体存在性的撕裂被国家机器以“法律不容违背”为由粗暴地压制。在这种权力结构中，士兵的个体生命被彻底客体化，他们不再是拥有家庭和情感的“人”，而仅仅是航母这个“巨型浮动城市”里的一个零件。

海军声称在下一艘航母上增加了“娱乐设备”和“卫生用品”，这不过是典型的表演性让步。只要“战争叙事”依然垄断着解释权，只要士兵的主体性依然被要求服从于某种不可质疑的“宏大意义”，那么增加几台健身器材并不能抵消 250 天无法触地所带来的存在性虚无。这种“优化”只是为了让下一批燃料在被烧尽之前，能稍微感觉舒服一点。

When Trump claimed the deployment of the USS Abraham Lincoln was 'not nearly long enough,' he executed a textbook move of meta-violence: erasing physical limits and mental collapse from the narrative, replacing them with a masculine-centric story of 'victory' and 'readiness.'

Within Galtung's Violence Triangle, this carrier is a miniature laboratory of violence. The direct layer consists of the eight suicide attempts and jumps overboard. The structural layer is the 250 consecutive days at sea, food shortages, and medical neglect caused by a total failure in war planning. The cultural layer is the 'warrior ethos' that Secretary Hegseth seeks to restore. The logic here is sinister: if you break under abysmal conditions, it is not because the system is exploiting you, but because your 'warrior spirit' is lacking. This narrative transforms structural deprivation into individual psychological failure, granting the oppressive regime moral immunity.

The most repulsive complicity occurs at the top. The administration portrays soldiers as 'heroic war machines' in public, while treating them as disposable fuel in practice. When a sailor laments his father's detention by immigration authorities while forced into 12-hour shifts, the state brutally suppresses this existential tear under the guise of 'law.' In this structure, the sailor is completely objectified—no longer a human with a family, but a mere cog in a 'giant floating city.'

The Navy's promise to add 'entertainment equipment' and 'hygiene products' to the next carrier is mere performative concession. As long as the war narrative monopolizes the power of interpretation, and as long as the subjectivity of the soldier is demanded to be subservient to some unquestionable 'grand meaning,' a few gym machines cannot offset the existential void of 250 days without touching land. This 'optimization' is simply designed to make the next batch of fuel feel slightly more comfortable before they are burnt out.
