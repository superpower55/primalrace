唯一被点名的人，是报告的祭品The Only Name in the Report Was the Sacrifice

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Athletic ↗ 2026-10-24 § 链接
联盟需要一个名字来证明调查发生过，贝尔特兰恰好是那个没有工会的人。
MLB needed one name to prove the investigation happened. Beltrán was the one without a union.

MLB 那份报告不是调查，是献祭。所有球员都拿到豁免，报告里只写一个名字，这个名字恰好属于唯一一个已经退役、没有工会代表的人。贝尔特兰自己说得最准："把我的名字写进报告，那就是我的禁赛"（来源：The Athletic）。联盟不需要禁他，联盟只需要让大都会在 2020 年 1 月替它动手。

按他的说法，2017 年的垃圾桶是"全队从上到下一起决定的"，不是某一个人的主意。那么整个休斯敦、整个联盟、还有他顺口带出的"其他球队也有自己的系统"，全是一张桌上的共谋者。要维持"比赛是干净的"这条叙事，联盟不可能给整支冠军队定罪，于是把一个结构性的作弊压缩成一个人的污点。有人负责犯罪，有人负责抓罪犯，联盟有了 integrity，球员有了豁免，球队保住了冠军。贝尔特兰的罪不是偷暗号，是偷暗号的时候身后没有组织。

但他周一的辩护，说的是同一套共谋的语言：棒球打了一百年，每个队都在破译对手，"我们的系统比任何人的都好"。这是把违禁改写成传统，把电子作弊说成竞争优势。他一边拆穿了联盟的献祭，一边替献祭所保护的那套东西背书——他要的从来不是真相，是回到桌边的座位。

所以周日在库珀斯敦铸铜，周一在 Zoom 上谈大都会教练席，这两件事是同一个动作。联盟当年用他的名字洗了自己，现在再用他的铜像证明自己宽宏，解释权被回收得干干净净。垃圾桶没人敲了，"大家都这么干"的系统还在原地运转，只是下一个祭品还没退役。

MLB's report wasn't an investigation. It was a sacrifice. Every player got immunity, the report named exactly one of them, and that one happened to be retired and without union representation. Beltrán put it better than anyone: putting his name in the report "was my suspension" (source: The Athletic). The league never had to suspend him. It just let the Mets do the firing in January 2020.

By his own account, the trash can in 2017 was a decision made "from top to bottom," not one player's idea. Which means all of Houston, the league itself, and the "other organizations" he casually mentions having "their system" were seated at the same table of co-conspirators. To keep the story that the game is clean, the league could not convict a championship roster, so it compressed a structural cheat into one man's stain. Someone commits the crime, someone catches the criminal, and everybody keeps their value: the league gets integrity, the players get immunity, the team keeps its ring. Beltrán's offense was not stealing signs. It was stealing signs with no institution behind him.

But his defense on Monday speaks the same conspiratorial language: baseball has been played for a hundred years, everyone deciphers the other side, "our system was better than any other system." That rewrites a banned practice as tradition and electronic cheating as competitive edge. He exposes the sacrifice while vouching for everything the sacrifice was built to protect. He was never after the truth. He wants his seat back at the table.

So bronze in Cooperstown on Sunday and a Zoom chat about the Mets' dugout on Monday are one motion, not two. The league once used his name to clean itself; now it uses his plaque to prove its generosity, and the right to interpret the whole affair is quietly repossessed. Nobody bangs the trash can anymore, but the system where everybody does it is still running. The next sacrifice just hasn't retired yet.
