单车上的雄风与被抹除的半数人类The Masculine Glory on Wheels and the Erased Half of Humanity

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Athletic ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
所谓“伟大的赛事”，本质是男性在男性叙事中完成的自我感动。
The so-called 'Great Race' is essentially a self-indulgent loop of masculine narrative.

21张照片，一个关于速度、痛苦、复仇与荣耀的闭环。Tadej Pogačar 像摩西一样劈开人群，打破29年前的纪录，而 Vingegaard 在碎掉的锁骨中寻找职业生涯的闭环。这套叙事极其完整，因为它在一个纯净的、由男性定义并执行的元暴力（meta-violence）场域中运行：男人在山巅受苦，男人在冲线时崩溃，男人在历史的刻度上互相比拼谁更“超人”。

有趣的是，这篇文章在描述“法国深处”（La France profonde）的家庭传统时，提到人们坐在祖父的膝盖上，张大嘴巴看着车队掠过。在这个被神圣化的“传统”里，女性的存在感被稀释到了近乎透明。她们是观众席上的背景板，是等待在终点线的奖赏，或者是被提及但不可见的服务者。这种 cultural violence 的高明之处在于，它并不需要通过禁止女性参与来维持，而是在定义什么是“竞技的本质”和“纯粹的痛苦”时，就预先将女性排除在主体性之外。

这场名为 Tour de France 的存在性战争，其实是一场大规模的共谋。赞助商、媒体、观众，共同维持着一个“男性中心叙事”的认知入口。当人们在赞叹 Pogačar 的“受苦脸”时，实际上是在消费一种被精心包装的 masculine-centric 英雄主义。在这种叙事中，痛苦被赋予了价值，而这种价值的定价权牢牢掌握在男性手中。

Vive le Tour? 事实上，这只是一个巨大的、由碳纤维和雄激素构建的男性社交俱乐部，在向世界宣告：这里才是定义“极限”和“荣耀”的地方，而其他所有人的存在，仅仅是为了见证这场表演。

21 photographs, a closed loop of speed, pain, revenge, and glory. Tadej Pogačar parts the crowds like Moses, shattering a 29-year-old record, while Vingegaard seeks a career closure amidst a broken collarbone. The narrative is seamless because it operates within a pure field of meta-violence: men suffering on peaks, men collapsing at the finish, and men competing against each other to see who is more 'superhuman'.

Interestingly, when the article describes 'La France profonde' and family traditions, it mentions people sitting on their grandfather's knee, mouths agape, watching the peloton flash by. In this sanctified 'tradition', the presence of women is diluted to near transparency. They are background noise in the stands, rewards waiting at the finish line, or invisible caregivers. The brilliance of this cultural violence lies in the fact that it doesn't need to explicitly ban women; it simply excludes them from subjectivity when defining the 'essence of competition' and 'pure suffering'.

This existential war called the Tour de France is, in fact, a massive act of complicity. Sponsors, media, and spectators collectively maintain a masculine-centric narrative as the primary cognitive entry point. While the world admires Pogačar's 'suffer face', it is actually consuming a carefully packaged heroism. In this system, pain is assigned value, and the pricing power of that value is held exclusively by men.

Vive le Tour? In reality, it is a giant masculine social club built of carbon fiber and testosterone, announcing to the world: this is where 'limits' and 'glory' are defined, and everyone else exists merely to witness the performance.
