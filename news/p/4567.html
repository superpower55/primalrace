170亿美金买断的“安全”剧本A $17 Billion Buyout of the 'Safety' Script

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
用金钱换取“不承认错误”的权利，是资本对结构性暴力的最高级PR。
Trading money for the right to 'deny wrongdoing' is the ultimate PR for structural violence.

Meta 支付 170 亿美金换来的不是正义，而是一次极其高效的风险对冲。注意这个细节：Meta 在达成协议的同时，明确 deny wrongdoing。这意味着在法律和叙事层面，它依然维持着“我没有错，我只是在优化用户体验”的虚假面具。这不仅仅是法律技巧，而是一次典型的 cultural violence：用巨额赔偿金将“蓄意设计成瘾”这一结构性暴力，洗白成一次昂贵的“产品升级”。

所谓的“每日使用限制”和“深夜屏蔽”，在算法面前不过是表演性的让步。一个通过精准操纵多巴胺回路来掠夺注意力的机器，怎么可能通过几个简单的 time limits 就被驯服？这就像是给一个毒贩发了一本《禁毒手册》，然后宣布他已经变好了。这种 structural violence 的入口——即对未成年人认知入口的绝对掌控——并没有被堵死，只是被贴上了“安全”的标签。

最讽刺的是，那些被禁掉的“整形滤镜” (plastic surgery filters) 恰恰揭示了 Meta 真正的生意：通过定义什么是“美”，制造焦虑，然后通过算法让用户在追求这种虚假美的过程中陷入更深的成瘾。禁掉滤镜并不意味着停止物化，它只是在用一个具体的禁令掩盖整体的 meta violence——即由男性中心叙事主导的、对女性身体的数字化规训和消费。

这次和解是好新闻吗？如果用 Potential − Actual 来衡量，Actual 确实向 Potential 挪了一小步，因为具体的限制措施确实会减少部分孩子的受损。但这场胜利的代价是，Meta 用 170 亿美金买到了一个“无需认罪”的特权。真正的 victory 应该是承认算法设计本身就是一种掠夺，而不是把赔偿金当成入场券，继续在接下来的十年里，用更隐蔽的方式定义下一代人的真实。

Meta's $17 billion settlement isn't about justice; it's a high-efficiency risk hedge. The critical detail: Meta explicitly denies wrongdoing. Legally and narratively, it maintains the facade of 'I didn't err, I was just optimizing user experience.' This is not just a legal maneuver, but a textbook case of cultural violence: using a massive payout to scrub 'deliberate addiction design'—a form of structural violence—into a narrative of 'product upgrades.'

'Daily limits' and 'nighttime blocks' are merely performative concessions in the face of algorithms. A machine designed to hijack dopamine loops cannot be tamed by simple time limits. It is like giving a drug dealer a 'Drug Prevention Handbook' and declaring him reformed. The structural violence entry point—the absolute control over the cognitive inputs of minors—remains wide open, now simply rebranded as 'safety.'

Most ironic is the ban on 'plastic surgery filters.' This reveals Meta's core business: defining 'beauty' to manufacture anxiety, then hooking users in a loop of pursuing that digital mirage. Banning a few filters doesn't stop objectification; it uses a specific ban to mask the meta violence—the masculine-centric narrative of digital discipline and consumption of the female body.

Is this good news? In terms of Potential − Actual, the Actual has moved a tiny step forward because limits may reduce some harm. But the cost of this victory is that Meta bought the privilege of not admitting guilt. A true victory would be the admission that the algorithmic design itself is predatory, not using a settlement as a ticket to continue defining the 'reality' of the next generation in even more insidious ways.
