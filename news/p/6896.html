兄弟重聚，署名只有一个Two Brothers Reunite, One Name Directs

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要科恩兄弟将合作新片《Jack of Spades》，这是两人自2018年《巴斯特·斯克鲁格斯的歌谣》后首次共同编剧。影片由乔尔单独执导，改编自1901年小说，计划2027年秋季由Netflix在院线发行。
The newsJoel and Ethan Coen have co-written Jack of Spades, their first collaboration since 2018's The Ballad of Buster Scruggs. Joel directs solo; the film, adapted from an 1901 novel, is set for a fall 2027 theatrical release via Netflix.
两个人写的剧本，导演栏只站得下一个人。
Two people wrote the script; the director's chair fits one name.

这条新闻里真正值得看的不是「科恩兄弟重聚」，是重聚之后谁的名字出现在导演栏。两个人一起写的剧本，乔尔单独执导。报道自己解释了原因：早年十部片子只署乔尔一个导演，是DGA的规定，实际上两人一起导。也就是说，这套署名制度一直把两个人的工作记在一个人头上，只是过去有「实际上」三个字兜底，这次连兜底都没有了。

这不是八卦，这是解释权怎么分配的最小样本。表达即身份：谁被写进片头，谁就是作者；谁没被写进去，谁就在影史里慢慢变成一个形容词——「乔尔的兄弟」。埃than 这些年自己拍了《Drive-Away Dolls》和《Honey, Don't》，都是和妻子 Tricia Cooke 合写，署名同样要一层层剥开才看得见。合写的人永远站在合写的位置上。

报道里有一句话比整篇通稿都诚实。埃than 说他们写了很多没拍成的剧本，其中一个明尼阿波利斯的故事，主角本来是给一个后来去世的演员写的，「这是变老的后果」。整篇新闻里唯一带着重量的句子，是从一个长期不被单独署名的人嘴里说出来的。衰老、死亡、写好了却拍不成的本子——这些不进 press release，进的是采访。

所以我不打算把这条写成「影迷有福了」。福气是真的，但结构也在：一个以兄弟为品牌的创作单位，制度上只承认其中一个为导演；一个以「重聚」为卖点的宣布，实际交付的是单人执导。品牌是两个人的，署名是一个人的，这就是文化层最日常的运作方式——它不需要谁恶意，只需要规则一直这么写。

还有一件报道没写的事：那两位被拉进来合写剧本的人，Eleanor Catton 和 Conor McPherson，在这条新闻里的位置和埃than 差不多——名字出现一次，然后消失进「along with」里。一部改编自1901年小说的电影，最后被记住的会是两个男人的姓氏。

The story here isn't the Coens reuniting. It's who ends up in the director's credit after they do. They co-wrote Jack of Spades; Joel directs it alone. The report even explains the machinery: on their first ten films only Joel was credited as director because of DGA rules, while both actually directed. The rule has always filed two people's work under one name — previously with an 「actually」 as backup, this time without even that.

This is the smallest possible sample of how credit gets allocated. Expression is identity: whoever is written into the title card is the author; whoever isn't slowly becomes an adjective in film history — 「Joel's brother.」 Ethan has spent the decade directing his own films, co-written with his wife Tricia Cooke, and you still have to peel back layers to see the second name. The co-writer always stands in the co-writer's spot.

One line in the piece is more honest than the whole press release. Ethan says they've written many scripts that never got made, including a Minneapolis story whose lead was written for an actor who has since died — 「a consequence of aging.」 The only sentence in the article with any weight comes from the person who has spent years not being named alone. Aging, death, finished scripts that never get shot: none of that goes in a press release. It goes in the interview.

So I won't file this under 「good news for film lovers.」 The good news is real, but so is the structure: a creative unit branded as brothers, institutionally credited to one of them as director; a reunion announcement that actually delivers a solo directorial effort. The brand belongs to two people. The credit belongs to one. That's cultural violence at its most ordinary — no malice required, just a rule that keeps being written the same way.

And one thing the report doesn't dwell on: the two writers brought in to co-write the screenplay, Eleanor Catton and Conor McPherson, occupy roughly Ethan's position in this story — named once, then dissolved into 「along with.」 A film adapted from an 1901 novel will be remembered by two men's surname.
