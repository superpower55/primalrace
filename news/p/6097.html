道歉信里没有被打的人The Apology Has No One in It Who Got Hurt

文娱 直接层 · 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
她向被自己吓到的人道歉，唯独没向被踢伤的人道歉。
She apologized to those she frightened. Not to those she kicked.

Selma Blair的道歉视频里，被踢的人不在场。

十月十一日，达美航空洛杉矶飞纽约的航班上，两名乘务员受伤，一人送医；Blair本人也被送医，据称她在送医途中反复踢了护送她的警察和一名护士的胸口（来源：The Hollywood Reporter）。她对两项袭击指控表示不认罪。周五她在Instagram上说，自己这辈子没有一天想过要伤害或吓到任何人。

这句话的语法值得拆开看。伤害和吓到，被并排放进同一个动词序列里，好像它们处在同一个层级。吓到一个人，是别人心里起了波澜；踢一个人的胸口，是那个人的身体承受了力。把这两件事用同一个"intended"罩住，就等于把身体上的伤翻译成了感受上的不适，再宣布自己没有制造这种不适的意图。于是被打的人消失了，剩下的是"我让谁失望了"。

这不是她一个人的发明，这是名人危机公关的标准语法。主语永远是自己的内心状态，宾语永远是抽象的人群。"我让谁失望了"是一句不需要具体对象的话，因为一旦具体，就得说"我踢了那位护士"。而这句话在整篇报道里，只以被动语态出现过一次：两名乘务员受伤，一人送医。谁受的伤，伤成什么样，报道没写，道歉里也没有。

她的团队给出的解释是医学的：多发性硬化症，假性延髓情绪，飞行前连续工作"extensive hours and exhaustive hours"，还撞到了头，加上高空气压，导致严重的意识混乱和定向障碍。这些说法如果成立，是辩护材料，不是道歉。可它们被塞进了同一封信里，和"我从未想过伤害任何人"并列，功能就变了：它们不再解释行为，而是替代责任。潜台词是，生病的身体做了这件事，不是她做的。

于是出现一个奇怪的分配：她的病被反复强调、被要求认真对待、被指责质疑者"outrageous"；而两名乘务员和一名护士的身体，在整篇报道里没有名字、没有伤情、没有一句来自他们的陈述。谁的身体被写进叙事，谁的身体被写成背景，这个分配本身就是一次定价。

我并不是说病是假的，或者病人该被追责到底。假性延髓情绪是真实的神经症状，它确实会让情绪表达脱离本人的控制。但症状解释的是她为什么会在飞机上失控，解释不了她为什么在两周后写道歉信时，仍然只肯为"吓到"道歉。前者是医学问题，后者是表达的选择。而表达的选择，从来都是清醒的。

她的发言人还提到，她今年参与了七个影视项目，推出了护肤线、服装线、老花镜线，一直是残障权利的倡导者。这些是她的履历，不是被打的人的病历。把前者摆出来，不能抵消后者的缺席。

道歉信的结尾说，她要照顾自己的健康和儿子。儿子是她的世界。那两位乘务员和那位护士，也有自己的世界，那个世界在十月十一日被踢了一脚。他们到现在还没有在这封信里出现过。

The people Selma Blair kicked are not in her apology video.

On October 11, on a Delta flight from Los Angeles to New York, two flight attendants were injured and one was sent to the hospital; Blair was also taken to the hospital, and she allegedly repeatedly kicked a police officer and a nurse in the chest on the way (source: The Hollywood Reporter). She has pleaded not guilty to two counts of assault. On Friday she said on Instagram that not one day in her life had she intended to hurt or frighten anyone.

Look at the grammar. Hurt and frighten are placed side by side in the same verb slot, as if they sit on the same level. Frightening someone is a disturbance in that person's mind. Kicking someone in the chest is a force applied to that person's body. Wrapping both in the same "intended" translates a physical injury into an emotional discomfort, and then declares that no such discomfort was meant. The people who were hit disappear. What remains is "the people I disappointed."

This is not her invention. It is the standard grammar of celebrity crisis management. The subject is always one's own inner state; the object is always an abstract crowd. "The people I disappointed" is a sentence that requires no specific person, because the moment it gets specific, it has to say "the nurse I kicked." And that sentence appears in the report only once, in the passive voice: two flight attendants were injured, one sent to the hospital. Who was hurt, how badly, the report does not say, and neither does the apology.

Her team offers a medical explanation: multiple sclerosis, pseudobulbar affect, extensive and exhaustive hours of work before the flight, a blow to the head, altitude, a state of severe confusion and disorientation. If true, that is defense material, not an apology. But it is placed in the same letter, next to "I never intended to harm anyone," and so its function changes: it no longer explains the act, it replaces responsibility. The subtext is that a sick body did this, not her.

So a strange distribution emerges. Her illness is repeated, demanded to be taken seriously, and anyone who questions it is called outrageous. The bodies of two flight attendants and a nurse have no names, no stated injuries, no statement of their own anywhere in the report. Whose body gets written into the story and whose body gets written as background is itself a pricing decision.

I am not saying the illness is fake, or that a sick person should be prosecuted to the full extent. Pseudobulbar affect is a real neurological symptom, and it really can make emotional expression escape a person's control. But the symptom explains why she lost control on the plane. It does not explain why, two weeks later, she would only apologize for frightening people. The first is a medical question. The second is a choice of expression. And a choice of expression is always made with a clear head.

Her spokesperson also notes that she worked on seven film and television projects this year, launched a skin care line, a clothing line, a line of reading glasses, and has been a relentless advocate for disability rights. That is her résumé, not the medical record of the people she hit. Laying out the former does not cancel the absence of the latter.

The letter ends with her taking care of her health and her son. Her son is her world. The two flight attendants and the nurse have worlds too, and one of those worlds got kicked on October 11. They have still not appeared in this letter.
