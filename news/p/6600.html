一条毯子的定价权The Price Tag on a Blanket

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
卖的不是毯子，是“好莱坞同款”这五个字的席位。
What's for sale isn't the blanket. It's a seat in the "Hollywood-approved" club.

这条新闻里唯一值得看一眼的东西是它的定价逻辑：Lola Original 从 425 美元砍到 212.50 美元，五折，还是“年度最大促销之一”。一块毯子能标到 425，靠的不是保暖，是“好莱坞最抢手”这个标签本身。

《好莱坞报道》写它“真的就是那么好”，同一段里承认从链接购买能拿佣金。这不是隐瞒，是摆出来的——媒体把编辑判断和分成放在同一句话里，读者自己决定信几分。问题不在它有没有披露，在于披露之后这套结构照常运转：明星用、Oprah 榜单收、媒体写、读者点链接买。每一环都在给“贵”找理由，没有一环在问这块布为什么值这个价。

限量联名是这套机器里最精巧的零件。Aviator Nation、纽约洋基、LoveShackFancy——每加一个联名，就把一条毯子从日用品挪进收藏品，价格跟着挪。品味在这里不区分好坏，只区分谁进得来。买的是毯子，付的是入场费。

五折是另一个动作：先把价格定在没人会付的位置，再用“省了一半”让人觉得自己赢了。省下的 212.50 美元从来没有存在过，它只存在于标价牌上。

报道里没有一个人为这条毯子付出代价——没有工厂工人，没有供应链，没有谁的身体。所以这件事的暴力不在生产端，在注意力端：它占掉的是读者本可以用来看见别的东西的那几分钟。

The only thing worth looking at in this story is the pricing logic: the Lola Original drops from $425 to $212.50, half off, and it's still billed as one of the year's biggest sales. A blanket commands $425 not because it's warm, but because "Hollywood's most coveted" is the product.

The Hollywood Reporter says it really is that good, and in the same breath notes it may earn a commission from your click. That's not concealment—it's disclosure. The editorial judgment and the cut sit in the same sentence, and you decide how much to trust it. The problem isn't whether they told you. It's that the machine runs exactly the same after they tell you: celebrities use it, Oprah's list anoints it, the magazine writes it up, you click and buy. Every link supplies a reason for the price. None asks why the cloth is worth it.

Limited-edition collabs are the machine's finest part. Aviator Nation, the Yankees, LoveShackFancy—each one moves a blanket from household item to collectible, and the price moves with it. Taste here doesn't sort good from bad. It sorts who gets in. You're buying a blanket and paying an entrance fee.

Then the half-off: set the price where nobody will pay it, then let "saving half" make the buyer feel like the winner. The $212.50 you saved never existed. It lived on the tag.

No one in this story pays a price for the blanket—no factory worker, no supply chain, no body. So the violence isn't at production. It's at attention: the minutes you spend here are minutes you don't spend seeing anything else.
