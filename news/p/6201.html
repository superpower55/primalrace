投票站被砍掉三分之一They Cut a Third of the Polling Sites

其他 结构层 · 文化层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要得州塔兰特县共和党主导的县委员会在9月取消了92个选举日投票站，约占2024年总数的三分之一。曾因投票被判刑、后于今年9月最终脱罪的克里斯特尔·梅森，如今在当地运营投票权组织，逐户动员选民。塔兰特县是得州最后一个由共和党掌控的大城市县。

他们关掉的是投票站，改的是谁能被算作选民
They didn't close polling places. They redrew who counts as a voter.

Crystal Mason 打赢了官司，然后回去接着敲人家的门。

她在 2016 年投了一张临时选票，那张票从来没有被计入。德州共和党官员当时在找投票欺诈的案例，于是她被判了五年。她一直说没人告诉过她不能投。2024 年法院推翻了这个判决，Tarrant County 的共和党地区检察官又上诉。今年九月，她终于彻底赢了（来源：Mother Jones）。

赢完之后她做了什么？十月中旬，94 华氏度，她带着两个女儿、一个儿子、一个孙辈去敲门，在手机上核对选民名单。她平时开箱式货车上班，其余时间运营一个叫 Crystal Mason: The Fight 的投票权组织。

这就是结构暴力最诚实的样子：它不需要判你坐牢，它只需要让你在投票站门口多走几英里。

九月，共和党主导的县委员会砍掉了 92 个选举日投票站，接近 2024 年总数的三分之一。倡导者说，受影响最大的是黑人和西语裔社区。Mason 说这“不真实”。

92 个站点，不是 92 张票。它不写在任何一张选票上，它写在从家到投票站的那段路上。加尔通的公式在这里算得很清楚：Potential 是你本可以走到最近的那个投票站投一票，Actual 是你得请半天假、搭别人的车、或者干脆不去。差额就是暴力，只是它没有施暴者，只有一份会议决议。

同一批人还在做别的事。县法官 Tim O'Hare 在州议会重划国会选区之前，就先动手重划了县委员选区，目标是拿到 4 比 1 的多数。委员会会议砍掉一半，所有发言限时一分钟，鼓掌的人被法警带出去。一个在听证里说了“bullshit”的活动人士，一周后被控扰乱秩序。

这些加在一起是什么？是把“谁有资格说话、说多久、在哪里投票”这一整套规则，攥在一只手里。这不叫治理，这叫把入口焊死，然后宣布门是开的。

Tarrant County 是德州最后一个由共和党掌管的大城市县。今年 CPAC 在它的郊区 Grapevine 开，想驱逐所有穆斯林的铁路委员会候选人 Bo French 是前县共和党主席，True Texas Project 办十五周年活动，主题是“针对美国白人的战争”。

但同一片地方，今年一场特别选举里，工会领袖 Taylor Rehmet 翻掉了一个特朗普赢了 17 个百分点的州参议院选区，数据显示他靠的是说服，不是 turnout。Fort Worth 的共和党市长 Mattie Parker 背书了两位民主党州级候选人。做了 16 年县法官的 Glen Whitley 拍广告支持民主党人。

所以这不是“红县变蓝”的童话。这是有人在共谋，也有人在倒戈。倒戈的代价是失去自己党内的位置，回报是一个还能讲道理的县。

Mason 说了一句最准确的话：他们想让她站在那里，当众宣布自己再也不投票，用来吓退黑人和棕色人种。“我意识到那正是他们想要的。”

这就是武器化的核心：不是让你投不了票，是让你自己决定不去投。当恐惧被内化成常识，鞭子就可以收起来了。

现在她出现在 James Talarico 的竞选广告里，坐在厨房桌边说：Crystal 没有退，你也不该退。

一场官司赢了，92 个投票站还没有回来。差额缩小了一点，剩下的那部分，得靠有人在 94 度的天里继续敲门。

Crystal Mason won her case, then went back to knocking on doors.

In 2016 she cast a provisional ballot that was never counted. Texas Republican officials were hunting for voter fraud, so she got five years. She said no one ever told her she couldn't vote. A court threw out the conviction in 2024; Tarrant County's Republican district attorney appealed. This September she beat it for good (source: Mother Jones).

Then what did she do? Mid-October, 94 degrees, out with two daughters, a son, and a grandkid, checking a voter list on her phone. She drives a box truck for a living and runs a voting rights group called Crystal Mason: The Fight the rest of the time.

This is what structural violence looks like when it's being honest: it doesn't have to jail you. It just has to put a few more miles between you and the ballot box.

In September the Republican-led county commission eliminated 92 Election Day voting sites—nearly a third of the 2024 total. Advocates said the cuts would fall hardest on Black and Hispanic areas. Mason calls it "unreal."

Ninety-two sites, not ninety-two votes. It doesn't appear on any ballot. It appears on the walk from your door to the polling place. Run Galtung's equation: the potential is the short trip to a nearby site, the actual is a half day off work, a ride you have to beg for, or staying home. The gap is violence. It just has no perpetrator—only a commission vote.

The same people are doing other things. County Judge Tim O'Hare tried to gerrymander the commissioner districts before the Legislature even redrew congressional maps, aiming for a 4–1 majority. Commission meetings cut in half. Every speaker limited to one minute. People hauled out by sheriff's deputies for clapping. An activist who said "bullshit" in testimony was charged with disorderly conduct a week later.

Add it up: who gets to speak, for how long, and where you vote—all gripped in one hand. That isn't governance. That's welding the entrance shut and announcing the door is open.

Tarrant is the last big urban county in Texas still run by Republicans. CPAC met in its suburb of Grapevine this year. Bo French, the Railroad Commission nominee who wants to remove all Muslims from the US, is a former county GOP chair. The True Texas Project held its 15th anniversary with a conversation on "the war on white America."

And yet: in a special election this year, union leader Taylor Rehmet flipped a state Senate district Trump won by 17 points, and the data says persuasion did it, not turnout. Fort Worth's Republican mayor, Mattie Parker, endorsed Democrats for two statewide offices. Glen Whitley, county judge for 16 years, cut an ad for a Democratic state rep.

So this is not a red-county-to-blue fairy tale. Some people are conspiring; some are defecting. Defection costs you your place in the party. The payoff is a county that still argues in good faith.

Mason said the truest thing in the piece: they wanted her put on display, announcing she'd never vote again, to scare Black and brown voters off. "I realized that's exactly what they wanted me to do."

That's the heart of weaponization. Not stopping you from voting—getting you to decide not to. Once fear becomes common sense, the whip can go in the drawer.

Now she's in a James Talarico ad, at a kitchen table: Crystal didn't back down, and you shouldn't either.

One case won. Ninety-two polling sites still gone. The gap closed a little. The rest gets closed by someone knocking doors in 94-degree heat.
