把选民做成流量池Turning Voters Into a Traffic Pool

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要巴西总统选举第二轮投票中，左翼的卢拉·达席尔瓦击败极右翼候选人弗拉维奥·博索纳罗。报道将胜利部分归功于「卢拉军团」——一个千万成员的 WhatsApp 群组，由议员安德烈·雅诺内斯指挥，给支持者派发转发模板、刷话题、动员邻里投票。文章由此劝美国民主党照搬这套线上组织。
The newsBrazil's leftist president Lula da Silva won the second round of the presidential election against far-right candidate Flávio Bolsonaro. The report credits part of the win to "Lula's Army," a 10-million-member WhatsApp group run by lawmaker André Janones that handed supporters sharing templates, hashtag boosts and neighborhood voter recruitment. It urges US Democrats to copy the playbook.
他们学会的不是组织，是操作。
They didn't learn organizing. They learned operations.

这篇报道从头到尾没写一件事：卢拉赢了之后，那千万人里谁的日子会变。它写的是一套分发系统怎么运转，然后把运转本身当成了胜利。

「卢拉军团」的机制很清楚：议员雅诺内斯在群里发模板、派任务，「紧急！」「先别睡」「任务完成前不许吃饭」。这不是组织，这是把支持者变成内容分发的执行终端。报道说这套做法既是公民教育也是社媒素养，还教人别去和右翼内容互动以免给对方算法加权——听上去像是清醒，其实是把政治判断外包给了一个群管理员。

第三章讲的就是这个：认知入口一旦被夺取，接下来夺取的是解释权，最后是「什么是事实」的制造权。谁在群里发模板，谁就决定了这一千万人今天该看见什么、该说什么、该觉得什么紧急。这不是共和国的公民在议事，这是军队在等口令。报道自己也用了这个词——army、troops、missions、dismissed。

真正值得看的是它承认了另一半：报道说 Meta 被指在算法上偏向博索纳罗，又说特朗普阵营已经有近六百个账号、七千多条质疑选举公正的帖文。也就是说，双方在同一个池子里抢同一套分发管道。谁抢到了，谁就赢。输赢的标准从「谁的政策让更多人活得像人」滑到了「谁的模板转发得更快」。这个滑移本身就是暴力——它让人以为自己在参与，实际上只是被编入了队列。

文章最后说，一起做事能把艰苦的政治变成一场游戏，能带来快乐。我信这句话的前半段。游戏的问题是，游戏里的人是玩家还是道具，要看谁在写规则。这一次写规则的是个议员，下一次可能是平台，再下一次是那个买下平台的人。

报道里唯一没被分配任务的人，是那些既不在群里、也不在算法里、等着看新政府会不会让日子好过一点的人。他们没被写进去，因为他们不产生流量。

The piece never once says who among those ten million will have a better life because Lula won. It describes a distribution system and treats the running of it as the victory itself.

The mechanism is plain: lawmaker André Janones fires templates and assignments into the WhatsApp group—"Urgent!" "Don't go to sleep yet" "No food before the mission!" That is not organizing. That is turning supporters into execution terminals for content distribution. The report calls it civic education and social media literacy, and notes the army was told to stop engaging with right-wing content so as not to boost it algorithmically. That sounds like discipline. It is actually political judgment outsourced to a group admin.

This is Chapter Three exactly: seize the cognitive entry point, then the interpretive authority, then the power to manufacture what counts as fact. Whoever sends the templates decides what a million people find urgent today. The report's own vocabulary gives it away—army, troops, missions, dismissed.

What it also admits is the other half: Meta is accused of tilting its algorithm toward Bolsonaro, and the Trump camp has nearly 600 accounts pushing 7,000-plus posts questioning election integrity. Both sides are fishing the same pipe. Whoever lands the bigger haul wins. The measure of victory slides from whose policies let more people live like people to whose templates travel faster. That slide is the violence—it makes people feel like participants while filing them into a queue.

The ending says doing it together turns hard politics into a game and brings joy. I believe the first half. The question in any game is whether the players are players or pieces, and that depends on who writes the rules. This time it was a congressman. Next time it's the platform. After that, whoever bought the platform.

The only people left unassigned are those outside the group and outside the algorithm, waiting to see whether the new government makes their days any easier. They aren't in the story, because they generate no traffic.
