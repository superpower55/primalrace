给两百个富豪配专属客服A Dedicated Account Manager for 200 Billionaires

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
富豪欠税3.6亿，罚单是五百亿，还配管家。
The rich underpay £3.6bn; the fix is a concierge and £500m.

HMRC 给两百多个亿万富翁各配一名“客户合规经理”。名字起得好，客户。不是稽查对象，是客户；不是查账，是“自愿对话”；不是追缴，是“帮助建立共同理解”。

这条新闻里唯一被量化的暴力差额是 3.6bn 英镑——富人没交够的那部分，占全英税收缺口 6%。它对应的是医院排队、学校预算、地方政府的破产名单。加尔通的公式在这里很好用：本可以收上来的钱和实际收上来的钱之间的差，就是结构暴力，具体到谁少看一次病、谁多等三个月。

HMRC 的方案是给这个差额配一支服务团队。两百多个人，一人一个专属经理，在他们报税之前上门“自愿讨论”。这套安排的结构很清楚：被追缴的一方拿到的是提前沟通、协调、比例相称的对待；被这笔钱挤掉服务的一方，拿到的是预算公告。

再看数字。2024-25 年度富人税收缺口 3.6bn，这次行动承诺贡献“至少 500m”，是缺口的七分之一，而且是往 2030 年增收 10bn 的大目标里凑的一份。剩下 3.1bn 去哪了，报道没写。谁的名字在这两百人名单上，报道也没写。HMRC 只说首次编制了名单，不公布。

这就是文化层在替结构层打掩护：把“追缴”改写成“客户关系管理”，把“欠税”改写成“复杂的税务事务，跨多个税种、实体和司法辖区”。语言一换，掠夺变成了技术难题，技术难题需要的是专家，不是罚单。

去年七月议会公共账目委员会已经质询过 HMRC，说它对约 130 万富裕纳税人的合规情况根本不清楚。一年多之后的回应，是给其中最有话语权的那两百人配管家，再上 AI 和“自动触发”去监控年收入二十万英镑以上的人。前者是对话，后者是监控。这个顺序不是偶然。

报道里引的只有一位律师，RPC 的 Adam Craggs，他提醒高收入者“反思自己是否清楚并能证明自己的英国税务立场”。注意这句话的方向：需要反思的是纳税人自己准没准备好，不是系统有没有能力追。

所以这五百亿不是从富豪口袋里掏出来的暴力差额，是从公共账本上被记成“已处理”的那一格。真正的差额还挂在那里，等着被下一份预算公告再引用一次。

HMRC is giving more than 200 billionaires a dedicated "customer compliance manager." The name is the tell. Not a target of investigation — a customer. Not an audit — a "voluntary discussion." Not collection — "helping build a shared understanding."

The only violence gap quantified in this story is £3.6bn: what the wealthy failed to pay, 6% of the UK's entire tax gap. That gap has a downstream address — waiting lists, school budgets, councils going under. Galtung's formula fits cleanly: the difference between what could be collected and what is collected is structural violence, itemized as who waits another three months for a scan.

HMRC's answer to that gap is a service team. Two hundred-odd managers, one per billionaire, visiting before the return is filed. The structure is clear: the side being chased gets advance engagement, coordination, proportionality; the side losing services to the shortfall gets a budget announcement.

Now the numbers. The 2024-25 wealthy tax gap is £3.6bn. This crackdown contributes "at least £500m" — a seventh of the gap, and one installment toward £10bn by 2030. Where the other £3.1bn goes, the report doesn't say. Who is on the list of 200, the report doesn't say. HMRC compiled it for the first time and isn't publishing it.

This is the cultural layer covering for the structural one: renaming collection as customer relationship management, renaming unpaid tax as "complex affairs spanning multiple taxes, entities and jurisdictions." Swap the words and extraction becomes a technical problem, and technical problems need experts, not penalties.

MPs on the public accounts committee grilled HMRC last July over not understanding compliance among about 1.3 million wealthy taxpayers. The response a year later is a concierge for the two hundred with the most leverage, plus AI and "automated triggers" to monitor anyone earning over £200,000. Conversation for the top, surveillance for the rest. That ordering is not an accident.

The only voice quoted is a lawyer, Adam Craggs of RPC, advising high earners to "reflect on how clearly they understand and can evidence their UK tax position." Note the direction: the taxpayer is asked whether he is ready, not the system whether it can collect.

So the £500m isn't a violence gap closed. It's a line item marked handled while the £3.1bn sits there, waiting to be cited in the next budget statement.
