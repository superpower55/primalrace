瘦身药：一场精准定价的阶级入场券Weight-Loss Drugs: A Precisely Priced Class Ticket

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-15 § 链接
当健康被定价为奢侈品，瘦身药成了对底层身体的二次剥削。
When health is priced as a luxury, weight-loss drugs become a secondary exploitation of the lower-class body.

所谓的“瘦身税” (tax on being thin) 其实是个伪命题。真正的真相是：GLP-1 类药物正在将“身体形态”这一表达，从生物学领域彻底迁移到资本定价领域。当年薪不到 10 万英镑的人无法通过减少伙食费来抵消药费时，这意味着“苗条”不再是一种健康状态，而是一种只有高净值人群才能维持的阶级符号。

这不仅是 structural violence，更是一场极其阴险的文化共谋。药企通过制造“快速瘦身”的认知入口，诱导底层女性进入“开始-停止-反弹”的债务循环。60% 的使用者是女性，这绝非偶然。在元暴力的叙事下，女性的价值被高度绑定在“白瘦幼”的审美表达上。药企和医疗系统精准地利用了这种存在性焦虑，将女性对主体性的渴求，转化为对昂贵化学制剂的依赖。

最讽刺的是，行业专家在看到底层用户因贫困而停药反弹后，竟然得出结论说这证明了“生活方式改变”的重要性。这简直是典型的 scam。一个被结构性剥夺了时间、金钱和心理能量的底层女性，被告知应该通过“饮食和运动”来维持体重，而药企则在背后通过垄断定价权，心安理得地收割那些试图通过药物逃离审美霸权的绝望者。

这不再是医疗救助，而是一场关于“谁有权瘦”的博弈。当身体的轻盈需要通过年薪十万英镑来兑换时，肥胖就成了底层阶级身上最后一块无法被洗掉的、带有阶级烙印的生物墙。

The so-called “tax on being thin” is a pseudo-proposition. The truth is that GLP-1 drugs are migrating the expression of “body shape” from the biological realm entirely into the realm of capital pricing. When those earning less than £100,000 cannot offset drug costs through food savings, “slenderness” ceases to be a health state and becomes a class symbol maintainable only by high-net-worth individuals.

This is not just structural violence; it is an insidious cultural complicity. By creating a cognitive entrance of “quick weight loss,” pharmaceutical companies lure lower-class women into a debt cycle of “start-stop-regain.” That 60% of users are women is no accident. Under the meta-violence narrative, female value is heavily tied to the “thin and youthful” aesthetic expression. Big Pharma and healthcare systems precisely weaponize this existential anxiety, converting women's longing for subjectivity into a dependence on expensive chemicals.

Most ironic is the industry expert’s conclusion: seeing lower-class users discontinue treatment due to cost and regain weight is somehow “proof” that “lifestyle changes” are important. This is a complete scam. Telling a lower-class woman—who has been structurally stripped of time, money, and mental energy—that she should rely on “diet and exercise” is a gaslighting tactic, while pharma companies comfortably harvest those desperate enough to seek escape from aesthetic hegemony through medication.

This is no longer medical aid; it is a game of “who has the right to be thin.” When physical lightness must be traded for a six-figure salary, obesity becomes the final, indelible biological wall marking the lower class.
