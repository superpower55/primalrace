把生存危机包装成“睡眠指南”的温情骗局The Gentle Scam of Packaging Survival Crisis as a 'Sleep Guide'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-28 § 链接
当结构性暴力将人们推向高温极限，个体的“睡眠技巧”只是在为绝望做心理按摩。
When structural violence pushes people to thermal limits, individual 'sleep tips' are merely psychological massages for despair.

这是一篇典型的、充满善意的 cultural violence 样本。它用一种极其专业且温柔的语气，教你如何通过换掉床单、在枕套上涂冰块、或者在睡前少喝一杯 Espresso Martini 来应对极端高温。它把一个关于生存环境、能源分配和气候正义的 structural violence 问题，巧妙地通过“心理学”和“生理学”的认知入口，降维成了一个个体的“睡眠质量”问题。

文章最阴险的地方在于它在教你如何“自我规训”。它告诉你：如果你睡不着，不要在床上焦虑，因为焦虑会提高体温，让你更睡不着。这套逻辑在本质上是要求受害者在面对无法控制的客观环境（如缺乏空调的廉租房或日益恶化的热浪）时，通过管理自己的情绪来获得所谓的“最优解”。这不是在解决问题，而是在通过内化压力，让人们在绝望中保持一种“体面的安静”。

注意文中提到的“脆弱群体”——老人、孩子和宠物。这种叙事将他们定义为生理上的“脆弱”，却闭口不谈他们是结构上的“被抛弃者”。在一个由男性中心叙事主导的城市规划和资源分配体系中，谁决定了哪些区域有绿地、哪些社区有足够的电力支撑空调？谁在决定气候危机的成本由谁承担？

当一个人的生活环境已经变成了某种形式的生物学地狱，告诉他“尝试不要与你无法控制的事情对抗”就是最大的 scam。这种建议不是在提供救济，而是在通过一种伪装成科学的理性，消解人们对结构性不公的愤怒，将潜在的政治反抗转化为一次次失败的、个人的睡眠尝试。

This is a textbook sample of cultural violence wrapped in benevolence. With a professional and gentle tone, it teaches you to cope with extreme heat by swapping bedsheets, freezing pillowcases, or skipping that pre-sleep Espresso Martini. It cleverly reduces a problem of structural violence—concerning living environments, energy distribution, and climate justice—into a mere issue of individual 'sleep quality' through the cognitive entries of psychology and physiology.

The most insidious part is the instruction on self-regulation. It tells you: if you can't sleep, don't lie there anxious, because anxiety raises body temperature, making it harder to sleep. In essence, this logic demands that victims, facing uncontrollable objective environments (like AC-less slums or worsening heatwaves), achieve a 'pseudo-optimal expression' by managing their emotions. This isn't solving the problem; it's using internalization to ensure people remain 'decently quiet' in their despair.

Note the mention of 'vulnerable groups'—the elderly, children, and pets. The narrative defines them by biological fragility while ignoring their status as the structurally abandoned. In a city planning and resource allocation system driven by masculine-centric narratives, who decides which areas get green spaces and which communities have the power grid to support air conditioning? Who decides who bears the cost of the climate crisis?

When one's living environment has become a biological hell, telling them to 'try not to fight with things you can't control' is the ultimate scam. Such advice isn't providing relief; it is using a facade of scientific rationality to dissolve anger toward structural injustice, transforming potential political resistance into a series of failed, personal attempts to get some sleep.
