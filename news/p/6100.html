钱没有立场，只有入口Money Has No Party, Only Entry Points

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Intercept ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
PAC 不买候选人，买的是认知入口的租约。
A PAC doesn't buy candidates. It leases cognitive entry points.

Fellowship PAC 一年前成立时被报道会专注支持共和党，从六月起，它花钱的对象全是民主党。这不是转向，是换了一条更便宜的入场路线。

看钱怎么走：六十万美元经 Nxum Group 流向纽约州众议员 Ritchie Torres、新罕布什尔州众议员 Maggie Goodlander、弗吉尼亚州参议员 Mark Warner。Nxum Group 由 Bo Hines 参与创办，此人在 2022 和 2024 年的国会竞选中把自己包装成热爱特朗普的 MAGA 信徒，后来被特朗普任命为白宫数字资产顾问委员会负责人，任上推动通过了一项利好 Tether 的稳定币监管法律。据《华尔街日报》上周报道，Hines 在政府任职期间，Tether 正在向 Nxum Group 付钱。Hines 在 2025 年 6 月的道德披露里说，他对这家公司只有“被动投资权益”。2025 年 8 月他离开政府，一个月后 Tether 宣布他出任其美国分支的 CEO。

这条链上没有一个环节需要候选人本人知情。Goodlander 的竞选团队说她和那十五万美元的数字广告毫无关系，还说所有竞选在法律上都被禁止与这类外部团体协调，所以“你得去问他们为什么在这个选区花钱”。这句话是真的，也是这套系统最精巧的地方：候选人可以一边痛骂特朗普的 meme coin 是“敲诈”，一边收下经由特朗普前官员的公司转手的广告投放，而两边都不算撒谎。

Warner 最近自己投广告攻击特朗普的 meme coin，说“这不是治理，这是诈取”。同一双手，接住了从 Tether 的银行伙伴 Cantor Fitzgerald 一月份那笔一千万美元捐款里流出来的钱。Fellowship PAC 四月还宣布 Tether 的监管事务副总裁出任其主席。

所以这不是党派问题。PAC 成立时宣称有 1 亿美元战争基金，官方披露里从来没到过这个数，十月十四日手上只剩二十五万。钱快烧完了，就换一个更划算的投放方向——民调显示共和党中期选举形势越来越差之后，它开始只投民主党。意识形态在这里是零，认知入口的租金才是全部。

三个被投的民主党人都在行业记分卡上被评为加密产业的坚定支持者，而且今年秋天都没有竞争性选举。花钱买一个稳赢的人的好感，是最便宜的保险。这不是支持，是预付。

报道没写的是：谁在 Nxum Group 里实际决定这笔钱怎么花。Hines 说不知情，公司说他没参与，PAC 不回应。三个人都不在案发现场，六十万美元自己走完了全程。

Tether 用一家银行、一个 PAC、一个前官员的公司和三个不需要赢的候选人，给自己在华盛顿铺了一条不需要任何人负责的路。这条路的下一个租客，不会只租给民主党。

Fellowship PAC was reported a year ago as a vehicle for supporting Republicans. Since June, every candidate it has spent on has been a Democrat. That's not a pivot. It's a cheaper route to the same entrance.

Follow the money: $600,000 since this summer, routed through Nxum Group to Rep. Ritchie Torres of New York, Rep. Maggie Goodlander of New Hampshire, and Sen. Mark Warner of Virginia. Nxum Group was co-founded by Bo Hines, who presented himself as a Trump-loving MAGA acolyte in his 2022 and 2024 congressional runs, then was tapped by Trump to head the White House Council of Advisers on Digital Assets, where he helped pass stablecoin regulations that benefited Tether. According to a Wall Street Journal report last week, Tether was paying Nxum Group while Hines served in the administration. Hines said in a June 2025 ethics disclosure that he held only a "passive investment interest" in the company. He left the administration in August 2025; a month later, Tether announced he would be CEO of its U.S. branch.

No link in this chain requires a candidate to know anything. Goodlander's campaign says she had nothing to do with the $150,000 in digital ads spent in her race, and that campaigns are legally barred from coordinating with outside groups, so you'd need to ask them why they're spending. That statement is true, and it is also the most elegant part of the machine: a candidate can denounce Trump's meme coin as grifting while accepting ad money routed through a former Trump official's firm, and neither side is lying.

Warner recently ran his own ad attacking Trump over the meme coin: "That's not governing. It's grifting." The same hands received money that flowed from the $10 million January donation to Fellowship PAC by Cantor Fitzgerald, Tether's U.S. banking firm. In April, the PAC announced that Tether's vice president of regulatory affairs would serve as its chair.

So this was never about party. The PAC launched claiming a $100 million war chest; official disclosures never showed that much, and by October 14 it had $250,000 on hand. As public polls showed Republicans' midterm fortunes worsening, it began spending exclusively on Democrats. Ideology is zero here. Rent for cognitive entry points is everything.

All three Democrats are rated strong crypto supporters on an industry scorecard, and none faces a competitive race this fall. Buying goodwill from someone who is going to win anyway is the cheapest insurance available. This isn't support. It's prepayment.

What the report doesn't say: who at Nxum Group actually decides where the money goes. Hines says he wasn't involved. The company says he wasn't involved. The PAC doesn't respond. Three people absent from the scene, and $600,000 walked the whole route by itself.

Tether used a bank, a PAC, a former official's firm, and three candidates who didn't need to win to pave itself a road through Washington where nobody has to be responsible. The next tenant on that road won't be a Democrat.
