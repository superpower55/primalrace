折现率就是杀人许可证The Discount Rate Is a License to Kill

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
给未来打折，就是给死亡定价。
Discounting the future is pricing death.

主流经济学里那个叫 discount rate 的东西，不是技术参数，是一份定价表：它规定未来的命值今天的几折。斯特恩 2006 年的报告真正干的事，就是把这份定价表翻过来——未来的孩子和我们有同等的出生权，所以不能打折。二十年后回头看，这句话仍然是对的，也仍然没有被接受。

诺德豪斯在 1990 年代初断言各国应当按升温 3 到 4 摄氏度做预算，理由是低于这个数，减排成本会超过气候损失。2018 年他又发论文，说 3 度升温只削掉全球 GDP 的 2.1%，6 度也只削 8.5%。同一年他拿了诺贝尔奖，气候工作是授奖词和获奖演讲里的重点。这篇报道里有一句写得最准：6 度升温下，数十亿人在经济意义上会变得"次优地有生产力"——或者用普通人话说，死了。

这就是元暴力在经济学里的样子。它不靠喊口号，靠的是一套看起来中立的工具：折现率、GDP、成本收益分析。工具一旦被设定，结论就自己长出来了。冰川在账本上是零，树只有被砍倒才计入 GDP，一个能住人的气候连一行表格都不配占。不是经济学家心肠坏，是这把尺子从一开始就只量得到能被交易的东西。而"客观""理性""标准方法"这些词，就是让这把尺子免于被质疑的那层壳。

斯特恩赢了吗？报道说，2026 年看起来他像是赢了辩论。但传统模型没有退场：全世界数百所大学还在用诺德豪斯那套增长模型教学生，各国政府、企业、国际机构还在用"气候只削几个百分点 GDP"的乐观预测做决策，化石燃料公司被市场高估，敢为低碳做准备的企业被投资者惩罚。保险公司——本该是资本市场里最讲诚信的执法者——据称在明知故犯地修饰数字，把气候风险抹平。这不是一场已经结束的辩论，是一场赢了口径、输了执行的辩论。

把这条新闻放进暴力三角里看，结构层和文化层是咬合的。文化层生产出"不行动更划算"的叙事，结构层就据此拒绝行动、给化石燃料估值、惩罚先行者。差额落在哪里？落在那些还没出生的人身上，落在沿海城市、落在会变成沙漠的农地上。他们不在这场博弈的谈判桌上，所以他们的命可以被折现成一个很小的数。

斯特恩本人现在说，如果重做这份报告，他会更强调自然的作用，因为生物多样性崩溃是被严重低估的经济和国家安全威胁。这话值得注意：连他都认为当年那套算法还不够狠。

这篇报道没有写的一件事是：谁在为这套折现率付账，谁在靠它收租。保险公司的股东、化石燃料的持有者、靠旧模型发论文拿奖的经济学家，他们不是不知道，是这套算法对他们有利。二十年前被骂的是斯特恩，二十年后被供在神坛上的是诺德豪斯，而账本上那行"未来"，至今仍然是空的。

The thing mainstream economics calls the discount rate is not a technical parameter. It is a price list: it specifies what a future life is worth in today's currency. What Nicholas Stern's 2006 review actually did was flip that price list over — future generations have as much right to be born as we did, so they cannot be discounted. Twenty years on, that sentence is still correct, and still not accepted.

William Nordhaus asserted in the early 1990s that countries should budget for 3C to 4C of heating, because below that the cost of cutting emissions would outweigh the damage. In 2018 he published a paper claiming 3C would shave just 2.1% off global GDP, and 6C only 8.5%. That same year he won the Nobel prize, with his climate work prominent in the citation and his lecture. The report puts it best: at 6C, billions of people would become, in economic terms, suboptimally productive — or as the rest of us might say, dead.

This is meta-violence wearing the clothes of economics. It doesn't shout; it uses tools that look neutral — discount rates, GDP, cost-benefit analysis. Set the tool, and the conclusion grows itself. Ice caps are worth zero on the ledger, trees only count once cut down, a livable climate doesn't merit a line on a spreadsheet. The economists aren't cruel; the ruler only measures what can be traded. And "objective," "rational," "standard method" are the shell that keeps the ruler beyond questioning.

Did Stern win? The report says that in 2026 it might look that way. But the old models never left: hundreds of universities still teach Nordhaus's growth models, governments and firms and international institutions still plan on sunny predictions that climate change shaves a few percent off GDP, fossil fuel companies are over-valued by markets, and investors punish businesses that dare prepare for a low-carbon world. Insurers — supposedly the enforcers of probity in capital markets — are alleged to be fudging their figures to massage climate risk away. This is not a settled debate. It is a debate won on rhetoric and lost on execution.

In Violence Triangle terms, the structural and cultural layers are interlocked. The cultural layer manufactures the narrative that inaction is cheaper; the structural layer then refuses to act, values fossil fuels, punishes the early movers. Where does the gap land? On people not yet born, on coastal cities, on farmland that will become desert. They are not at the negotiating table, so their lives can be discounted into a small number.

Stern himself now says that if he were redoing the review he would emphasise nature more, because biodiversity collapse is a grossly underestimated economic and national security threat. Note that even he thinks the original accounting was not harsh enough.

What the report does not say is who pays for this discount rate and who collects rent from it. The shareholders of insurers, the holders of fossil fuel assets, the economists who win prizes for the old models — they are not ignorant. The accounting works for them. Twenty years ago Stern was the one attacked; now Nordhaus is the one enthroned. And the line marked "future" on the ledger is still blank.
