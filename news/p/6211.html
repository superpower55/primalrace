五百个公民被国家抓走Five Hundred Citizens, Zero on the Books

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 ProPublica ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要非营利调查新闻机构ProPublica统计了特朗普政府自就职日至2026年10月21日期间美国公民被移民执法人员拘留的案例，共找到逾500起，并称因政府不追踪此类案件，实际数量很可能更多。该机构表示，每起案例都至少有两个信息来源，并已就每起事件询问国土安全部；国土安全部多次表示并未逮捕美国公民。
The newsProPublica cataloged more than 500 cases of U.S. citizens reportedly detained by immigration agents from Inauguration Day through Oct. 21, 2026. It said each case had at least two sources and that the count is likely an undercount since the government does not track such cases. DHS has repeatedly said it is not arresting U.S. citizens.
政府说没有，五百个人身上有手铐。
The government says it doesn't detain citizens. Five hundred people have the handcuffs to prove otherwise.

ProPublica 数出了 519 个被移民执法人员拘留的美国公民，从就职日到 2026 年 10 月 21 日。政府说它不抓公民。所以这五百多起事件，在官方记录里不存在——不是被否认，是没有被计数。加尔通的公式在这里第一次显得太客气：暴力不只是把实际压到潜力之下，还包括让被压下去的那部分从账本上消失。

看细节。一个骑自行车的公民在长滩被 ICE 车辆撞倒、电击、受伤、铐走，视频显示那辆车逆行单行道后转向他；DHS 否认撞人，指控他砸后视镜，而现有视频里没有这个画面。在埃文斯顿，执法人员去找一个长相相似的人，把一个公民弄伤送进医院，到场警察说一名执法人员手上有血。在安纳波利斯，一个刚出咖啡店的公民被铐了近两小时，不给打电话，DHS 的理由是外面聚集了“煽动者”。在拉法耶特，四个孩子的母亲在自家公寓外的检查点被拘留、转入 ICE 羁押、登记进拘留设施，直到律师把她弄出来，ICE 和当地执法部门至今还在争她是不是公民。

这些不是“误抓”，误抓意味着系统本来会纠正自己。布朗克斯 19 岁的 Jeury Concepcion 被三个蒙面人按倒，认错了人，放他走时头在流血，一名执法人员向他道了歉——道歉是个体的，制度没有道歉，制度说这是“为了安全完成问询而临时拘留”。丹佛那个 25 岁公民在得州被拦车，拿不出身份证件，被关押数日、没有电话没有律师，最后“同意”离开这个国家，八月拿着科罗拉多出生证明走回来。一个公民要证明自己不是可以被驱逐的对象，靠的是同意被驱逐，再靠一张出生证明走回来。这就是“自愿”在权力不对等下的含义。

真正在动的是结构层和元层一起动。结构层：政府不统计这类案件，所以“没有系统性抓公民”这个结论不需要证据，只需要不建那张表。元层：谁有资格定义谁是公民、谁有资格被相信——被拦下的人要先自证，执法者的叙述先成立，DHS 的声明先发布，视频后到、律师后到、ProPublica 的表格最后到。ProPublica 做的事本质上是一次解释权的回收：政府拒绝计数，新闻室替它计数，而且只收有两个来源的事件，还逐条问过 DHS，还查过有没有联邦刑事指控。这不是情绪，这是把“什么是事实”的制造权从声明里拿回来一点。

共谋在这里不需要阴谋。一个不建表的机构、一套“合法命令”的话术、一份“我们没抓公民”的声明，加上地方执法把检查点设在公寓楼下、把羁押转交给 ICE——每一环的人都只是在执行自己的那一步，回报是没有人需要为总数负责。总数不存在，责任就不存在。

519 是下限，报道自己说得很清楚：因为政府不追踪，这份名单几乎肯定是少算的。所以这条新闻削掉的差额是具体的——五百多起个案被记录、被追问、被公开——但它没削掉的是那个让差额可以无限增长的东西：一个不承认自己会犯这个错的系统，永远不会知道自己犯了多少次。

ProPublica counted 519 U.S. citizens detained by immigration agents between Inauguration Day and October 21, 2026. The government says it does not arrest citizens. So none of these 500-plus incidents exist in the official record — not denied, just never counted. Galtung's formula is almost too polite here: violence isn't only pushing the actual below the potential, it's also making the pushed-down part disappear from the ledger.

Look at the details. A citizen on a bicycle in Long Beach was tasered, injured and handcuffed after an ICE vehicle collided with him; footage shows the vehicle driving the wrong way down a one-way street then swerving toward him. DHS denied hitting him and accused him of smashing the mirror — which the available video does not show. In Evanston, agents looking for someone who matched a description injured a citizen and sent him to the hospital; a responding local officer said one agent had blood on his hands. In Annapolis, a citizen leaving a coffee shop was handcuffed for nearly two hours and denied a phone call; DHS said it moved him because "agitators" had gathered. In Lafayette, a mother of four was detained at a checkpoint outside her apartment, transferred into ICE custody, booked into a detention facility, and released only after her attorney won it — ICE and the local agency still dispute her citizenship.

These are not "mistakes," because a mistake implies a system that corrects itself. Jeury Concepcion, 19, was tackled in the Bronx by three masked agents who had the wrong person; he says they released him far from home with his head bloodied, and that an agent apologized. The apology was individual. The institution did not apologize; it said agents temporarily detained him to safely finish their questioning. The 25-year-old from Denver was stopped in Texas, couldn't produce ID, held for days without phone or lawyer, and then "agreed" to leave the country; in August he walked back in with his Colorado birth certificate. A citizen proves he isn't deportable by consenting to deportation and then walking back with a birth certificate. That is what "voluntary" means under asymmetric power.

What's actually moving here is structural and meta at once. Structural: the government does not track these cases, so the conclusion "we don't systematically detain citizens" requires no evidence — only the absence of a table. Meta: who gets to define who is a citizen, and who gets to be believed. The person stopped must prove himself first; the agent's account stands first; the DHS statement goes out first; the video, the lawyer, and ProPublica's spreadsheet arrive last. What ProPublica did is essentially a recovery of interpretive power: the government refused to count, so a newsroom counted for it — two sources minimum per incident, every case put to DHS, every case checked for federal charges. That isn't sentiment. That's taking a slice of the power to manufacture facts back from a press release.

Conspiracy here needs no conspiracy. An agency that builds no table, a vocabulary of "lawful commands," a statement that citizens are not arrested, a local checkpoint outside an apartment building, a handoff into ICE custody — everyone in the chain is just doing their step, and the payoff is that no one is responsible for the total. If the total doesn't exist, the responsibility doesn't either.

519 is a floor. The reporting says so plainly: because the government doesn't track this, the list is almost certainly an undercount. So the gap this story closes is concrete — 500-plus individual cases recorded, questioned, published. What it doesn't close is the thing that lets the gap grow without limit: a system that won't admit it makes this mistake will never know how many times it has.
