燕麦粥冠军与被遮蔽的厨房劳役Porridge Champions and the Masked Domestic Labor

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
当家务被包装成‘冠军赛’，它就成了特权者的游戏。
When housework becomes a 'championship', it transforms into a game of privilege.

一场关于煮燕麦粥的‘全国锦标赛’在澳大利亚举办，人们欢呼、穿苏格兰裙、争夺名为 Spurtle 的搅拌棒。这看起来像是一场纯真的 retro 狂欢，但本质上是一次典型的 cultural violence：将长期被贬低为‘无价值’的家务劳役，在特定时刻抽离出其结构性压迫，将其‘武器化’为一种中产阶级的审美情趣和竞技体育。

请注意这场比赛的参与者和叙事。当煮粥变成一场 competition，它就从一种 survival-based 的日常重复变成了 an optimal expression 的博弈。在现实的 structural 层面上，全球数亿女性在厨房中进行的重复性劳作被定义为‘无偿家务’，是她们在存在性战争中被剥夺主体性的标志。而当这种劳作被搬上舞台，加上‘冠军’、‘奖金’和‘飞往苏格兰’的奖励时，它突然变得‘酷’了起来。这种‘酷’是由谁定义的？是由那些拥有 leisure time 和社交资本的人定义的。

最讽刺的细节在于文中提到的‘家庭活动被竞争化’。对于很多女性来说，厨房从来不是一个可以‘选择’进入的竞技场，而是一座生物墙与社会规训共同构建的监狱。当男性参与者在比赛中‘紧张’地搅拌燕麦时，他们是在 perform 一种‘热爱生活’的身份；而当女性在日常中搅拌燕麦时，她们是在执行一种被内化的共谋。

这场比赛并没有缩小 Potential − Actual 的差额，它只是给结构性暴力穿上了一件苏格兰格子裙。它让人们在一种‘简单’的幻觉中，忘记了谁在为这个世界的‘简单’支付最沉重的劳动力代价。

A 'national championship' for porridge-making in Australia, complete with kilts and cheering crowds, presents itself as a whimsical retro carnival. In reality, it is a textbook case of cultural violence: stripping domestic labor of its structural oppression to weaponize it as a middle-class aesthetic and a competitive sport.

Observe the participants and the narrative. When cooking porridge becomes a competition, it shifts from survival-based repetition to a game of optimal expression. Structurally, the repetitive labor performed by millions of women in kitchens is dismissed as 'unpaid domestic work'—a marker of their stripped agency in the existential war. Yet, when this same labor is staged with 'titles', 'cash prizes', and 'flights to Scotland', it suddenly becomes 'cool'. Who defines this 'coolness'? Those with the leisure time and social capital to treat labor as a hobby.

The most ironic detail is the mention of 'turning wholesome family activities into competitions'. For many women, the kitchen is not a chosen arena for competition, but a prison built by the biological wall and social discipline. When male contestants 'nervously' stir oats on stage, they are performing an identity of 'loving life'; when women stir oats in daily life, they are executing an internalized complicity.

This event does not reduce the gap between Potential and Actual; it merely dresses structural violence in a tartan kilt. It allows people to buy into a fantasy of 'simplicity' while ignoring who pays the heaviest labor cost to maintain that very simplicity for others.
