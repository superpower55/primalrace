心理健康清单里的定价权The Price Tag on the Pain List

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
能被讲出来的痛苦，先得被市场定价
Pain gets heard only after the market prices it.

Billboard 这份清单的第一句话就把框架交代清楚了：过去人们「putting on a happy face for the rest of the world」。这句话本身没错，问题是这份清单把「不再假笑」当成了解药，而它真正记录的是另一件事——痛苦被讲出来之后，谁拿到了它的定价权。

看 Chappell Roan 那一条。她在 2025 年格莱美领奖台上要的是「healthcare — including mental health — for artists」。这是清单里唯一一个把痛苦指向制度而不是指向个人心态的句子。其余几十条，落点几乎全是「说出来」「去治疗」「我学会了照顾自己」。Ariana Grande 说「Healing isn’t linear」，Halsey 说「Mental health isn’t a destination」。这些话对个体是真的，但它们把结构问题翻译成了个人修行。翻译一旦完成，行业就不必回答任何问题了。

行业的问题在这份清单里到处都是，只是没有被命名。Becky G 说巡演让她「low blood circulation, muscle cramps, dehydration, anxiety」；JENNIE 说上一轮世界巡演结束后「I became sick, both mentally and physically」；TWICE 的 Mina 和 Jeongyeon 分别在 2019 年和 2020-2021 年因心理健康停工；Jisoo 说「there are difficulties that are my own」；Lewis Capaldi 说「It’s only making music that does this to me」，并且明说如果损伤不可逆他就退出。这些句子拼起来是一条生产线的说明书：高强度巡演、持续曝光、以身体为燃料。清单把它们一条条列出来，却始终用「brave」这个词包装。勇敢是个体的美德，生产线不需要 bravery，生产线需要的是有人继续上车。

产后抑郁那几条更值得停一下。Adele 说「I felt very inadequate; I felt like I’d made the worst decision of my life」；Alanis Morissette 把它叫作「post partum tar-drenched trenches」，还列了「Hormonal. Sleep deprivation. Fogginess. Physical pain. Isolation.」；Halle Bailey 说「it’s hard for me to be separated from my baby for more than 30 minutes at a time before I start to kind of freak out」。三个人说的是同一件事：生育的身体代价，以及代价之后没有任何承接。清单把她们放进「心理健康」栏目，等于把这件事定性为一种需要个人去治疗的情绪。它不是。它是生物墙和结构之间那道没有被填上的差额，而这个差额在行业里通常由母亲一个人扛。

还有一层：这份清单本身是生意。它挂在 Billboard 的网站上，每一条都链回这些艺人的作品、专辑、纪录片、巡演。Noah Kahan 的 The Busyhead Project 跟着巡演走，G Herbo 的 Swervin’ Through Stress，Megan Thee Stallion 的资源网站，Brian “Head” Welch 投资了 Atlantic Behavioral Health，J Balvin 和 Equinox 合作。我不怀疑这些人的痛苦，也不怀疑其中一些人真的想帮忙。但当「说出痛苦」同时是公关资产和商业入口时，说出来的那部分就会被挑选：适合被讲述的抑郁、适合被展示的康复、适合被引用的金句。Sinead O’Connor 那一条里她自己说过「what’s worse is the stigma who doesn’t give a s–t who you are」，她 2012 年因双相障碍取消巡演时写的是「attempting to be stronger than I actually am」。这句话和清单的语调不在一个频道上，它太不体面，也太接近真相。

所以这份清单里真正缺席的，是那些没有平台、没有 memoir、没有纪录片的人：巡演团队里的乐手和工作人员，签了长约的练习生，产后回不去岗位的普通母亲。他们的痛苦不会被收集，因为他们不产生流量。清单最后给了两个热线电话，1-800-662-HELP 和 988。热线能接住一个人，接不住一条把人推到热线上的生产线。

Chappell Roan 在领奖台上要的是 healthcare。这份清单把她的那句话收进来，然后接着列下一位艺人的名字，仿佛那句话和「I meditate, do yoga, Pilates」是同一类建议。

The first line of Billboard's list states the frame: people, musicians included, were "putting on a happy face for the rest of the world." True enough. The problem is that the list treats not pretending as the cure, when what it actually documents is something else — once pain is spoken, someone gets to price it.

Look at the Chappell Roan entry. On the 2025 Grammys stage she asked for "healthcare — including mental health — for artists." That is the only sentence in the whole list that points the pain at an institution rather than at a private mindset. The other dozens land on "speak up," "go to therapy," "I learned to take care of myself." Ariana Grande says "Healing isn't linear"; Halsey says "Mental health isn't a destination." True for a person. But these sentences translate a structural problem into personal practice, and once the translation is done, the industry owes no answer to anything.

The industry's problem is all over the list, just never named. Becky G describes touring giving her "low blood circulation, muscle cramps, dehydration, anxiety." JENNIE says after the last world tour "I became sick, both mentally and physically." Mina and Jeongyeon of TWICE took breaks for their mental health in 2019 and 2020-2021. Jisoo says "there are difficulties that are my own." Lewis Capaldi says "It's only making music that does this to me," and adds that if it does irreparable damage he'll quit. String those together and you have a spec sheet for an assembly line: relentless touring, constant exposure, the body as fuel. The list prints each item and calls the people "brave." Bravery is an individual virtue. An assembly line doesn't need bravery; it needs people to keep boarding.

The postpartum entries deserve a pause. Adele: "I felt very inadequate; I felt like I'd made the worst decision of my life." Alanis Morissette calls it "post partum tar-drenched trenches" and lists "Hormonal. Sleep deprivation. Fogginess. Physical pain. Isolation." Halle Bailey: "it's hard for me to be separated from my baby for more than 30 minutes at a time before I start to kind of freak out." Three women describing the same thing — the biological cost of birth, and no structure to absorb it afterward. Filing them under "mental health" reclassifies that cost as a mood to be treated. It isn't. It's the gap between the biological wall and the structure, and in this industry the mother usually pays it alone.

One more layer: the list is a business. It lives on Billboard's site, and nearly every entry links back to an album, a documentary, a tour. Noah Kahan's Busyhead Project rides along with his tour; G Herbo has Swervin' Through Stress; Megan Thee Stallion built a resource site; Brian "Head" Welch invested in Atlantic Behavioral Health; J Balvin partnered with Equinox. I don't doubt the pain, and I don't doubt that some of them mean to help. But when speaking pain is simultaneously a PR asset and a commercial entry point, the pain that gets spoken is the pain that gets selected: the depression that photographs well, the recovery that can be quoted, the line that fits a caption. In the Sinead O'Connor entry she herself says "what's worse is the stigma who doesn't give a s–t who you are," and in 2012, canceling a tour over bipolar disorder, she wrote that she had been "attempting to be stronger than I actually am." That sentence is on a different frequency from the rest of the list. Too unglamorous, too close to the truth.

What's missing from the list are the people with no platform, no memoir, no documentary: the session players and crew on the road, the trainees on long contracts, the ordinary mothers who can't get back to work after giving birth. Their pain doesn't get collected because it doesn't generate traffic. The list ends with two hotline numbers, 1-800-662-HELP and 988. A hotline can catch a person. It cannot catch the assembly line that pushed them there.

Chappell Roan asked for healthcare. The list files that sentence next to "I meditate, do yoga, Pilates," as if they were the same kind of advice.
