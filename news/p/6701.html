钱不会说话，账户会Money Doesn't Talk, Accounts Do

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Democracy Now! ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要调查记者 Matthew Cole 的新书《Princes and Thieves》节选称，特朗普首个任期内，沙特和阿联酋领导人秘密向库什纳的银行账户注入至少 20 亿美元并提供账户访问权，当时库什纳任总统高级顾问。库什纳否认全部指控，其律师要求《名利场》撤稿并威胁起诉出版社；众议员 Jamie Raskin 呼吁库什纳回避中东与乌克兰谈判并公开账户清单。
The newsAn excerpt from Matthew Cole's new book Princes and Thieves alleges that during Trump's first term, Saudi and Emirati leaders secretly put at least $2 billion into bank accounts and gave access to Jared Kushner, then a senior adviser. Kushner denies the claims; his lawyers demand a retraction and threaten to sue the publisher, and Rep. Jamie Raskin calls on Kushner to recuse himself from Middle East and Ukraine negotiations.
外交的定价权，从来不在谈判桌上
The price of diplomacy is never set at the negotiating table.

库什纳的律师说，那些账户不存在。这句话本身就是这条新闻里最值得读的部分——不是因为它假，而是因为它把整件事的争点从「钱有没有进」挪到了「账户有没有」。一个否认，精确到只否认一个可以被查的物件。

Cole 说这本书来自五十多个信源，经过了大量事实核查和法律审查。他的说法是「不神圣的联盟」：库什纳、阿联酋领导人、沙特领导人，加上当时的内塔尼亚胡。五十个信源换不来一个账户号码，这就是调查报道在这类事情上的天花板——你能证明钱在动，你证明不了钱停在哪。

这里动的不是直接暴力，是结构层的定价。一个同时握着中东谈判和家族生意的人，他每一次坐上谈判桌，都在给「美国的中东政策」和「库什纳的资产负债表」之间做套利。武器化的入口不在色情、不在浪漫爱，在外交本身——「和平方案」是一个认知入口，谁起草它，谁就决定了哪块地、哪条管道、哪个港口算「和平的代价」。

Raskin 要求他回避中东和乌克兰谈判，并要求他交出在政府期间能访问的银行账户和其他金融工具的清单。这个要求的方向是对的，但它落在一个人身上，不落在制度上。回避是自愿的，清单是自报的。没有强制披露机制，没有独立审计，Raskin 的这封信就只是一封信——结构暴力最擅长的事，就是把追责做成一个请求。

真正的问题不是库什纳是不是收了钱。是他这个位置本身，让「收钱」和「做外交」变成同一件事的两个说法。一个人一边代表国家谈判，一边拥有可以被外国领导人充值的账户，这个结构不需要证据就已经在施暴了——它把「美国的中东政策值多少钱」这个问题，从公共领域搬进了私人账户。

五十个信源、一本被起诉威胁的书、一封要求回避的公开信。这三样东西加起来，仍然没能让任何人说出那些账户的号码。差额就在这里：我们知道钱在动，我们不知道钱停在哪，而制度的设计让「不知道」成为可以接受的终点。

Kushner's lawyers say the accounts don't exist. That sentence is the most instructive part of this story — not because it's false, but because it moves the dispute from whether money moved to whether an account existed. A denial precise enough to deny only the thing that can be checked.

Cole says the book draws on more than fifty sources, with extensive fact-checking and legal vetting. His phrase is an unholy alliance: Kushner, the leader of the UAE, the leader of Saudi Arabia, and Netanyahu. Fifty sources and still no account number — that is the ceiling of investigative reporting here. You can show money moving; you cannot show where it stopped.

What moves is not direct violence but structural pricing. A man holding Middle East negotiations and family business at once arbitrages between US foreign policy and his own balance sheet every time he sits down. The weaponized entry point isn't porn or romance — it's diplomacy itself. A peace plan is a cognitive entry: whoever drafts it decides which land, which pipeline, which port counts as the price of peace.

Raskin asks Kushner to recuse himself from Middle East and Ukraine negotiations and to produce a comprehensive list of bank accounts and financial instruments he could access while in government. The direction is right; the target is a person, not an institution. Recusal is voluntary. The list is self-reported. Without mandatory disclosure and independent audit, the letter is just a letter — and turning accountability into a request is structural violence's specialty.

The question isn't whether Kushner took money. It's that his position makes taking money and conducting diplomacy two names for the same act. A man who negotiates for a state while holding accounts foreign leaders can top up is already doing violence without evidence: he moves the question of what US Middle East policy is worth from the public domain into a private account.

Fifty sources, a book under legal threat, one public letter. Together they still haven't produced an account number. That's the gap: we know money moved, we don't know where it stopped, and the system is designed so that not knowing is an acceptable place to stop.
