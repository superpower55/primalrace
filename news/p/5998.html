变装是表达，不是玩笑Dressing Up Is Expression, Not a Joke

文娱 文化层 Vulture ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要2026年万圣节前夕，人们已在网上晒出各类小众流行文化装扮，包括SNL空中缆车表情包、Trisha Paytas的孩子们、多个NeNe Leakes造型，以及《南方公园》的Ike等。报道盘点了这些年度最佳流行文化万圣节装扮。
The newsAhead of Halloween 2026, people are posting niche pop culture costumes, including the SNL aerial tramway emoji, Trisha Paytas's children, multiple NeNe Leakes looks and South Park's Ike. The report rounds up the year's best pop culture Halloween costumes.
万圣节变装是表达权的临时开放，也是被定价的表演。
Halloween costumes are expression let out on parole, priced by taste.

万圣节变装是少数几个普通人可以公开「扮演他者」的场合。Vulture 这篇盘点列出的每一套服装，都是一次表达：鱼皇后、SNL 的空中缆车 emoji、Trisha Paytas 的三个孩子、穿高跟鞋跑马拉松的 NeNe Leakes、共产主义水手月亮。这些不是「谁穿了什么」的空洞记录，它们是被挑选、被命名、被排行的表达。

问题在于，谁在给这些表达定价。Vulture 的清单有明确的排序逻辑：越冷门、越需要「懂梗」的变装越靠前。这是第三章说的品味筛选——用「你懂不懂这个 reference」区分圈内和圈外，把「我认得出这个梗」变成一种文化资本。变装的人付出了创意和手工，收获的是一句「你懂」，而榜单本身收割的是点击。

清单里还有一条隐线：被扮演的对象几乎都是女性或女性化的符号。Gal Gadot、Trisha Paytas、NeNe Leakes、Zendaya、Madame X。男性角色被扮演时，通常是作为梗的载体（Tim Curry 的台词、Magritte 的画）。这不是这篇清单的发明，是流行文化里女性身体和女性名字天然更适合被当作 costume 的默认设置——她们是可供穿戴的符号，男性是穿戴者。

所以我不打算把这篇当成「万圣节好有趣」来读。它是一份关于表达如何被定价的样本：你穿什么，取决于你属于哪个圈层、懂哪些梗、愿意为「被认出来」付多少手工时间。万圣节给了表达一个临时出口，但出口外面站着的仍然是那个给品味排座次的系统。

真正值得问的是：当万圣节结束，这些变装的人脱下 costume，回到日常，她们还有多少空间可以这样公开地扮演、公开地被看见？一年一次的表达权，不叫表达权。

Halloween is one of the few occasions when ordinary people get to publicly "play the other." Every costume in this Vulture roundup is an act of expression: the fish who is a queen, SNL's aerial tramway emoji, Trisha Paytas's three children, NeNe Leakes running a marathon in heels, Communist Sailor Moon. These aren't empty records of "who wore what." They are expressions that have been selected, named, and ranked.

The question is who is pricing them. The list has an explicit ordering logic: the more niche, the more reference-dependent, the higher it ranks. That is taste as a filter—using "do you get the reference" to separate inside from outside, turning "I recognize this meme" into cultural capital. The costumed pay in creativity and craft time; what they get back is a nod of recognition. The list itself collects the clicks.

There is a second thread: almost every figure being played is female or feminized. Gal Gadot, Trisha Paytas, NeNe Leakes, Zendaya, Madame X. When male figures get played, it's usually as vehicles for a bit (Tim Curry's line, Magritte's painting). This isn't the list's invention. It's the default setting of pop culture: female bodies and female names are naturally more wearable as costumes. They are symbols available to be put on; men are the ones putting them on.

So I'm not reading this as "Halloween is fun." It's a sample of how expression gets priced: what you wear depends on which circle you belong to, which memes you know, how much craft time you'll spend to be recognized. Halloween gives expression a temporary exit, but outside that exit stands the same system that ranks taste.

The real question: when Halloween ends and the costumes come off, how much room do these people have to play, and be seen playing, in ordinary life? Expression permitted once a year is not expression rights.
