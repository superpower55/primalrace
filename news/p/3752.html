Cal Raleigh 的自我鞭笞：一场典型的男性共谋表演Cal Raleigh's Self-Flagellation: A Classic Performance of Masculine Complicity

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Athletic ↗ 2026-08-13 § 链接
通过扮演‘罪人’来保护权力结构，是男性在失败博弈中的最优解表达。
Playing the 'sinner' to shield power structures is the optimal expression for men in a losing game.

Cal Raleigh 在媒体面前的表演非常标准：用‘I’m awful’这种极端的自我贬低，迅速将球队沉没的责任全部扛在自己肩上。这种表达看起来是勇于担当，但在博弈论视角下，这是一种极其精明的‘假.最优解表达’。通过主动扮演一个‘失败的罪人’，他成功地为球队的管理层——总经理 Jerry Dipoto、主教练 Dan Wilson 以及那些拿高薪却同样低效的领导层——构建了一道完美的防火墙。

这就是典型的男性共谋 (complicity)。在体育这个高度男性中心化的叙事场域里，‘承担责任’被神圣化为一种英雄主义。Raleigh 明确声明‘Dan 不是在场上打球的’，这种表述本质上是在维护一种元暴力 (meta violence)：即执行层（球员）必须为结果负责，而制定规则和资源分配的权力层（管理层）永远是免责的。当一个领袖开始用‘我太糟糕了’来掩盖‘系统太糟糕了’时，他实际上是在共谋维护那个让他和队友一起沉没的结构。

这种叙事武器化 (weaponization) 的最高境界，就是让受害者（被糟糕管理折磨的球员）通过自我规训，将结构性暴力转化为个人能力问题。Raleigh 试图通过‘放下自尊’来寻找突破口，但真正的突破口不在于他是否能把球击远，而在于谁在定义这场失败。在这种‘只要我不指责上司，我就是好男人’的共谋逻辑中，主体性已经死亡，剩下的只有对既定秩序的顺从。

Cal Raleigh's performance before the media is textbook: using extreme self-deprecation like 'I’m awful' to swiftly shoulder the entire burden of the team's collapse. While this looks like accountability, from a game-theory perspective, it is a 'fake optimal expression.' By proactively playing the 'failed sinner,' he constructs a perfect firewall for the team's leadership—GM Jerry Dipoto, manager Dan Wilson, and the overpaid, inefficient executives.

This is pure masculine complicity. In the masculine-centric narrative of professional sports, 'taking responsibility' is sanctified as a form of heroism. Raleigh's explicit statement that 'Dan isn't out there playing' reinforces a specific meta-violence: the executors (players) must answer for the results, while the power structure (management) remains exempt. When a leader uses 'I'm too terrible' to mask 'the system is too terrible,' he is collaborating to maintain the very structure that is sinking him and his teammates.

The weaponization of this narrative reaches its peak when the victims—players tortured by poor management—internalize structural violence as a personal failure. Raleigh seeks a breakthrough by 'putting his pride aside,' but the real breakthrough isn't about whether he can hit a home run; it's about who defines the failure. In this complicity logic of 'I'm a good man as long as I don't blame the boss,' subjectivity dies, leaving only submission to the established order.
