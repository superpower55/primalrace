停车场不是突袭的豁免区A Parking Lot Is Not a Raid Exemption Zone

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Truthout ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要纽约市长马姆达尼致信国土安全部长和ICE代理局长，指联邦移民执法人员未经授权使用市政停车场作为突袭行动集结地，要求立即禁止并威胁提起诉讼。此前数周，一名便衣ICE探员在布朗克斯开枪击中一名多米尼加裔移民，其5岁儿子当时在车内。
The newsNew York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani sent a letter to Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and ICE's acting director, demanding that federal agents stop using city-owned parking lots to stage raids and threatening legal action. The letter follows an ICE agent's shooting of a Dominican immigrant in the Bronx weeks earlier, with the man's 5-year-old son in the car.
当暴力机器借用地皮，地皮的主人就是下一个靶子
When the violence machine borrows your land, the landowner becomes the next target.

马姆达尼这封信的核心不是停车场，是「授权」两个字。ICE没有拿到市里的任何许可，就把市政停车场当成突袭行动的集结地，其中一处挨着公园，一处挨着市立幼儿园。这不是程序瑕疵，这是把「谁有权在城市里制造恐惧」这个问题摆到了台面上。

加尔通的暴力三角里，直接暴力需要一个落脚点。便衣探员在布朗克斯开枪，打中一名多米尼加移民，他5岁的儿子坐在车里——这是direct violence，血是看得见的。但血之前有structural violence：联邦机构能在市政土地上自由布置行动，市府不知情、不授权、也拦不住。血之后还有cultural violence：这套动作被包装成「执法」，而「执法」这个词本身就是让暴力看起来不像暴力的那层糖衣。三层一起动，才凑成一次突袭。

马姆达尼说ICE「烂到根子里」，这话说得对，但他现在做的不是拆根，是把根从自己的地皮上拔出去。十月中旬他和一百多名纽约民选官员联名要求私人停车场业主不要租地给ICE——这是同一套逻辑的延伸：切断落脚点，让暴力机器无处集结。问题是，私人业主会不会听？报道没写。谁在共谋、回报是什么，报道也没写。这恰恰是最该追问的地方：一块空地借给ICE，业主拿到的是租金，还是某种更模糊的「配合联邦」的政治保险？

威胁诉讼是一个动作，但诉讼的对象是「未经授权使用市政财产」，不是ICE本身。就算赢了，ICE换一个停车场继续干，5岁孩子坐在车里的那一幕不会因为一纸禁令消失。马姆达尼要求废除ICE，这是他公开的立场，但废除一个机构不是市长能签的字。

所以这封信真正的分量，不在于它能不能告赢，而在于它把「授权」这个词钉进了公共讨论：联邦暴力机器在城市里行动，需不需要地皮主人的同意？如果答案是「需要」，那下一个问题就是——那些没有市长写信、没有一百名官员联名的城市，它们的停车场归谁管？

The point of Mamdani's letter isn't the parking lots. It's the word "authorization." ICE didn't secure any permission from the city before using municipal lots as staging grounds for raids—one near a park, one near a city-run preschool. That's not a procedural hiccup. It puts the question on the table: who has the right to manufacture fear inside a city?

In Galtung's violence triangle, direct violence needs a place to land. A plainclothes agent shot a Dominican immigrant in the Bronx while his 5-year-old son sat in the car—that's direct violence, blood you can see. But before the blood there was structural violence: a federal agency moving freely across city land, with the city uninformed, unauthorized, and unable to stop it. And after the blood there's cultural violence: the whole operation gets packaged as "law enforcement," and that phrase is the sugar coating that makes violence look like it isn't violence. All three layers move together. That's what a raid is.

Mamdani says ICE is "rotten to its core." He's right. But what he's doing now isn't pulling out the root—it's pulling the root off his own turf. In mid-October he and more than 100 New York elected officials asked private parking lot owners not to rent or lend space to ICE. Same logic, extended: cut off the staging ground, and the violence machine has nowhere to assemble. The open question is whether private owners will listen. The report doesn't say. Who is complicit, and what they get for it, the report doesn't say either. That's exactly where the追问 should go: when a lot owner lends space to ICE, is the payoff rent, or some fuzzier political insurance called "cooperating with the feds"?

The threat of legal action is a move, but what it targets is "unauthorized use of city property," not ICE itself. Win the suit, and ICE finds another lot. The image of a 5-year-old in the car doesn't disappear because of an injunction. Mamdani wants ICE abolished—that's his stated position—but abolishing an agency isn't something a mayor signs into law.

So the real weight of this letter isn't whether it wins in court. It's that it drives the word "authorization" into public debate: does a federal violence machine need the landowner's consent to operate inside a city? If the answer is yes, the next question is—what about the cities with no mayor writing letters, no hundred officials signing on? Whose parking lots are those?
