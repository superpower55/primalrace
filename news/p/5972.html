同名的把戏，选票的定价权The Same-Name Trick and the Pricing of Votes

国际 结构层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
选票可以被稀释，解释权从来不会被稀释。
Ballots can be diluted. The power to explain never is.

阿拉斯加有两个 Dan Sullivan，一个是在任共和党参议员，一个是退休教师。报道说，和 Mary Peltola 阵营有关的人被指招募了后者，内部管这个项目叫“ballot composition project”。特朗普说这是“election fraud”，Peltola 说她“knew nothing about any other candidate on the ballot”。这场戏里最值得看的不是谁撒了谎，而是：一个名字，怎么就成了可以调配的资源。

Ballot stacking 的机制很简单：不改变任何人的立场，只改变选票在纸面上的分布。佛罗里达 2020 年那场州参议院选举，Frank Artiles 被指付钱让 Alex Rodriguez 以独立候选人身份进场，和民主党在任者 Jose Javier Rodriguez 同姓。结果在任者输了 32 票，那个没搞过任何像样竞选的 Rodriguez 拿了六千多票。选民的意志没有被改变，被改变的是意志被计数的路径。这是结构层的操作：规则允许同名并列，于是同名就成了武器。

所以别把这当成道德丑闻来读。这是一门生意，成本极低，回报是参议院的席位。谁付得起招募一个同名候选人的钱，谁就能给选票重新定价。报道里 Peltola 说“I am not interested in tricking voters”，这句话可能是真的，也可能只是最优解表达——在阿拉斯加这个席位关乎参议院控制权的时候，一个阵营的最优解表达和公正的表达之间，差的就是那几个同名者的名字。

真正没被追问的是那两个被招募的人。Daniel J Sullivan Jr 三天前才宣布参选，一个退休教师，没有政治经验，他的名字被放上 ballot 的那一刻，他的表达就不再属于他。报道没有写他拿到了什么、失去了什么。这就是共谋者理论里最容易被漏掉的一环：共谋不总是强者对弱者，有时候是把一个普通人变成一枚棋子，然后所有人都假装那是他自己的选择。

报道最后列了印度和肯尼迪 1946 年的例子，证明这招有时灵有时不灵。但“灵不灵”不是重点。重点是：当一个系统需要靠同名来争夺席位时，它已经承认了选票本身是可以被制造的，而不是被表达的。

阿拉斯加这一局谁赢还不知道。但有一件事已经定了：那两个 Dan Sullivan 里，有一个人的名字从此不再只属于他自己。

Alaska has two Dan Sullivans: the incumbent Republican senator and a retired teacher. According to reporting, people aligned with Mary Peltola's camp helped recruit the latter, and the effort was internally called the "ballot composition project." Trump calls it "election fraud." Peltola says she "knew nothing about any other candidate on the ballot." The interesting question isn't who lied. It's how a name became a resource that can be deployed.

The mechanism of ballot stacking is simple: it doesn't change anyone's position, only the distribution of votes on paper. In Florida's 2020 state Senate race, Frank Artiles was accused of paying Alex Rodriguez to enter as an independent sharing the incumbent's surname, Jose Javier Rodriguez. The incumbent lost by 32 votes; the other Rodriguez, who ran no meaningful campaign, took over 6,000. Voters' wills weren't changed. The path by which those wills get counted was. That's structural: the rules allow identical names on the ballot, so identical names become a weapon.

Don't read this as a morality scandal. It's a business with low costs and a Senate seat as the return. Whoever can afford to recruit a namesake can reprice the vote. Peltola's "I am not interested in tricking voters" may be true, or it may just be an optimal expression—when a seat decides Senate control, the gap between a camp's optimal expression and a just one is exactly the width of a few identical names.

What goes unasked is what happened to the recruits. Daniel J Sullivan Jr announced three days before the deadline: a retired teacher, no political experience. The moment his name hit the ballot, his expression stopped belonging to him. The report doesn't say what he got or lost. This is the part of co-conspirator theory easiest to miss: conspiracy isn't always the strong against the weak. Sometimes it's turning an ordinary person into a piece, then everyone pretending it was his choice.

The report lists India and JFK's 1946 primary to show the trick sometimes works, sometimes doesn't. Whether it works isn't the point. When a system needs identical names to contest a seat, it has admitted that votes can be manufactured rather than expressed.

Who wins Alaska is still open. One thing is settled: of the two Dan Sullivans, one man's name no longer belongs only to him.
