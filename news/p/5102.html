主权外包与数字殖民的共谋The Complicity of Sovereign Outsourcing and Digital Colonialism

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-18 § 链接
当国家将核心权力数据托管给他国公司，主权就变成了合同上的文字游戏。
When a state hosts its core power data with foreign firms, sovereignty becomes a mere word game in a contract.

这是一场典型的 structural violence。英国警方将包含刑事记录、受害者陈述在内的顶级敏感数据托管在 Microsoft Azure 上，这根本不是技术升级，而是一次彻头彻尾的“主权外包”。在 2017 年的风险评估中，警方早已明确知道美国政府内部人员（US government insiders）可以接触这些数据，但他们依然选择了 migrate。这种行为的本质是：为了追求所谓的“云优先”（cloud first）效率，而心甘情愿地将国民的生存安全置于他国法律的管辖之下。

这里存在一个极其恶劣的共谋机制 (complicity)。英国政府通过每年数十亿英镑的采购合同与美国科技巨头绑定，而微软则用一套典型的“律师话术”（lawyers’ wriggling）来掩盖事实。微软声称“从未提供数据”，这在逻辑上是 scam——因为根据美国《云法案》（Cloud Act），如果美国政府要求调取数据且要求保密，微软不仅必须提供，还必须对此闭口不谈。这种“我没做过”的声明，在权力不对等的博弈中毫无价值。

最令人心惊的是文化层面的元暴力 (meta violence)。决策层将“信任”这种情感词汇武器化，用来替代法律上的“主权”概念。当高级官员说“我们被微软安抚了”时，他们实际上是在用一种非理性的、依赖性的叙事，去覆盖掉结构性的安全漏洞。他们把一个国家最敏感的认知入口（警察数据库）交给了一个全球化、去中心化的商业机器，而这个机器的最高解释权在华盛顿，而非伦敦。

这种 deep dependency 已经让英国警方在存在性战争中失去了防守能力。现在他们甚至无法确认数据是否被泄露，因为日志记录权在对方手里。这不再是技术漏洞问题，而是一个主权国家在数字时代被殖民的具象化过程：你拥有法律上的名义，但你的事实定义权和数据控制权早已被他人夺走。

This is a textbook case of structural violence. The UK police migrating top-secret data—criminal records, victim statements—to Microsoft Azure is not a technical upgrade; it is a total outsourcing of sovereignty. As early as 2017, officials knew that "US government insiders" could compromise this data, yet they proceeded. The essence of this move is the willingness to sacrifice the existential security of citizens for the sake of "cloud first" efficiency, placing national data under the jurisdiction of a foreign power.

There is a sinister mechanism of complicity at play here. The UK government is bound to US tech giants through billion-pound contracts, while Microsoft employs "lawyers’ wriggling" to obscure the truth. Microsoft’s claim that it has "never provided data" is a scam. Under the US Cloud Act, the US government can demand data and legally mandate secrecy. In a game of asymmetric power, such assurances are worthless.

More disturbing is the meta-violence at the cultural level. Decision-makers have weaponized "trust" to replace the legal concept of "sovereignty." When senior leaders say they have been "reassured by Microsoft," they are using a narrative of dependency to override structural vulnerabilities. They have handed the cognitive entry point of their state—the police database—to a global commercial machine whose ultimate interpretative power resides in Washington, not London.

This deep dependency has stripped the UK police of any defensive capability in this existential war. They cannot even verify if a breach has occurred because the logging authority belongs to the provider. This is no longer about a technical bug; it is the concretization of digital colonialism: you retain the nominal title of sovereignty, but your power to define facts and control data has already been seized.
