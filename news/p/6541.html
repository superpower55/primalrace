两千美元的折痕The Crease in a $2,000 Screen

科技 结构层 404 Media ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要苹果售价2000美元的折叠屏手机iPhone Duo上市两天后，用户发现屏幕中间出现明显折痕，部分设备在折痕处开裂。有人在苹果店通宵排队购买，社交媒体上已出现多张折痕和开裂的照片，这一情况被网友称为「crackgate」。
The newsTwo days after Apple's $2,000 folding iPhone Duo launched, users are reporting visible creases down the middle of the screen, with some devices cracking along the crease. Photos of creased and cracked phones have spread on social media, and the issue is being called "crackgate."
一块屏幕的折痕，是能力配不上定价的裂缝
A crease in the glass is a crack between capability and price.

苹果两千美元的折叠屏，开卖两天，中间已经出现一道大折痕，有些机器还在同一处直接裂开。这不是品控事故，这是一次定价权的公开处刑。

折叠屏的物理约束一直摆在那里：铰链处的材料要反复弯折，应力集中，任何厂商都绕不过去。苹果不是不知道，它只是选择把这道约束的成本转嫁给用户，然后照旧收取两千美元。用户排队过夜买到的不是一台手机，是一张参与实验的入场券，实验对象是他们自己的钱包。

更值得看的是舆论的反应。裂了，社交平台上开始叫它 crackgate，愤怒是真实的。但愤怒的落点很微妙：人们骂的是苹果品控不行，而不是折叠屏这个品类本身在现阶段就不该被卖到这个价格。这就是叙事入口在起作用。一个结构性的物理问题，被翻译成一个品牌的质量问题，于是讨论的边界被悄悄框住了。苹果只要换一批货、发一份声明，就能把这件事收进售后流程里。

两千美元这个数字才是关键。它不只是一个售价，它是一个信号：买它的人相信苹果定义了什么叫做高端。定价权从来不只是钱的问题，是谁有资格说这件东西值这个价。当一块两天就出折痕的屏幕还能被标成两千美元并且有人排队，说明这个资格暂时还没有被真正动摇。

折痕会留在屏幕上，也会留在买它的人心里。但真正的问题不是这一道折痕什么时候修好，而是下一次苹果再拿出一件半成品、标上一个数字的时候，还有多少人会继续排队。

Apple's $2,000 folding phone, the iPhone Duo, has been out for two days and there's already a fat crease down the middle of the folding screen. Some units are cracked in the same spot. This isn't a quality-control accident. It's a public execution of pricing power.

The physical constraint of a folding screen has always been there: the material at the hinge bends repeatedly, stress concentrates, and no manufacturer gets around it. Apple knew this. It chose to pass the cost of that constraint onto the buyer and charge $2,000 anyway. What people waited overnight in line for wasn't a phone. It was an admission ticket to an experiment, and the experimental subject is their own wallet.

The more interesting part is the reaction. It cracked, and social media started calling it crackgate. The anger is real. But where the anger lands is telling: people are blaming Apple's quality control, not the fact that folding screens at this stage shouldn't be sold at this price at all. That's a narrative entry point doing its work. A structural physical problem gets translated into a brand's quality problem, and the boundary of the discussion quietly closes in. Apple only needs to swap some units and issue a statement, and the whole thing gets absorbed into the after-sales process.

The number $2,000 is the real story. It isn't just a price. It's a signal: whoever buys it believes Apple gets to define what premium means. Pricing power was never only about money. It's about who has the authority to say this thing is worth this much. When a screen that creases in two days can still be tagged at $2,000 and people still line up, that authority hasn't really been shaken yet.

The crease stays on the screen, and it stays in the buyer's head. But the real question isn't when this particular crease gets fixed. It's how many people will line up the next time Apple rolls out a half-finished product with a number attached.
