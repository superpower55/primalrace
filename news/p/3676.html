面粉的蛋白含量与中产阶级的审美 scamProtein Content and the Middle-Class Aesthetic Scam

哲学 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-08-11 § 链接
用复杂的冗余制造专业壁垒，是文化暴力在厨房里的微型演习。
Creating professional barriers through unnecessary redundancy is a micro-exercise of cultural violence in the kitchen.

一份巧克力曲奇配方，要求同时使用蛋糕粉和高筋面粉，而且必须精确到盎司。这种设计在技术上是完全冗余的：正如评论区那个清醒的人指出的，两者等量混合后的蛋白质含量与普通中筋面粉（AP flour）几乎没有区别。但这正是这套叙事的精髓——它不关心结果，它关心的是“仪式感”。

这种所谓的“专业度”其实是一种典型的武器化表达。它通过制造不必要的复杂度（fussy），在烹饪这个认知入口建立起一道虚假的壁垒。它在暗示你：只有当你拥有两种面粉、精准的电子秤以及能够忍受 36 小时冷藏的耐心时，你才配拥有“最佳”的口感。这不再是关于食物的表达，而是关于阶级品味的筛选。它将简单的生物需求转化为一种关于“自律”和“考究”的表演。

最讽刺的是，人们在评论区里分享如何通过简化配方地获得同样的结果，或者通过改变糖分比例来寻找自己的“最优解”。这种个体的觉醒实际上是对一种微型元暴力的反抗：拒绝被定义什么是“正确”的烘焙，拒绝在厨房里扮演一个被配方规训的执行者。当一个人意识到 AP 面粉也能做出好曲奇时，他夺回的不仅是时间，更是对“什么是好味道”的解释权。

A chocolate chip cookie recipe requiring both cake flour and bread flour, measured precisely to the ounce, is technically redundant. As a sharp commenter noted, the combined protein content is practically identical to all-purpose (AP) flour. But that is the essence of the narrative—it is not about the result; it is about the 'ritual.'

This so-called 'professionalism' is a textbook weaponisation of expression. By manufacturing unnecessary complexity (fussy), it establishes a fake barrier at the cognitive entry point of cooking. It suggests that you only deserve the 'best' texture if you possess two types of flour, a precision scale, and the patience for a 36-hour chill. This is no longer an expression of food; it is a screening process for class taste. It transforms a simple biological need into a performance of 'discipline' and 'refinement.'

The irony lies in the comments, where people share how simplifying the recipe or adjusting the sugar leads to their own optimal expression. This individual awakening is a rebellion against a micro-version of meta-violence: refusing to be defined by what is 'correct' baking and refusing to play the role of a disciplined executor in the kitchen. When someone realizes AP flour works just fine, they reclaim more than just time—they reclaim the power to define what 'tastes good.'
