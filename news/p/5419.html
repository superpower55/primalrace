特鲁普的狂欢：一场关于“存在性”的自嗨与共谋Trump's Carnival: An Existential Self-Indulgence and Its Complicity

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-05 § 链接
当表达脱离了现实博弈，它就从武器变成了自慰的祭坛。
When expression detaches from real-world gaming, it ceases to be a weapon and becomes an altar for masturbation.

特鲁普（Trump）的集会从来不是为了选举，而是一场关于“我是谁”的存在性确认。在第一章的逻辑里，表达决定了身份。对于特鲁普来说，那些尖叫的追随者、红色的帽子、以及被他定义为“强壮、英俊”的俄克拉荷马白人男性，共同构成了一个闭环的认知入口。在这个场域里，他不需要处理通胀或伊朗战争这种 structural 层的暴力，他只需要享受被神化的 meta-violence 带来的快感。

这场集会是典型的“假.最优解表达”。他扮演一个被爱戴的领袖，追随者扮演忠诚的信徒，双方通过这种共谋（complicity）获得短期的心理满足。但这种表达是脱离博弈的。真正的博弈发生在生活成本、移民政策这些 actual 状态与 potential 期望的差额中。当特鲁普在红州（Red States）的安全区巡演时，他实际上是在用文化层面的狂欢来掩盖结构层面的溃败。他试图通过制造一个“我依然强大”的叙事，来抵消现实中支持率暴跌的事实。

最讽刺的是共和党内部的共谋者。像肯·帕克斯顿（Ken Paxton）这样的人，在私下承认集会损害了选情，但在公开场合依然扮演“最忠诚的人”。这种共谋是为了在特鲁普构建的男本位权力结构中苟且生存。他们害怕失去这个认知入口的入场券，即便这张票正带着他们一起下沉。这不再是政治博弈，而是一场集体性的 psychosis。

特鲁普现在像极了《日落大道》里的诺玛·戴斯蒙德，他在一个由他自己定义的现实里拒绝醒来。当他要求观众“假装我在竞选”时，他已经承认了自己主体性的丧失——他不再是规则的制定者，而成了自己表演的囚徒。

Trump's rallies have never been about the election; they are a confirmation of his existential identity. According to the logic of Chapter 1, expression determines identity. For Trump, the screaming crowds, the red hats, and the 'strong, handsome' white males he defines create a closed cognitive entry point. In this space, he doesn't need to address structural violence like inflation or the Iran war; he only needs to feast on the pleasure provided by a meta-violence that deity-fies him.

These rallies are a classic 'False Optimal Expression.' He plays the beloved leader, the followers play the loyal believers, and both gain short-term psychological satisfaction through this complicity. But this expression is detached from the actual game. The real gaming happens in the gap between the actual state of living costs and immigration policies and the potential expectations of the people. By touring safe Red States, Trump is using a carnival at the cultural layer to mask a collapse at the structural layer. He attempts to neutralize the fact of his plummeting approval ratings by manufacturing a narrative of 'I am still powerful.'

Most ironic are the complicitors within the Republican Party. People like Ken Paxton admit privately that the rallies damage their prospects, yet publicly perform as 'the most loyal.' This complicity is a survival strategy within the masculine-centric power structure Trump built. They fear losing the ticket to this cognitive entry point, even as that ticket drags them down.

Trump has become the Norma Desmond of politics, refusing to wake up from a reality he invented. When he begs the audience to 'pretend I'm running,' he admits the loss of his own subjectivity—he is no longer the rule-maker, but a prisoner of his own performance.
