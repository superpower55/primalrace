被误伤的‘75’号车牌与匮乏的生存博弈The '75' Plate Misfire and the Game of Scarcity

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
当结构性匮乏被转化为身份标签，暴力便在误认中完成了闭环。
When structural scarcity is converted into identity labels, violence completes its loop through misrecognition.

这出在布列塔尼上演的‘巴黎人滚出去’闹剧，本质上是一场关于生存资源的低端博弈。在加尔通的暴力三角里，这里的 structural violence 极其清晰：疫情后远程办公带来的‘第二家园’潮，通过资本杠杆推高了当地房价，挤占了基础设施，让原住民的 Potential 状态（宁静、可负担的生活）被 Actual 状态（拥挤、昂贵的物价）暴力地拉低。差额就是愤怒。

但有趣的是，这种愤怒在表达时迅速被 weaponized（武器化）成了简单的身份标签——车牌号‘75’。当人们不再关注‘谁在买房’而只关注‘谁来自巴黎’时，认知入口被极度简化。这种简化导致了极具讽刺意味的误伤：那些离开家乡去巴黎打拼、回乡探亲的当地人，因为挂了‘75’号牌而被当作入侵者攻击。这证明了身份政治在缺乏理性审视时，会迅速沦为一种盲目的共谋。原住民在潜意识中共谋了一种‘非我族类’的敌对叙事，结果这把火烧到了自己的亲人身上。

最荒诞的细节在于墙上的那句‘难民欢迎，游客滚蛋’。这是一种典型的 cultural violence 伪装，试图用一种‘道德高地’的叙事（人道主义）来掩盖其排外主义的本质。他们欢迎的是没有威胁、需要被救赎的客体，而排斥的是拥有资本、能够改变当地生态的主体。这种选择性欢迎，不过是另一种形式的权力筛选。

这场冲突没有赢家。它揭示了一个残酷的事实：在资源匮乏的结构性压力下，人们倾向于寻找一个可见的‘敌人’来宣泄，而不是去挑战那个制定游戏规则的资本结构。下次他们可能会发现，即便‘75’号车牌消失了，房东依然在涨价。

The 'Parisians Out!' farce in Brittany is essentially a low-end game of survival resources. In Galtung's Violence Triangle, the structural violence here is crystal clear: the post-pandemic 'second home' surge, driven by capital levers, inflated local housing prices and choked infrastructure. The Potential state of residents—peace and affordability—was violently dragged down by the Actual state of overcrowding and inflation. The gap is the anger.

Interestingly, this anger was quickly weaponized into a simple identity label: the '75' license plate. When people stop asking 'who is buying the land' and start asking 'who is from Paris,' the cognitive entry point is dangerously simplified. This simplification led to a satirical misfire: locals who moved to Paris for work and returned home were attacked as invaders simply because of their plates. It proves that identity politics, devoid of critical scrutiny, rapidly devolves into a blind complicity. The residents subconsciously conspired in a 'non-us' hostile narrative, only to find the fire burning their own kin.

The most absurd detail is the graffiti: 'Refugees welcome, tourists go home!' This is classic cultural violence in disguise, attempting to mask xenophobia with a 'moral high ground' narrative of humanitarianism. They welcome the powerless objects who need rescue, but reject the capitalized subjects who can alter the local ecosystem. This selective welcome is merely another form of power filtering.

There are no winners here. It reveals a brutal truth: under structural pressure, people prefer to find a visible 'enemy' to vent at rather than challenging the capital structure that sets the rules. Next time, they might find that even if the '75' plates vanish, the landlords are still raising the rent.
