数据中心建起来，水先没了The Data Center Goes Up, the Water Goes First

The newsThe link points to an external page outside LinkedIn, which LinkedIn says it cannot verify for safety. The URL leads to a Rest of World report on protests against an AI data center in South Africa.
算力的账本上，水费记在别人头上
On the ledger of compute, the water bill is sent to someone else.

这条新闻的正文在 LinkedIn 上被一道安全提示挡住了，只剩一个外链标题：南非，AI 数据中心，抗议。就这三个词，机制已经完整了——AI 算力的扩张，在南非撞上了当地人的抵抗。至于抗议的是水、是电、是地，还是别的什么，报道没写，我不替它编。

但结构是清楚的。数据中心是这一轮最典型的“制造可能性的艺术”：它不生产任何看得见的东西，只消耗水、电和土地，然后把结果卖给离它几千公里之外的人。加尔通那个公式在这里很好用：一块地原本可以有的样子，和它被数据中心占掉之后的样子，差额就是暴力。这个差额不出现在任何一份财报里，它出现在当地人的水表和电费单上。

谁在共谋？建数据中心的公司、给地给电的政府、以及所有把“AI 进步”当成不需要追问的默认前提的人。这里没有一个人需要亲手做什么，所有人都在既定秩序里各自拿到自己那一份：公司拿到算力和补贴，政府拿到“投资落地”的政绩，用户拿到更便宜的推理。代价被分配给了不在谈判桌上的那一方。

值得记一笔的是，这条新闻是从 LinkedIn 上发出来的——一个职业社交平台，一条外链，一道“无法验证安全性”的提示。这本身就是元暴力的一层：信息被生产出来，然后在流通环节被平台以安全为名降权。抗议者的声音，先要过一道平台的闸，再要过一道语言的闸，才能到达能改变决策的人那里。

报道里写了人在承受代价，也写了一个值得拆穿的叙事——“技术进步惠及所有人”。所以我评。但我要说清楚：这篇评论能用的材料，只有标题里的三个词。剩下的，等原文。

The body of this story is blocked on LinkedIn by a safety notice. All that's left is an external link title: South Africa, AI data center, protests. Three words, and the mechanism is already complete—the expansion of AI compute runs into local resistance in South Africa. Whether the protest is over water, power, land, or something else, the report doesn't say, and I won't invent it.

But the structure is clear. A data center is the most typical "art of manufacturing possibilities" of this round: it produces nothing visible, only consumes water, electricity, and land, then sells the output to people thousands of kilometers away. Galtung's formula works well here: the difference between what a piece of land could have been and what it becomes once a data center takes it over—that gap is violence. The gap appears in no financial report. It appears on local water meters and electricity bills.

Who is complicit? The companies building the data centers, the government handing over land and power, and everyone who treats "AI progress" as a default premise that needs no questioning. No one here has to do anything with their own hands. Everyone takes their share within the established order: the company gets compute and subsidies, the government gets the credit of "investment landed," the user gets cheaper inference. The cost is allocated to the party not at the negotiating table.

Worth noting: this story was posted from LinkedIn—a professional social network, an external link, a "we're unable to verify it for safety" notice. That is itself a layer of meta-violence: information is produced, then downranked at the distribution stage in the name of safety. The protesters' voices must pass one gate at the platform, then another at the language barrier, before reaching anyone who can change the decision.

People are bearing a cost in this report, and there is a narrative worth dismantling—"technological progress benefits everyone." So I'm commenting. But let me be clear: the only material available to this commentary is three words in a title. The rest waits for the original.
