黑名单的表演性正义与结构性共谋Performative Justice and Structural Complicity of the Blacklist

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
没有强制执行力的名单，只是给结构性暴力贴的一张心理安慰标签。
A list without enforcement is merely a psychological comfort label for structural violence.

联合国这次给 61 家公司贴上“人权违规”的标签，本质上是一次典型的 cultural violence 掩盖 structural violence 的表演。在加尔通的暴力三角里，巴勒斯坦人的 Potential（拥有土地和基本人权）与 Actual（被殖民、被驱逐）之间的巨大差额，就是最血淋淋的暴力。而联合国这份所谓的 blacklist，其核心机制竟然是 "name and shame"——通过“点名和羞辱”来试图改变现状。这种逻辑极其 naive：它假设资本在面对一个没有强制执行力的名单时，会因为“感到羞愧”而放弃利润。

事实上，这份名单是共谋者理论的绝佳样本。联合国的人权委员会通过投票建立名单，给予了西方国家一种“我们在关注人权”的叙事入口，从而在 cultural 层面上完成了正义的自我确认。而与此同时，这些公司在 occupied West Bank 的商业纽带依然稳固。只要没有实质性的经济制裁或法律强制，这种“点名”就成了一种 weaponized 的公关手段，让国际组织在不触动既得利益结构的前提下，向公众交付一份“正义报告”。

好在，英国、法国和加拿大的贸易禁令提供了一个不同的方向。从 "name and shame" 转向具体的贸易禁令，是将暴力从 cultural 层面的叙事博弈，推向 structural 层面的资源切断。这才是真正尝试缩小 Potential 和 Actual 差额的操作。但我们依然要警惕：当这些国家宣布禁令时，他们是在反思自身的殖民历史，还是在利用这种“进步”的表达来重新定义自己在国际秩序中的道德定价权？

真正的胜利不是名单上多了多少名字，而是那些通过剥削原初种族而获利的资本，在物理上被踢出局。

The UN's addition of 61 firms to its 'human rights violation' list is a classic performance of cultural violence masking structural violence. In Galtung's Violence Triangle, the vast gap between the Palestinians' Potential (land and basic rights) and their Actual (colonization and expulsion) is the raw essence of violence. The UN's blacklist operates on a naive mechanism: "name and shame." It assumes that capital, when faced with a list devoid of enforcement power, will abandon profit out of a sense of 'shame.'

In reality, this list is a textbook example of complicity. By voting to create this database, the UN Human Rights Council secures a narrative entrance to claim they are 'monitoring human rights,' achieving a self-confirmed sense of justice at the cultural level. Meanwhile, the business ties in the occupied West Bank remain intact. As long as there are no substantive economic sanctions or legal mandates, this 'naming' is merely a weaponized PR tool, allowing international organizations to deliver a 'justice report' to the public without disturbing the structural interests.

Fortunately, the trade bans from Britain, France, and Canada point in a different direction. Moving from "name and shame" to concrete trade prohibitions shifts the battle from cultural narrative games to the structural severance of resources. This is the only way to actually narrow the gap between Potential and Actual. However, we must remain skeptical: are these nations reflecting on their own colonial histories, or are they using this 'progressive' expression to redefine their moral pricing power in the international order?

True victory is not measured by how many names are added to a list, but by whether the capital profiting from the exploitation of the Primal Race is physically evicted from the game.
