战争叙事里没有女人的位置In the War Narrative, Women Have No Seat

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要伊朗最高领袖穆杰塔巴·哈梅内伊向军方下达「重要战略指示」，要求若遭攻击作出「不受地理限制」的立即有效回应。卡塔尔外交官周五在德黑兰举行会谈，调解方继续努力防止伊朗与美国重燃战火。周六下午，伊朗南部格什姆岛以南两艘渔船遭美军无人机轰炸，数名渔民受伤。
The newsIran's Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei told military commanders to prepare an "immediate and effective response, without geographical restriction" if Iran is attacked. Qatari diplomats held talks in Tehran on Friday as mediators try to prevent a new Iran-US war. On Saturday, two fishing boats south of Qeshm island were bombed by a US drone, wounding several fishermen.
一切暴力皆性别暴力，这次连遮羞布都省了。
All violence is gender violence. This time they didn't even bother with the fig leaf.

这篇报道里没有一个女性说话。最高领袖、总统、副总统、参议员、外长、安全主管、军方发言人、谈判代表、分析人士——全部是男的。一场可能重新点燃的战争，从决策到评论，解释权被同一群人握在手里。这不是偶然，这是元暴力的标准配置：谁有资格定义"威慑"、"冒险主义"、"红线"，谁就有资格把人命换算成筹码。

伊朗最高领袖的命令是"不受地理限制"的即时有效回应。翻译过来：报复不设边界。不设边界的报复意味着什么，报道没有写，但报道写了另一件事——两艘渔船在格什姆岛以南被美军无人机轰炸，数名渔民受伤。渔民。不是军人，不是IRGC，是打鱼的。"不受地理限制"和"无人机炸渔船"是同一套语法：强者的表达是射程，弱者的表达是坐标。

美国中央司令部司令说伊朗海军"全在海底"。这句话被当成战果来播报。同时UKMTO记录了霍尔木兹海峡一周的通行量比战前低七成。七成的差额由谁承担？报道没说，但油轮、保险、货运、沿岸生计，这些账不会消失，只会转移到不在谈判桌上的人身上。战争的成本从来不是由决定战争的人支付的。

德国总理说欧洲八个月来为战争付出超过一千亿欧元。伊朗安全主管回了一句"现在轮到你们付了"。两个男人在算账，算的是谁该为这场火烧到谁家门口买单。外长补了一句"一千亿欧元不算什么"。整段对话里没有一个人提到，这八个月里谁的房子没了，谁的孩子没了，谁在海峡边上的渔船上被炸伤了。一千亿欧元是个数字，数渔民的时候用的是"several"。

报道里唯一被引用的、对升级提出质疑的声音，来自一个美国参议员和一个核材料专家。参议员说计划"疯了"，专家说"军事上没有解决方案"。这两句话值得认真对待，但它们仍然是同一套框架内部的异议：讨论的是操作是否划算，不是这场博弈本身是否正当。没有人问，如果霍尔木兹海峡的通行量恢复到战前水平，代价由谁承担；也没有人问，伊朗国内那些不在军事指挥链上的女性，在这场"最大威慑"里被安排在了什么位置。

伊朗国家电视台的社论说："我们绝不能允许战争被接受为伊朗不可避免的命运。"这句话是对的，但它出现在一份由国家机器控制的报纸上，而同一个国家机器刚刚发布了不受地理限制的报复指令。同一双手，一边说不要战争，一边准备让战争烧到更远的地方。这不是矛盾，这是共谋的标准形态：每个人都在自己的位置上做"最优解表达"，而系统性的代价被推给了不在场的人。

报道没有写伊朗的女性。没有写她们在这八个月里如何生活，没有写如果战争扩大她们会先失去什么。这不是报道的疏漏，这是战争叙事的默认设置——女性身体在战争叙事里只有两种出场方式：作为需要被解放的理由，或者作为需要被保护的借口。这一次连借口都省了，直接谈地理和红线。省掉的部分，就是这篇报道最该被追问的部分。

Not a single woman speaks in this report. Supreme leader, president, vice president, senator, foreign minister, security chief, military spokesman, negotiators, analysts—all men. A war that may reignite, from decision to commentary, has its interpretive authority held by one group. This is not accident. This is the standard configuration of meta-violence: whoever has the standing to define "deterrence," "adventurism," "red lines" has the standing to convert human lives into bargaining chips.

Iran's supreme leader orders an "immediate and effective response, without geographical restriction." Translated: retaliation with no boundaries. What unbounded retaliation means, the report does not say, but it does say something else—two fishing boats bombed by a US drone south of Qeshm island, several fishermen wounded. Fishermen. Not soldiers, not the IRGC. Men who fish. "No geographical restriction" and "drone strikes on fishing boats" are the same grammar: the strong express themselves as range, the weak as coordinates.

The head of US Central Command says Iran's navy is "all at the bottom of the sea." This is broadcast as an achievement. Meanwhile UKMTO records that transits through the Strait of Hormuz in one week were 70 percent below pre-war levels. Who absorbs that 70 percent gap? The report doesn't say, but tankers, insurance, freight, coastal livelihoods—these bills don't vanish, they transfer to people not at the negotiating table. The cost of war is never paid by those who decide on it.

The German chancellor says Europe has spent over 100 billion euros on the war in eight months. Iran's security chief replies that it's Europe's turn to pay. Two men doing arithmetic, calculating who should foot the bill for a fire spreading to whose doorstep. The foreign minister adds that 100 billion euros is nothing. Nowhere in this exchange does anyone mention whose house is gone, whose child is gone, who was wounded on a fishing boat by the strait. A hundred billion euros is a number; counting fishermen, the word is "several."

The only voices in the report questioning escalation come from a US senator and a nuclear expert. The senator calls the plan "insane"; the expert says there is "no military solution." Both deserve to be taken seriously, but they remain dissent within the same frame: debating whether the operation is worthwhile, not whether the game itself is just. No one asks who bears the cost if Hormuz traffic returns to pre-war levels; no one asks where Iran's women—those outside the military chain of command—are placed within this "maximum deterrence."

An editorial in an Iranian state-linked newspaper says: "We must not allow war to be accepted as the inescapable fate for Iran." True. But it appears in a paper controlled by the same state apparatus that just issued the order for unrestricted retaliation. The same hands: one saying no to war, one preparing to let it burn farther. This is not contradiction. It is the standard form of complicity—everyone making their "optimal expression" from their own position, while the systemic cost is pushed onto those not present.

The report does not mention Iran's women. Not how they have lived these eight months, not what they would lose first if the war widens. This is not an oversight. It is the default setting of war narrative—the female body appears in only two ways: as a reason to be liberated, or as a pretext to be protected. This time even the pretext is skipped. Straight to geography and red lines. What gets skipped is exactly what this report should be asked about.
