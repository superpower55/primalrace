把公开雇员的名字叫成doxing，才是真正的暴力
Calling public employees' names 'doxing' is the real violence

DHS 把「公开雇员姓名和职务」重新定义成 doxing，然后拿这个定义去起诉抗议者。这一手比泄密本身值得看：先改词，再改法，最后改谁有资格说话。

词的定义权就是解释权。doxing 本来指的是把家庭住址这类信息抛出去让人上门，现在被扩到「说出一个公务员叫什么、在哪上班」。FIRE 的 Steinbaugh 说得很直白，这些是印在名片上的东西。名片上的东西被当成需要刑法保护的国家机密，说明这个机构想要的不是安全，是隐身。

隐身不是中性的。ICE 探员在明尼阿波利斯 infiltrate 群聊、监控抗议团体，被监控的人不知道对面是谁；同一批人现在要求自己的名字不能被写出来。监控是单向的，这才是结构。谁能看见谁，谁被允许不被看见——这不是隐私问题，是权力分配问题。

所以这次泄密真正撬动的是元层：解释权换了一次手。41,000 条记录、55,000 多个权限组，把 DHS 从部长 Noem 一路排到县监狱里的 287(g) 警员，谁向谁汇报、谁在 CUC（certified undercover operation）里、谁用 Palantir 的 Falcon 和 PenLink 拉电话记录和社媒，全摊在一张可搜索的表上。

这里面有值得玩味的东西。「Executive Leadership」和「AI Access」两个组是同一批 13 个人，是 DHS 网页过滤器白名单的例外——包括 Noem、刚回 Anduril 的前 CIO McCord、部长高级顾问 Philip Hegseth（国防部长 Pete Hegseth 的弟弟）、以及 2024 年特朗普竞选团队的地区外勤主任 Krachenfels。一个部门的网络过滤规则，为 13 个人开后门，其中一半是政治任命和家族关系。这就是结构暴力的日常形态：规则写给下面的人，例外留给上面的人。

但这篇报道里没有受害者。被泄的是加害机器的人员名单，不是被它伤害的人。把这件事写成「隐私灾难」是错的框架——Recorded Future 的 Leslie 担心的是对手拿它拼凑情报，那是国家安全的语言，不是人的语言。真正该问的是：明尼苏达那 15 个因为发 ICE 探员信息被控 stalking 的居民，现在看到同一批名字被 DDoSecrets 挂上网站，法律会怎么处理？答案大概率是：法律从来不是为对称设计的。

最后，别忘了 Okta 说「我们没有泄露」。DHS 说「正在积极处理」。两边都在保自己的壳。真正被暴露的不是 41,000 个人的邮箱，是一个机构如何组织监控、如何给自己开例外、如何把「别看我」写进法律。这张表可以搜，但搜完之后，那 15 个被告还在被告席上。

DHS redefined "doxing" to include publishing the names and job titles of public employees, then used that definition to prosecute protesters. That move is worth more attention than the leak itself: change the word, change the law, change who gets to speak.

Whoever defines the word owns the interpretation. Doxing used to mean dropping someone's home address so strangers show up at the door. Now it covers saying a civil servant's name and where they work. Steinbaugh of FIRE puts it plainly: this is information that would be on a business card. When a business card needs criminal protection, the agency isn't after safety. It's after invisibility.

Invisibility is not neutral. ICE agents infiltrated group chats and surveilled protest groups in Minneapolis; the people under watch didn't know who was on the other side. The same people now insist their own names must not be written down. Surveillance runs one direction. That's the structure: who can see whom, and who is allowed not to be seen. Not privacy. Power.

So what this leak actually moves is the meta layer: interpretation changed hands once. 41,000 records, over 55,000 permission groups, DHS laid out from Secretary Noem down to 287(g) officers in county jails—who reports to whom, who sits in CUC (certified undercover operation), who pulls phone records and social media through Palantir's Falcon and PenLink—all on a searchable table.

There's a detail worth sitting with. The "Executive Leadership" and "AI Access" groups contain the same 13 people, the exceptions to DHS's web filter. Noem, recently departed CIO McCord (now back at Anduril), senior adviser Philip Hegseth (the defense secretary's younger brother), and Krachenfels, a former regional field director for Trump's 2024 campaign. A department's web filter opens a back door for 13 people, half of them political appointments and family. That is structural violence in its everyday form: rules for the people below, exceptions for the people above.

But there is no victim in this story. What leaked is the roster of a coercive machine, not the people it coerces. Framing this as a privacy disaster is the wrong frame. Leslie of Recorded Future worries about adversaries correlating the data—that's the language of national security, not of human beings. The real question: the 15 Minnesota residents charged with stalking for posting information about ICE officers—what happens to them now that the same names sit on DDoSecrets' website? The answer is probably that the law was never designed to be symmetrical.

And note: Okta says it wasn't breached. DHS says it's "actively working to address the situation." Both are protecting their shells. What got exposed isn't 41,000 email addresses. It's how an agency organizes surveillance, grants itself exceptions, and writes "don't look at me" into law. The table is searchable. The 15 defendants are still in the dock.
