和平协议碎了，医院先塌The Peace Deal Broke; the Hospitals Fell First

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要世界卫生组织总干事谭德塞表示，埃塞俄比亚北部提格雷叛军与阿达比斯阿贝巴政府重启战事，无人机空袭击中阿姆哈拉州流离失所者营地，救援消息人士称至少15人死亡，其中包括儿童。医院缺医护、药品和设备，救护车服务严重受限，世卫组织警告本已脆弱的医疗系统正走向崩溃，2022年11月的和平协议已基本瓦解。
The newsWHO Director-General Tedros Adhanom Ghebreyesus said renewed fighting between Tigray rebels and Ethiopia's government has pushed the country's fragile health system towards collapse. Drone strikes hit a camp for displaced civilians in Amhara, with rescue sources reporting at least 15 dead, several of them children. Hospitals lack staff, medicines and equipment, ambulance services are severely limited, and the November 2022 peace deal has all but shattered.
停火签在纸上，炸弹落在营地上。
Ceasefires are signed on paper. Bombs land on camps.

谭德塞的声明里有一句话值得盯住：他没有说谁发射了无人机。世卫组织总干事，全世界最出名的提格雷人，在讲自己家乡的死者时，把施暴者的名字留成了空白。这不是疏忽，这是联合国机构在交战方之间求一条运物资的通道时必须付的代价——要进去送药，就不能点名炸人的是谁。

于是这条新闻呈现出来的暴力是断头的：至少15人死亡，包括儿童，在阿姆哈拉州的Jaari 2流离失所者营地；在提格雷的Rama，几个人在找食物时被杀。有尸体，没有凶手。加尔通的三层在这里只露出了中间那层——医院缺人、缺药、缺设备，救护车开不出去，物资送不进某些地区。结构暴力最擅长的姿态就是这副样子：看起来像“局势”，像“条件所限”，像天灾。

但直接层明明在动。无人机不是气候。2022年11月的和平协议被“几乎彻底摧毁”，这句话在报道里只占一行，可它是全部后续死亡的前提。协议签的时候被当成胜利来宣传，签完两年，同一片土地上的流离失所者营地成了打击目标。和平协议是一种典型的表演性让步：它改变的是新闻标题，不是炸弹的落点。

报道里最该被追问的缺席有两处。一是施暴者，谭德塞没说，路透的消息源也没说，读者拿到的是一个没有主语的屠杀。二是那些数字背后的性别结构——声明特意点了“母婴医疗”会中断。战争叙事里“保护妇女儿童”从来是最顺手的开战理由，而真正先塌的，恰恰是让女人活过生产的那套系统。谁在为这场重启的战事付身体代价，报道给了医院，没给名字。

世卫组织和联合国机构在协调运物资。这是好事，是structural层的一点修补，但它修补的是暴力造成的结果，不是暴力的入口。入口在谈判桌上，在那些把2022年协议当作业绩交上去、如今又默许它作废的办公室里。

谭德塞呼吁保护平民、让医护和物资通行。这句话他说过很多次，每次都对，每次都没能让无人机停一晚。

One line in Tedros's statement deserves a hard look: he did not say who fired the drones. The head of the WHO, the world's most prominent Tigrayan, describing deaths in his own region, left the perpetrator blank. That is not an oversight. It is the price a UN agency pays for a corridor to move medicine between warring parties — to get supplies in, you cannot name who is doing the bombing.

So the violence in this report arrives decapitated. At least 15 people killed, several of them children, at the Jaari 2 camp for displaced civilians in Amhara; several killed in Rama, in Tigray, while seeking food. Bodies, no killer. Of Galtung's three layers, only the middle one is visible — hospitals short of staff, medicines, equipment; ambulances barely running; supplies blocked from some areas. This is structural violence's favourite posture: it looks like "the situation," like "conditions on the ground," like weather.

But the direct layer is moving. Drones are not climate. The November 2022 peace agreement has been "all but shattered" — one line in the report, and the premise of every death that follows. When it was signed it was sold as a victory. Two years later, camps for the displaced are targets. A peace deal is the classic performative concession: it changes headlines, not where bombs land.

Two absences deserve pursuit. First, the perpetrator — Tedros doesn't name them, Reuters's sources don't either, and the reader is handed a massacre with no subject. Second, the gender structure behind the numbers: the statement specifically notes that maternal and child healthcare will be cut off. "Protecting women and children" is the most convenient pretext for war; what collapses first is the system that lets women survive childbirth. Who pays with their body for this renewed fighting — the report gives us hospitals, not names.

WHO and other UN agencies are coordinating deliveries. That is real, a small repair at the structural layer — but it repairs the results of violence, not its entry point. The entry point is at the negotiating table, in the offices that filed the 2022 deal as an achievement and now let it lapse.

Tedros calls for civilians to be protected and for health workers and supplies to be allowed through. He has said it many times. It is always right, and it has never once stopped the drones for a night.
