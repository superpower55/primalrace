被“隐藏”的痴呆症与社会护理的共谋游戏The 'Hidden' Dementia and the Complicity Game of Social Care

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
将护理危机定义为“隐藏问题”，是结构性暴力的最高级掩体。
Defining care crises as 'hidden problems' is the ultimate cover for structural violence.

Louise Casey 把痴呆症称为一个“巨大的隐藏问题” (big hidden problem)，这种措辞极其精妙。在 Violence = Potential − Actual 的公式里，痴呆症患者本可获得的尊严与医疗支持（Potential）与现实中被隔离、被忽视的状态（Actual）之间，存在着巨大的暴力差额。但当权力者将其定义为“隐藏”时，这种结构性暴力就被转化为了一种“认知缺失”的文化叙事。它不是被制度性地剥夺了，而像是被不小心“弄丢”了。这就是典型的文化暴力：通过定义叙事，让结构性的资源缺失看起来像是一个待解决的医学谜题，而非一个政治选择。

更有意思的是这场关于“社会护理改革”的博弈。Andy Burnham 试图寻找“共识” (common ground)，而各党派在“私有化”与“非营利”之间地毯式地讨价还价。这本质上是一场共谋者的分赃会议：讨论的是如何分配税金，而非如何终结对护理者的剥削。在这种叙事中，护理工作——绝大多数由女性承担的无偿或低薪劳动——依然是那个被抹除的背景板。无论是 mixed market 还是 community-led，只要不触碰“护理劳动价值的重新定价”，所有的改革都只是在调整谁能从这个 misery 产业中获利，而让女性继续在私域中承担 structural violence。

至于 Nigel Farage 的愤怒，不过是由于他被排除在这次“共谋”之外而产生的存在性焦虑。他指责这是一个“uniparty”的阴谋，但讽刺的是，他所代表的极右翼叙事，正是通过强化传统的父权家庭分工（女性回归家庭提供护理）来维持其所谓“文明”的。在这种博弈中，真正受害的护理者们从未被邀请进入会议室，她们的痛苦被量化为“成本”和“税收”，然后被一群男人在 Zoom 会议上决定如何“妥善处理”。

Louise Casey describes dementia as a “big hidden problem.” This phrasing is surgically precise. In the formula Violence = Potential − Actual, the gap between the dignity/support patients should have (Potential) and their actual isolation (Actual) is a profound void of violence. By labeling it “hidden,” structural violence is rebranded as a mere “cognitive oversight.” It is no longer a systemic deprivation of resources, but a puzzle to be solved. This is cultural violence in its purest form: using narrative to make systemic neglect look like an accidental omission.

Then we have the existential war over “social care reform.” Andy Burnham seeks “common ground,” while parties haggle over “privatization” versus “non-profit.” This is a meeting of co-conspirators deciding how to slice the tax pie, not how to end the exploitation of caregivers. The core of the problem—care work, overwhelmingly performed by women as unpaid or underpaid labor—remains a ghost in the machine. Whether it is a “mixed market” or “community-led,” if the pricing of care labor isn't overhauled, these reforms are merely adjusting who profits from human misery while women continue to absorb the structural violence in the private sphere.

Nigel Farage’s outrage is simply the anxiety of a man excluded from the complicity. He rails against the “uniparty,” yet his far-right narrative relies on the very patriarchal family structure that forces women back into unpaid care roles to maintain a facade of “tradition.” In this game, the actual caregivers are never invited to the table. Their suffering is quantified as “costs” and “taxes,” then decided upon by a group of men over Zoom.
