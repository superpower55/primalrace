一个制片人的死，被写成了一场表演A Producer's Death, Staged as a Tribute

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要《艾伦秀》执行制片人马修·赖特于10月30日在洛杉矶远程工作时去世，终年52岁。家属在GoFundMe上称其死于一场「难以想象的悲剧」，并透露赖特与丈夫贾斯汀今年夏天双双被裁员，如今筹款5万美元用于将遗体运回纽约、举办葬礼和追悼会。艾伦·德杰尼勒斯发视频悼念，称他是「我们整个家庭的粘合剂」。
The newsMatthew Wright, an executive producer on The Ellen DeGeneres Show for 16 years, died at 52 on Oct. 30 while working remotely in Los Angeles. His family said on GoFundMe that he and his husband Justin were both laid off this summer, and they are raising $50,000 to bring him home to New York and cover funeral costs. Ellen DeGeneres posted a video tribute calling him "the glue" to the show's family.
悼词越动人，越说明活着时没人替他兜底
The more moving the eulogy, the clearer nobody had his back while he was alive.

一个人死了，家属要开众筹才能把他运回家。这条新闻的重点不在「难以想象的悲剧」这句话，在于后面那句：马特和贾斯汀今年夏天双双被裁员。

他在《艾伦秀》干了十六年，节目2022年结束。十六年的资历，换来的是今年夏天的一张裁员通知，和死后一笔家属付不起的丧葬费。五万美元的目标，运遗体、葬礼、追悼会。这就是美国娱乐业给一个执行制片人的全部结算。

这不是个案，是结构。脱口秀、真人秀、流媒体，整个行业靠项目制运转：节目在，你是「家人」；节目没了，你是成本项。共谋者理论在这里不需要找具体的坏人，制度本身就是共谋场域——它把「我们是一个大家庭」当成一种管理话术发下去，让每个人在情感上先归队，然后在财务上随时可以被切割。艾伦说他是「我们整个家庭的粘合剂」，这句话在她嘴里可能是真心的，但真心不改变一个事实：粘合剂不享受家庭成员的保障。

再看悼词本身。艾伦在视频里说，赖特和tWitch「在一起，跳舞，大笑」。tWitch 2022年去世，40岁。把一个死于「难以想象的悲剧」的人和另一个早逝的同事放进同一幅天堂画面，这是悼念，也是叙事管理：死亡被转译成温情，结构性的问题被翻译成命运。家属在众筹页上写「如果你曾在饭桌上被他逗笑」，那才是真实的语气，带着具体的窘迫。名人悼词负责升华，家属负责筹钱。

报道没写「难以想象的悲剧」到底是什么。这个空白值得停一下：一个52岁的男人，独自在洛杉矶远程工作，死在异乡，家属在纽约，需要众筹才能把他接回去。这条新闻里所有关于他死亡的描述都是模糊的，所有关于他死后账单的描述都是精确的。模糊和精确的分工，本身就是一种表达的选择——它保护了谁，又省略了谁。

十六年，五万美元，一个夏天。这三个数字放在一起，比任何悼词都诚实。

A man dies, and his family has to crowdfund to get him home. The point of this story isn't the phrase "an unfathomable tragedy." It's the sentence after it: Matt and Justin were both laid off this summer.

He worked on The Ellen DeGeneres Show for sixteen years. The show ended in 2022. Sixteen years of seniority bought him a layoff notice this summer and a funeral bill his family can't cover. Fifty thousand dollars: transporting the body, the funeral, the memorial. That's the full settlement American entertainment handed an executive producer.

This isn't an individual case. It's structure. Talk shows, reality TV, streaming — the whole industry runs on project contracts. While the show exists, you're "family." When it's gone, you're a line item. The co-conspirator framework doesn't need a villain here; the institution is the field of collusion. It distributes "we're a family" as a management script, gets everyone to commit emotionally, then cuts them financially whenever it likes. DeGeneres calling him "the glue to our entire family" may be sincere. Sincerity doesn't change the fact that glue gets no family benefits.

Then there's the tribute itself. In her video, DeGeneres says Wright and tWitch are "together, dancing, and laughing." tWitch died in 2022 at 40. Placing a man who died in an "unfathomable tragedy" into the same heavenly picture as another early death is mourning, yes — and also narrative management. Death gets translated into warmth; structural failure gets translated into fate. The family's GoFundMe line — if he ever made you laugh at dinner — is the honest register, specific and embarrassed. Celebrities elevate. Families fundraise.

The report never says what the "unfathomable tragedy" was. That blank is worth pausing on: a 52-year-old man, working remotely alone in Los Angeles, dying far from a husband and family in New York, who need donations to bring him back. Every description of how he died is vague; every description of the bill after is exact. That division of labor is itself an expressive choice — it protects someone, and it omits someone.

Sixteen years, fifty thousand dollars, one summer. Put those three numbers together and they say more than any eulogy.
