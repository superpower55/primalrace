皇家莎士比亚剧团的“血统”交易The Bloodline Trade of the RSC

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
当文化地标用 IP 换血，它在拯救财务，也在杀死主体性。
When a cultural landmark trades its soul for IP, it saves its budget but kills its subjectivity.

皇家莎士比亚剧团（RSC）现在的处境是个典型的 existence war 样本：在财务危机和文化垄断之间，它选择了最便捷的生存最优解——出卖解释权，换取流量。

把《权力的游戏》前传《疯王》搬上舞台，这在商业上是完美的 scam。它利用了 George RR Martin 构建的男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）作为认知入口，用一个关于权力、血统和战争的男本位史诗来填补亏空。有趣的是，那些所谓的“莎士比亚式悲剧共鸣”，本质上是元暴力的互认：无论是疯王还是麦克白，其核心都是关于支配权的博弈，以及在支配失败后的毁灭。这种叙事在文化层（cultural layer）上极其稳固，因为它精准地契合了男性对权力结构的迷恋。

最讽刺的是，RSC 试图通过这种方式“吸引新观众”。这是一种典型的武器化表达：用大众 IP 作为诱饵，将剧院这个原本具有阶级筛选功能的场所，暂时转化为一个粉丝集会所。但这种“多样性”是表演性的。当一个以莎士比亚命名的剧团需要靠《权力的游戏》和吉卜力工作室来维持生存时，它其实是在承认：它原有的、基于经典文本的文化解释权已经失效了，或者说，它不再能在这个定量的时间与注意力市场中赢得投票。

那个抱怨“背叛了剧团初衷”的 66 岁观众 Diane，其实触碰到了结构性暴力的核心。虽然她自认 snobby，但她指出了一个事实：当文化机构为了 survival 而全面向商业 IP 妥协时，它在结构层（structural layer）上完成了从“艺术创造者”到“IP 分发渠道”的降级。这不仅是财务的重组，更是主体性的死亡。

The Royal Shakespeare Company (RSC) is currently a textbook case of an existential war: caught between financial ruin and cultural monopoly, it has chosen the most convenient optimal expression—trading its interpretative power for traffic.

Bringing the Game of Thrones prequel *The Mad King* to the stage is a perfect commercial scam. It utilizes the masculine-centric narrative of George RR Martin as a cognitive entry point, using a male-centric epic of power, bloodlines, and war to fill its coffers. Ironically, the so-called "Shakespearean tragic resonance" is merely a mutual recognition of meta-violence: whether it is the Mad King or Macbeth, the core is a game of dominance and the subsequent destruction of failure. This narrative is incredibly stable at the cultural layer because it precisely aligns with the masculine obsession with power structures.

Most cynical is the RSC's attempt to "welcome new audiences." This is a classic weaponisation of expression: using mass IP as bait to temporarily transform a venue—which originally functioned as a class filter—into a fan convention. But this "diversity" is performative. When a company named after Shakespeare must rely on *Game of Thrones* and Studio Ghibli to survive, it is admitting that its original interpretative power, based on classical texts, has expired—or rather, it can no longer win votes in the finite market of time and attention.

Diane, the 66-year-old audience member complaining about the "betrayal" of the company's raison d'être, has touched upon the core of structural violence. Though she calls herself snobby, she points to a fact: when a cultural institution fully compromises to commercial IP for survival, it completes a structural downgrade from an "artistic creator" to an "IP distribution channel." This is not just a financial restructure; it is the death of subjectivity.
