工会拿到了门禁卡，没拿到钥匙A Badge to the Building, Not the Keys

其他 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
进得了办公室，不等于分得动利润
Access to the office is not access to the surplus.

英国这条新规真正动的不是 Google 的善意，是结构层：工会第一次有了合法入口，可以走进 Google 在英国的办公场所，见到那约 7000 名员工，包括 YouTube，不包括 DeepMind。Prospect 和 Meta 已经先签了一份，覆盖约 5000 人。入口是实的，之前 GMB 在考文垂 Amazon 仓库那场承认战，就是因为没有门，只能在墙外喊。

但看清楚 Google 的说法：签这份协议是为了合规，公司强调自己本来就有良好的内部员工关系，这份协议是在“履行新要求”的同时“保留我们文化所建立的那种尊重、直接的对话”。翻译一下——门是法律撬开的，不是公司让开的。Google 把工会定义成一道需要被满足的程序，而不是一个需要被谈判的对手。

Clancy 提到 AI 部署。这才是关键。科技公司的结构暴力不在工位上，在“谁决定算法替代谁”这件事上——裁员的决定、模型上线的节奏、绩效的定义权，全在管理层手里。工会现在能进会议室了，但会议室的议程是谁写的，报道没写。access agreement 给的是接触权，不是承认权，更不是谈判权。从“能见到人”到“能坐上桌”，中间还有一整个承认程序。

从一月起，雇主必须告知员工有加入工会的权利。这是把信息不对称削掉一块，值得记一笔。可信息不等于筹码。工会能发传单、能开宣讲会、能电子联络，这些都是在需求侧做工，而定价权在供给侧那头。

真正的检验在明年：TUC 的 Nowak 预期一波承认协议。如果这一波停在“访问”层面，那就是一场表演性让步——法律给了个门禁卡，公司给了个笑脸，工资单和裁员名单一个字没变。差额缩小了多少，不看签了几份协议，看第一轮薪资谈判桌上，工会是被当作一方，还是被当作一项需要完成的合规事项。

What the UK rule actually moved is the structural layer: unions now have a legal door into Google's UK offices, to reach about 7,000 workers, YouTube included, DeepMind excluded. Prospect already signed a similar deal with Meta, covering about 5,000 people. The door is real. GMB's failed recognition fight at the Amazon warehouse near Coventry two years ago failed precisely because there was no door — only a wall to shout at.

Look at how Google framed it: the agreement exists to comply with the new legislation, and the company insists it already had good internal employee relations. The door was pried open by law, not opened by the company. Google is treating the union as a procedural requirement to be satisfied, not a counterpart to be bargained with.

Clancy names AI deployment. That is the real site. A tech company's structural violence does not live at the desk; it lives in who decides which worker the model replaces — the layoff list, the release schedule, the definition of performance. The union can now enter the meeting room. Who writes the agenda is not in this report.

An access agreement grants contact, not recognition, and certainly not bargaining. From meeting workers to sitting at the table, there is an entire recognition process in between.

From January, employers must tell workers they have the right to join a union. That shaves off a slice of information asymmetry, and it counts. But information is not leverage. Leaflets, sessions, emails — all of it is demand-side work, while the pricing happens on the supply side.

Next year is the test. Nowak expects a wave of recognition deals. If the wave stops at access, it is a performative concession: a badge at the door, a smile from the company, and not one line changed on the payroll or the layoff list. Measure the gap closed not by how many agreements get signed, but by the first pay negotiation — whether the union arrives as a party, or as a compliance item to be checked off.
