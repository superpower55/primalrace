文明晚餐下的权力共谋：‘言论自由’是强者的遮羞布Power Complicity Over a Civil Dinner: 'Free Speech' as a Strongman's Shroud

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-04 § 链接
所谓的‘言论自由’绝对主义，本质上是掌握解释权的强权者在实施文化暴力时的防御性叙事。
Free speech absolutism is a defensive narrative used by those holding the monopoly of interpretation to exercise cultural violence.

这场由《卫报》策划的‘跨分歧晚餐’，表面上是一场关于民主、包容与沟通的温情实验，实际上却是一次极其典型的 Meta Violence（元暴力）现场。24 岁的保守派青年 Ted 扮演着一个‘自由言论绝对主义者’的角色，而 58 岁的 Nicky 则扮演着‘温和的引导者’。这种叙事陷阱在于，它将‘权力不对等’的结构性压迫，简化为了‘观点不同’的个人博弈。

Ted 所谓的 Free Speech Absolutism 是一场典型的 Scam。在一个资源分配极度不均、叙事权被男性中心主义垄断的社会里，‘绝对的言论自由’从来不是为了保护弱势者的发声，而是为了确保强权者在 Diminish（贬低）他人时无需支付代价。当 Ted 说‘我想面对我的敌人’时，他预设了自己处于一个可以‘面对’且不被对方物理或结构性摧毁的安全位置。对于被边缘化的群体，这种‘自由’带来的 Actual 结果是精神折磨与生存空间的进一步被挤压，而这种差额正是 Violence 的定义。

更令人不安的是 Nicky 的反应。她试图通过‘他长大后会明白’这种带有母性色彩的 Patronising（屈尊俯就）姿态来消解冲突。这种‘温和’实际上是一种 Complicity（共谋）。她将结构性的暴力转化为一种‘成长过程中的认知偏差’，从而在潜意识中维护了既定的秩序：即强权者的傲慢可以通过‘时间’和‘教化’来修正，而不是通过权力的重新分配来根除。在这种叙事下，受害者的痛苦被定义为‘不能接受冒犯’，而施暴者的权力被定义为‘追求自由’。

这场晚餐最讽刺的时刻在于他们对君主制的讨论。Ted 崇拜那个运作了 1000 年的 Institution（制度），而 Nicky 勉强接受它带来的旅游收入。他们共同地认同了一套基于等级和特权的系统，只是一个在崇拜，一个在妥协。这种共谋在‘文明’的餐桌上被包装成了‘有趣且愉快的夜晚’，而那些被这种制度和‘自由言论’真实伤害的人，在这次叙事中被彻底客体化，成了被讨论的‘潜在受害者’，却没有任何一个席位属于他们。

This 'Dining across the divide' experiment by The Guardian is presented as a heartwarming exercise in democratic inclusion, but it is actually a textbook scene of Meta Violence. 24-year-old Ted plays the 'free speech absolutist,' while 58-year-old Nicky plays the 'gentle guide.' The narrative trap here is the reduction of structural oppression—rooted in power asymmetry—into a simple personal game of 'differing opinions.'

Ted's Free Speech Absolutism is a total scam. In a society where resources are unevenly distributed and the narrative is monopolized by masculine-centric structures, 'absolute free speech' has never been about protecting the marginalized; it is about ensuring that the powerful can diminish others without paying a price. When Ted claims he wants to 'look his enemy in the face,' he assumes a position of safety where he can 'face' someone without being physically or structurally destroyed. For the marginalized, the actual result of this 'freedom' is mental anguish and the further shrinking of their survival space. This gap between potential and actual safety is exactly what defines Violence.

Even more disturbing is Nicky's reaction. She attempts to dissolve the conflict through a patronising, maternal lens, suggesting that 'as he grows older, he'll tune in.' This 'gentleness' is a form of Complicity. She transforms structural violence into a 'cognitive bias of youth,' thereby subconsciously maintaining the status quo: the idea that the arrogance of the powerful can be cured by 'time' and 'education' rather than by a redistribution of power. In this framing, the victim's pain is defined as 'an inability to take offence,' while the oppressor's power is rebranded as 'the pursuit of liberty.'

The most ironic moment occurs during their discussion of the monarchy. Ted worships the Institution that has functioned for 1,000 years, while Nicky grudgingly accepts it for the tourism revenue. They both implicitly validate a system based on hierarchy and privilege—one through adoration, the other through compromise. This complicity is packaged as a 'pleasant evening' at a civil table, while those actually harmed by such institutions and 'free speech' are completely objectified as 'potential victims,' with no seat ever reserved for them.
