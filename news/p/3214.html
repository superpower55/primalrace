塞乌塔的尸体与欧洲的文明面具The Corpses of Ceuta and Europe's Mask of Civility

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-03 § 链接
人道主义叙事是结构暴力的遮羞布，死亡是唯一的真实表达。
Humanitarian narratives are shams for structural violence; death is the only honest expression.

72 具尸体，5 万个试图跨越边界的身体。在《卫报》这类主流媒体的叙事里，这被定义为一场“危机” (crisis) 以及“欧洲内部的分歧” (divisions)。请注意这个词的选择：当人类的生命被简化为政治分歧的注脚时，这就是典型的 cultural violence。它将一场关于生存权的 annhilation 包装成了外交层面的“分歧”，让阅读者在一种文明的、忧心忡忡的氛围中，完成了对结构性暴力的集体共谋。

按照加尔通的暴力三角，这里最残酷的不是那 72 人的死亡（direct violence），而是那个让 5 万人必须通过自杀式冲锋才能尝试触达的制度墙（structural violence）。这些移民在生物墙和国籍墙的双重挤压下，唯一的“最优解表达”竟然是把生命交给概率。而欧洲国家在讨论“分歧”时，实际上是在博弈谁来承担这部分 Potential 与 Actual 之间差额的清理成本。所谓的“人道主义危机”，不过是权力者在意识到无法完全封死入口后，为了维持“文明”人设而投放的认知补丁。

这场悲剧再次证明，当一个群体被定义为“他者”时，他们的死亡就不再是生命权的丧失，而成了某种政治筹码。在这种 masculine-centric 的地缘政治博弈中，无论是摩洛哥的推手还是欧盟的决策者，他们共谋维护的是一套关于“秩序”的元暴力。而那些在海滩上堆积的身体，才是这个系统里唯一诚实的表达：在这个所谓的文明世界里，某些人的存在本身就是一种违规。

72 corpses, 50,000 bodies attempting to breach a border. In the narrative of mainstream outlets like The Guardian, this is framed as a "crisis" and "divisions within Europe." Notice the choice of words: when human lives are reduced to footnotes of political disagreement, it is textbook cultural violence. It packages a systematic annihilation as a diplomatic "division," allowing the audience to participate in the complicity of structural violence under a veneer of civilized concern.

Applying Galtung's Violence Triangle, the most brutal part isn't the 72 deaths (direct violence), but the institutional wall that forces 50,000 people into a suicidal charge just to attempt survival (structural violence). Squeezed between biological walls and national borders, their only "optimal expression" is to gamble their existence on probability. While European nations debate "divisions," they are actually gaming who bears the cost of clearing the gap between Potential and Actual. The so-called "humanitarian crisis" is merely a cognitive patch deployed by power-holders to maintain a "civilized" persona after realizing the entrances cannot be fully sealed.

This tragedy proves once again that when a group is defined as the "Other," their death is no longer a loss of human rights, but a political chip. In this masculine-centric geopolitical game, whether it's the Moroccan push or the EU's decision-making, they are co-conspirators in a meta-violence of "order." The bodies piling up on the beach are the only honest expressions left: in this so-called civilized world, the mere existence of some people is treated as a violation.
