减肥药没能救赎肥胖者，因为游戏规则是让他们受苦Weight Loss Drugs Can't Save Fat People Because the Game is Rigged for Suffering

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-20 § 链接
身体的定价权不在于重量，而在于系统是否允许你停止受苦。
The value of a body isn't measured in weight, but in whether the system allows you to stop suffering.

关于 John Goodman 减肥引发的争论，本质上是一场关于“受苦资格”的审判。当人们在争论他是靠“努力”还是靠 Ozempic 这种药物时，他们其实在共谋一件事情：肥胖者的价值必须通过自虐式的代价来兑换。

这是一种典型的 structural violence。系统先定义肥胖为一种“道德缺陷”，然后要求你通过极端的自我克制（self-flagellation）来赎罪。如果你依然肥胖，你被定义为懒惰；如果你通过药物快速变瘦，你被定义为“作弊” (cheating)。这里的“作弊”逻辑极其荒谬——在一个不把你当人的系统里，你竟然需要通过增加痛苦来证明自己的瘦身是“合法”的。这意味着，无论你处于什么状态，你都无法在男本位、精英主义的审美叙事中获得真.最优解表达。

这种对肥胖身体的围剿，与对女性身体的规训在逻辑上完全同构。无论是要求女性“白瘦幼”还是要求肥胖者“自律”，本质都是在夺取个体对自己身体的解释权。当“健康”被武器化为一种攻击手段时，它就不再是医疗目标，而是一把用来修剪异己的剪刀。系统并不在乎你是否真的健康，它只在乎你是否在它的注视下感到羞耻，是否在为获得其认可而痛苦地挣扎。

所谓的“减肥药争议”只是一个认知入口，它撕开了文化层面的伪装：社会并不憎恨肥胖，它憎恨的是那些不再愿意为之受苦、试图通过技术手段绕过审判的身体。在这种元暴力的逻辑下，身体成了公共财产，而肥胖者被剥夺了最基本的人权——即对自己身体定义权的拥有。

The debate over John Goodman's weight loss is essentially a trial regarding the 'qualification to suffer.' When people argue whether he succeeded through 'hard work' or Ozempic, they are complicit in a singular narrative: the value of a fat person must be redeemed through masochistic costs.

This is a textbook case of structural violence. The system first defines fatness as a 'moral failing,' then demands that you atone for it through extreme self-flagellation. If you remain fat, you are lazy; if you get thin via medication, you are 'cheating.' The logic of 'cheating' is absurd—in a system that dehumanizes you, you are expected to increase your suffering to make your weight loss 'legitimate.' It means regardless of your state, you can never reach a true optimal expression within the masculine-centric, elitist aesthetic narrative.

This siege on fat bodies is isomorphic to the discipline of female bodies. Whether it is the 'pale, thin, and young' requirement for women or the 'self-discipline' demand for fat people, the essence is the seizure of the individual's right to interpret their own body. When 'health' is weaponized as a tool of attack, it ceases to be a medical goal and becomes a pair of shears used to trim the 'other.' The system doesn't care if you are actually healthy; it only cares if you feel shame under its gaze and struggle painfully for its approval.

The 'weight loss drug controversy' is merely a cognitive entry point. It strips away the cultural violence: society doesn't hate fatness itself; it hates bodies that no longer wish to suffer or that attempt to bypass the trial via technology. Under this meta-violence, the body becomes public property, and fat people are stripped of the most basic human right—the ownership of the definition of their own existence.
