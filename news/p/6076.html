一部电影拖了二十四年Twenty-Four Years Is a Long Time to Milk a Joke

文娱 文化层 A.V. Club ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
怀旧是一门生意，不是艺术
Nostalgia is a business, not an art form.

迈克·迈尔斯说《王牌大贱谍4》还在路上，而且这次重点放在邪恶博士身上。这条消息里唯一值得分析的东西，不是电影，是这句话的发布场合——他刚把这个角色从坟墓里拖出来拍了一支手机广告。

一个角色被反复复活，通常不是因为创作者还有话要说，而是因为有人还想继续收钱。二十四年前的片子，今天先卖给汽车公司，再卖给手机公司，然后预告一部新电影。这条链条很清楚：角色是资产，观众的记忆是提款机。

第三章讲表达的武器化，最常见的入口之一就是怀旧。它不需要说服你，只需要让你想起当年笑过。你想起的是自己二十几岁在电影院里笑，不是这部电影本身有多好。商业要的正是这个错位——把“我当年很快乐”偷偷换成“这个东西值得再买一次”。

报道里写得很直白：迈尔斯似乎已经完全没法让这个角色好笑了，至少在一支手机广告的语境里。写这句话的记者是在开玩笑，但它其实说出了整件事的机制。角色已经被榨到只剩一个光头和一句口头禅，而他们还在预告第四部。

这不是什么大恶。它甚至不算暴力，只是又一次把观众的记忆定价、打包、出售。真正的问题在于：为什么一个二十四年没有新东西的角色，还能让一家公司相信“怀旧粉丝群体”值得投放？因为那个群体确实还在，而且他们的注意力确实还值钱。

迈尔斯说这是“警告”，语气是自嘲的。但自嘲也是一种话术——先替观众把“这大概会很烂”说出来，观众就不好意思再认真批评了。这是共谋者最温和的一种形态：不强迫你，只让你觉得较真很扫兴。

所以这条新闻真正的问题不是第四部会不会拍，而是：当一部电影的卖点只剩下“你还记得它”的时候，观众手里还剩多少判断力？

Mike Myers says Austin Powers 4 is still coming, and this time it leans hard on Dr. Evil. The only thing worth analyzing in this news isn't the movie—it's where the announcement was made. He'd just dragged the character out of the grave for a phone commercial.

A character gets resurrected over and over, usually not because the creator has something left to say, but because someone still wants to keep collecting. A film from twenty-four years ago gets sold to a car company, then to a phone company, and then teases a new movie. The chain is clear: the character is an asset, and your memory is the ATM.

Chapter Three covers the weaponization of expression, and one of its most common entry points is nostalgia. It doesn't need to persuade you—it only needs to remind you that you laughed once. What you remember is being twenty-something in a theater, not how good the film actually was. That's exactly the mismatch commerce wants: quietly swapping "I was happy then" for "this is worth buying again."

The report says it plainly: Myers seems completely incapable of squeezing laughs out of the character these days, at least in the context of a phone commercial. The reporter means it as a joke, but it describes the whole mechanism. The character has been squeezed down to a bald head and a catchphrase, and they're still announcing a fourth film.

This isn't great evil. It isn't even violence—just another round of pricing, packaging, and selling your memory. The real question is why a character with nothing new for twenty-four years still convinces a company that the "nostalgic Austin Powers fan" demographic is worth targeting. Because that demographic is still there, and its attention is still worth money.

Myers calls it a warning, with self-deprecation. But self-deprecation is also a tactic—say "this will probably be bad" first, and the audience feels too rude to criticize seriously. It's the gentlest form of complicity: no coercion, just the implication that caring is a buzzkill.

So the real question isn't whether a fourth film gets made. It's this: when a movie's only selling point is that you remember it, how much judgment does the audience have left?
