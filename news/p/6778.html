公开住址不是把桌子摆平Publishing Home Addresses Is Not Leveling the Field

国际 文化层 · 结构层 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要一个名为 Capytistas 的地下活动组织宣布，将分批公开亿万富翁的住址与档案，首批目标是马斯克、其弟 Kimbal 及特斯拉董事会成员、投资方和供应商。该组织称一年来搜集材料，已募得超过 120 万美元，计划公布 150 多个此前未被报道的住址和 3500 多份记录。
The newsAn underground activist group called the Capytistas says it will begin publishing the home addresses and dossiers of billionaires, starting with Elon Musk, his brother Kimbal, Tesla's board, investors and suppliers. The group says it spent a year compiling the material, raised over $1.2 million, and plans to release more than 150 previously unreported home addresses and over 3,500 records.
他们能拿走的只有隐私，拿不走的是权力
They can take privacy. They cannot take power.

Capytistas 把这件事说成「让寡头尝一尝他们每天对我们做的事」。这句话里有一半是真的，另一半是 scam。真的是：富豪和他们的公司每天在收购、转卖普通人的位置数据，报道里那位成员说的「doxmaxxing」不是修辞。假的是：隐私不是富豪最值钱的东西，是富豪最容易补上的东西。搬一次家、加一道门、雇一队保安，成本对他们来说约等于零。真正被这份名单改变处境的，是那些名字挂在「tertiary targets」一栏里的近 40 家供应商——工厂、矿场、办公室，报道说只列公司地址，可工厂和矿场是有具体位置的，那里上班的人没有搬家预算。

这个组织自己也知道马斯克不是重点。报道写得很清楚：他的档案是准备得最小的之一，在目标里排「最不重要」，选他第一只是因为「没有别人能激起这么大的公众愤怒」。也就是说，马斯克在这里是一件工具，用来把愤怒导流到别处。这套操作在第三章的语言里叫武器化：先占住认知入口，再决定愤怒往哪走。问题是，谁来决定导流的下一站？报道说这个团体靠「silos」运作，很多人彼此不知道名字，钱来自「a heartening number of wealthy class traitors」。一个连成员互相都不认识的团体，解释权最后落在谁手里，报道没写。

再看它给出的理由：一位成员拿联合健康 CEO 布莱恩·汤普森被杀一事说，他们做这些是为了防止这类流血。这是把「我们公开你家地址」和「有人可能会杀你」放进同一个句子里，然后说我们在帮你。报道里还写着，四月有人往 OpenAI 首席执行官 Sam Altman 的住宅投掷燃烧装置，被警方逮捕。这两件事之间不需要有组织关系，只需要有同一套「这些人的地址是公共信息」的氛围。制造氛围的人和扔东西的人不是同一个人，但氛围是共用的。

该组织引用的那句话是：「民主不是正在死去，它是被杀的，而杀它的人有名字和地址。」前半句对。后半句是元暴力的老配方：把结构问题压缩成一份名单，再让名单看起来像答案。马斯克往联邦选举里砸了七亿多美元，这个数字说明的不是他住哪儿，而是钱能买到什么。住址公开之后，七亿还在，制度还在，参议院的那张票还在。

报道里最值得看的一段其实是另一边：国土安全部把「拍摄执法人员」也算进 doxing，同时拒绝公开被可信指控犯罪的员工身份；上周黑客公布了四万多名 DHS 员工和承包商的姓名与工作联系方式。也就是说，doxing 这个工具已经在两边同时使用，一边用来对付富豪，一边用来对付移民和抗议者。工具本身没有立场，谁有国家暴力机器，谁用得更多、更准、更不容易被追责。

所以这件事的暴力差额缩小了吗？在 cultural 层，它确实撬开了一道缝：富豪的隐私不再是天然的。但这条缝是用另一群人的安全换来的——供应商工厂的工人、被点名的中层、以及将来任何一个地址被贴到网上却请不起保安的人。报道最后那句「我们可能是在帮他们的忙」，说得比他们以为的更准：给富豪一次免费的安保升级，顺便证明这套秩序经得起被看见。

The Capytistas call it giving oligarchs “a taste of the invasions of privacy they commit, and monetize, every day.” Half of that is true. The other half is a scam. True: billionaires and their companies buy and resell ordinary people's location data every day; the “doxmaxxing” line in the piece is not rhetoric. False: privacy is not what the rich value most. It is what they can most cheaply replace. A move, a gate, a security detail — the cost rounds to zero. What actually changes is the situation of the nearly 40 suppliers listed as “tertiary targets”: factories, mines, corporate offices. The report says only company addresses. Factories and mines have locations, and the people who work in them do not have a relocation budget.

The group knows Musk is not the point. His file is among its smallest, he ranks among its “least important” targets, and he was chosen first because no one else draws as much public anger. Musk is a funnel. That is the weaponization play from Chapter 3: seize the cognitive entry point, then decide where the anger flows. The question the report leaves open is who decides the next stop. The group runs in silos, many members do not know each other's names, and the money comes from “a heartening number of wealthy class traitors.” Who holds the interpretive power at the end is not written down.

Then the justification: one member cites the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson and says the project is meant to prevent such bloodshed. That puts “we publish your address” and “someone may kill you” in the same sentence and calls it a favor. The report also notes that in April a 20-year-old was arrested for throwing an incendiary device at Sam Altman's home. No organizational link is needed between the two. Only a shared atmosphere in which an address counts as public information. The people building the atmosphere and the people throwing things are not the same people. The atmosphere is shared.

The group's creed, adapted from Utah Phillips: democracy is not dying, it is being killed, and those killing it have names and addresses. The first half is right. The second half is the old meta-violence recipe: compress a structural problem into a list, then let the list look like an answer. Musk put more than $700 million into federal elections since 2023. That number is not about where he sleeps. It is about what money buys. Publish the address and the $700 million is still there, the institutions are still there, the Senate seat is still there.

The most telling passage is on the other side. DHS stretched “doxing” to cover essentially any videotaping of law enforcement while refusing to identify officers credibly accused of crimes; last week hackers published the names and work contacts of more than 40,000 DHS employees and contractors. The tool is already in use on both sides — against billionaires, and against immigrants and protesters. The tool has no politics. Whoever holds the state's violence machine uses it more, more precisely, and with less accountability.

So did the gap close? At the cultural layer, a crack opened: billionaire privacy is no longer天然的. That crack was paid for with other people's safety — the workers in those supplier factories, the named middle managers, and anyone whose address goes up later and who cannot afford a guard. The closing line, that they may be doing them a favor, is more accurate than they know: a free security upgrade for the rich, plus proof that this order survives being seen.
