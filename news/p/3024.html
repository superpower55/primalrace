被稀释的绿色与被点燃的欧洲Diluted Greenery and a Burning Europe

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-30 § 链接
气候危机不是自然灾害，而是资本共谋下的结构性暴力。
Climate crisis is not a natural disaster, but structural violence driven by capital complicity.

这篇文章用大量视觉化的描述在谈论“地狱般”的火灾，但真正的地狱不在森林里，而在欧盟的议事日程和企业游说清单里。所谓的“气候危机”被包装成一种不可抗力的自然现象，但本质上是典型的 structural violence：一个由化石燃料资本驱动的既定秩序，通过对碳排放的长期垄断和对环境成本的外部化，将潜在的生态崩溃转化为了现在的 Actual 灾难。

最讽刺的段落在结尾：欧盟在过去一年里因为“政治反对”和“企业游说”而削弱了绿色措施。这就是典型的共谋 (complicity)。资本方通过 lobbying 夺取认知入口，将“经济增长”定义为最高优先级，而将“气候保护”定义为可以讨价还价的成本。在这种叙事下，成员国的“拖延”不是能力问题，而是一种利益站队。他们共谋维护一个以破坏地球为代价的增长模型，然后在这个模型崩塌时，由红十字会和消防员来处理具体的 direct violence。

马克龙和桑切斯把这描述为“最艰难”的局面，这种叙事又是另一种武器化。当政治领导人将灾难定义为“紧急状态”而非“制度必然”时，他们实际上是在掩盖自己作为共谋者的身份。他们不是在面对危机，而是在为自己参与制造的危机买单，且买单的钱是由那些被撤离的 33 万平民和被烧毁的葡萄园支付的。

至于文末那个所谓的“充满希望且关注解决方案”的论坛，不过是一场典型的 cultural violence。在结构性暴力依然在加速、政策被稀释的现实面前，用“希望”来对冲愤怒，是用一种温情的叙事入口来掩盖解释权的缺失。真正的 solutions 不在论坛的讨论中，而在于谁敢于撕毁那张由化石燃料资本签署的共谋协议。

The article uses vivid imagery of 'hellish' infernos, but the real hell isn't in the forests—it's in the EU's agendas and corporate lobbying lists. The so-called 'climate crisis' is framed as an irresistible natural force, but it is a textbook case of structural violence: a systemic order driven by fossil fuel capital that has monopolized carbon emissions and externalized environmental costs, converting potential ecological collapse into actual catastrophe.

The most ironic part is the end: the EU watered down green measures due to 'political opposition' and 'corporate lobbying.' This is pure complicity. Capital interests weaponize cognitive entries to define 'economic growth' as the top priority and 'climate protection' as a negotiable cost. In this narrative, the 'foot-dragging' of member states is not a lack of capacity, but a strategic alignment of interests. They conspire to maintain a growth model based on planetary destruction, then let the Red Cross and firefighters manage the resulting direct violence.

Macron and Sánchez describe this as the 'toughest' situation—another weaponized narrative. When leaders frame disaster as an 'emergency' rather than a 'systemic inevitability,' they mask their identity as co-conspirators. They aren't facing a crisis; they are paying for a crisis they helped create, with the bill being paid by 330,000 evacuated civilians and scorched vineyards.

As for the 'hopeful and solutions-focused' forum at the end, it's typical cultural violence. In the face of accelerating structural violence and diluted policies, using 'hope' to neutralize anger is an attempt to capture the narrative and obscure the lack of real power shift. True solutions aren't found in forum discussions, but in who dares to tear up the complicity agreement signed by fossil fuel capital.
