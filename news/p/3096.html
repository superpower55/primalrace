用神圣叙事给新自由主义打补丁，是救赎还是高级共谋？Patching Neoliberalism with Sacred Narratives: Redemption or High-Level Complicity?

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
用宗教的“仁慈”替代市场的“冷漠”，本质是更换一套更温情的解释权。
Replacing market indifference with religious mercy is merely swapping one set of interpretive powers for another.

这篇文章试图在 Andy Burnham 的天主教背景和他的治理风格之间建立一种浪漫的因果关系。作者 Julian Coman 将其描述为一种对撒切尔主义“新自由主义”的修正——用天主教的“优先关注穷人” (preferential option for the poor) 和“共同利益” (common good) 来对抗个体主义的荒原。但这在我的眼睛里，不过是另一种形式的表达武器化。

我们要警惕这种“好人”叙事。当一个权力持有者宣称他的政策源于宗教的慈悲时，他实际上是在夺取解释权。他把一个结构性的资源分配问题（structural violence），转化为一个道德层面的“关怀”问题。把关注流浪汉的行为定义为“神圣的召唤”，而非对一个失效的住房制度的结构性追责，这本身就是一种 cultural violence。它让受害者在被施舍的温情中，忘记了权力在顶端是如何通过共谋来制造贫困的。

而且，这篇文章揭示了一个典型的共谋机制：进步派自由主义者对天主教的“礼貌冷漠”或“有限接纳”。只要罗马教廷在移民和人权问题上站队，他们就愿意给这个古老的父权机器发通行证。但他们忘了，天主教的底层逻辑依然是等级森严的，是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。这种“仁慈”是自上而下的俯视，而非平等的权利让渡。

Burnham 可能会在街头与流浪汉交谈，但只要他依然在那个由男性定义、由党派共谋的权力结构中运作，他提供的就不是 Just Expression，而是一种能够维持系统稳定的“最优解表达”。他用温情在缝补一个已经烂掉的体制，让人们在一种“终于有个好人来管我们”的错觉中，继续接受那个元暴力的框架。

This piece attempts to build a romantic causality between Andy Burnham's Catholic upbringing and his governance. Julian Coman frames it as a correction to Thatcherite neoliberalism—replacing rugged individualism with the "preferential option for the poor" and the "common good." To me, this is simply another form of the weaponisation of expression.

We must be wary of this "good man" narrative. When a power-holder claims their policies stem from religious mercy, they are seizing the power of interpretation. They transform a problem of structural violence—systemic resource distribution—into a moral issue of "care." Defining the act of talking to the homeless as a "sacred calling" rather than a structural indictment of a failed housing system is, in itself, cultural violence. It allows the victim to forget how power at the top conspires to manufacture poverty while basking in the warmth of charity.

Furthermore, the article reveals a classic mechanism of complicity: the "polite indifference" or "limited acceptance" of Catholics by progressive liberals. As long as the Vatican aligns with them on migration and human rights, they grant a pass to this ancient patriarchal machine. They forget that the bedrock of Catholicism remains a rigid hierarchy—a quintessential masculine-centric narrative. This "mercy" is a top-down gaze, not a surrender of power to equals.

Burnham may talk to the homeless on the street, but as long as he operates within a power structure defined by men and maintained by party complicity, he is not offering a Just Expression. He is providing an "optimal expression" designed to stabilize the system. He is using warmth to patch a decayed regime, leading people into the illusion that "finally, a good man is in charge," while they remain trapped within the framework of meta-violence.
