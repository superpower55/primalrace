艺术之名下的施虐与共谋Sadism and Complicity in the Name of Art

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-05 § 链接
当施虐被包装成“艺术探索”，受害者与施暴者便在共谋中完成了权力兑换。
When sadism is packaged as 'artistic exploration', victims and perpetrators complete a power exchange through complicity.

Tim Pope 的回忆录是一场典型的关于“权力”的自白。他将把歌手关在淹水的衣柜里、用相机撞击 Neil Young 的额头、甚至让 Bowie 处于被刺穿的风险中，轻描淡写地定义为“sadism”或“艺术”。在这种叙事里，施暴被浪漫化为一种“反叛”和“定制”，而那些被折磨的巨星们则通过“自愿受难”来证明自己的艺术纯度。这不过是一场关于主体性让渡的博弈：巨星用肉体的不适换取视觉上的“先锋”标签，而导演则在这种共谋中获得了掌控他人身体的快感。

这种权力逻辑在 Pope 遇到 Harvey Weinstein 时发生了戏剧性的反转。当他面对一个真正的、拥有结构性权力的掠夺者时，他终于体会到了什么叫“We own you”。讽刺的是，Pope 在书中将这种经历描述为“brutal experience”，却对他之前对艺术家的施虐之举感到“mischievous”。这种双标揭示了一个事实：当你是那个定义规则、掌控镜头的人时，暴力是“艺术”；当你成为被掌控的客体时，暴力才是“伤痕”。

从 MTV 的“大胸美女”规训到导演的“施虐艺术”，本质上都是在将人客体化。无论是被要求穿 Spandex 的模特，还是被关在衣柜里的 Robert Smith，他们都成了某种叙事武器的耗材。Pope 以为自己通过反抗 MTV 的套路实现了“个人主义”，但实际上他只是在另一套男性中心叙事的权力游戏中，从一个执行者变成了另一个掌控者。

Tim Pope’s memoir is a classic confession of power. He describes locking singers in flooded wardrobes and denting Neil Young’s forehead with a camera as mere "sadism" or "art." In this narrative, abuse is romanticized as "rebellion" and "bespoke tailoring," while the tortured superstars prove their artistic purity through "voluntary suffering." This is nothing more than a game of yielding subjectivity: the stars exchange physical discomfort for a "vanguard" label, and the director gains the pleasure of controlling other bodies through this complicity.

This power logic takes a dramatic turn when Pope encounters Harvey Weinstein. Facing a true predator with structural power, he finally understands the meaning of "We own you." The irony is that Pope describes this as a "brutal experience," yet views his own torture of artists as "mischievous." This double standard reveals a core truth: when you define the rules and control the lens, violence is "art"; when you become the controlled object, violence is a "scar."

From MTV’s regulation of "big-boobed babes" to the director’s "art of sadism," the essence is the objectification of humans. Whether it is the models forced into Spandex or Robert Smith trapped in a wardrobe, they are all consumables for a narrative weapon. Pope believed he achieved "defiant individualism" by rejecting MTV’s clichés, but in reality, he merely transitioned from an executor to a controller within another masculine-centric power game.
