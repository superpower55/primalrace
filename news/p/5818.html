换个人坐那把椅子Just Find Someone to Sit in the Chair

其他 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
没有候选人，只有候选人的位置
No candidate, only a candidate-shaped seat

Uefa 在找一个人去和因凡蒂诺竞选。找得很急，截止日是 11 月 18 日，据说下周就可能定下来。报道里写得最诚实的一句是：就算定了人选，也要面对“击败因凡蒂诺的前景存在严重疑虑”。

这就是全部内容。一场选举，反对派的核心工作是找到一个人来输。

看清楚这是什么机制：Fifa 的 106 票门槛，欧洲一个 bloc 不够，得去亚洲、中北美、非洲凑。这些票在秘密投票里怎么流动，报道自己都说了“欧洲 bloc 本身也不能视为理所当然”。也就是说，这场博弈的筹码从来不是理念、不是治理方案、不是 FFE 丑闻到底谁贪了多少钱，是票。而票是可以被拜访出来的——因凡蒂诺这个秋天的魅力攻势包括访问拉脱维亚、捷克、阿塞拜疆。一个国家一张票，一趟航班换一张票。

这不是选举，这是分赃会议的议程安排。

有人会说，那 boycott 呢？报道里确实提到了这种看法：与其找个人去输，不如集体抵制或者不投票，让因凡蒂诺被孤立，因为他从不掩饰 Fifa 的收入大量依赖欧洲。这个思路至少承认了真实的权力结构——钱在欧洲，不在票箱里。但它也只是把“输”换成了“不出席”，因凡蒂诺照样坐在那把椅子上，2029 年的世俱杯照样没有主办地，照样冻结着。

再看那个被反复提到的名字：温格。报道说他被一些欧洲足球圈的人视为潜在的团结候选人，但他本人“据信并未认真进入考虑范围”。他这周在哪？在阿塞拜疆的 Fifa U15 世界杯上，赞美他雇主的新赛事。

这就是共谋者理论最干净的标本。温格不是坏人，他只是把“Fifa 全球足球发展主管”这份工作和“挑战 Fifa 主席”这件事放在了天平上，然后选了前者。他的回报是清楚的：一个职位、一个赛事、一个可以在 U15 世界杯上讲话的舞台。代价是清楚的：他不出面，就没有人能说他背叛了谁。

报道里还有一条线：Uefa 向美国四家法院申请调取 FFE 计划的文件，打算在瑞士提起刑事诉讼，指控财务管理不善。Fifa 的回应是 Uefa 在“骚扰”和“散布虚假信息”。这条线比选举有意思得多，因为它是唯一一条不靠票数、不靠航班、不靠魅力攻势的路径。报道自己也点破了：如果 Uefa 在这个案子上有实质进展，“任何选举或候选人进程都可能变得无关紧要”。

换句话说，真正能动的不是那把椅子，是椅子底下的地板。而 Uefa 一边在法院撬地板，一边在找人来坐椅子——这两件事它同时在做，说明它自己也不确定哪条路能走通。

还有一条：美国中期选举在周二。众议院司法委员会的民主党人 Raskin 七月启动了对因凡蒂诺、Fifa 和特朗普政府之间关联的调查，要求提供文件并接受问询，至今没有发生。如果民主党翻盘众议院，这件事可能重新获得动力。

所以因凡蒂诺真正的风险不在拉巴特的投票箱里，在华盛顿的委员会席位里，在瑞士的法庭里。选举是给外面看的那一层，真正的压力在别处。

而 Uefa 最怕的是什么？报道写得很清楚：重演 U20 女足世界杯前夜的退缩——当时它威胁抵制，然后在开赛前撤回了。那次撤回之所以丢人，不是因为抵制本身有多正义，是因为它暴露了 Uefa 的威胁从来不是真的。

这次也一样。找一个候选人来输，比不找更安全；因为找了，就证明 Uefa 还在场，还有 credibility。至于那个人是谁、能不能赢，不重要。重要的是 11 月 18 日之前，有一个名字可以被念出来。

那把椅子上坐的是谁，从来不是这整套流程要回答的问题。

Uefa is looking for someone to run against Infantino. It is looking in a hurry — the deadline is 18 November, and the pick could be made as early as next week. The most honest line in the report is the one about anyone who stands having to confront "severe doubts about the prospects of defeating Infantino."

That is the whole story. An election in which the opposition's core task is to find a person to lose.

Look at the mechanism: 106 votes, a European bloc that isn't enough on its own, and the need to pull in Asia, Concacaf and Africa. How those votes move in a secret ballot — the report itself says the European bloc "may not be taken for granted." So the currency of this game was never ideas, governance, or who actually pocketed what in the FFE scandal. It is votes. And votes can be visited into existence — Infantino's autumn charm offensive included trips to Latvia, Czechia and Azerbaijan. One country, one vote; one flight, one vote.

This is not an election. It is the agenda-setting of a division-of-spoils meeting.

Someone will say: what about a boycott? The report does mention that view — that rather than field a candidate to lose, a mass boycott or abstention in Rabat would isolate Infantino, who has never hidden that his organisation depends on Europe for vast amounts of revenue. That line of thinking at least acknowledges the real power structure: the money is in Europe, not in the ballot box. But it only swaps "losing" for "not showing up." Infantino still sits in the chair. The 2029 Club World Cup still has no host and is still frozen.

Then there is the name that keeps surfacing: Wenger. The report says some in European football circles favour him as a potential unity candidate, but he is "not believed to have seriously entered the reckoning." Where is he this week? At the Fifa Under-15 World Cup in Azerbaijan, extolling the virtues of his employer's new tournament.

This is the cleanest specimen of the co-conspirator mechanism. Wenger is not a villain. He simply weighed "Fifa's chief of global football development" against "challenging Fifa's president" and chose the former. His reward is legible: a job, a tournament, a stage at the U15 World Cup. The cost is legible too: by not stepping forward, no one can accuse him of betraying anyone.

There is another thread in the report: Uefa has applied to four US courts for the release of documents on the failed FFE scheme, with a view to a criminal complaint in Switzerland alleging financial mismanagement. Fifa's response is that Uefa is engaged in "harassment" and "misinformation." This thread is more interesting than the election, because it is the only path that doesn't run on vote counts, flights or charm offensives. The report itself says it: if Uefa makes significant progress with the case, "any election or candidature process may come to feel inconsequential."

In other words, what can actually move is not the chair but the floor beneath it. And Uefa is prying at the floor in court while simultaneously looking for someone to sit in the chair — doing both at once suggests it isn't sure either path works.

One more thread: the US midterms are on Tuesday. In July, Jamie Raskin, the top Democrat on the House judiciary committee, launched an investigation into links between Infantino, Fifa and the Trump administration, asking for documents and a transcribed interview. Neither has happened. If the Democrats flip the House, that quest may get fresh impetus.

So Infantino's real risk is not in the ballot box in Rabat. It is in a committee seat in Washington and a courtroom in Switzerland. The election is the layer for public consumption; the actual pressure is elsewhere.

And what is Uefa most afraid of? The report is explicit: a repeat of its climbdown on the eve of the Under-20 Women's World Cup, when it withdrew its boycott threat. That retreat was humiliating not because the boycott was especially righteous, but because it exposed that Uefa's threat was never real.

Same here. Finding a candidate to lose is safer than not finding one — because finding one proves Uefa is still in the room, still has credibility. Who that person is, and whether they can win, does not matter. What matters is that by 18 November, a name can be read aloud.

Who sits in that chair was never the question this whole process was built to answer.
