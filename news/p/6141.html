一个不存在的人签了所有人的解雇信A Man Who Didn't Exist Signed Everyone's Termination Letter

国际 结构层 · 文化层 WIRED ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要2025年3月18日，国务卿鲁比奥将美国国际开发署（USAID）的管理权交给28岁的杰里米·莱文，后者随即取消救生援助项目并签署解雇数千名员工。内部邮件显示，莱文未完成入职所需文书，USAID安全部门因无记录无法为其发放证件。
The newsOn March 18, 2025, Secretary of State Marco Rubio handed control of USAID to 28-year-old Jeremy Lewin, who then canceled lifesaving aid programs and signed off on firing thousands of staff. Internal emails show Lewin never completed required onboarding paperwork, and USAID security could not issue him an ID card due to no record of his employment.
程序不是形式，程序是暴力的分配权。
Procedure isn't formality. Procedure is who gets to allocate violence.

一个28岁的人，在美国国际开发署的HR系统里查无此人，却签下了终止所有剩余员工的那封信。这不是管理混乱，这是权力在演示它不需要经过任何入口就能生效。

报道写得很清楚：3月18日鲁比奥把USAID副署长的权限交给莱文，3月20日白宫联络人通知HR办入职，3月26日莱文还没接受offer、没交OF-306背景审查表，3月28日他签了终止全体员工的信。到3月底，安全部门发不出工牌，因为SEC数据库里没有这个员工。一位HR高管的话我原样搬过来：他是行政意义上的幽灵。

幽灵不需要存在，因为签字这个动作需要的不是身份，是授权链条上某个人的一句话。加尔通的公式在这里可以改写成一句更狠的话：暴力的差额，很多时候就藏在“谁有资格签字”这个问题的空白里。直接层是几千人当场失业、60个海外使团被清盘、全球救命项目被取消；结构层是一个从未完成入职的人，绕过了国会确认、绕过了背景审查、绕过了《联邦空缺改革法》规定的三种人选；而这两层之所以能运转，靠的是文化层的一句默许——紧急状态、效率、精简，这些词一出现，程序就变成了可以商量的东西。

莱文自己的说法是，他一直是持有国务院有效安全许可的正式政府雇员，HR只是“困惑了几天”。可HR高管的说法是，直到4月下旬他们才看到任何授权文件。两边的话摆在一起，问题就不是“几天”的行政摩擦，而是这“几天”里他签掉的东西已经无法撤回。

更值得看的是时间线。3月18日，马里兰联邦法院刚对DOGE发出初步禁令，理由是特别政府雇员无权执行大规模联邦行动；同一天，鲁比奥把权限授予莱文。3月28日，第四巡回法院批准紧急中止，理由是这些行为是莱文以联邦任命官员身份做出的——而那时他的入职表格还没交。禁令和授权在同一天完成对冲，程序在这里不是被违反，是被当成一件可以随时换上的外套。

报道里最锋利的一句来自乔治城大学的Super：你不能就这么走进联邦机构的门，然后变成一个雇员。这句话的潜台词是，这个国家的整个行政体系，本来就建立在“不能随便走进来”这个前提上。国会设这道门，是为了保证从街上走进来的人拿不到这么大的权力。现在门还在，人已经进去了，还签了字。

谁在共谋？鲁比奥签了豁免文件，白宫联络人补办了日期，法院在关键节点给了中止，而HR从头到尾在追着一个不存在的人要表格。每个人都在自己的位置上做了“合规”的动作，合起来的效果是一个没有记录的人清空了整个机构。这不是某个人失职，这是元暴力级别的操作——规则的解释权被拿走了，剩下的规则只是用来给结果盖章。

报道没写的是：那几千个被解雇的人，后来怎么样了。Byrnes律师要求恢复他们的职位并补发工资，诉讼还在走。莱文现在在国务院做政策规划主任，头衔漂亮，履历干净。差额还在那里，只是签字的人已经不在系统里了。

A 28-year-old with no record in USAID's HR system signed the letter terminating all remaining staff. This isn't administrative chaos. This is power demonstrating that it doesn't need to pass through any entrance to take effect.

The reporting is precise: on March 18 Rubio handed Lewin the authority of USAID's deputy administrator; on March 20 the White House liaison told HR to start onboarding him; by March 26 Lewin still hadn't accepted the offer or filed the OF-306 background screening form; on March 28 he signed the letter terminating all remaining employees. By late March, security couldn't issue him a badge because the agency's secure database had no record of him as an employee. One HR executive's phrase I'll quote as is: he was the equivalent of an administrative ghost.

A ghost doesn't need to exist, because the act of signing requires not an identity but a sentence from someone on the authorization chain. Galtung's formula can be rewritten here into something harsher: the gap of violence often hides in the blank space of the question "who is qualified to sign." The direct layer is thousands of people fired on the spot, all 60 overseas missions liquidated, lifesaving programs cancelled worldwide. The structural layer is a man who never completed onboarding, bypassing Senate confirmation, bypassing background screening, bypassing the three categories of people the Federal Vacancies Reform Act says may fill a vacant Senate-confirmed post. And both layers run on a cultural permission slip—emergency, efficiency, streamlining—words that, once uttered, turn procedure into something negotiable.

Lewin's own account: he was a regular government employee with a valid State Department clearance the entire time, and HR was "confused about this paperwork for a period of days." HR's account: no one in their office saw any delegation documents until late April. Put the two together and the issue isn't a few days of administrative friction. It's that during those days he signed things that cannot be unsigned.

Look at the timeline. On March 18, a Maryland district court issued a preliminary injunction against DOGE on the grounds that special government employees lacked authority to execute sweeping federal actions. The same day, Rubio delegated the powers to Lewin. On March 28, the Fourth Circuit granted an emergency stay, accepting the argument that the actions were taken by Lewin as a federally appointed official—at a moment when his onboarding forms still weren't filed. The injunction and the delegation were executed as a hedge on the same day. Procedure here isn't being violated. It's being worn as a coat you can change at will.

The sharpest line in the piece comes from Georgetown's David Super: you can't just walk in the door of a federal agency and become an employee. The subtext is that the entire administrative state rests on the premise that you can't. Congress built that door so that someone walking off the street wouldn't be handed these enormous powers. The door is still there. The man walked through it and signed.

Who conspired? Rubio signed the waiver, the White House liaison backfilled a start date, the appellate court supplied the stay at the decisive moment, and HR spent the whole time chasing forms from a person who didn't exist in the system. Everyone performed a compliant action in their own slot; the combined effect was a phantom emptying an agency. This isn't one person's negligence. This is meta-level: the interpretation of the rules was taken away, and what remained was only there to stamp the outcome.

What the piece doesn't say is what happened to the thousands who were fired. Byrnes is asking the court to reinstate all plaintiffs with back pay; the litigation continues. Lewin is now director of policy planning at the State Department—clean title, clean résumé. The gap is still there. The man who signed is no longer in the system.
