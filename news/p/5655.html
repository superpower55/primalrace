所谓“口才教育”：一场关于入场券的阶级共谋The 'Oracy' Scam: A Class Complicity Over Entry Tickets

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-19 § 链接
表达能力不是软技能，而是阶级用来筛选异己的生物墙。
Communication skills are not soft skills, but biological walls used by classes to filter out others.

Lucy Powell 试图把“口才教育” (oracy) 普及化，这听起来像个 good_news，但本质上是在试图给底层孩子发一张进入精英俱乐部的“入场券”。我们要问的是：为什么自信的表达在私立学校是“标配”，而在公立学校被视为“降低标准” (dumbing down)？

这根本不是教育标准的讨论，而是一场典型的 structural violence。精英阶层通过垄断特定形式的表达——那种自信的、具有说服力的、符合中产审美的话语体系——在社会中建立了一道隐形的生物墙。他们并不在乎底层孩子是否“会说话”，他们在乎的是通过定义“什么是正确且自信的表达”，来快速筛选出谁是“自己人”，谁是需要被管理和服从的底层。这种表达方式本身就是一种 weaponized 的工具，用来在面试、谈判和权力博弈中夺取解释权。

那些批评普及口才教育的人，其实是在维护一种共谋关系：让底层孩子保持沉默或使用“非标准”的表达，从而在潜意识中确立他们的从属地位。当政府试图将这种能力“民主化”时，反对者感到不安，是因为他们意识到，如果底层孩子也能熟练运用这套精英叙事，那么原本由表型和背景决定的阶级筛选机制就会失效。

但我们要警惕这种“普及”的陷阱。如果仅仅是教会孩子如何“自信地表达”以适应现有的职场需求，这不过是让他们学会扮演一个被认可的角色，是假.最优解表达。真正的公正表达不应该是让底层孩子学会模仿精英的腔调，而是拆掉那堵定义“正确表达”的墙，让多样性的存在性不再被单一的精英叙事所抹杀。

Lucy Powell attempts to democratize 'oracy,' which sounds like good_news, but it is essentially trying to hand out 'entry tickets' to the elite club for underprivileged children. The real question is: why is confident communication a 'standard' in private schools, yet viewed as 'dumbing down' in public ones?

This is not a debate over educational standards; it is a textbook case of structural violence. The elite maintain a hidden biological wall by monopolizing a specific form of expression—confident, persuasive, and aligned with middle-class aesthetics. They don't care if underprivileged children 'can speak'; they care about using the definition of 'correct and confident expression' to swiftly filter who is 'one of us' and who is the subordinate to be managed. This mode of expression is a weaponized tool used to seize the right of interpretation in interviews, negotiations, and power struggles.

Those critics of universal oracy are actually maintaining a system of complicity: keeping the underprivileged silent or using 'non-standard' expressions to subconsciously cement their subordinate status. When the government tries to 'democratize' this, the opposition feels uneasy because they realize that if the lower class masters this elite narrative, the class screening mechanism based on phenotype and background will fail.

However, we must be wary of the trap of this 'universalization.' If it only teaches children how to 'speak confidently' to fit current workplace demands, it is merely teaching them to perform a recognized role—a fake optimal expression. A just expression should not be about teaching the underprivileged to mimic the elite's tone, but about tearing down the wall that defines 'correct expression,' ensuring that diverse existences are no longer erased by a singular elite narrative.
