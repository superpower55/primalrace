一岁孩子的命，三句话结案Three Sentences for a Baby's Life

其他 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
解释权在谁手里，事实就是谁写的
Whoever owns the explanation owns the facts.

密西西比州总检察长用三句话宣布：一岁孩子 Kohen Wiley 之死，警察使用武力“正当”。三句话。一个黑人幼儿的生命，换三句话的行政效率。这就是元暴力在工作——不是谁开了枪，而是谁有权定义开枪这件事意味着什么。

调查局说调查“仍在进行”，转头又说“涉及警员的调查已经结束”。家属直到周一才知道结论，祖母是在 Facebook 上看到的。孩子的母亲 Vel'lesiya Wiley 当时抱着孩子坐在副驾驶座上，调查人员至今没有采访过她——报道原文写的是，截至九月中旬调查已经完成、摆在总检察长办公桌上，也没有人问过她一句话。一个声称“彻底而公正”的调查，跳过了现场唯一的当事人。这不是疏漏，这是筛选：把会说出不利事实的声音排除在记录之外，剩下的“事实”自然就干净了。

警方最初的版本是车冲向警察。至今没有任何证据支持这个说法。家属聘请的外部病理学家根据伤口外观和致命子弹的路径判断，警员是从车身侧面开枪的。而那个版本——车冲向警察——在报道里被写成“迅速得到州公共安全部门呼应”。两个机构，一个说法，零证据。这不是巧合，这是共谋：一方提供叙事，一方为叙事背书，双方都不需要拿出东西。

更值得看的是这个叙事在社区里做了什么。孩子下葬的时候，谣言已经在传：要起诉的不是警察，是母亲和司机。一个抱着婴儿的女人，在叙事里被改写成嫌犯。这是第三章说的武器化最标准的操作——把受害者的身体变成责任的容器，让围观者去指责她，而不是去问那颗子弹为什么飞出来。报道没写这些谣言是谁放出来的，但谣言的方向和官方沉默的方向完全一致，这本身说明问题。

背景不是空白。同一个沃尔玛停车场，2025 年母亲节，一名女警把一个女人从车里拉出来，当着她的孩子。一个十岁男孩因为当街小便被关押。两个十三四岁的姐妹被前警察局长命令“把你的黑屁股从路上挪开”。前局长被换掉了，换成了一个“恢复信任”的姿态。然后 Kohen Wiley 死了。换人没有换制度，因为制度不是靠换一个名字运转的。

开枪的警员 Hunter Foster 仍在警队，带薪休假。开枪前几天，他被一名黑人同事指控发表种族主义言论，那名同事后来离开了警队。临时局长 Vanderford 自己发过十几条带 N 字的短信，先说是 AI 生成的，被董事会保下来，九月一日试图辞职，董事会宣布已经把他解雇了。接任的是黑人女性 Angelica Maze，她说了前任们没说过的话：向家属致哀。一句话，在整篇报道里显得格外突出——因为它是唯一一句把 Kohen Wiley 当作一个人来提的官方表述。

家属现在等的，是随身摄像头的画面。活动人士 Patrick Lumumba 那句话应该被印在每一份警方声明上：“如果录像能证明他们说的，他们第二天就会放出来。”执法记录仪的画面几天内可能公布，但公布什么、怎么剪、配什么说明，解释权还在同一批人手里。

一个一岁孩子的死，被压缩成三句话；一个母亲的证词，被排除在调查之外；一个社区的多年警告，被换一个局长打发掉。差额就在这里：Kohen Wiley 本可以活着，而所有本可以阻止这件事的机制——调查、问责、媒体、换人——全部选择了维护叙事，而不是维护人。

The Mississippi attorney general announced in three sentences that the killing of a 1-year-old was justified. Three sentences. A Black toddler's life, met with the efficiency of a press release. This is meta-violence at work — not who pulled the trigger, but who holds the power to define what the trigger means.

The Bureau of Investigation said the probe "remains open and active," then said in a phone call that the investigation "involving the officer is closed." The family learned of the conclusion on Facebook. Kohen's mother, Vel'lesiya Wiley, was holding her child in her lap when he was shot. As of mid-September, with the investigation complete and on the attorney general's desk, no one had interviewed her. A probe that claims to be thorough and skips the only witness who was there is not thorough. It is a filter: remove the voices that would complicate the record, and what remains looks clean.

The police version was that the car drove toward officers. No evidence has been released to support it. An outside pathologist hired by the family concluded, from the wounds and the bullet's path, that the officer fired from the side of the car. That version — car as weapon — was "swiftly echoed" by the state Department of Public Safety. Two agencies, one story, zero evidence. That is collusion: one supplies the narrative, the other certifies it, neither has to show anything.

Watch what the narrative did inside the community. By the time Kohen was buried, rumors were circulating that authorities would charge the mother and the driver rather than the officer. A woman holding her baby, rewritten as the suspect. This is the textbook weaponization move: turn the victim's body into a container for blame, so the crowd argues about her instead of asking why the bullet left the barrel. The report does not say who spread those rumors, but they ran in exactly the direction the official silence pointed.

The backdrop is not empty. In the same Walmart parking lot on Mother's Day 2025, a female officer pulled a woman from her car in front of her children. A 10-year-old boy was jailed for public urination. Two sisters, 13 and 14, were violently arrested; the former chief personally ordered one to "get your Black ass out of the road." The chief was replaced in a move meant to restore trust. Then Kohen Wiley was killed. Replacing a name is not replacing a structure.

Officer Hunter Foster remains on the force, on paid leave. Days before the shooting, he was accused of racist remarks by a Black colleague who later left the department. Interim chief Vanderford sent more than a dozen texts containing the N-word, claimed they were AI-generated, was kept on by the board, tried to resign, and was then declared fired. His replacement, Angelica Maze, said what none of her predecessors had: condolences to Kohen Wiley's family. One sentence stands out in the whole report because it is the only official statement that treats Kohen as a person.

Now the family waits for body camera footage. Activist Patrick Lumumba's line should be printed on every police statement: if the video proved their version, they would have released it the next day. The footage may come out within days. But what gets shown, how it is cut, and what story accompanies it remain in the same hands.

A 1-year-old's death compressed into three sentences. A mother's testimony excluded from the investigation. Years of community warnings answered with a personnel swap. The gap is right here: Kohen Wiley could have lived, and every mechanism that could have prevented it — the probe, the accountability, the press, the replacement — chose to protect the narrative instead of the person.
