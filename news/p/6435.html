国庆生日会，钱进了自己公司A Birthday Party Where the Money Went Home

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
纳税人出钱办庆典，庆典的广告费流回总统自己的公司。
Taxpayers fund the celebration; the ad money flows back to the president's own company.

美国建国250周年这件事，被做成了Trump Media的一笔营收。TMTG因投放Freedom 250的宣传广告赚了17.5万美元，另有17.5万美元应收款——服务已经做完、钱还没到账的那部分（来源：The New Republic）。庆典的钱从哪来？National Park Foundation的CEO Jeff Reinbold在国会作证时说得很清楚：Freedom 250的运营资金大部分来自国会为250周年纪念专门拨的联邦款，也就是纳税人的钱。

这条链条上每一个接口都坐着同一个人。Freedom 250是特朗普自己组建的团体，接手了国会设立的两党纪念委员会；它挂在National Park Foundation名下；而TMTG的董事Meredith O'Rourke，同时是National Park Foundation的董事。特朗普本人还持有TMTG 41%的股份。钱从联邦预算出发，绕一圈，落回他自己的资产负债表。

这不是腐败的例外，是腐败的语法。第三章讲的武器化，入口从来不只是媒体和宗教，最直接的一个是：把公共仪式变成自家公司的客户。国庆、纪念、爱国，这些词在别处是用来动员情感的，在这里是采购单上的项目名称。而活动办得冷清、廉价、俗气，恰恰不影响这门生意——报道里写National Mall最后一天人群稀疏，可稀疏的人群不耽误17.5万到账。庆典的质量和庆典的收益，本来就是两个账本。

真正被武器化的是“250周年”这个认知入口。一个国家生日，本该是公共记忆的再分配，现在成了私人收入的通道。谁在共谋？O'Rourke坐在两个董事会之间，是那个把两边缝起来的针脚；National Park Foundation接受这个安排，是那个提供合法外壳的机构；国会拨的款，是那条不问流向的输血管。每一方都拿到了自己的那份：名声、职位、或者一笔不用解释的收入。

报道没有写的是，这笔钱最终由谁审计、有没有人问过国会的拨款能不能这样回流。17.5万在联邦预算里是零头，但它证明的不是金额，是通道已经修好了。下一个纪念日，下一场国家仪式，同一根管子还在那儿。

建国250年，值得庆祝的本来是这个国家还没被掏空的部分。现在被庆祝的，是掏空它的手法足够熟练。

America's 250th birthday has been turned into a revenue line for Trump Media. TMTG earned $175,000 running ads for Freedom 250, plus another $175,000 in outstanding receivables—services rendered, money not yet collected (source: The New Republic). Where does the celebration's money come from? National Park Foundation CEO Jeff Reinbold told Congress plainly: the majority of Freedom 250's operating funds come from federal funds appropriated specifically for the 250th anniversary commemorations. Taxpayer money.

At every joint in that chain sits the same person. Freedom 250 is a Trump-created group that took over a bipartisan committee commissioned by Congress. It is housed under the National Park Foundation. And TMTG board member Meredith O'Rourke also sits on the National Park Foundation's board. Trump himself still owns 41 percent of TMTG. The money leaves the federal budget, loops around, and lands on his own balance sheet.

This isn't an exception to corruption; it's corruption's grammar. The weaponization in Chapter 3 was never only about media and religion. The most direct entry point is this: turn a public ritual into a client for your own company. Anniversary, patriotism, national memory—elsewhere these words mobilize feeling; here they are line items on a purchase order. And the event being lackluster, cheap, and gaudy does nothing to the business. The report notes sparse crowds on the National Mall's final day. Sparse crowds don't stop $175,000 from arriving. The quality of the celebration and the profit from the celebration were always two separate ledgers.

What's actually weaponized is the cognitive entry point called "the 250th." A nation's birthday should be a redistribution of public memory. It has become a private revenue channel. Who is complicit? O'Rourke, sitting on both boards, is the stitch joining the two sides. The National Park Foundation, accepting the arrangement, is the legal shell. The congressional appropriation is the transfusion line that never asks where the blood goes. Each party got its share: prestige, a seat, or an income that needs no explanation.

The report doesn't say who audits this money, or whether anyone asked if congressional funds can flow back like this. $175,000 is a rounding error in the federal budget. What it proves isn't the amount—it's that the pipe is already built. Next anniversary, next national ritual, the same pipe will still be there.

Two hundred fifty years of a country, and what was worth celebrating was the part not yet hollowed out. What got celebrated instead was how skilled the hollowing has become.
