保护欲是控制女性最古老的入口Protectionism: The Oldest Entry Point for Controlling Women

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-20 § 链接
所有以“担心女性”为起点的政治叙事，本质上都是在维护客体地位。
Any political narrative starting with 'worrying about women' inherently reinforces their status as objects.

Bridget Phillipson 的这句“我担心我们的女儿”，是典型的 paternalistic narrative。这种表达看似充满了母爱或政治关怀，但其潜意识逻辑依然是：女性（尤其是年轻女性）是需要被保护的、脆弱的客体，而权力持有者（无论是部长还是政党）是那个定义危险并提供保护的主体。

把 misogyny 归结为右翼政治和社交媒体的“ cesspit ”，是在进行一种极其廉价的 structural violence 掩盖。厌女不是某种外部侵入的“病毒”，而是 meta violence（元暴力）的自然延伸。男性中心叙事垄断了解释权，使得女性在公共空间中的主体性被系统性地抹除。社交媒体不是制造了厌女，它只是让原本就潜伏在文化层（cultural layer）的暴力变得可见且高效。

一个真正的 Just Expression 应该是：我们如何通过制度重构，让女性不再需要被“担心”或“保护”，而是拥有定义自身安全与权利的绝对解释权。如果一个女性部长的认知入口依然停留在“担心”，那么这种政治表达就只是在父权结构的边缘修补，而不是在拆除生物墙。

这种“保护主义”叙事最阴险的地方在于，它通过扮演救世主，让女性在潜意识中接受了“我是弱者”的设定，从而完成了新一轮的自我规训。在这种共谋下，权力者获得了道德高地，而女性依然被困在客体的囚笼里。

Bridget Phillipson’s phrase, “I worry about our daughters,” is a textbook paternalistic narrative. While it wears the mask of maternal or political concern, the underlying logic remains: women, especially the young, are fragile objects in need of protection, while the power-holder—be it a minister or a party—is the subject who defines danger and grants safety.

Attributing misogyny to right-wing politics and the “cesspit” of social media is a cheap way to mask structural violence. Misogyny is not some external "virus"; it is a natural extension of meta violence. The masculine-centric narrative has monopolized the right to interpret reality, systematically erasing female agency in public spaces. Social media didn't invent misogyny; it simply made the violence already latent in the cultural layer visible and efficient.

A truly Just Expression would ask: How do we restructure our systems so that women no longer need to be "worried about" or "protected," but instead hold the absolute power to define their own safety and rights? If a Minister for Women’s cognitive entry point is still rooted in "worry," her political expression is merely patching the edges of the patriarchy rather than tearing down the biological wall.

The most insidious part of this protectionist narrative is that it casts the protector as a savior, coaxing women into accepting the identity of the "weak." This is a form of complicity where the powerful claim the moral high ground, while women remain trapped in the cage of objectification.
