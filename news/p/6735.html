粉丝爱的是战争罪犯Fans Love a War Criminal

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要在洛杉矶漫展上，《阿索卡》第二季主演罗莎里奥·道森、刘承羽和埃曼·埃斯凡迪透露了新季信息：反派索龙将获得更多戏份，来自《义军崛起》的多个角色回归，剧组也更多采用实景拍摄。该剧将于2027年1月20日上线Disney Plus。
The newsAt Los Angeles Comic-Con, Ahsoka season 2 stars Rosario Dawson, Natasha Liu Bordizzo and Eman Esfandi teased more screen time for villain Thrawn, the return of several Rebels characters, and more practical outdoor sets. The series returns to Disney Plus on January 20, 2027.
「人人都爱看的战争罪犯」——英雄叙事的糖衣下，暴力被包装成魅力。
"A war criminal everybody loves to see" — the hero narrative wraps violence as charisma.

「一个大家都爱看的、非常具体的战争罪犯。」这是演员在漫展上介绍索龙时说的话。这句话不需要拆穿，它自己就把底牌亮出来了：一个帝国元帅、一个指挥过种族清洗式镇压的军事头目，被当成卖点来宣传，而全场没有人觉得这句话有任何问题。

索龙这个角色的历史本身就是一部武器化的教科书。1991年蒂莫西·赞恩把他写进《帝国传承》，迪士尼收购卢卡斯影业后宣布整个衍生宇宙非正典，2016年又请赞恩重写一本《索龙》，把这个角色重新收编进正典。一个虚构人物的「正典性」由一家公司的收购决定来裁定——谁掌握解释权，谁就决定什么是事实、什么被抹掉、什么被复活。这不是星战粉丝圈的小事，这是第三章说的「制造可能性的艺术」最干净的样本：认知入口不需要说服你相信什么，只需要决定哪些故事算数。

然后是这个角色的功能。索龙被反复强调的是「天才战术家」「魅力」「值得爱」。一个战争罪犯的魅力被精心打磨成可消费的审美对象，观众爱上的不是他的罪行，而是他被包装出来的那种冷静、优雅、智力上的优越感。这正是文化暴力的标准操作：让暴力看起来不像暴力，甚至看起来是对的、酷的、值得追的。直接暴力在这一层被洗成了人物魅力。

有意思的是同一场漫展上另一个话题：剧组从「The Volume」那块270度的LED环形屏幕搬到了户外实景。道森说在外面拍「有时非常残酷」，热到融化、冷到僵、雨天湿滑。这段话本身没什么可指摘的，演员在真实环境里工作，观众得到更真实的画面，是一笔诚实的交换。但它和索龙那条新闻放在一起，构成一个不太舒服的对照：剧组在物理上离开了那个被制造出来的环境，叙事上却更深入地走进了另一个被制造出来的环境——一个让战争罪犯变得可爱、让帝国美学变得迷人的环境。

报道里没有写的是：谁在为这套叙事付代价。不是演员，他们拿到了工作；不是迪士尼，它拿到了订阅；是那些把「索龙很酷」内化成审美判断的观众，他们的判断力被让渡出去了一部分，而且不会有任何感觉。这就是元暴力最省力的运作方式——不需要鞭子，只需要一个足够有魅力的反派，和一群自愿为他辩护的粉丝。

至于那些「回归的深受喜爱的角色」，埃斯凡迪说「大家都会非常满意」。满意这个词用得很准。它不承诺任何新的东西，只承诺你熟悉的、你已经被训练成会喜欢的东西再来一遍。

"A very specific war criminal… that everybody really loves to see." That's how the cast introduced Thrawn at the panel. The line doesn't need decoding. It lays its cards on the table: an Imperial Grand Admiral, a military commander who ran campaigns of suppression, is being sold as a selling point, and nobody in the room thought that sentence was strange.

Thrawn's own history is a textbook case of weaponization. Timothy Zahn wrote him into Heir to the Empire in 1991; Disney bought Lucasfilm and declared the Expanded Universe non-canonical; in 2016 Disney brought Zahn back to write a new Thrawn novel and folded the character into canon. A fictional person's canonicity is decided by a corporation's acquisition. Whoever holds the interpretive authority decides what counts as fact, what gets erased, what gets resurrected. This isn't fandom trivia. It's the cleanest sample of the art of manufacturing possibilities: the cognitive entrance doesn't need to convince you of anything, it only needs to decide which stories count.

Then there's the character's function. What gets repeated about Thrawn is "genius tactician," "charisma," "worth loving." A war criminal's appeal is polished into a consumable aesthetic object. What the audience falls for isn't the crimes, it's the calm, the elegance, the intellectual superiority packaged around them. This is cultural violence doing exactly its job: making violence look like it isn't violence, or better, making it look right, cool, worth following. Direct violence gets laundered into character appeal.

The same panel had another topic: the production moved from The Volume, that 270-degree LED wall, to practical outdoor sets. Dawson said shooting outside was "sometimes very brutal" — melting in heat, freezing in cold, slippery rain. Nothing wrong with that on its own. Actors working in real conditions, audiences getting a more honest image: a fair trade. But placed next to the Thrawn news it forms an uncomfortable contrast. The production physically left one manufactured environment and moved deeper into another — one where war criminals become lovable and Empire aesthetics become seductive.

What the report doesn't say is who pays for this narrative. Not the actors; they got work. Not Disney; it gets subscriptions. It's the viewers who internalize "Thrawn is cool" as an aesthetic judgment, who surrender a piece of their own discernment and never notice. That's meta-violence at its most efficient — no whip required, just a sufficiently charismatic villain and a fandom willing to defend him.

As for the returning beloved characters, Esfandi says everyone will be "really happy." Happy is the right word. It promises nothing new, only that the familiar — which you've already been trained to like — will come around again.
