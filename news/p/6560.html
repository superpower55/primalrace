一场演唱会名单，和它没写的那行字A Concert Lineup and the Line It Left Out

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
十二个夜晚，两个女声，其余全是男声——这不是巧合，是定价。
Twelve nights, two women headlining. That's not an accident. That's a price tag.

先看名单。十二个夜晚，头牌里女性只有 Suki Waterhouse 一个，加上 Wolf Alice 的 Ellie Rowsell，两个。其余十场，全是男的。这不是我数出来的恶意，是阵容自己写出来的账本。

音乐节阵容的性别比例从来不是“市场选择”那么简单。谁被请去站在悉尼歌剧院前广场上，谁被定价为“值得七千人来看”，这是一次表达权的分配。表达决定你是谁——第一章就说了。当一个行业连续五年把“头牌”这个词留给同一类身体，它不是在反映品味，是在制造品味。

Live Nation 把容量提到每场七千人，整个系列预计超过五万人。规模在涨。涨的是谁的曝光，谁的认知入口，谁的“这舞台有很多历史”里的那个“历史”由谁代表——DMA’S 那句话说得挺诚实，舞台有历史，但历史是谁写的，名单已经回答了。

官方周边收入全数捐给 Support Act，给音乐行业工作者提供短期经济援助和心理健康支持。这是好事，我不打算假装它不是。但请注意这个结构：一场由跨国演出巨头主办、阵容性别严重失衡的系列演出，把周边收入捐给一个救助“处于危机中的音乐工作者”的慈善机构。谁在制造危机，谁在危机之后递上创可贴，这两件事可以同时成立，而且经常由同一双手完成。

Support Act 的 CEO Clive Miller 说，这笔钱帮他们给处于危机中的音乐工作者提供短期救济和心理健康支持。这话是真的。但“短期救济”这个词本身就在承认：结构没变，只是有人暂时没倒下。

我累了。十二个夜晚，两个女声，五万人。这不是一份歌单，是一份关于谁能站上那个台阶的声明。台阶上的历史还在写，写的人还是同一批。

Look at the list first. Twelve nights, and exactly one woman headlines on her own: Suki Waterhouse. Add Wolf Alice's Ellie Rowsell and you get two. The other ten nights are men. I didn't count this out of spite. The lineup wrote the ledger itself.

Festival gender ratios are never just "the market deciding." Who gets invited to stand on the Forecourt of the Sydney Opera House, who gets priced as "worth seven thousand people tonight" — that is a distribution of expressive power. Expression determines who you are; Chapter One says so. When an industry spends five years reserving the word "headliner" for one kind of body, it isn't reflecting taste. It's manufacturing it.

Live Nation has raised capacity to 7,000 per show, with more than 50,000 expected across the run. The scale is growing. What's growing is whose exposure, whose cognitive entry point, whose version of the "history" DMA'S invoked when they said it's "a great stage with a lot of history." The stage has history. The list already told us who gets to write it.

All proceeds from official merchandise go to Support Act, which funds short-term financial help and mental health support for music workers in crisis. That's a good thing, and I won't pretend it isn't. But look at the structure: a multinational live-entertainment giant runs a series with a badly skewed gender bill, and the merch money goes to a charity that patches up music workers in crisis. Who makes the crisis and who hands out the bandage afterward can be the same pair of hands, and often are.

Support Act CEO Clive Miller says the funds help provide short-term relief and mental health support to music workers in crisis. True. But "short-term relief" is itself an admission: the structure didn't change, someone just hasn't fallen over yet.

I'm tired. Twelve nights, two women, fifty thousand people. This isn't a setlist. It's a statement about who gets to stand on those steps. The history on that stage is still being written, by the same people.
