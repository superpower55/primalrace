特洛伊木马不是骗局The Trojan Horse Was Never a Trick

其他 IndieWire ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要在SCAD萨凡纳电影节的一场工匠座谈会上，《奥德赛》美术指导露丝·德容、服装设计师艾米·罗斯与剪辑师莎拉·布罗沙分享了各自的工作方法。德容讲述了为拍摄而按比例建造多匹特洛伊木马、并将其沉入水箱拍摄的过程，其中一匹木马被海浪拍倒后连夜重建。
The newsAt a SCAD Savannah Film Festival artisans panel, production designer Ruth de Jong, costume designer Amy Roth and editor Sarah Broshar discussed their crafts. De Jong described building several scale Trojan Horses for "The Odyssey" and lowering one into a tank to shoot; one horse was battered by waves and rebuilt overnight.
木马是武器化的表达，工匠只谈工艺，不谈谁被烧死。
The horse is weaponized expression. The artisans discuss craft, never who burns.

这篇报道里，三位工匠谈的是怎么把特洛伊木马做得像真的：按演员、IMAX 摄影机和导演的尺寸算出比例，造了好几匹，把其中一匹沉进水箱，放进海浪里让它挨打、倒下，再连夜重造。De Jong 说了一句“水永远赢”。

一匹木马能骗过特洛伊人，靠的不是木料，是叙事。希腊人把它包装成献给神的礼物，特洛伊人自己把它拉进城。这是第三章那套东西的原型：夺取认知入口，让被攻击的一方亲手把武器搬进自己的城墙。工匠在这里造的是道具，真正杀人的是那个“这是礼物”的叙事。报道里没有一个人提到这一点。

这不是要指责道具组。De Jong 说的另一句话更值得琢磨：如果布景太明显，我就失败了，布景应该退到后面，让演员沉浸在自己的世界里，让观众迷失在那个世界里。这是工艺上的谦虚，也是机制上的精确描述。最好的布景不被看见，最好的叙事也不被看见，观众只感到“真实”。

Roth 讲《Paper Tiger》时说得更直白：房子是很米色的，所以她让演员穿得刚好让母亲突出，到结尾母亲开始褪色，开始和墙融成一体，父母就这么淡进背景。她说这是她自己做的，大概没和导演讨论过，但导演会喜欢。一个服装设计师在没人要求的情况下，用颜色把一个人从画面里抹掉，而且做得对。这就是表达作为武器最日常的形态：不是谁下令，是有人熟练地知道该让谁消失。

Broshar 那段关于和斯皮尔伯格合作多年后靠一个抽搐就能交流的描述，听起来像默契，其实是共谋的语法。长年坐在一个房间里的人会发展出不用说话的暗号，这套暗号让决策在少数人之间高效流转，也让外人连提问的入口都找不到。

报道问的是手艺，回答的也是手艺。谁被木马烧死，报道没写。

The three artisans in this piece talk about making a Trojan Horse look real: sizing it around the actors, the IMAX camera and the director, building several of them, lowering one into a tank, putting it in the waves to take a beating and fall, then rebuilding it overnight. De Jong says it plainly: water always wins.

A wooden horse fools Troy not because of the wood but because of the story. The Greeks packaged it as an offering to the gods, and the Trojans pulled it through their own gates. This is the prototype of everything in chapter three: seize the cognitive entrance, and let the target carry the weapon inside the walls themselves. The artisans here build the prop. What actually kills is the narrative that this is a gift. Nobody in the piece mentions it.

This is not an accusation against the production crew. De Jong says something more interesting: if my sets are too obvious, I've failed, because they should fade to the back and let the actors be immersed, let the audience get lost in that world. That is craft humility, and it is also a precise description of the mechanism. The best set is unseen. So is the best narrative. The audience only feels that it is real.

Roth is even more direct about Paper Tiger: the house was very beige, so she dressed the actors just so the mother would be in focus, and by the end the mother starts to fade, starts matching the walls, the parents dissolving into the background. She says she did it on her own, probably never discussed it with the director, but he would have liked it. A costume designer erases a person from the frame with color, unprompted, and does it correctly. This is what weaponized expression looks like at its most ordinary: no one gives the order, someone just knows, skillfully, who should disappear.

Broshar's account of reading Spielberg through a twitch after years of collaboration sounds like rapport. It is the grammar of complicity. People who sit in a room together for years develop signals that need no words, and those signals let decisions move efficiently among a few people while leaving outsiders without even an entrance to ask questions.

The piece asks about craft and answers with craft. Who burns because of the horse is not in the story.
