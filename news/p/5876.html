一场凶案，谁的名字被写掉了A Murder Story, and a Name That Got Overwritten

其他 直接层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
报道写了他用出生名，然后继续叫他Daniel Jones
The story notes he prefers his birth name, then keeps calling him Daniel Jones.

两条人命，六项谋杀指控，三百万保释金。这些数字都清楚。不清楚的是，一个31岁、母亲说他患有精神疾病需要服药的人，怎么拿到枪、怎么走到父亲家门口、中间那一个多小时里发生了什么。报道没写。报道写的是他车里的钱包、护照、弹药，写他脸上有伤、手和手臂受伤，写他自称是一个“湿漉漉、衣冠不整的男人”去敲陌生人的门。事实摆得很整齐，机制一个字没有。

真正刺眼的是名字。他的律师说，他更愿意用他的出生名：Asia Nguyen-Duy。报道转述了这句话，然后整篇继续叫他Daniel Jones。这不是笔误，这是编辑流程在替一个人决定他是谁。表达是存在的确证——你被叫什么，你就是什么。一个人在被控六项谋杀之后，唯一还能自己做的表达，就是说出自己希望被怎样称呼，而新闻业把这个表达降级成一句引语，放回它自己选定的名字下面。

再往上一层。父亲1975年以政治难民身份从越南来到美国，在Oberlin教了近三十年摄影，是Guggenheim fellow，是系主任。继母56岁。这个家庭跨了半个地球、跨了一代人，在美国的一所文理学院里站稳。然后儿子用枪把这一切结束了。

报道把这件事放在“校园震惊”的框架里：一位受尊敬的教授死了。这个框架没有错，但它把继母放在了括号里——Tran Thi Phong，56岁，和丈夫一起被发现，然后从叙事里淡出。一个家庭里死了两个人，报道的重心是一个人的履历。这不是记者的恶意，这是默认设置：谁的头衔更长，谁就是主角。

精神疾病这一层，报道只给了母亲的一句话：他有病，需要吃药。这句话被放在文章靠后的位置，像一条补充说明。一个需要服药的人，在枪和弹药之间，在俄勒冈的车牌和俄亥俄的停车场之间，这条路是怎么铺出来的，报道没有追问。它只负责记录结果：人死了，人被抓了，保释金定了。

最后是那个名字。如果这篇报道消失，什么差额会变大？不是那两条命——它们已经没了。是那个被反复覆盖的表达：一个人说自己叫Asia，而公共记录坚持他叫Daniel。这个差额不会因为一篇更好的报道而消失，但一篇更好的报道至少不会亲手把它扩大。

Two people dead, six counts of murder, $3m bail. Those numbers are clear. What isn't clear is how a 31-year-old whose mother says he has a mental illness and needs medication got a gun, got to his father's door, and what happened during the hour-plus in between. The report doesn't say. What it says is: wallet, passport, ammunition in the car; cuts to his face, injuries to his hand and arm; a resident reporting a "wet, disheveled man" at her front door. The facts are laid out neatly. The mechanism is absent.

The sharpest thing in the piece is the name. His lawyer says he prefers to go by his birth name, Asia Nguyen-Duy. The report relays that sentence, then spends the rest of the article calling him Daniel Jones. This isn't a typo. It's an editorial process deciding, on someone's behalf, who he is. Expression is the confirmation of existence — what you are called is what you are. After six murder charges, the one expressive act still available to a person is stating how he wants to be named, and journalism downgrades that act to a quotation, filed under the name it picked for him.

One layer up. The father came from Vietnam in 1975 as a political refugee, taught photography at Oberlin for nearly thirty years, was a Guggenheim fellow, chaired the studio art department. The stepmother was 56. This family crossed half the world and a generation, and made a place for itself at an American liberal arts college. Then a son ended it with a gun.

The report frames this as a campus in shock: a respected professor is dead. The frame isn't wrong, but it puts the stepmother in parentheses — Tran Thi Phong, 56, found with her husband, then fading out of the narrative. Two people died in that house; the story's center of gravity is one man's résumé. Not malice on the reporter's part. Default settings: whoever has the longer title is the protagonist.

The mental illness layer gets one sentence, from the mother: he's ill, he needs medication. It sits late in the piece, like a footnote. A person who needs medication, between the guns and the ammunition, between the Oregon plates and the Ohio parking lot — how that road got paved is not asked. The report records outcomes: people dead, man arrested, bail set.

And then the name. If this story vanished, what gap would widen? Not the two lives — those are gone. It's the expression that keeps getting overwritten: a person says his name is Asia, and the public record insists it is Daniel. A better report wouldn't close that gap. But it wouldn't spend its column inches widening it, either.
