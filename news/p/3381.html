香槟、狮子与蚊子：权力对事实的定价权Champagne, Lions, and Mosquitoes: The Pricing Power of Power

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
谋杀是最高效的认知封锁，而庆祝谋杀则是元暴力的极致表达。
Murder is the most efficient cognitive blockade; celebrating it is the ultimate expression of meta-violence.

在马耳他的这场审判中，最令人作呕的不是那15万欧元的买命钱，而是那瓶准备庆祝谋杀的香槟。Yorgen Fenech 这种房地产帝国继承者，在他眼中，一名揭露真相的记者不是一个活生生的人，而是一个可以通过金钱定价、用炸弹抹除的“认知障碍”。

这就是典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 运作机制：拥有资源的人不仅垄断了财富，还试图垄断“什么是事实”的解释权。当 Daphne Caruana Galizia 的报道威胁到权势者的利益时，他们不选择在公共空间进行公正的表达 (just expressions) 或博弈，而是直接通过 direct violence 将对方从物理世界中抹除。在他们看来，身体的消灭就是叙事的终结。

最耐人寻味的是证人 Theuma 的那句“他是狮子，我是蚊子”。这句话精准地勾勒出了共谋者 (complicity) 的生态位。在权力结构中，蚊子通过为狮子服务来换取生存资源，而狮子则将这种不对等关系内化为理所当然的支配权。这种“狮子与蚊子”的共谋，让谋杀变成了一次简单的商业外包，而凶手在游艇上准备香槟的姿态，则是对受害者主体性最彻底的践踏。

好在，这个差额正在缩小。当共谋者因为承受不住心理压力而倒戈，当证据从 burner phone 和录音中被还原，这种 structural violence 正在被强行拉回法律的审视之下。但我们必须警惕：杀死一个记者很容易，但要杀死那种认为“只要钱足够多，事实就可以被买断”的元暴力叙事，还需要更长久的战争。

In this Maltese trial, the most revolting detail isn't the €150,000 blood money, but the champagne prepared to celebrate a murder. For Yorgen Fenech, an heir to a property empire, a journalist uncovering the truth is not a human being, but a 'cognitive obstacle' that can be priced and erased with a bomb.

This is the textbook operation of meta-violence: those with resources monopolize not only wealth but the power to define 'what is fact.' When Daphne Caruana Galizia's revelations threatened the interests of the powerful, they didn't engage in just expressions or public debate; they used direct violence to physically excise her from the world. To them, the destruction of the body is the termination of the narrative.

Most telling is the witness Theuma's description: 'He is a lion and I am a mosquito.' This perfectly captures the ecology of complicity. In the power structure, the mosquito secures survival by serving the lion, while the lion internalizes this asymmetry as a natural right to dominate. This complicity transforms murder into a simple business outsourcing, and the act of preparing champagne on a yacht is the ultimate desecration of the victim's subjectivity.

Fortunately, the gap is closing. As complicitors buckle under guilt and evidence is recovered from burner phones and recordings, this structural violence is being dragged back into the light of the law. But we must remain vigilant: killing a journalist is easy, but dismantling the meta-violence narrative that 'facts can be bought if the price is high enough' requires a much longer war.
