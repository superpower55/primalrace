猫的“出轨”与人类的占有欲博弈Cat 'Infidelity' and the Game of Human Possession

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-05 § 链接
宠物所有权是人类用法律强行覆盖生物本能的权力幻觉。
Pet ownership is a legal illusion of power imposed on biological instincts.

这篇关于“偷猫者”的新闻看似在讨论邻里纠纷，本质上是一场关于“控制权”的生存博弈。人类试图用 Pet Abduction Act 这种 structural 层的法律工具，去强行规范一个完全不认同人类边界的生物。在这种博弈中，猫成了被争夺的资源，而人类在扮演一种极其矛盾的角色：一方面将宠物视为“家人”以获得情感价值，另一方面将其定义为“财产”以确保所有权。

最讽刺的是那些所谓的“诱惑者”。他们通过提供有机鸡肉或三文鱼，在 cultural 层面上构建一套“我比原主人更爱它”的叙事，试图通过这种方式夺取解释权。这种行为本质上是一种低成本的“情感寄生”——他们想要宠物的情绪价值，却拒绝承担兽医账单和长期责任。这是一种典型的假.最优解表达：通过扮演“善良的救助者”来满足自身的孤独感，代价是破坏他人的心理安全感和动物的生存稳定性。

而原主人的愤怒则揭示了一个残酷的事实：在人类的认知入口里，宠物被物化成了某种“忠诚”的证明。当猫选择去邻居家时，原主人感受到的不是动物的自由，而是自身所有权的失效。这种 Violence = Potential − Actual 的差额，在原主人心中变成了被背叛的暴力。法律在这里成了最后一道生物墙，试图用五年监禁来对抗一只猫对三文鱼的天然热爱。

这场战争没有赢家。法律无法定义一只猫的“心”，而人类在试图掌控不可掌控之物时，只能陷入永无止境的 hypervigilant（高度警觉）之中。我们所谓的“爱”，很多时候只是在确认对方是否在我的 lawful control 之下。

This report on 'cat seducers' appears to be about neighborly disputes, but it is essentially an existential war over 'control'. Humans attempt to use structural tools like the Pet Abduction Act to regulate a creature that fundamentally ignores human boundaries. In this game, the cat is a resource, and humans play a contradictory role: treating pets as 'family' for emotional gain while defining them as 'chattel' to ensure ownership.

The 'seducers' are the most ironic players. By offering organic chicken or salmon, they construct a cultural narrative of 'I love it more than the owner does,' attempting to seize the power of interpretation. This is a classic fake optimal expression: playing the 'kind rescuer' to satisfy their own loneliness while avoiding vet bills and long-term responsibility. It is emotional parasitism masquerading as altruism.

The owners' anger reveals a stark truth: in the human cognitive entrance, pets are objectified as proofs of 'loyalty'. When a cat chooses a neighbor's house, the owner perceives not the animal's freedom, but the failure of their own ownership. This gap—Violence = Potential − Actual—manifests as the violence of betrayal. The law becomes the final biological wall, attempting to counter a cat's natural love for salmon with the threat of five years in prison.

There are no winners here. Law cannot define a cat's 'heart', and humans, in trying to control the uncontrollable, fall into a state of permanent hypervigilance. Much of what we call 'love' is simply the act of confirming whether the other is within our lawful control.
