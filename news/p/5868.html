处决失败后的痛，谁来定价Who Prices the Pain of a Botched Killing

其他 直接层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
国家杀人失手，疼的是她的身体，被讨论的却是程序。
The state failed to kill her; the review will fix the needle, not the right to use it.

田纳西没能杀死 Christa Pike，然后她的身体成了这场失败的现场。5 克戊巴比妥推进去，执行队花了将近一小时找静脉，没找到，人活了下来。她现在住在纳什维尔的监狱医务室，之前在医院治了十天，大部分时间被单独关押。她说自己的身体疼、胳膊疼、手疼、喉咙疼，说话仍然困难。

这件事在公共讨论里被读成一次程序事故：州长 Bill Lee 叫停了后续处决，等外部审查。审查的对象是执行队的技术、是流程、是“ incompetence ”。Pike 自己也用了这个词——她说自己被这种无能深深伤害，因为它把这么多人放进了这个糟糕的位置。但被审查的从来不是“国家有没有资格把一个人绑上台子、把针扎进她的胳膊”。这一层不在审查范围里。

加尔通的算式在这里非常干净：Potential 是一个不必被扎穿身体的人，Actual 是一个被扎了几十针、在剧痛里醒来、被单独关着的人。差额就是暴力，而制造这个差额的主体是州，不是某个笨手笨脚的技术员。程序审查只能修好针头，修不好“谁有权扎”这个问题。

Pike 十八岁时杀了同学 Colleen Slemmer，1995 年。Slemmer 死了，这是不能绕过去的事实，她的家人也不在这篇报道里。但一个已经被判死刑的人，她的身体在行刑失败之后依然属于国家——国家可以决定她疼多久、关在哪里、见不见人、下一次还扎不扎。Lee 说除非法院另有裁定，她的死刑判决预计维持不变。这句话的意思是：她还得再被绑一次。

她还说了一件事，比疼痛更值得停一下：她在死囚单独监禁里过了二十八年，现在又被“远离他人、远离那些对我意义重大的人际连接”地关着。她把这种隔离称为另一种形式的痛。国家先用二十八年把她从人群里摘出去，再在失败之后把她放回同一个格子里，然后管这叫康复。

她感谢世界各地的人看见她“是一个生命有意义的人”。一个被判处死刑的人，需要靠陌生人的来信才能确认自己是人——这本身就是关于谁在定义“人”的证据。

真正的差额不在那 5 克里。在于这场审查会问针为什么扎不进去，而不会问为什么还要再扎。

Tennessee failed to kill Christa Pike, and her body became the scene of that failure. Five grams of pentobarbital went in; the execution team spent nearly an hour trying to find a vein and didn't, and she survived. She is now in the infirmary at a Nashville prison, after ten days of hospital treatment, held largely in solitary. Her body hurts, her arms hurt, her hands hurt, her throat hurts, she still struggles to speak.

The public reading of this is a procedural accident: Governor Bill Lee paused further executions pending an outside review. The review examines the team's technique, the process, the "incompetence." Pike uses that word herself—she is deeply hurt by the incompetence that put so many people in this terrible position. What is not under review is whether the state has any business strapping a person to a gurney and pushing a needle into her arm. That layer is outside the scope.

Galtung's arithmetic is clean here: the potential is a person who need not have her body punctured; the actual is a person who woke in agony after dozens of attempts, alone in a cell. The difference is violence, and the agent is the state, not some clumsy technician. A procedural review can fix the needle. It cannot fix the question of who gets to use it.

Pike killed a classmate, Colleen Slemmer, in 1995, when she was 18. Slemmer is dead; that cannot be walked around, and her family is not in this report. But a person already sentenced to death still has a body that belongs to the state after the execution fails—the state decides how long she hurts, where she is held, whom she sees, whether she gets strapped down again. Lee says he expects the death sentence to stand unless courts decide otherwise. That means she gets strapped down again.

She says something else worth stopping on: she spent the first twenty-eight years on death row in solitary, and now she is held "away from other people and the human connections that mean so much to me." She calls that isolation its own form of pain. The state pulled her out of the human world for twenty-eight years, put her back in the same box after the failure, and calls it recovery.

She thanks people around the world for seeing her as "a human being whose life matters." A person under a death sentence needing letters from strangers to confirm she is human—that is itself evidence about who gets to define "human."

The real gap is not in the five grams. It is that the review will ask why the needle wouldn't go in, and never why it goes in at all.
