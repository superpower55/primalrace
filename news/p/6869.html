复刻版保住了一款烂游戏The Remaster Kept a Bad Game Alive

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《哥斯拉：怪兽大乱斗》2002年登陆GameCube，当年评价平平，如今由Pipeworks复刻、Atari发行，加入在线多人模式。原作Metacritic 73分，复刻版72分。评测认为它保留了原作的笨拙手感与派对乐趣，但把解锁角色改成了刷研究点的长线 grind。
The newsGodzilla: Destroy All Monsters Melee, a 2002 GameCube brawler that got middling reviews, has been remastered by Pipeworks and published by Atari with online multiplayer. The original scored 73 on Metacritic; the remaster scores 72. The review praises its preserved clunky party fun but criticizes the new grind-based unlock system.
复刻的意义不是翻案，是让烂游戏照样活着
A remaster's job isn't redemption; it's letting a bad game keep existing

这篇评测最诚实的一句话是：复刻不该是给游戏翻案。73分的原作，72分的复刻，二十四年过去，分数几乎没动，这不是失败，这是复刻该有的样子。

游戏行业的默认逻辑是，只有经典才配重制。烂游戏被丢给收藏市场，变成复古展会上标价离谱的塑料盒。这套筛选不是市场自然选择，是评价体系在决定谁值得被记住。Metacritic 就是那个尺子，它量的是深度、是打磨、是“值不值”，不是“这东西有没有人真的在周末租回家玩过”。原作当年被大量当成 Blockbuster 的周末租赁，玩几个小时就见底，然后还回去。它从来没有打算成为经典，它只是存在过。

复刻版做对的事，是把这种存在原样搬回来。新贴图做得更像橡胶，打斗的笨拙感保留下来，抓取键还是那么难按，飞行怪兽还是欺负地面怪兽。这些在评测里被列为缺点，但它们恰好是这款游戏的真实形状。一个2002年的开发组还在摸索3D格斗该是什么手感，这种摸索的痕迹本身就是遗产。今天的大厂把格挡帧数打磨到科学级别，游戏变得可预测、整齐划一，反而没有这种毛边。

它做错的事只有一件，但很典型：把条件解锁换成了刷研究点。原作里你打着打着突然掉出一个新怪兽，那是个惊喜；复刻版让你重复刷无聊模式攒点数，那是把玩家的时间重新定价。这不是技术限制，是设计选择，背后是当代游戏工业对留存率和时长的迷信。复刻一款老游戏，却在最核心的奖励节奏上向现代 grind 投降，等于一边致敬一边改写。

所以这篇评测真正在说的，不是这款游戏好不好玩。它在说，保存和评价是两件事。评价体系负责排序，保存负责让东西不被排序淘汰。Atari 这次做的是后者，虽然夹带了一点前者的坏习惯。

刺在这里：如果连一款73分的游戏都要靠“它承载了一代人的杂志记忆”来论证复刻的正当性，那那些连杂志都没上过、连海报都没有的游戏呢？它们没有怀旧溢价，没有邪典身份，它们只是消失了。保存的门槛如果还是“值得被怀念”，那被保存的永远只是评价体系漏掉的那一小撮幸运儿。

The most honest line in this review is that a remaster shouldn't engineer a redemption arc. 73 for the original, 72 for the remaster, twenty-four years apart. That's not failure. That's what a remaster is supposed to look like.

The industry's default logic is that only classics deserve remasters. Bad games get abandoned to the collector market, turning into overpriced plastic at retro conventions. That filter isn't natural selection. It's a review system deciding what deserves to be remembered. Metacritic is the ruler. It measures depth, polish, whether something is "worth it" — not whether anyone actually rented it for a weekend and played it to the bottom.

The remaster does one thing right: it brings that existence back as it was. The new textures look more like rubber. The clumsiness of the brawling is preserved. The grab button is still finicky. Flying monsters still bully grounded ones. The review lists these as flaws, but they're the game's actual shape. A 2002 team was still figuring out how 3D fighting should feel, and the traces of that figuring-out are the heritage. Today's big studios polish parry frames to a scientific degree, and games become predictable and uniform. The rough edges are gone.

It does one thing wrong, and it's telling: conditional unlocks became a research-point grind. In the original, a new monster dropped out of nowhere mid-session. That was a surprise. The remaster makes you replay dull modes to accumulate points. That's repricing your time. It's not a technical limit. It's a design choice, and behind it is the modern industry's faith in retention and playtime. Remastering an old game while surrendering its reward rhythm to contemporary grind is homage and rewrite at once.

So the review isn't really about whether this game is good. It's about preservation and evaluation being two different things. Evaluation ranks. Preservation keeps things from being eliminated by ranking. Atari did the latter, with a bit of the former's bad habits smuggled in.

The sting: if even a 73-point game needs the argument that it carries a generation's magazine memories to justify its remaster, what about the games that never made it into a magazine, never got a poster? No nostalgia premium, no cult status. They just disappear. If the threshold for preservation is still "worth being remembered," then what gets preserved is always just the lucky few the evaluation system happened to miss.
