差评不是失败，没人看才是Bad Reviews Aren't the Failure. Empty Seats Are.

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
他骂的是影评人，躲的是票房。
He attacked the critics to avoid the box office.

维塔提把 28% 的影评人分数和 78% 的观众分数并排贴出来，说影评人是"可悲的迷失灵魂"，是当年也恨《乔乔的异想世界》的那批人。他漏掉了同一篇报道里更硬的一组数字：开画周末 72.5 万美元，880 家北美影院，没进前十，全球累计约 170 万美元。

这不是影评人和观众的分裂，这是没人看。把 78% 拿出来当民意授权，等于拿一个极小的分母给自己发奖状。真正把他从榜单上挤下去的，不是那 28%，是空着的座位。

"我已经快十五年没读过任何针对我或我同行的评论了。"紧接着下一句是"但我听观众的"。这两句话放在一起，是一个很干净的筛选机制：不听批评，只听掌声，然后把掌声命名为"人民"。这不是表达，这是把认知入口焊死在自己这一侧——凡是不利的评价，先取消其发言资格，再宣布它无关、脱节、过时、扔进垃圾桶。四个词连发，气势很足，功能只有一个：让解释权不落到别人手里。

这套动作在第三章的意义上很标准。一个创作者最脆弱的时候，不是被骂，是被无视；被骂至少还承认你值得评价。所以他必须把"被骂"重新叙述成"被一群失败者嫉妒"，把市场的冷淡翻译成精英的阴谋。观众分数成了他的武器，影评人成了他的敌人，而 170 万美元这个数字，从头到尾没有出现在他的 Instagram 里。

有意思的是同一篇报道里他谈 AI 的那段："只要它被人类当工具用，而不是当替代品——我不想替代人类，我想和人一起工作。"话说得漂亮。可他对影评人做的，恰恰是反过来的事：不是和人一起工作，是把提出异议的人从"人"的名单里划出去，划成"可悲的迷失灵魂"。工具和替代品的区分他讲得很清楚，轮到自己被评价时，他选择把评价者降格成工具之外的杂物。

这不是他一个人的毛病。当一个作品的公开叙事从"它好不好"滑向"谁有资格说它不好"，讨论就已经不在作品层面了。维塔提赢不了票房，但他可以赢这一场：让所有关于这部电影的对话，都先经过他那条 Instagram。

至于那 78%，它证明的只是看过的人里有一部分喜欢它。它不证明没看的人错了，更不证明批评的人不存在。银幕暗下来的时候，坐满的厅和空着的厅，用的是同一套放映机。

Waititi put the 28 percent critics score next to the 78 percent audience score and called the critics "sad lost souls" — the same people, he says, who hated Jojo Rabbit. He left out the harder numbers in the same report: $725,000 opening weekend, 880 North American theaters, outside the top 10, roughly $1.7 million worldwide.

This isn't a split between critics and audiences. This is nobody showing up. Holding up 78 percent as a mandate means handing yourself a certificate with a tiny denominator. What pushed him off the chart wasn't the 28 percent. It was the empty seats.

"I haven't read a review of anything aimed at me or my peers in almost 15 years. But I do listen to the people." Two sentences, one clean filter: ignore criticism, hear applause, then rename the applause "the people." That isn't expression. That's welding the cognitive entrance shut on your own side — disqualify the unfavorable judgment first, then declare it irrelevant, out of touch, obsolete, in the bin. Four words in a row, plenty of force, one function: keep the interpretive authority from landing anywhere but his own hands.

The move is textbook. A creator's most fragile moment isn't being attacked; it's being ignored. Being attacked at least concedes you were worth evaluating. So he has to re-narrate "attacked" as "envied by failures," and translate a cold market into an elite conspiracy. The audience score becomes his weapon, the critics become his enemy, and the $1.7 million never appears on his Instagram.

What's telling is his AI quote in the same piece: "as long as it's used by humans as a tool, as opposed to a replacement — I don't want to replace humans. I want to work with them." Nicely put. But what he does to the critics is the exact inverse: not working with people, but striking the people who disagree off the list of people altogether, reclassifying them as "sad lost souls." He can draw the line between tool and replacement with precision. When the evaluation turns on him, he demotes the evaluators to clutter.

This isn't one man's quirk. When the public story about a work slides from "is it good" to "who is allowed to say it isn't," the discussion has already left the work behind. Waititi can't win the box office, but he can win this: make every conversation about the film pass through his Instagram first.

As for the 78 percent, all it proves is that some of the people who saw it liked it. It doesn't prove the ones who stayed home were wrong, and it certainly doesn't prove the critics don't exist. When the lights go down, the full house and the empty house run on the same projector.
