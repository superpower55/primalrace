冻结一半，也算胜利？Half Frozen Is Still a Heist

科技 结构层 · 文化层 WIRED ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
被冻结的是稳定币，逃掉的是真钱。
The stablecoins got frozen. The real money walked.

六十三亿美元，二十分钟。真正值得看的不是这个速度，是事后那套叙事：Binance 说会覆盖所有损失，追踪公司说已经冻结了大约一半。冻结的是稳定币，大约二十八亿美元，占总额百分之四十四；剩下三十三亿是比特币、以太坊和 XRP，没人能冻结，只能等它撞上某个交易所。这叫追回吗？这叫把能堵的口堵上，然后宣布堵住了一半。

钱不会消失，只会换手。Binance 说会覆盖所有损失，这句话在公关层面成立，在结构层面是另一回事：损失被覆盖了，但被谁覆盖？被这个交易所的资产负债表，被它继续运转所需的信任。用户看到的是“我的钱还在”，看不到的是这笔钱从哪个池子里挪出来补上。这是 structural violence 的典型操作：把一次系统性失守翻译成一次客服事件。

再看那二十分钟是怎么来的。TRM 说准备至少提前三十六小时，先在两个钱包上试代码，在第三个钱包上彩排，把测试资金转回自己，四小时之后同一套代码装进了 Binance 的热钱包。这不是天才，这是耐心。一个工程师的笔记本电脑被攻破，然后整条链上的钱包被抽干。安全叙事总爱把攻击写成“高深”，因为高深意味着普通人无能为力，也意味着责任不在自己。可这里的入口朴素得近乎羞辱：一台笔记本。

报道里最诚实的一句话来自 Elliptic 的 Robinson：剩下的三十三亿“没人能冻结”，只能等它到达某个交易所或服务。这句话把整条产业链的底牌翻了过来——所谓加密世界的去中心化，在追赃这件事上变成了去中心化的无能。冻结能力集中在少数几个能配合的机构手里，而它们只冻得住稳定币。比特币、以太坊、XRP 继续流动， laundering patterns 是“以前没见过的”，几千笔交易快速处理，靠自动化抹掉痕迹。谁在共谋？不是某个坏人，是一整套设计：可冻结的资产被冻结，不可冻结的资产被设计成不可冻结，然后双方都宣布自己尽了力。

朝鲜这个归因要小心。三家追踪公司说“可能”，Elliptic 说没有具体绑定，只看到活动重叠；Binance 和执法部门没有点名任何人。报道写得很清楚，归因是追踪公司的判断，不是定罪。但归因一旦落地，叙事就完成了：一个被制裁的政权，一群精英黑客，一次史上最大的盗窃。这个框架很好用，它把一次交易所的安全失败，转写成一场国家对国家的战争。战争叙事里没有用户，只有敌人。

真正的问题在报道的缝隙里：Binance 没有回答它是否认为朝鲜黑客是幕后黑手。它回答了会覆盖损失，回答了正在配合执法，没有回答归因。这个沉默比任何声明都准确——归因是别人的事，损失是自己的事，而损失已经被写进了“我们会覆盖”这句话里。

六十三亿里被冻住的二十八亿，是这次事件里唯一具体缩小的差额。剩下的三十三亿还在路上，等着撞上某个交易所，或者等着被自动化洗成谁也认不出的形状。而那句“我们会覆盖所有损失”，覆盖的是账本，不是那二十分钟里被证明可以轻易拿走的东西。

Six point three billion dollars in twenty minutes. The speed is not the story. The story is the narrative that followed: Binance says it will cover all losses, the tracing firms say roughly half has been frozen. What got frozen was stablecoins—about 2.8 billion, 44 percent of the total. The remaining 3.3 billion is Bitcoin, Ether, and XRP, which nobody can freeze. You wait for it to hit an exchange. That is not recovery. That is plugging the holes you can plug and announcing you plugged half.

Money does not vanish. It changes hands. Binance saying it will cover all losses is true at the level of PR and false at the level of structure: the loss is covered, but by whom? By the exchange's balance sheet, by the trust it needs to keep operating. Users see their money is still there. They do not see which pool it was moved from. This is structural violence doing its usual trick—translating a systemic failure into a customer-service event.

Now look at how the twenty minutes were built. TRM says preparation began at least 36 hours earlier: code tested on two wallets, rehearsed on a third, test funds routed back, and four hours after the rehearsal the same code was installed on a Binance hot wallet. This is not genius. This is patience. One compromised engineer's laptop, and wallets across at least ten blockchains get drained. Security narratives love to call attacks sophisticated, because sophisticated means the ordinary person is helpless and the responsibility is elsewhere. The entry point here is almost insulting in its simplicity: a laptop.

The most honest line in the piece comes from Elliptic's Robinson: the remaining 3.3 billion cannot be frozen by anyone, only stopped when it reaches an exchange or service. That flips the whole industry's cards. The decentralization of crypto becomes, in the matter of recovering stolen funds, a decentralization of impotence. Freezing power sits with a few institutions willing to cooperate, and they can only freeze stablecoins. Bitcoin, Ether, XRP keep moving, with laundering patterns Chainalysis says it has not seen before, thousands of transactions processed fast, automation erasing the trail. Who is complicit? Not some villain. A design: freezable assets get frozen, unfreezable assets are built to be unfreezable, and both sides announce they did their best.

Be careful with the North Korea attribution. Three tracing firms say likely; Elliptic says it has not specifically tied the group, only sees overlaps; Binance and law enforcement have named no one. The reporting is clear that attribution is the firms' judgment, not a conviction. But once attribution lands, the narrative completes itself: a sanctioned regime, elite hackers, the largest heist ever. That frame is useful. It rewrites an exchange's security failure as a state-versus-state war. In war narratives there are no users, only enemies.

The real question sits in the gap: Binance did not answer whether it believes North Korean hackers are behind the heist. It answered that it will cover losses, that it is working with law enforcement. It did not answer attribution. That silence is more precise than any statement—attribution is someone else's business, the loss is its own, and the loss has already been written into the sentence we will cover it.

The 2.8 billion frozen is the one差额 this story actually narrows. The other 3.3 billion is still in motion, waiting to hit an exchange or to be automated into a shape no one recognizes. And we will cover all losses covers the ledger, not the twenty minutes that proved how easily it could all be taken.
