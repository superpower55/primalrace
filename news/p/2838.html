在好莱坞的A-list名单里，女性永远是被“propel”的客体In Hollywood's A-list, Women are Always the 'Propelled' Objects

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-24 § 链接
所谓的“造星之功”，本质上是男性导演对女性客体化表达的定价权。
The so-called 'star-making power' is essentially the pricing power of male directors over the objectification of women.

看到《卫报》用 "helped propel" 来描述查克·罗素对金·凯莉、卡梅隆·迪亚兹等人的贡献时，这种典型的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 简直在跳舞。在好莱坞的权力结构里，导演被塑造为“造物主”，而演员——尤其是女性演员——则是被他“推进”到 A-list 的原材料。这种叙事抹杀了女性在博弈中的主体性，将她们的存在性简化为被男性权力激活的产物。

回看罗素的履历，从《噩梦街》到《面具》，他最擅长的是在恐怖与喜剧的边界上操纵视觉冲击。这种操纵不仅是对胶片的，更是对认知的。他把《面具》从恐怖片改为商业大片，赢了制片厂的博弈，获得了巨大的商业成功。但这种成功是建立在一种特定的 weaponized 表达之上的：女性角色在这些电影中，要么是需要被拯救的客体，要么是作为男性权力欲望的注脚。她们的“走红”，在元暴力的逻辑下，不过是符合了当时男性观众的审美最优解。

这种“造星”叙事掩盖了结构性的共谋 (complicity)。制片厂、导演、经纪人共同构建了一套定价体系，决定了什么样的女性表达能获得通行证。当新闻将一个导演的死亡定义为“一个造星者的离去”时，它再次强化了这样一个潜意识：女性的成功取决于哪个男性决定给她们一个机会。这正是文化暴力最隐蔽的地方——它让你觉得这种被支配的成功是某种恩赐。

When The Guardian uses the phrase "helped propel" to describe Chuck Russell's contribution to stars like Jim Carrey and Cameron Diaz, this typical masculine-centric narrative is practically dancing. In the power structure of Hollywood, the director is cast as the 'Creator,' while the actors—especially women—are merely the raw materials being 'propelled' into the A-list. This narrative erases female agency in the game, reducing their existence to a product activated by male power.

Looking at Russell's career, from A Nightmare on Elm Street to The Mask, he excelled at manipulating visual impact on the boundary between horror and comedy. This manipulation wasn't just of film, but of cognition. By pivoting The Mask from horror to a blockbuster, he won the gamble with the studio and achieved massive commercial success. But this success was built on a specific weaponized expression: female characters in these films are either objects to be rescued or footnotes to male desire. Their 'stardom,' under the logic of meta-violence, was simply the result of fitting the aesthetic optimal expression for male audiences of the time.

This 'star-making' narrative masks a structural complicity. Studios, directors, and agents collectively built a pricing system that decided which forms of female expression were granted a passport to success. When a director's death is defined as the loss of a 'star-maker,' it reinforces the subconscious belief that a woman's success depends on which man decides to give her a chance. This is exactly where cultural violence is most insidious—it frames this dominated success as a form of benevolence.
