格莱美扩招，换的是尺子The Grammys Grew the Voting Pool, Not the Ruler

其他 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要美国录音学院7月9日向4000多名音乐创作者和业内人士发出入会邀请，为迄今规模最大的一批。其中3100多人为有投票权会员，900多人为非投票专业会员；受邀者中50%在40岁以下，43%为女性，55%为有色人种。
The newsThe Recording Academy sent invitations on July 9 to more than 4,000 music creators and industry professionals, its largest class to date. Over 3,100 were invited as voting members and more than 900 as non-voting professional members; 50% are under 40, 43% are women and 55% are people of color.
人数翻倍不等于解释权换手
More voters is not the same as a new definition of the award

格莱美把投票会员扩到史上最大一批，四千多人，其中三千一百多人有投票权。报道说得很清楚：这是为了增加女性、有色人种和四十岁以下成员的比例，43% 是女性，55% 是有色人种，一半不到四十岁。数字是真的，方向也是真的。但先问一句：改变的是能力，还是评价方式？

一个奖项的投票池扩大，改的是谁有票，不改的是票投给什么。格莱美奖的类别、评审规则、报名窗口、电视转播的时段分配，这些才是定价的地方。报道里唯一提到规则的地方是报名流程的日期，其余全是人数。人数是结构层的入口，但入口不等于门里面的房间被重新分配了。

报道自己给了最有说服力的证据：2017 年 Adele 横扫，2018 年 Bruno Mars 横扫，2019 年 Childish Gambino 的《This Is America》成为第一首拿下年度制作和年度歌曲的嘻哈作品，2025 年 Kendrick Lamar 的《Not Like Us》是第二首，2026 年 Bad Bunny 成为第一张拿下年度专辑的西班牙语专辑。这些变化发生在会员模型推出前后，报道把它归因于投票池的变化。可以这么读，也可以反过来读：一个西班牙语专辑拿年度专辑，是拉美市场大到不能忽视的结果，不是四千张新票的良心发现。市场先动，奖项后跟。

真正值得看的是谁被邀请。报道列了名字：EJAE、Lola Young、Sombr、Paris Jackson，还有 Griff、Cash Cobain、Sampa the Great、Ela Taubert 等等。这些人的共同点不是“被边缘化”，是“已经在榜上”。Paris Jackson 的头衔是“ budding artist 和十三次格莱美得主的女儿”，报道自己把父系写进了身份说明。这不是丑闻，这是机制：邀请的是已经被产业承认的人，不是还没被承认的人。

所以这条新闻的暴力层在结构，不在文化。结构层的暴力是资源分配，格莱美把投票权发给了更多女性、更多有色人种、更多年轻人，这是实打实的资源再分配。但结构暴力被削掉多少，要看这批人进去之后能不能改类别设置、能不能改评审权重、能不能决定哪些音乐被放进“主要”类别。报道说，2026 年新会员班的完整报告要在今年晚些时候才发布，包含整体会员的人口结构和工种细分。也就是说，现在能确认的只有邀请人数，不能确认的是这些人进去之后有没有动到规则。

报道里有一个细节很能说明问题：格莱美现在管 Harvey Mason jr. 叫“Grammys CEO”。一个奖项组织把自己的人叫 CEO，是在用公司的语言说自己。公司语言的逻辑是增长、是用户、是市场覆盖。四千人的新会员班，在这个逻辑里是用户增长，不是解释权转移。

这不是说扩招是假的。四千多张票投进去，投票池的构成确实变了，某些类别的结果可能真的会不一样。但奖项的解释权不在投票池里，在谁定义“年度专辑”该颁给什么样的作品。Bad Bunny 拿奖，可以是拉美市场赢了，也可以是格莱美学会了把拉美市场算进“年度”这个词里。这两件事看起来一样，实际上差很远。

报道没写的是：这四千多人里，有多少人真的会去投类别奖，有多少人只投四大通类，有多少人接了邀请但从不参与报名流程。报道也没写这四千多人里有多少是词曲作者、多少是制作人、多少是厂牌员工。这些数字要等今年晚些时候的报告。在那之前，唯一能确定的是邀请函发出去了，票还没投。

The Recording Academy is sending invitations to more than 4,000 music creators and industry professionals, its largest class ever, with more than 3,100 invited as voting members. The report is explicit about the goal: expand and diversify membership. 43% women, 55% people of color, half under 40. The numbers are real. So is the direction. But the first question is not how many people got a ballot. It is whether the ballot is the thing that decides what the award means.

A bigger voting pool changes who votes. It does not change what the vote is for. The categories, the eligibility rules, the entry window, the televised slots — those are where the pricing happens. The only rule mentioned in the report is a date: the Online Entry Process opened July 7 and runs through Aug. 21. Everything else is headcount. Headcount is the door. It is not the room behind the door.

The report supplies its own best evidence. Adele swept album, record and song of the year in 2017; Bruno Mars swept the same three in 2018. Then Childish Gambino's “This Is America” became the first hip-hop hit to win record and song of the year in 2019. Kendrick Lamar's “Not Like Us” became the second in 2025. Bad Bunny's Debí Tirar Más Fotos became the first Spanish-language album to win album of the year in 2026. The report frames this as a shift in voting patterns following the new membership model. It can also be read the other way: a Spanish-language album winning album of the year is what happens when the Latin market gets too large to ignore. The market moved first. The award followed.

What matters is who got invited. The report names EJAE, Lola Young, Sombr, Paris Jackson, plus Griff, Cash Cobain, Sampa the Great, Ela Taubert and others. Their common trait is not marginalization. It is that they are already on the board. Paris Jackson is described as “a budding artist and the daughter of 13-time Grammy winner Michael Jackson.” The report itself writes the father into the credential. That is not a scandal. It is the mechanism: the Academy invites people the industry has already recognized, not people it has not.

The violence here sits in the structural layer, not the cultural one. Structural violence is resource allocation, and handing voting rights to more women, more people of color, more young people is a real reallocation. But how much of that violence gets cut depends on whether the new members can change category structure, voting weights, and which music gets sorted into the “major” categories. The report says a comprehensive breakdown of the 2026 class and overall membership demographics will be released later this year. Until then, the only confirmed fact is the invitations. What the invitees do inside is unconfirmed.

One detail is telling: the Academy now calls Harvey Mason jr. the “Grammys CEO.” An awards body calling its head a CEO is speaking the language of a company. Company language is growth, users, market coverage. A class of 4,000 is user growth in that language. It is not a transfer of interpretive power.

None of this makes the expansion fake. Four thousand ballots do change the composition of the pool, and some categories may genuinely produce different results. But the power to define what album of the year is does not live in the pool. It lives in whoever decides what kind of record counts as an album of the year. Bad Bunny winning can mean the Latin market won. It can also mean the Grammys learned to count the Latin market inside the word “year.” Those look identical from the outside. They are not.

What the report does not say: how many of the 4,000 will actually vote in category races, how many will only vote the big four, how many will accept the invitation and never touch the entry process. It does not break down how many are songwriters, producers, label staff. Those numbers wait for the later report. Until then, the only thing confirmed is that the invitations went out. The votes have not been cast.
