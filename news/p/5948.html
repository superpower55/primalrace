道歉的是她，被拿走的狗没人道歉She Apologized. Nobody Apologized for Taking Her Dog.

文娱 结构层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
一个病人的身体被当成罪证，狗被当成物证。
A sick body gets processed as evidence; her service dog gets booked as property.

Selma Blair 道了歉，为「花了这么久」道歉。真正该被追问的是：一个多发性硬化症患者在空中发作，为什么流程里没有一格叫「医疗紧急情况」，只有一格叫「袭击乘务员」。

报道写得很清楚：她被控在达美航班上咬了一名乘务员、打了另一名，被带下飞机后又打了警察和护士，10 月 12 日在纽约法庭对两项袭击指控作无罪答辩。她的发言人说的是另一套事实：她在飞机上经历了医疗紧急情况，她没有试图打开舱门，她每天带着疼痛生活，还患有多发性硬化症继发的假性延髓情绪（pseudobulbar affect）——这种病的表现就是情绪和动作不受控，在不知情的人眼里，正好长得像「发疯」。

两套事实摆在一起，不是谁在撒谎，是只有一套能被写进笔录。假性延髓情绪不会说话，它只能通过一个正在痉挛、哭泣、失控的身体表达自己，而航空安保和执法系统读不懂这种表达，它只读得懂「威胁」和「制服」。这就是 structural violence 最日常的样子：制度不是故意要害她，制度只是没给她留位置。

然后是最刺眼的一笔：当局把她的服务犬带走了。报道只写了这一句，没写狗后来怎么样、什么时候还、依据是什么。一只受过训练、寸步不离的服务犬，在程序里不是医疗设备，是「现场物品」。一个人最基础的行动支持被从她身边拿走，理由不需要写进新闻，因为根本不需要理由。

她道歉了。她说她从未想过伤害或惊吓任何人，说她现在要照顾自己的健康和儿子。她为「花了这么久」道歉，为「让一些人失望」道歉。一个在飞机上失去身体控制的人，醒来后第一件事是替这套读不懂她的系统收拾情绪。

这不是说袭击指控一定是错的，法律程序会走。但一个社会怎么对待发作中的病人，和病人事后怎么道歉，是两件事。前者是制度的选择，后者是她的选择。报道里所有人都在，律师、发言人、警察、护士、法庭——唯独没有一个人需要为「把狗带走」这件事出面说一句话。

她为惊吓了别人道歉。没人需要为惊吓了她道歉。

Selma Blair apologized for taking so long. The question worth asking is why a woman with multiple sclerosis having a medical episode in the air has no box to check labeled "medical emergency," only one labeled "assault on crew."

The report is precise: she was accused of biting one flight attendant and punching another, and of striking a police officer and a nurse after being removed from the plane. On Oct. 12 in a New York courtroom she pleaded not guilty to two counts of assault. Her spokesperson tells a different set of facts: she experienced a medical emergency on the flight, there was no attempt to open the aircraft door, she lives with pain daily, and she has pseudobulbar affect, a neurological condition secondary to her MS that bears directly on how an episode presents to people who don't know her — which is to say, it looks exactly like losing it.

These aren't two versions where one is lying. They're two versions where only one can be written into a report. Pseudobulbar affect cannot speak for itself; it can only express through a body that is seizing, crying, out of control. Airport security and law enforcement don't read that expression. They read threat, and they read restraint.

Then the detail that should stop you: the authorities took her service dog. The report gives one sentence and no follow-up — not where the dog went, not when it comes back, not on what grounds. A trained service animal that never leaves her side is, in the procedure, a piece of property at the scene. The support that makes her functional gets removed from her, and no one has to justify it, because no one is asked.

So she apologized. She said she never intended to hurt or frighten anyone, that she is now going to take care of her health and her son. She apologized for taking so long, for disappointing people. A person who lost control of her body mid-flight woke up and did the emotional cleanup for a system that cannot read her.

None of this settles the assault charges; the legal process will run. But how a society treats a person mid-episode and how that person apologizes afterward are two different things. The first is the institution's choice. The second is hers. The report has lawyers, a spokesperson, police, a nurse, a courtroom — and not one person who has to answer for taking the dog.

She apologized for frightening people. Nobody has to apologize for frightening her.
