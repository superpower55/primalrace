围城里的六千一百个孕妇Six Thousand One Hundred Pregnant Women Behind the Siege

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要也门国际承认政府称，其部队重新夺回了焦夫省首府哈兹姆以东的军事阵地和战略高地，并缴获武器弹药库。胡塞武装则表示已挫败政府军在焦夫和马里布的行动。自8月以来，超过29万人因战事升级逃离家园。
The newsYemen's internationally recognised government said its forces recaptured military positions and strategic heights east of al-Hazm, the capital of al-Jawf, seizing weapons and ammunition depots. The Houthis said they thwarted advances in al-Jawf and Marib. More than 290,000 people have fled their homes since fighting escalated in August.
战略高地易手，被围的孕妇连两周都等不到。
Strategic heights change hands; the besieged pregnant women can't wait two weeks.

al-Jawf 的军火库换了手，塔伊兹的产房没有换到一箱燃料。这就是这条新闻的全部结构：直接暴力在前线记账，结构暴力在围城里慢慢结算，文化暴力负责把后者写成背景。

政府军说 1st Emergency Brigade 夺回 al-Hazm 以东的阵地和制高点，缴获武器弹药，打死打伤并俘获胡塞武装人员，摧毁装甲车；胡塞说他们挫败了政府军在 al-Jawf 和邻近 Marib 的推进。双方各发布一份战报，谁在赢，读者无从核实。核实得了的是联合国人道协调厅的数字：八月战事升级以来，超过 29 万人逃离家园。

塔伊兹没有被胡塞控制，但胡塞握着进出它的关键道路。医院被拖垮，食物、燃料、药品的运送被限制，平民在周边山头的狙击火力下连离开都难。围城不需要攻进去——控制道路就够了。这是结构暴力最干净的形态：没有一个具体的施暴者站在产房门口，但补给进不来，产妇出不去。

联合国人口基金说，近期流离失所的人里有近 6100 名孕妇，其中 600 人预计在简报后两周内分娩。两周。这 600 个分娩不构成任何一方的战果，不会出现在 X 上的战报里，也不会出现在“Operation Dawn of Yemen”的战况总结里。她们是这场博弈里最没有议价权的一方，连被当作筹码的资格都没有，只是被路过。

报道说，也门媒体发布的视频显示，第一批医疗援助从 Marib 运抵，支援伤者、流离失所的家庭和其他居民；后续物资被道路封闭挡住。第一批到了，第二批卡在路上。人道主义援助的叙事在这里露出了它的结构：援助是一条可以被切断的线，而切断它的人不需要为切断负责，因为封锁被写成“路况”。

还有 65000 多名在也门的难民和寻求庇护者，服务中断、粮价油价上涨，处境在恶化。他们连“也门人”这个身份都不占，在交战双方的账本上，他们是零。

这篇报道里没有一个女性开口说话。6100 和 600 是人口基金给的统计，不是她们的声音。她们被计数，不被引用——这正是原初种族的常规待遇：作为数字出现在人道简报里，作为背景出现在战报的第三段之后。

战报会继续更新，高地会继续易手。那 600 个两周内要生的女人，需要的不是谁夺回了 al-Hazm 以东的制高点，而是一条能通车的路。这条路不在任何一方的“战略”里。

The weapons depots in al-Jawf changed hands. The maternity ward in Taiz did not get a shipment of fuel. That is the whole structure of this story: direct violence keeps the books at the front, structural violence settles slowly inside the siege, and cultural violence files the latter under background.

Government forces say the 1st Emergency Brigade recaptured positions and strategic heights east of al-Hazm, seized weapons and ammunition depots, wounded, killed and captured Houthi fighters, and destroyed armoured vehicles. The Houthis say they thwarted government advances in al-Jawf and neighbouring Marib. Two communiqués, and the reader has no way to check either. What can be checked is the UN humanitarian office's figure: more than 290,000 people have fled their homes since fighting escalated in August.

Taiz is not held by the Houthis, but they hold the key routes in and out of it. Hospitals are strained, deliveries of food, fuel and medical supplies are restricted, and civilians struggle to leave under reported sniper fire from the surrounding hillsides. A siege does not require entering the city. Controlling the roads is enough. This is structural violence in its cleanest form: no single perpetrator stands at the maternity ward door, but supplies cannot come in and labouring women cannot get out.

The UN Population Fund says nearly 6,100 pregnant women are among those recently displaced, including 600 expected to give birth within two weeks of Friday's briefing. Two weeks. Those 600 births count as no one's victory. They will not appear in a post on X, nor in the tally of Operation Dawn of Yemen. They are the party with the least bargaining power in this game — not even valuable enough to be used as a chip, merely passed through.

Yemeni media published videos of what they described as the first shipment of medical aid arriving from Marib for the wounded, displaced families and other residents; further shipments were reportedly held up by road closures. The first batch arrived, the second is stuck on the road. Humanitarian aid reveals its structure here: it is a line that can be cut, and whoever cuts it bears no responsibility, because a blockade gets written up as road conditions.

More than 65,000 refugees and asylum seekers in Yemen face worsening conditions as services are disrupted and food and fuel prices rise. They do not even hold the status of Yemeni. On both sides' ledgers, they are zero.

No woman speaks in this report. The 6,100 and the 600 are statistics from a population fund, not voices. They are counted, not quoted — the standard treatment of the Primal Race: a number in a humanitarian briefing, background after the third paragraph of a war report.

The communiqués will keep updating; the heights will keep changing hands. Those 600 women giving birth within two weeks do not need anyone to recapture the strategic heights east of al-Hazm. They need one road that cars can drive on. That road is not in anyone's strategy.
