精神病院的鬼魂只是在给 structural violence 做陪衬The Ghosts in the Asylum are Mere Extras to Structural Violence

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
真正的恐怖不是超自然恶灵，而是被制度化了的男性中心暴力。
True horror isn't supernatural spirits, but the institutionalized masculine-centric violence.

这部剧试图用一个超自然恶灵来包装精神病院的恐怖，但真正让人脊背发凉的不是那个在走廊里飘荡的鬼，而是那个把人变成客体、彻底剥夺其主体性的 US mental healthcare system。这其实是一场关于“定义权”的战争：谁有权定义一个人是“疯了”的？谁有权决定谁该被关在银色大门之后？

剧中 Dorry 的遭遇是典型的 meta violence 样本。一个女性因为“不停地哭泣”就被丈夫送进精神病院，这在父权结构中简直是标准操作——当女性的表达不再符合男性定义的“温柔/稳定”时，这种表达就被标记为“病态”，进而通过医疗制度被物理性地抹除。在这种叙事里，精神病院不是治疗场所，而是一个存放“不听话女性”的仓库。 lobotomy（额叶切除术）则是这种暴力的极致：通过物理摧毁大脑，强制性地完成对主体性的阉割。

至于 Pepper 被轻易地卷入系统，药物控制和行政疏忽让一个“好人”瞬间变成囚徒，这揭示了 structural violence 的高效与冷漠。系统并不需要一个具体的恶魔，它只需要一套运行良好的流程——药物、锁门、缺失的文书。在这种机制下，每个人都是共谋者：那些被环境困住的护士，以及那些通过定义他人之“疯”来确认自身“正常”的权力持有者。

最讽刺的是，剧集把怪物作为隐喻。但事实上，这种隐喻是多余的。当一个社会能如此轻而易举地将边缘人群（有色人种、贫穷者、不顺从的女性）定义为“需要被管理”的客体时，这个社会本身就是那个最巨大的、不可名状的 monster。超自然恐怖只是给观众提供了一次廉价的惊吓，而真正的恐怖是：你可能在不知不觉中，就成了那个被锁在银色大门后的人。

The show tries to wrap the horror of a psychiatric hospital in a supernatural spirit, but the real chill comes not from the ghost haunting the corridors, but from the US mental healthcare system—a machine that objectifies humans and strips them of their subjectivity. This is essentially a war over the 'right to define': who has the power to label someone as 'insane'? Who decides who stays behind the locked silver door?

Dorry's arc is a textbook case of meta violence. A woman committed by her husband for 'endless crying fits' is standard operating procedure in a patriarchal structure—when a woman's expression no longer fits the masculine definition of 'gentle/stable,' it is labeled as 'pathological' and physically erased via medical institutions. In this narrative, the asylum is not a place of healing, but a warehouse for 'disobedient women.' The transorbital lobotomy is the apex of this violence: a forced castration of subjectivity through physical brain destruction.

Pepper's effortless entrapment in the system—drugged and lost in administrative negligence—reveals the efficiency and indifference of structural violence. The system doesn't need a specific villain; it only needs a functioning process: drugs, locked doors, and missing paperwork. In this mechanism, everyone is a complicitor: the nurses trapped by circumstance, and the power-holders who confirm their own 'sanity' by defining others as 'mad.'

It is ironic that the show uses the monster as a metaphor. In fact, such metaphors are redundant. When a society can so easily define marginalized groups—people of color, the poor, non-compliant women—as objects 'to be managed,' the society itself is the greatest, most indescribable monster. Supernatural horror provides only a cheap jump scare; the real horror is the realization that you could, unnoticed, become the one locked behind that silver door.
