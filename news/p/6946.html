谁在给未来定价Who Prices the Future

其他 文化层 CNBC ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要高盛发布11月欧洲「确信名单」，新增德意志银行与嘉士伯，并给出目标价：德意志银行44.25欧元、隐含47%上行空间，嘉士伯1200丹麦克朗、隐含24%上行空间。名单中Ceres Power、flatexDEGIRO、莱茵金属的上行空间分别被标为250%、112%和102%。同时移出汇丰、Haleon与CTP。
The newsGoldman Sachs added Deutsche Bank and Carlsberg to its November European Conviction List, with target prices implying 47% and 24% upside respectively. Ceres Power, flatexDEGIRO and Rheinmetall were assigned upside of 250%, 112% and 102%. HSBC, Haleon and CTP were removed from the list.
价格目标是一种叙事，不是事实。
A price target is a narrative, not a fact.

高盛给德意志银行定了个44.25欧元的目标价，给嘉士伯定了个1200丹麦克朗。数字看起来精确到小数点，但这不是预测，是定价权的展示：我说它值多少，它就朝那个方向走一段。

德意志银行那一段里，真正的增长点被一句话带过——德国政府的基础设施支出，重点机会在国防和科技。翻译一下：财政扩张的钱先流进银行，再流进军工。莱茵金属被留在名单上，目标价2100欧元，隐含102%的上行空间，和德意志银行的「国防机会」是同一笔账的两头。

这套叙事里没有受害者，因为受害者不在名单上。军费扩张的代价、基础设施支出的钱从哪来、谁的教育和医疗被挤掉，这些不进分析师的模型。模型里只有return on tangible equity从多少升到13%以上，只有回购。

嘉士伯那段更干净。啤酒在中国卖不动，就用软饮补，靠的是收购Britvic和百事可乐的灌装授权。利润率的来源不是酿得更好，是拿到了别人的牌照。至于印度IPO、北欧授权变更、和札幌的合作，全是「潜在上行」。潜在的意思就是还没发生，但已经计入价格。

「确信名单」这个词本身值得停一下。它把一份买方报告包装成信念，把评级包装成信仰。移出汇丰的理由写得挺诚实：美国商业地产的风险敞口砍了40%。也就是说，之前推荐的时候，这个敞口是在的。

整篇报道里没有一个具体的人在承受代价，只有公司在被加减。这恰好是这类文本的性质：它把世界写成一张可以买卖的清单，清单之外的一切都不存在。

所以我不评论这条新闻的好坏，我指出它的位置：它在制造「什么是事实」的那一层工作。目标价一旦发布，就会有人按它行动，行动又让价格靠近目标价，于是预测自我实现，然后被引用为预测准确的证据。

真正的问题不是高盛看对了没有，是谁有资格把一句话写进别人的决策里，而写这句话的人不承担写错的后果。

Goldman puts a 44.25-euro target on Deutsche Bank and 1,200 Danish kroner on Carlsberg. The numbers look precise to the decimal, but this isn't forecasting. It's a display of pricing power: I say what it's worth, and it moves that way for a while.

The Deutsche Bank paragraph buries the real growth driver in one clause: German government infrastructure spending, with key opportunities in defense and technology. Translated: fiscal expansion flows into banks first, then into arms. Rheinmetall stays on the list with a 2,100-euro target and 102% implied upside — the same ledger, other end.

No victims appear in this narrative, because victims aren't on the list. The cost of military expansion, where infrastructure money comes from, whose schools and clinics get squeezed — none of it enters the model. The model has return on tangible equity rising past 13%, and buybacks.

Carlsberg is cleaner still. Beer is slowing in China, so soft drinks fill the gap, via the Britvic acquisition and PepsiCo bottling licenses. Margin comes not from brewing better but from holding someone else's license. India IPO, Nordic licensing changes, the Sapporo partnership — all "potential upside." Potential means it hasn't happened, and it's already in the price.

The phrase "Conviction List" deserves a pause. It dresses a sell-side report as belief, a rating as faith. The reason for dropping HSBC is candid: its U.S. commercial real estate exposure was cut 40%. Which means the exposure was there when it was recommended.

Not one specific person bears a cost in this text; only companies get added and subtracted. That is exactly the nature of the genre: it writes the world as a tradable list, and everything off the list ceases to exist.

So I won't rate this news good or bad. I'll locate it: it works on the layer that manufactures what counts as fact. Once a target is published, people act on it, action pulls price toward the target, the prediction fulfills itself, and then gets cited as proof of accuracy.

The question isn't whether Goldman got it right. It's who gets to write a sentence into other people's decisions while bearing none of the cost of being wrong.
