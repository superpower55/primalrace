初选输了，就把票投给对面She Lost the Primary, So She Endorsed the Other Side

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
被自己党内击败的人去给对方站台，这不是背叛，是共谋的价码
When the loser of a primary campaigns for the opponent, that's not betrayal. That's the price of complicity.

Haley Stevens 在密歇根初选输给 Abdul El-Sayed，几个月来拒绝为他站台，最后干脆走到台上为共和党的 Mike Rogers 背书。民主党人说这是“背叛”。背叛这个词用得太轻了。她没有背弃任何东西，她只是把一直以来的立场摆到了明处。

报道写得很清楚：分歧的核心是以色列。Stevens 要求 El-Sayed 谴责一个把物价上涨归因于美国支持以色列的外部团体广告，El-Sayed 拒绝，说这是把已经结束的初选重新打开。于是她转身去了底特律的教堂，站在 Rogers 身边。这不是情绪失控，这是一次有条件的交易没谈成之后的明码标价。

值得注意的是她的未婚夫 Jason Turkish。报道说他公开谴责 El-Sayed、支持 Rogers，过去给民主党大额捐款，今年也给包括 Susan Collins 在内的共和党候选人捐了钱。一个家庭里两笔政治投资，一笔押在民主党，一笔押在共和党。这不叫立场，这叫对冲。

真正说明问题的是她身边的人。八年的幕僚长 Justin German 和传播总监 Logan Basch 辞职，German 代表整个团队发声明支持 El-Sayed，说他们“震惊且心碎”。跟着她干了八年的人，比她更清楚这一步意味着什么。Bernie Sanders 说他们辞职是对的，因为“密歇根的选举远比 Haley 脆弱的自尊重要”。这句话说到了点子上：这不是原则之争，是一个输了初选的人不肯认输。

Trump 的反应最诚实。他在艾奥瓦的集会上夸她，说“我很乐意她加入共和党，我觉得这有可能”。一个网站整页写着谴责 Trump 政策的人，被 Trump 当场招降，而且没有人觉得突兀。

Schumer 的账号把这件事称为“可鄙的背叛”，同时强调民主党团结支持 El-Sayed，并谴责针对他的种族主义和伊斯兰恐惧症攻击。这段话的后半句才是重点：共和党那边对 El-Sayed 的攻击是种族主义和伊斯兰恐惧症，而 Stevens 选择在这个时刻站到攻击者那一边。她不是不知道，她只是算过账。

报道没有写的是：Stevens 从 Rogers 那里得到了什么。没有职位承诺，没有委员会席位，什么都没有。她付出的代价是八年的团队、党内的位置、以及“背叛者”这个标签，换来的只是站在一个她声称要击败的人身边。这笔交易连她自己的利益都算不上最优解，只能算是一次赌气式的自毁。

真正被摆上桌的是密歇根的选民。一个以微弱差距赢下初选的人，被自己党内的对手在选举前夜拆台，而拆台的理由和密歇根的工作、物价、堕胎权都没有关系。El-Sayed 说“我不太想 Haley Stevens”，这句话是这场闹剧里唯一清醒的一句。

最刺眼的不是她背书了谁，而是整件事里没有一个人问：那些在初选里投给 Stevens 的选民，他们的票被带到了哪里。

Haley Stevens lost the Michigan Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, refused for months to campaign for him, and then walked onto a stage to endorse his Republican rival, Mike Rogers. Democrats are calling it a "betrayal." That word is too generous. She didn't betray anything. She moved her long-standing position into plain sight.

The reporting is clear: the fault line was Israel. Stevens demanded that El-Sayed denounce an outside group's ad blaming US support for Israel for the rising cost of living. El-Sayed refused, saying it reopened a settled primary. So she went to a church in Detroit and stood next to Rogers. This wasn't a tantrum. It was a conditional deal that fell through, and then got priced at market rate.

Her fiancé, Jason Turkish, is worth noting. The report says he has been publicly condemning El-Sayed while supporting Rogers. He donated heavily to Democrats in the past, but this year also gave to Republican candidates including Susan Collins. Two political investments in one household, one on each side. That's not a position. That's a hedge.

The people around her tell the real story. Her chief of staff of eight years, Justin German, and her communications director, Logan Basch, resigned. German released a statement on behalf of the campaign team endorsing El-Sayed, saying they were "shocked and heartbroken." The people who worked for her for eight years understood what this step meant better than she did. Bernie Sanders said they were right to resign, because "the election in Michigan is far more important than Haley's fragile ego." That's the point: this isn't a clash of principles, it's a loser who won't concede.

Trump's reaction is the most honest thing in the story. At his Iowa rally he praised her and said, "I'd love her to join the Republican party. I think that could happen." A congresswoman with an entire page on her website denouncing Trump's policies got recruited on the spot, and nobody found it strange.

Schumer's account called it a "despicable betrayal" while insisting Democrats are united behind El-Sayed, and condemned the "racist, Islamophobic, and over-the-top false attacks on Abdul by Republicans." That second half is the real content: the Republican attacks on El-Sayed are racist and Islamophobic, and Stevens chose this moment to stand with the attackers. She knew. She did the math.

What the report doesn't say is what Stevens got from Rogers. No job offer, no committee seat, nothing. She paid with her eight-year team, her standing in the party, and the label "traitor," and got back a spot next to a man she claimed to want to defeat. This isn't even her own optimal expression. It's a sulk dressed as strategy.

The people actually on the table are Michigan's voters. A candidate who won the primary by a narrow margin gets undermined on the eve of the election by her own party's runner-up, over a dispute that has nothing to do with Michigan jobs, prices, or abortion rights. El-Sayed's line — that he doesn't think about Haley Stevens — is the only clear sentence in the whole mess.

The sharpest thing isn't who she endorsed. It's that nobody in this story asks where the votes of the people who voted for Stevens in the primary just went.
