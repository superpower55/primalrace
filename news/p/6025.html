名字就是选票的武器A Name Is a Weapon on the Ballot

国际 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要美国全国共和党参议院委员会要求联邦选举委员会调查民主党阿拉斯加参议员候选人玛丽·佩尔托拉的竞选团队，指其推动一名与共和党现任参议员丹·沙利文同名的候选人参加竞选。此前《纽约时报》报道了全国民主党人和佩尔托拉团队招募丹·J·沙利文参选的努力。司法部也在调查此事。
The newsThe National Republican Senatorial Committee asked the FEC to investigate Democrat Mary Peltola's Alaska Senate campaign over efforts to recruit a spoiler candidate named Dan J. Sullivan, the same name as GOP incumbent Dan Sullivan. The request follows New York Times reporting on the recruitment effort. The Justice Department is also investigating.
选票上多一个同名的人，就是一次用名字制造的假表达。
One extra name on the ballot is a fake expression engineered to steal a vote.

阿拉斯加这场选举里最锋利的东西不是政策，是一个名字。全国民主党人和 Peltola 的竞选团队把一个叫 Dan J. Sullivan 的人拉进参议院选战，让他和现任共和党参议员 Dan Sullivan 共享同一个名字出现在选票上。共和党全国参议院委员会现在要求联邦选举委员会调查，并要求在认定违规属于「明知且故意」时移交司法部。

这不是什么地下操作，这是表达的武器化最朴素的一种：不需要说服任何选民改变立场，只需要在投票那一刻让他们的眼睛认错人。选票是一个极度压缩的表达空间，一个名字、一个方框，几秒钟内完成一次投票。在这个空间里，名字就是唯一的认知入口。多塞一个同名的人进去，等于在入口处放了一面哈哈镜。

报道里最值得注意的一句话来自 Lisa Murkowski：「招募候选人操纵候选人名单、影响投票结果，这本身就是错的。」说这话的是一个共和党人，她不是 Peltola 的对手，她是本州同党的参议员。这句话之所以有分量，是因为它没有停留在党派攻防上，它指出的是机制：用名字做局，不管谁做，都是在偷选民的票。

Peltola 一方和 DSCC 的回应是，FEC 没有法定人数，所以这个投诉无从推进；并指责共和党在「危险地干预阿拉斯加参议院选举」，把司法部武器化。这两句话在各自的层面都成立——FEC 确实没有 quorum，共和党确实在选举前几天急着抹黑一个关键席位的对手。但这不改变一个事实：投诉里描述的操作，如果属实，本身就是一次对选举表达的劫持。用「对方也在武器化」来回应「我武器化了」，是共谋者理论里最标准的对位。

报道还提到，投诉称 Peltola 团队试图招募的不止一个搅局候选人，另一个名字是 Gerald Heikes，同样在选票上。如果这个指控成立，那就不是一次临时的投机，而是一套「选票构成工程」（ballot composition Project），有规划、有外部超级政治行动委员会的资助、有协调。工程这个词用得很准：它不改变选民的想法，它改变选民看到的东西。

阿拉斯加用的是排序选择制，这套制度的设计初衷是让选民按真实偏好排序，减少策略性投票的压力。而这次的操作恰恰是绕开制度设计，从最上游的候选人名单下手。制度给了你一个更诚实的表达方式，有人就在名单上做手脚，让你连诚实的对象都分不清。

真正被削掉的东西在这里：选民的票本来可以准确落在他们想投的那个人身上，现在这个差额被一个同名的人吃掉了。这是 structural 层的暴力——不是有人被打，是投票这个机制被做了手脚，而做手脚的人不需要在场，只需要在名单上多写一行字。

共和党要求 FEC「在法律允许的最大范围内制裁」，但 FEC 没有 quorum。一个没有法定人数的机构，收到再严厉的投诉也只能搁着。于是这件事的结局很可能是：指控成立与否没人裁决，选举照常进行，选票上的两个 Dan Sullivan 照常并排站着。制度被利用的那个缺口，正好也是制度修补不了的缺口。

The sharpest thing in this Alaska race isn't policy. It's a name. National Democrats and Mary Peltola's campaign pulled a man named Dan J. Sullivan into the Senate race so that he would share a name with the incumbent Republican, Dan Sullivan, on the ballot. Now the National Republican Senatorial Committee is asking the FEC to investigate, and to refer the matter to the Justice Department if the commission finds the violations were "knowing and willful."

This isn't a backroom trick. It's the most primitive form of weaponized expression: you don't have to persuade a single voter to change their mind. You just have to make their eyes pick the wrong box at the moment they vote. A ballot is an extremely compressed expressive space — one name, one square, a few seconds. In that space the name is the only cognitive entrance. Sliding a second identical name in is like putting a funhouse mirror at the door.

The line worth pausing on comes from Lisa Murkowski: "recruiting candidates to manipulate the field and influence voting outcomes is simply wrong." She's a Republican, not Peltola's opponent — a senator from the same state and party. The sentence carries weight because it doesn't stop at partisan combat. It names the mechanism: engineering a name is stealing votes, no matter who does it.

The Peltola side and the DSCC respond that the FEC has no quorum, so the complaint can't go anywhere, and that Republicans are "dangerously" interfering in the Alaska Senate race and weaponizing the Justice Department. Both claims can be true on their own terms — the FEC really does lack a quorum, and Republicans really are rushing to damage a must-win opponent days before the midterms. Neither changes the fact that the operation described, if true, is a hijacking of electoral expression. Answering "they're weaponizing too" to "I weaponized" is the standard co-conspirator move.

The complaint also alleges that Dan J. Sullivan wasn't the only spoiler the Peltola campaign tried to recruit — Gerald Heikes is on the ballot too. If that holds, this isn't improvisation. It's a "ballot composition Project": planned, financed by multiple super PACs, coordinated. The word project is apt. It doesn't change what voters think. It changes what voters see.

Alaska uses ranked-choice voting, a system built to let people rank their real preferences and take pressure off strategic voting. This operation bypasses the design entirely by going upstream to the candidate list. The system hands you a more honest way to express yourself, and someone edits the list so you can't even tell who you're being honest about.

Here's the gap that gets carved out: a vote that could have landed precisely on the person the voter meant now lands on a namesake. That's structural violence — nobody gets hit, but the mechanism of voting is tampered with, and the tamperer never has to be in the room. One extra line on a form does the work.

Republicans want the FEC to "sanction to the fullest extent of the law." The FEC has no quorum. An agency with no quorum can receive the harshest complaint in the world and still do nothing. So the likely ending: the allegation is never adjudicated, the election proceeds, and two Dan Sullivans stand side by side on the ballot. The hole that was exploited is the same hole that can't be patched.
