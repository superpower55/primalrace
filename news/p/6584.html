打分是表达，表达要付代价Rating Is Expression, and Expression Has a Price

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
他撤回了评分，没撤回定价权
He withdrew the stars, not the pricing power

Rider Strong 停止在 Letterboxd 上打星，理由说得很直白：他要请人演戏，要找人投资，而“大家会互相查 Letterboxd”。这不是审美问题，是定价问题。

一个演员给同行打半星，等于在公开场合给对方的作品定价。这个价格会一直在那里，谁都能查。他删了一半评分，删不完，剩下的还在外面。他保留的是“看过”标记和爱心——喜欢可以公开，不喜欢要手动一条条撤回。这套不对称本身就是规矩：圈子里允许你赞美，不允许你贬低，因为贬低是有成本的表达。

有意思的是他对《Heat》的改口。年轻时觉得闷，现在觉得是完美电影。他说自己“不够精明”，没看懂 Michael Mann 的克制。这个转变可能是真的，也可能是他学会了怎么在圈子里说话。两种情况都指向同一件事：审美判断从来不是纯粹的个人品味，它是可以被职业前途重新校准的。

他自己承认是极端派，不是半星就是五星，评分曲线是一条平线。这种极端本来是最诚实的表达，因为他不装中间值。现在他撤了，不是因为他变温和了，是因为他发现极端有价格标签。

表达就是身份的确立。你给什么打几分，别人就知道你是谁、你站哪边、你值不值得合作。Strong 撤掉的不是评分，是让别人免费读他的入口。他还在用这个平台，只是把可被查的部分收起来了。

所以这件事的教训不是“名人要小心社交媒体”，而是：在一个所有人都互相查对方评分的行业里，诚实是有定价的，而定价权不在打分的人手里。他删了一半，剩下的一半还在替他说话。

Rider Strong stopped rating films on Letterboxd, and he said why plainly: he asks actors to be in movies he writes or directs, and "people check each other's Letterboxd." This isn't an aesthetic problem. It's a pricing problem.

An actor giving a peer half a star is publicly pricing that peer's work. The price stays there, visible to anyone who looks. He removed about half his ratings and couldn't remove the rest, because each one has to be deleted by hand. What he kept is the watched tag and the heart—liking can be public, disliking has to be manually retracted one entry at a time. That asymmetry is the rule itself: the industry permits praise and taxes criticism, because criticism is expression with a cost.

The Heat reversal is the interesting part. He found it boring when he was younger; now it's a perfect film. He says he wasn't savvy enough for Michael Mann's restraint. The change might be real. It might also be him learning how to talk inside the room. Either way it points at the same thing: taste is never purely personal. It can be recalibrated by career prospects.

He calls himself an extremist—half a star or five, a flat line with no middle. That extremity was the most honest thing about his account, because he refused to fake a three. He didn't stop because he became moderate. He stopped because he found the price tag on extremity.

Expression establishes identity. Your ratings tell people who you are, which side you're on, whether you're worth working with. Strong didn't withdraw his taste. He withdrew free access to the entrance that let people read it. He still uses the platform; he just closed the part that can be checked.

The lesson isn't "celebrities should be careful online." It's that in an industry where everyone checks everyone's ratings, honesty has a price, and the people setting that price aren't the ones doing the rating. He deleted half. The other half is still speaking for him.
