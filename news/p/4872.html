肌肉、器官与B级片：一次关于男性特权的视觉按摩Muscles, Organs, and B-Movies: A Visual Massage of Masculine Privilege

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-09 § 链接
过度肌肉化的身体是权力的视觉符号，而B级片叙事将其合法化为娱乐。
Hyper-muscular bodies are visual symbols of power, legitimized as entertainment through B-movie narratives.

看到 Alan Ritchson 在这部电影里的设定——一个“肌肉过度”的前军人，在澳大利亚的船屋里学法语、开 AA 互助会，然后用暴力护送一个肝脏——我首先想到的是一种极度典型的 masculine-centric narrative。这种设定本质上是在消费一种特定的男性幻象：拥有绝对的物理支配力（brawn），却在精神上处于一种“被驯化”的忧郁状态。这种“我不再伤害他人”的宣言，本身就是一种权力者的傲慢，因为它默认了该个体拥有随时重启暴力机器的能力。

这部电影被评价为“Camp”（坎普/刻意地庸俗），这其实是文化层面的某种共谋。当暴力被包装成 B 级片的荒诞感，当“骨折般的暴力”变成一种视频游戏逻辑的视觉快感时，这种 structural violence 被成功地转化为了一种消费品。观众在享受“大美国瓦里奥” flex 肌肉的同时，潜意识里在认同一种逻辑：只要你足够强壮，且拥有一个“悲剧性的背景故事”，你对秩序的践踏和对身体的摧毁就可以被视为一种“服务性”的英雄主义。

最讽刺的是，电影试图在“动作坎普”和“真诚情感”之间博弈，结果是前者完胜。这意味着，观众并不在乎那个需要肝脏的小女孩，也不在乎所谓的救赎，他们只在乎 Range Rover 的碰撞和肌肉男的下颌线。这种对主体性的抹除，将女性（病童及其母亲）简化为触发男性英雄主义的“道具”，而将男性身体的支配权推向极致。这不仅是一部 B 级片，这是一次关于男性特权在银幕上的视觉按摩，而我们被邀请在其中微笑。

Looking at Alan Ritchson’s setup in this film—an 'outlandishly muscled' ex-military fixer learning French in an Australian houseboat while transporting a liver—I see a textbook masculine-centric narrative. This setup consumes a specific male fantasy: absolute physical dominance paired with a 'tamed' spiritual melancholy. The proclamation 'I don’t hurt people any more' is an act of arrogance; it assumes the individual retains the inherent power to reactivate the machinery of violence at will.

The film is praised for its 'Camp' sensibility, which is essentially a form of cultural complicity. When violence is packaged as B-movie absurdity and 'shin-splitting' brutality is converted into video-game logic, structural violence is successfully rebranded as a consumer product. While the audience enjoys the 'big American Wario' flexing, they subconsciously validate a logic: as long as you are strong enough and possess a 'tragic backstory,' your disruption of order and destruction of bodies can be viewed as a 'serviceable' heroism.

Most ironically, the film's battle between action camp and sincerity is won by the former. This means the audience doesn't truly care about the little girl or the redemption; they care about Range Rover collisions and jawlines. This erasure of subjectivity reduces the women (the child and mother) to mere 'props' triggering male heroism, while pushing the dominance of the male body to the extreme. This isn't just a B-movie; it's a visual massage of masculine privilege on screen, and we are invited to smile along.
