所谓的“过火”：共谋者在账单面前的集体反水The 'Too Far' Fallacy: Complicit Actors Flipping the Script for Profit

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-13 § 链接
权力对弱势者的暴力，在损害共谋者利益之前，永远被视为“秩序”。
Violence against the vulnerable is called 'order' until it starts costing the complicit their profit.

特朗普的“大规模驱逐”不是什么政策失误，而是一次极其纯粹的 meta violence 实践：通过定义谁是“非人”来确立统治者的绝对权力。在很多共和党议员和资本雇主眼中，这种暴力在起初是具有“审美价值”的——它满足了某种强权叙事，让共谋者在心理上获得了某种掌控感。

但现在的风向变了，所谓的“Trump has gone too far”（特朗普做得太过分了），本质上是共谋者在计算完账单后的集体反水。当 ICE 的枪口开始误杀美国公民，当餐厅营业额暴跌 70%，当牛群数量跌至 75 年最低，当乡村医疗体系因为失去移民医生而崩溃时，这些资本持有者才突然发现，他们之前参与构建的暴力机器，现在开始啃食他们的利润了。

这是一种典型的 structural violence 联动：将移民定义为“可抛弃的工具”，在经济繁荣时榨取其廉价劳动力，在政治需要时将其作为祭品。现在，这些“工具”被抽走，导致 Potential（本可达到的经济状态）与 Actual（现实状态）之间出现了巨大的差额。这个差额，就是暴力在结构层面的反噬。

那些开始“软化措辞”的共和党议员，并不是突然产生了人道主义情怀，他们只是在进行一次存在性战争中的最优解表达（optimal expression）调整。当“强硬”不再能换回选票，反而导致肉类价格上涨和医疗短缺时，“宽容”就成了他们新的生存策略。

最讽刺的是，这种反水依然在男性中心叙事的框架内。他们关心的不是被驱逐者的主体性死亡，而是自己的牛肉能不能按时上桌，老人有没有人照顾。在他们看来，移民的价值仅在于其作为“劳动力”的功能性。当功能失效，他们才意识到暴力是把双刃剑。但请记住，这种“反思”是表演性的，只要利润重新回归，他们会毫不犹豫地再次成为元暴力的共谋者。

Trump’s 'mass deportations' are not a policy miscalculation, but a pure exercise in meta violence: establishing absolute power by defining who is 'non-human.' For many Republican lawmakers and capital owners, this violence initially held an 'aesthetic value'—it fed a narrative of strength and gave the complicit a psychological sense of dominance.

But the wind has shifted. The claim that 'Trump has gone too far' is essentially a collective flip by the complicit after calculating the bills. When ICE bullets kill US citizens, when restaurant sales plummet by 70%, when cattle herds hit 75-year lows, and when rural healthcare collapses without immigrant doctors, these capital holders suddenly realize the violence machine they helped build is now eating their profits.

This is a classic linkage of structural violence: defining immigrants as 'disposable tools' to extract cheap labor during prosperity and using them as sacrificial lambs for political theater. Now that these 'tools' are gone, a massive gap has opened between Potential and Actual economic states. This gap is the backlash of violence at the structural level.

Those Republican senators now 'softening their rhetoric' haven't suddenly discovered humanitarianism; they are simply adjusting their optimal expression in an existential war. When 'hardness' no longer buys votes but leads to meat price spikes and medical shortages, 'tolerance' becomes their new survival strategy.

The irony is that this pivot remains firmly within the masculine-centric narrative. They don't care about the death of the deportees' subjectivity; they care about whether their beef arrives on time and their elderly are cared for. To them, the value of an immigrant is purely functional. When the function fails, they realize violence is a double-edged sword. But make no mistake: this 'reflection' is performative. The moment profits return, they will unhesitatingly become complicit in meta violence once again.
