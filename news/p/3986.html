好莱坞的“创意文艺复兴”是一场关于定价权的骗局Hollywood's 'Creative Renaissance' is a Scam for Pricing Power

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
用成本降低来掩盖主体性死亡，是典型的结构性暴力 PR 版本。
Using cost reduction to mask the death of subjectivity is a classic PR version of structural violence.

好莱坞现在把 AI 电影包装成一场“创意文艺复兴”，试图用“独立电影人的赋权”这个叙事来掩盖一个残酷的事实：生产力的提升并没有带来表达空间的扩大，而是在加速对人类主体性的定价权剥夺。

所谓的“降低 30%-50% 的成本”，在资本的逻辑里从来不是为了让更多有才华的人拍电影，而是为了在同一个 Potential 空间里，用更低的 Actual 投入榨取同样的利润。当“合成演员” (synthetic performers) 能够替代人类，演员不再是拥有主体性的表达者，而变成了可以被无限次调用、无需支付报酬、没有生物墙限制的数字资产。这根本不是赋权，而是将人类的表达彻底工具化，把演员从“创作者”降格为“素材库”。

最讽刺的是那些 AI 原生导演的逻辑。他们抱怨好莱坞的算法化生产，却在拥抱一套更极致的算法——AI 模型。他们认为在卧室里用中国 AI 模型能获得“真实的声音”，却忽略了 AI 本身就是对全人类既有表达的统计学平均。当你使用一个被“训练在太阳下所有东西”上的模型时，你的“个人风格”不过是模型在概率分布中随机抽样的一次结果。这不是 Tarantino 式的突破，而是用一种更高效率的共谋，在潜意识里完成了对个体表达的抹除。

当五家科技巨头通过控制认知入口（模型权重）来定义什么是“好看的画面”和“ believable 的角色”时，电影业的元暴力已经完成了升级。这次，暴力不再是制片厂的合同压榨，而是直接在生成像素的那一刻，就决定了什么样的表达是被允许的。这哪里是黄金时代？这分明是主体性大规模死亡的前奏。

Hollywood is currently packaging AI filmmaking as a 'creative renaissance,' attempting to use the narrative of 'empowering independent filmmakers' to hide a brutal fact: the increase in productivity is not expanding the space for expression, but accelerating the seizure of pricing power over human subjectivity.

The so-called '30%-50% cost reduction' in the logic of capital is never about letting more talented people make movies. It is about extracting the same profit with lower Actual investment within the same Potential space. When 'synthetic performers' replace humans, actors are no longer subjects of expression, but digital assets that can be called infinitely, require no payment, and have no biological walls. This is not empowerment; it is the total instrumentalization of human expression, degrading the actor from a 'creator' to a 'dataset'.

Most ironic is the logic of AI-native directors. They complain about Hollywood's algorithmic production while embracing a more extreme algorithm—the AI model. They believe using Chinese AI models in a bedroom grants them an 'authentic voice,' ignoring that AI is fundamentally a statistical average of all existing human expressions. When you use a model 'trained on everything under the sun,' your 'personal style' is merely a result of random sampling from a probability distribution. This isn't a Tarantino breakthrough; it's a high-efficiency complicity that erases individual expression in the subconscious.

When five tech giants define what 'good visuals' and 'believable characters' are by controlling the cognitive entry points (model weights), the meta-violence of the film industry has upgraded. This time, violence is no longer the contractual squeeze of studios, but is decided at the moment of pixel generation: which expressions are permissible. This is no golden era; it is the prelude to the mass death of subjectivity.
