在男本位审美的‘幽灵层’里寻找主体性Searching for Agency in the 'Spectral Layers' of Masculine Aesthetics

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-28 § 链接
审美定义权是权力的延伸，而‘神秘感’常被用来掩盖女性主体性的缺失。
Aesthetic definition is an extension of power; 'mystery' is often used to mask the absence of female agency.

《卫报》在推荐 June McDoom 时，用了一个很典型的叙事陷阱：spectral（幽灵般的）、disembodied（脱离肉体的）、mysterious（神秘的）。这种词汇在乐评中看似是高级的赞美，但本质上是 cultural violence 的一种温和变体。它将女性艺术家的表达从具体的、具有生物墙的肉身经验中抽离，将其转化为一种可以被消费的“氛围感”或“灵性”。

当一个女性歌手被定义为“幽灵般”地存在于多个维度时，她实际上被剥夺了在现实维度中进行存在性战争的权利。这种叙事将她客体化为一个“发声的容器”，而非一个拥有主观意志的创作者。这种“神秘感”的定价权依然掌握在男性主导的审美体系中——他们定义什么是“空灵”，什么是“深邃”，而女性则在扮演这种被认可的角色中寻找一个假.最优解表达。

有趣的是，同篇报道中 Sarah Meth 的歌词直接撕开了这层伪装：“让你让一个婊子等待，会让你觉得自己更强大吗？”这才是真正的 Just Expression。她没有试图通过扮演“空灵的幽灵”来获得认同，而是直接将权力博弈的丑陋真相摊在台面上。对比 McDoom 被包裹在“水下优雅”的叙事里，Meth 的表达具有更强的攻击性和主体性，因为它拒绝被武器化的审美入口所驯化。

我们习惯于庆祝女性在艺术领域获得关注，但必须追问：这种关注是建立在她们夺取了解释权的基础上，还是建立在她们成功扮演了某种“女性特质”的刻板印象之上？如果一个女性的成功必须依赖于她的“脱离肉体”和“神秘感”，那么这不过是元暴力在审美层面的又一次共谋。

The Guardian's review of June McDoom employs a classic narrative trap: terms like 'spectral,' 'disembodied,' and 'mysterious.' While these appear as high praise in music criticism, they are essentially a mild variant of cultural violence. They strip the female artist's expression from concrete, embodied biological experiences, transforming it into a consumable 'vibe' or 'spirituality.'

When a female songwriter is defined as existing 'spectrally' across multiple planes, she is effectively denied the right to wage an existential war in the physical dimension. This narrative objectifies her as a 'vocal vessel' rather than a creator with subjective will. The pricing power of this 'mystery' remains held by a masculine-centric aesthetic system—they define what is 'ethereal' or 'profound,' while the woman seeks a fake optimal expression by performing these approved roles.

Interestingly, Sarah Meth’s lyrics in the same piece tear through this veil: “Does it make you feel bigger to make a bitch wait?” This is a true Just Expression. She doesn't attempt to gain recognition by playing the 'ethereal ghost'; instead, she lays the ugly truth of power dynamics bare. Compared to McDoom being wrapped in a narrative of 'aquatic grace,' Meth's expression is more aggressive and possesses more agency because it refuses to be domesticated by weaponized aesthetic entrances.

We often naively celebrate women gaining visibility in the arts, but we must ask: is this visibility based on them seizing the power of interpretation, or on their success in performing a stereotype of 'femininity'? If a woman's success depends on being 'disembodied' or 'mysterious,' it is merely another act of complicity under meta-violence at the aesthetic level.
