扒手的手，照护者的手The Pickpocket's Hands, the Caretaker's Hands

文娱 结构层 NPR ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要演员约翰·特图罗为新片《纽约唯一活着的扒手》接受 NPR 采访，谈到向手法艺术家阿波罗·罗宾斯学习扒窃，以及在片场反复练习。他回顾了担任患有精神分裂症的已故兄弟法定监护人的经历，以及童年在父亲战争创伤与家庭动荡中的成长。
The newsActor John Turturro told NPR he trained with sleight-of-hand artist Apollo Robbins to pick pockets for his new film The Only Living Pickpocket in New York, practicing on set. He also discussed serving as legal guardian to his late brother, who had schizophrenia, and growing up amid his father's wartime trauma.
教他偷钱包的是一双手，教他撑住一个家的也是。
One pair of hands taught him to steal; another taught him to hold a family together.

特图罗讲扒窃技巧讲得很细：竖口袋配紧身牛仔裤难下手，后袋容易，背包拉链没拉严最好。这段采访最诚实的地方就在这里——他把「怎么从别人身上取走东西」当成一门可以练习的手艺来讲，不装，也不忏悔。

有意思的是同一篇采访里另一门手艺：照护。他做弟弟的法定监护人，找住处、雇护工、再雇一个管护工的人，弟弟不高兴就把午饭往他身上扔，他站在那儿决定走还是留。他说弟弟给过他很多素材，「但代价很高」。扒手取走的是钱包，照护者付出的是自己——这两件事在这篇采访里挨着放，谁也没解释谁。

他讲母亲和她的女客人们是「胶水」，讲自己小时候帮母亲做裙子、被叫去穿上婚纱改下摆，父亲进门看见，他说我不在乎。这个细节他自己没往深处说，但它比任何一段表演方法论都更能说明表达是什么：一个男孩穿上婚纱站在那儿，判断的不是自己像不像女人，而是这件事值不值得在意。

整篇采访里最该被追问的一句，是他讲弟弟时说的「根本没有系统」。精神病人流落街头，家属独自扛下全部照护，扛到人去世——这不是哪个家庭的意外，是照护被默认成私事、默认成女人的事、默认成「你有天性你就扛」之后，结构省下来的那笔账。特图罗扛了，还扛得动，因为他有名气、有钱、雇得起人。雇不起的人呢？报道没写，因为没人问。

他六十九岁，说七十是新的五十，说工具箱满了，能蒸馏、能精确。这话我信。但工具箱里最沉的那几件，不是表演课给的。

Turturro explains pickpocketing like a craftsman: vertical pockets in tight jeans are hard, back pockets are easy, an unzipped backpack on someone's back is best. The honesty of this interview is right there — he treats taking things off other people as a skill you can drill, no confession, no apology.

The other craft in the same interview is caregiving. He was his brother's legal guardian: finding residences, hiring aides, then hiring someone to manage the aides, getting lunch thrown at him when his brother was in a bad mood, standing there deciding whether to leave or stay. His brother, he says, gave him a lot of material — at a high cost. A pickpocket takes your wallet. A caretaker pays with himself. The interview places these side by side and explains neither.

He describes his mother and her clients as the glue, and himself as a boy helping with dress patterns, once asked to put on a wedding dress so she could do the hems. His father walked in. He didn't care. He doesn't dwell on it, but it says more about expression than any acting theory: a boy in a wedding dress, judging not whether he looks like a woman but whether the thing is worth minding.

The line that should have been followed up is the one about his brother: there is no system. The mentally ill end up on the streets, families absorb the entire load alone, and the load runs until the person dies. That isn't one family's bad luck. It's what the structure saves by treating care as a private matter, a woman's matter, a matter of temperament. Turturro could carry it because he is famous, paid, able to hire. Who carries it when they can't? The interview doesn't ask.
