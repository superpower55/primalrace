食谱是最高级的共谋：被量化的家务与消失的劳动者Recipes as the Ultimate Complicity: Quantified Chores and the Vanishing Laborer

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-09-07 § 链接
食谱将复杂的生存劳动简化为步骤，掩盖了结构性的家务剥削。
Recipes simplify survival labor into steps, masking structural domestic exploitation.

一篇典型的 NYT 式食谱：精准的克数、50 分钟的 Ready In、以及一群在评论区讨论是否可以用马芬盘分装的精英中产。在这种 anemic 的叙事里，烹饪被剥离了所有生存的血腥味，被包装成一种名为“生活方式”的 consumer product。它通过量化 ingredients 和 steps，完成了一次极其隐蔽的 cultural violence：将原本琐碎、重复且无偿的家务劳动，转化为一种可被管理、可被量化的“项目”。

注意那些评论：“real hit with my family”、“great dish for brunches”。这里的 family 并不是一个生物学概念，而是一个共谋场域。谁在 whisk eggs？谁在 chop herbs？谁在洗那个 9-by-13-inch 的烤盘？在“简单易行”的叙事背后，是 structural violence 的默认设置——这些劳动通常由女性承担，且在 GDP 的统计中被视为 0。食谱通过定义“Easy”，实际上是在降低执行者的主体性：你不需要思考，只需要扮演一个高效的家务执行机器。

最讽刺的是，这种“高效”成了中产阶级的一种审美。当人们讨论是否增加 egg whites 以优化 consistency 时，他们讨论的是对食物的控制权，而非对劳动的分配权。这种对“完美早午餐”的追求，本质上是 masculine-centric narrative 的延伸：将家庭空间转化为一个可被精准操控的实验室，而那个在厨房里忙碌的身体，只是这个实验室里一个被忽略的 variable。

A typical NYT recipe: precise grams, a 50-minute 'Ready In', and a crowd of upper-middle-class users discussing muffin pan portions in the comments. In this anemic narrative, cooking is stripped of all the gore of survival and repackaged as a consumer product called 'lifestyle'. By quantifying ingredients and steps, it performs a subtle act of cultural violence: transforming tedious, repetitive, and unpaid domestic labor into a manageable, quantifiable 'project'.

Notice the comments: 'real hit with my family', 'great dish for brunches'. Here, 'family' is not a biological concept but a field of complicity. Who is whisking the eggs? Who is chopping the herbs? Who is scrubbing that 9-by-13-inch pan? Behind the narrative of 'easy' lies the default setting of structural violence—this labor is predominantly performed by women and recorded as zero in GDP statistics. By defining it as 'Easy', the recipe effectively erases the agency of the laborer: you don't need to think; you only need to play the role of an efficient domestic machine.

The irony is that this 'efficiency' has become a middle-class aesthetic. When users debate adding egg whites to optimize 'consistency', they are discussing the power to control food, not the power to allocate labor. This pursuit of the 'perfect brunch' is an extension of the masculine-centric narrative: transforming the domestic space into a precisely controlled laboratory, where the body toiling in the kitchen is merely an ignored variable.
