票房冠军是纪录片，这算什么新闻A Documentary Won the Weekend. So What

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
一部纪录片拿周末冠军，赢的只是档期空窗
A documentary took No. 1 because nobody else showed up.

这个周末的北美票房冠军很可能是一部三小时的纪录片，A24 的《You Can See Everything》。它第三个周末收 1060 万美元，累计 6380 万，跌幅约 43%。第二名《Clayface》1040 万，第二周跌 56%。

先看清这是什么。报道自己说得很清楚：万圣节落在周六，片厂集体避开，新片一部没上，全周末总票房 5560 万美元，比上周跌 42.1%，是全年最低之一。一部纪录片在这个空档里领先第二名二十万美元，这不叫赢，叫没人跟它抢。Rentrak 的 Dergarabedian 说得客气，叫片厂被“历史性的挑战”吓退了；翻译过来就是：档期是空的，谁在里面谁第一。

真正值得看的是《Clayface》。4400 万成本，DC Studios 在《Superman》和表现不佳的《Supergirl》之后的第三部，第二周跌 56%。这条曲线的意思不是“观众不喜欢这部片”，而是“观众根本没被叫来”。一部 body horror 放在这个档期，等于把片子扔进一个所有人都在外面要糖的周末，然后拿跌幅去解释它。第二周的跌幅从来不是电影质量的语言，是发行策略的语言。

同一种语言在榜单下半部分更清楚。《Wildwood》成本据报 1 亿美元，第二周只跌 38%，烂番茄 99%，但累计 2580 万——它跌得漂亮，是因为它本来就没多少可跌的。一个 1 亿预算的定格动画，第二周在 2712 家影院收 750 万，这个“impressive hold”是账面好看，不是生意好看。报道说它“需要继续走出长腿”，说得对，但长腿救不了一部开局就没进场的电影。

这就是票房报道这种体裁的问题：它把发行方选择在哪一周上映、选择开多少馆、选择跟谁错峰，全部翻译成“观众用脚投票”。观众没有投票。观众在要糖、在看橄榄球。这一周的数字不是关于电影的，是关于日历的。

所以这条新闻里没有人在承受代价，也没有一个值得拆穿的叙事——它是一份档期报表，用“冠军”这个词给一个空场加冕。真要拆，只能拆那个词：一部三小时纪录片拿到它第一个周末冠军，靠的不是它比《Clayface》更被需要，是《Clayface》被安排在了一个没人来的周末。把这个叫“battle”，是报道在替发行方写战报。

下周末《The Cat in the Hat》《Godzilla Minus Zero》《Wild Horse Nine》一起上，分析师说假日档会“crescendo”，年底可能进纪录册。到时候谁第一，才是有人在抢的第一。这一周的第一，只是空档期的占位符。

The likely North American box office champion this weekend is a three-hour documentary, A24's You Can See Everything: $10.6 million in its third frame, $63.8 million cume, a decline of around 43 percent. Second place, Clayface, at $10.4 million, down 56 percent in its sophomore frame.

Look at what this actually is. The report says it plainly: Halloween fell on a Saturday, the studios stayed away, no new titles opened, the whole weekend came in at $55.6 million, down 42.1 percent from last weekend, one of the lowest totals of the year. A documentary leading by two hundred thousand dollars in that gap is not a win. It's an uncontested slot. Rentrak's Dergarabedian puts it politely, studios spooked by "historical challenges"; translated, the calendar was empty and whoever was in it finished first.

The real story is Clayface. A $44 million budget, DC Studios' third theatrical title after Superman and an underperforming Supergirl, down 56 percent in week two. That curve doesn't say audiences rejected the film. It says nobody was ever called to it. A body horror release dropped into a weekend when everyone is outside trick-or-treating, then explained by its drop. A second-week decline is never the language of film quality. It's the language of release strategy.

The same language runs down the rest of the chart. Wildwood cost a reported $100 million, fell only 38 percent, sits at 99 percent on Rotten Tomatoes, and has $25.8 million to show for it. The pretty hold is pretty because there was so little to hold. A stop-motion feature at that budget taking $7.5 million from 2,712 theaters in week two is a good number on paper, not a good business. The report says it needs legs. True, but legs don't rescue a film that never entered the room.

This is the trouble with box office reporting as a genre: it translates the distributor's choice of week, theater count and counter-programming into "audiences voted with their feet." Audiences didn't vote. They were at parties and football games. This week's numbers are not about movies. They're about a calendar.

So there's no one here paying a price, and no narrative worth cracking open. It's a schedule report wearing the word "champion" on an empty field. The only thing to crack is that word: a three-hour documentary reaching its first No. 1 not because it was more wanted than Clayface, but because Clayface was booked into a weekend nobody came to. Calling that a battle is the trade press writing a war dispatch for the distributor.

Next weekend brings The Cat in the Hat, Godzilla Minus Zero and Wild Horse Nine, and analysts promise the holiday corridor will crescendo into a December that may enter the record books. Whoever finishes first then will have been contested. This week's first place is a placeholder for an empty slot.
