五角大楼的AI喇叭The Pentagon's AI Megaphone

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国调查媒体 The Intercept 发现，五角大楼运营一个名为 La Tilde 的AI内容网站，用西班牙语和英语向拉美读者发布文章，内容包括个人理财建议和赞扬美军在拉美行动（如抓捕马杜罗的行动）的报道。网站没有署名和员工信息，仅在页面底部小字注明由美国政府预算资助；报道称其由美军南方特种作战司令部运营，并计划推出厄瓜多尔、巴拿马等国专版。
The newsThe Intercept found that the Pentagon runs an AI content site called La Tilde, publishing in Spanish and English for Latin American readers, mixing personal finance tips with praise for U.S. military operations in the region, including the abduction of Nicolás Maduro. The site has no bylines or staff, discloses U.S. government funding only in small print, and reportedly is run by U.S. Special Operations Command South.
它不制造事实，它制造「什么是事实」
It doesn't manufacture facts. It manufactures what counts as fact.

先看这个网站的名字。La Tilde，西班牙语里的重音符。宣传片里的旁白说：重音不是装饰，是千年的箭头，用来提供方向、节省空间、把音量调大。这句话本身没错，错的是谁在按这个键。五角大楼按的。

它的内容是什么？一头是「即时支付对你的生意和钱包为什么重要」，另一头是《绝对决心行动：抓捕马杜罗并树立精准与协调新标准的任务》，文中称这是「史无前例规模的协调与精准的军事行动」，还说是「La Tilde 独家获得的信息」。理财贴把人吸引过来，军事通稿把人往一个方向推。据 The Intercept 报道，熟悉美军信息作战的国防官员说，这个网站由美国南方特种作战司令部（SOCSOUTH）作为军事信息平台运营；南方司令部（SOUTHCOM）则否认参与。

这就是第三章说的认知入口。武器化的表达不需要说服你，它只需要成为你刷到的那一条。AI 在这里的作用不是让宣传更聪明，而是让它更快、更便宜、可以随时换靶子。大西洋理事会的 Emerson Brooking 说得很直白：「AI 一路到底」。宣传片里的报纸标题印着「SO THEE HOUTIERRER TO TO GHAHOBATEE」，旁边站着两个中世纪修士。这就是他们想要的「方向」。

最值得看的是那个免责声明。「La Tilde 是由美国政府预算公开资助的国际媒体组织的产品」，这句话被塞在页面底部一个小链接里。The Intercept 说，这段措辞和另外两个五角大楼赞助的宣传网站一模一样。这不是疏忽，这是设计：技术上告知了，实际上没人会看到。这样美国政府就可以说，我们告诉读者信息来源了。合规的外壳，里面是空的。

再看它怎么写巴拿马。一篇文章讲美国与巴拿马联合丛林战训练的好处，说这是抵御中国在拉美类似演习的堡垒，建议各国学巴拿马，「在离家更近的地方，或与像美国这样的长期伙伴寻求训练机会」。文章不提 PANAMAX 演习引发的抗议，不提巴拿马反对党把美军以联合演习名义重返称为「伪装的入侵」，也不提参加 2025 年演习的包括美洲学校（后改名西半球安全合作研究所）——这个机构的毕业生里有几千名拉美行刑队枪手和诺列加。谁被写进文章，谁被从文章里删掉，这就是解释权。

还有一篇讲厄瓜多尔是国际可卡因贸易的枢纽。这个说法和特朗普政府用来扩大「南方之矛行动」的说法一致，而 The Intercept 说，这场 SOUTHCOM 的加勒比空袭行动迄今已造成 200 多名平民死亡。一篇关于厄瓜多尔的「新闻」，替一场死了两百多人的行动铺路。

Brooking 说，这套打法的意图大概是先用通用内容填满网站、建立受众，再塞进更露骨的宣传，比如那篇对抓捕马杜罗的详尽叙述。他说内容很懒，AI 很差，强制披露让整件事像个闹剧。闹剧是真的，但闹剧不等于无害。俄罗斯和中国的AI宣传网络也被他拿来类比：能一键生成新的内容和新闻门面，影响行动就能更快地换目标和焦点。拉美读者刷到的是理财建议，读到的是美军通稿，中间没有一道墙。

谁在付代价？拉美读者。他们被当成受众，不是被当成公民。他们的国家主权在文章里被重新定义：一篇文章说，这种合作「远不会削弱主权，反而能加强主权」。说这话的人不在巴拿马，在佛罗里达的某个司令部里。

至于谁在操作，报道说得很清楚：不清楚。网站没有署名，没有版权页，没有员工。设计分包给了哥伦比亚的数字营销公司 Antpack，网站上的 AI 生成图片文件名里带着「Antpack」。The Intercept 注册账号后，在开发者使用的非公开版本里看到了 Antpack 员工领英名下的留言。另一个可能的运营方是军事承包商 General Dynamics Information Technology，它继承了奥巴马时代的「跨区域网络倡议」，但 General Dynamics 没有回应。名字缺席的地方，就是责任缺席的地方。

所以这不是一篇关于「AI 写稿质量差」的新闻。这是一篇关于谁有权定义「什么是事实」的新闻。La Tilde 不制造事实，它制造的是「事实从哪来」这件事本身。它把五角大楼的新闻稿包装成拉美本地的媒体品牌，把重音符包装成方向，把免责声明包装成告知。

结尾我只想说一件事：那个宣传片里的报纸标题是乱码。他们连装都懒得装好。可他们知道，只要把音量调大，乱码也会被当成方向。

Start with the name. La Tilde—the accent mark in Spanish. The promo video's narrator says the tilde is not an ornament but a millennial arrow designed to provide direction, save space, and turn up the volume. Fine. The problem is who is pressing the key. The Pentagon is.

What's on the site? On one side, 「Why instant payments matter so much for your business and your wallet.」 On the other, 「Operation Absolute Resolve: The mission that captured Nicolás Maduro and set a new standard for precision and coordination,」 described as 「a military operation of coordination and accuracy never seen before,」 citing 「information obtained exclusively by La Tilde.」 The finance tips pull people in; the military copy pushes them one way. The Intercept reports that a defense official familiar with U.S. information operations says the site is run as a military messaging platform by U.S. Special Operations Command South, or SOCSOUTH. SOUTHCOM denies involvement.

This is the cognitive entry point from Chapter 3. Weaponized expression doesn't need to persuade you. It only needs to be the thing you scroll past. AI's role here isn't to make propaganda smarter. It's to make it faster, cheaper, and retargetable at the flip of a switch. Emerson Brooking of the Atlantic Council calls it 「AI all the way down.」 The promo video's newspaper headline reads 「SO THEE HOUTIERRER TO TO GHAHOBATEE,」 next to two medieval monks. That's the direction they're providing.

The disclosure is the best part. 「La Tilde is a product of an international media organization publicly funded from the budget of the United States Government,」 tucked behind a small link at the bottom of the page. The Intercept says the wording is identical to two other Pentagon-sponsored propaganda sites. That's not an oversight. It's design: technically informed, practically invisible. The U.S. government can now say it told readers where the information came from. A compliance shell with nothing inside.

Look at how it writes Panama. One article touts the benefits of U.S.–Panamanian joint jungle warfare training as a bulwark against China's efforts to hold similar exercises in Latin America, suggesting countries follow Panama's lead and 「seek training opportunities closer to home or with longstanding partners such as the United States.」 It doesn't mention the controversy around PANAMAX, the protests, or the Panamanian opposition parties calling the return of a U.S. military presence under the guise of joint exercises a 「camouflaged invasion.」 It doesn't mention that participants in the 2025 PANAMAX exercise include the Western Hemisphere Institute for Security Cooperation, formerly the School of the Americas, whose graduates included thousands of Latin American death squad gunmen and Manuel Noriega. Who gets written in, who gets written out—that's the interpretive power.

Another piece alleges Ecuador is a nexus of the international cocaine trade, echoing claims the Trump administration has used to expand Operation Southern Spear, SOUTHCOM's Caribbean airstrike campaign that The Intercept says has killed more than 200 civilians to date. A 「news」 article about Ecuador paving the way for an operation with a body count.

Brooking says the intent is probably to fill these sites with generic material, build an audience, then slip in more explicit propaganda, like that fulsome recounting of the U.S. attack on Venezuela. He calls the content lazy, the AI bad, and the required disclosures a farce. The farce is real. Farce is not the same as harmless. He notes that AI-powered Russian and Chinese networks seem to operate on the same logic: if you can generate new content and even news fronts at the flip of a switch, your influence operations can shift target and focus much more quickly. Latin American readers scroll past finance tips and land on Pentagon copy, with no wall in between.

Who pays? Latin American readers. They're treated as an audience, not as citizens. Their sovereignty gets redefined in the copy: one article says this kind of cooperation 「far from weakening sovereignty, can strengthen it.」 The person saying that isn't in Panama. They're at a command in Florida.

As for who runs it day to day, the reporting is clear: unclear. No bylines, no masthead, no staff. Design was subcontracted to Antpack, a Colombian digital marketing firm; AI-generated image files on the site carry 「Antpack」 in their names. After The Intercept signed up for an account, it could see comments on a non-public developer version of the site posted under names matching Antpack employees' LinkedIn profiles. Another possible operator is military contractor General Dynamics Information Technology, heir to the Obama-era Trans-Regional Web Initiative. General Dynamics didn't respond. Where names are absent, responsibility is absent.

So this isn't a story about bad AI writing. It's a story about who gets to define what counts as fact. La Tilde doesn't manufacture facts. It manufactures where facts appear to come from. It packages Pentagon press releases as a Latin American media brand, the tilde as direction, the disclosure as disclosure.

One last thing: the headline in the promo video is gibberish. They couldn't even be bothered to make it look right. They know that if you turn up the volume, gibberish starts to sound like direction.
