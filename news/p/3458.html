养老金补贴：一场关于“体面”的跨代共谋Pension Subsidies: A Cross-Generational Conspiracy of Decency

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
结构性暴力的本质，是用底层的时间与税金，为上位者购买名为“退休”的特权。
The essence of structural violence is using the time and taxes of the bottom to buy a privilege called 'retirement' for the top.

这不只是一次关于税收政策的讨论，而是一场典型的 structural violence。当高薪者享受 40% 的税收减免，而普通纳税人仅获得 20% 时，国家实际上在扮演一个极其卑劣的角色：它利用公共资源，在富人与穷人之间制造更深的鸿沟，并将其包装成“养老保障”这种温情叙事。

这里最令人作呕的是一种跨代的 complicity。那些 50 岁以上的工会代表与管理层，在 2010 年代的罢工中通过谈判，为自己锁定了金灿灿的 Defined Benefit 养老金，却给年轻人留下了一个依赖股市、充满不确定性的 Defined Contribution 烂摊子。这本质上是一次合法的掠夺——老一代通过垄断谈判权，将未来的风险转嫁给尚未入场的后辈，然后心安理得地在 60 岁时步入那个由公共补贴支撑的“舒适茧房”。

更深层的 meta violence 在于对“退休”定义的篡改。退休原本是为那些因病或衰老而无法工作的人提供的 safety net，现在却被武器化为一种身份标志：每年三四次度假、长达 35 年的 R&R。这种叙事让上位者相信，他们之前的“辛勤工作”赋予了他们享受第二次生命的权利。但事实是，真正的 graft（苦力）者在养老金面前几乎是透明的。这种“体面”的定义权被垄断在白领和管理层手中，而底层则在病痛与贫困中被定义为“缺乏技能”或“健康欠佳”。

所谓的“退休梦”，不过是利用公共补贴完成的一次财富转移。当法官、医生和公司董事在为自己的补贴大声疾呼时，他们其实是在维护一个让他们能够心安理得地占便宜的共谋系统。这场 heist 还没有结束，除非我们拆穿这个 scam：没有哪个人的“体面”，应该建立在剥夺另一个人的潜在生存空间之上。

This is not merely a debate over tax policy; it is a textbook case of structural violence. When high earners enjoy a 40% tax break while ordinary taxpayers get only 20%, the state acts as a vile agent, widening the gap between rich and poor under the tender narrative of 'retirement security.'

What is truly nauseating here is the cross-generational complicity. Shop stewards and managers over 50 used the strikes of the 2010s to lock in gold-plated Defined Benefit pensions for themselves, leaving younger workers with the precarious, stock-market-dependent Defined Contribution schemes. This was a legitimate heist—the older generation monopolized the bargaining power to shift future risks onto the newcomers, then retreated into a 'comfortable cocoon' subsidized by the public.

At a deeper level, there is a meta violence in the weaponization of the concept of 'retirement.' Once a safety net for the infirm, it has been redefined as a status symbol: multiple holidays a year and 35 years of R&R. This narrative convinces the elite that their 'hard graft' entitles them to a second lifetime of leisure. In reality, those who actually performed the graft are invisible in the pension system. The power to define 'decency' is monopolized by white-collar management, while the bottom are dismissed as 'unskilled' or 'ill.'

The 'retirement dream' is nothing more than a wealth transfer fueled by public subsidies. When judges and directors scream to protect their pots, they are defending a system of complicity that allows them to profit at others' expense. This heist continues unless we expose the scam: no one's 'decency' should be built on the erosion of another's survival space.
