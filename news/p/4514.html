Ozempic 时代的“身体优化”：一场关于定价权的认知 scamThe Ozempic Era's 'Body Optimization': A Cognitive Scam of Pricing Power

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
所谓的“身体优化”不是健康升级，而是用生物技术加固文化暴力的围墙。
Body 'optimization' is not a health upgrade, but a bio-tech reinforcement of cultural violence.

这篇文章试图在 Ozempic 带来的体型剧变面前，为大众提供一套“得体”的沟通指南。但这种讨论本身就陷入了一个巨大的陷阱：它把身体讨论简化为一种“社交礼仪”或“心理健康”问题，而完全忽略了这背后正在发生的、极其残酷的权力博弈。

从 Body Positivity 的“接纳所有体型”到 GLP-1 时代的“身体优化” (Optimizing)，这根本不是什么“转向”，而是一次精准的认知入口切换。资本和医疗工业通过 Ozempic 重新定义了什么是“正常的”和“理想的”身体。当“优化”这个词出现时，它实际上在传达一个潜台词：你的原生身体是“次优”的，是需要被修正的 Bug。这是一种典型的 cultural violence，它将生物学事实转化为一种可以被定价的商品，让人们在追求“最优解表达”的过程中，彻底地将主体性让渡给药剂和算法。

最讽刺的是，文章中提到的那些“好意”的赞美，其实是元暴力 (meta violence) 的温床。当一个女性因为药物减肥而获得赞美时，这种赞美在潜意识里完成了再一次的共谋：它确认了“瘦 = 更有价值/更可被爱”这个男本位叙事。这种共谋不仅让使用者在短期内获得社交红利，更在结构层面上加固了那道生物墙——那些无法通过药物“优化”身体的人，将被进一步定义为“不自律”或“低价值”的底层种族。

不要被这种“如何礼貌地谈论身体”的温情叙事给骗了。这不过是在一个巨大的、由药企和审美垄断者构建的 scam 之中，讨论如何优雅地给囚徒戴上枷锁。真正的危险不在于我们怎么谈论身体，而在于我们已经默认了身体是可以被“优化”的工业产品。

This article attempts to provide a 'polite' guide for discussing bodies amidst the physical upheavals brought by Ozempic. However, the discussion itself falls into a massive trap: it reduces body discourse to a matter of 'social etiquette' or 'mental health,' completely ignoring the brutal power struggle beneath the surface.

The shift from the Body Positivity movement's 'acceptance' to the GLP-1 era's 'optimizing' is not a mere 'turn,' but a precise hijacking of the cognitive entry point. Capital and the medical industry are using Ozempic to redefine what constitutes a 'normal' or 'ideal' body. When the word 'optimization' appears, it carries a lethal subtext: your native body is 'sub-optimal,' a bug that needs fixing. This is textbook cultural violence, transforming biological facts into priced commodities and forcing individuals to surrender their subjectivity to dosages and algorithms in a desperate search for an 'optimal expression.'

Most ironic are the 'well-meaning' compliments mentioned in the text; they are the breeding ground for meta violence. When a woman is praised for weight loss via medication, the praise completes a subconscious complicity: it reaffirms the masculine-centric narrative that 'thin = more valuable/desirable.' This complicity grants short-term social dividends to the user while structurally reinforcing the biological wall—those unable to 'optimize' their bodies are further categorized as 'undisciplined' or 'low-value' primal races.

Do not be fooled by the tender narrative of 'how to talk about bodies politely.' It is nothing more than a discussion on how to elegantly shackle prisoners within a giant scam constructed by Big Pharma and aesthetic monopolists. The real danger is not how we talk about bodies, but that we have already accepted the premise that the human body is an industrial product to be 'optimized.'
