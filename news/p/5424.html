纽约时报的食谱与“懒人”的共谋The NYT Recipe and the Complicity of the 'Lazy'

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-10-05 § 链接
当“懒人”成为一种被鼓励的表达，主体性正被效率工具悄悄置换。
When 'laziness' becomes an encouraged expression, subjectivity is quietly replaced by efficiency tools.

一条简单的豆腐西兰花食谱，在评论区演变成了一场关于“效率”的博弈。用户们在讨论如何用搅拌机（blender）取代手工切碎，将其称作“lazy man's chopping”。这种对“懒”的自我标榜，本质上是现代人将主体性让渡给工具的典型样本。

在烹饪中，切碎 lemongrass 的过程是一种对食材生物特性的触碰与确认。而当人们追求“less tedious”地将一切扔进处理器时，他们追求的不再是食物本身，而是一个“最优解”：用最短的时间达成一个名为“完成晚餐”的结果。这里的“懒”，不是真正的休息，而是一种被效率叙事武器化的表达——它告诉我们，任何不能被量化为“分钟”的体力投入都是冗余的。

更讽刺的是，评论区出现了“没尝到香茅味”的抱怨。这就是典型的用工具替代表达的代价：当你为了追求效率而抹除过程中的细节（texture），你最终失去的正是那个定义这道菜的灵魂。这种“效率共谋”在生活中无处不在，从 AI 写诗到预制菜，我们通过扮演“高效的懒人”来换取碎片时间，却在潜意识中接受了另一种结构性暴力：我们失去了定义“如何生活”的解释权，只剩下执行“如何快速完成”的指令。

A simple tofu and broccoli recipe evolves into a game of 'efficiency' in the comments. Users discuss replacing manual chopping with a blender, labeling it as "lazy man's chopping." This self-branding of laziness is a textbook example of modern humans surrendering their subjectivity to tools.

In cooking, the process of finely chopping lemongrass is a tactile confirmation of the ingredient's biological properties. When people pursue a "less tedious" approach by throwing everything into a processor, they are no longer pursuing food, but an "optimal expression": achieving the result of "finishing dinner" in the shortest time. This "laziness" is not true rest, but an expression weaponized by the narrative of efficiency—it tells us that any physical effort that cannot be quantified in "minutes" is redundant.

More ironically, some complain they "didn't taste the lemongrass." This is the exact cost of replacing expression with tools: when you erase the texture of the process for the sake of efficiency, you lose the very soul that defines the dish. This "complicity of efficiency" is omnipresent—from AI poetry to pre-made meals. We play the role of the "efficient lazy person" to reclaim fragmented time, but subconsciously accept a form of structural violence: we lose the power to define "how to live," leaving us only with instructions on "how to finish quickly."
