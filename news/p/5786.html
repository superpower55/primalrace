老虎机把穷人做成现金流The Slot Farms That Turn Poverty Into Cash Flow

其他 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
税加五个点，机器照转，人照输。
A five-point tax hike, and the machines keep spinning.

一百英镑在五分钟里消失，剩下一块免费的KitKat。这条街在彼得堡，几乎每家店都是博彩店或成人游戏中心（AGC），很多24小时营业。报道里写得清楚：全英老虎机去年吞掉27亿英镑，接近文化、媒体与体育部全年预算的两倍。

先把机制说清楚。B3机器允许每2.5秒下注2英镑，单次最高赢500英镑。看起来赌注很小，但每台机器一年平均赚4万英镑，比英国工人的中位工资还高。这不是运气生意，是数学生意：返奖率是固定的，长期玩的人一定输。所以它才是一门好生意——收入可预测，像收租。

这台收租机装在哪儿？报道说， disproportionately in poorer areas，不成比例地集中在更穷的地区。这就是结构层在动：不是某个人意志薄弱，而是有人把提款机搬到了没钱的人门口，还配了舒适的扶手椅、65英寸屏幕、免费的饮料零食、抽奖和“免费旋转”，让你不用出门就能取现。

再看文化层。这些店很多挂着宾果牌照开业，因为对地方规划委员会来说，“宾果”听起来无害。报道提到，实际上大多数店里只有一台宾果机，基本不用，甚至不能用。一个词换了个说法，就把一门生意洗成了社区娱乐。这是武器化最便宜的一种：不改变事实，只改变事实的名字。

还有一层更脏的：报道此前揭露，员工的奖金与顾客的亏损挂钩。让一个人去阻止另一个人输钱，同时告诉前者，后者输得越多他拿得越多。这不是监管漏洞，这是设计。Merkur在2025年被罚10万英镑，原因是让一位患晚期癌症的年长女性在两个时段里累计玩了16个小时。10万英镑，对一家靠机器年入4万英镑一台的公司来说，是营业成本。

财政大臣把机器游戏税从20%提到25%，说每年多收近1.5亿英镑，“通过对赌博征税来支持每条商业街的良好增长”。听起来像在打击。但报道里有一句最关键的话：预算公布后，三家上市老虎机运营商的股价大涨，因为投资者原本害怕的是40%。市场读懂了：25%不是惩罚，是准入费。交了这笔钱，生意照做。

所以这条新闻该在哪一层读？直接层是那位16小时的癌症病人，结构层是贫困街区里的24小时提款机，文化层是“宾果牌照”和“安全娱乐”的话术，元层是解释权：行业组织Bacta说自家场所是“最安全的赌博场所之一”，说游戏“低至10便士一次”。10便士一次是真的，一百英镑五分钟也是真的。谁在定义“低风险”，谁就在定义事实。

这不是“个人责任”的问题。一台机器每年赚的比一个工人多，它不需要你失控，只需要你路过。

预算案里还有一句：AGC和电子烟店将被排除在商业税率减免之外，合计多付1000万英镑。1000万，分到每条街，大概够换几块招牌。而报道的结尾是：对老虎机农场来说，一切照旧。

那块KitKat是这场交易里唯一被明确标价的东西。它值多少？一百英镑，五分钟。

A hundred pounds disappears in five minutes, and what's left is a free KitKat. This is a street in Peterborough where nearly every shopfront is a bookmaker or an adult gaming centre (AGC), many open 24 hours a day. The numbers in the report are plain: gamblers in Great Britain lost £2.7bn on slot machines last year, nearly double the annual budget of the department that oversees the sector.

Start with the mechanics. B3 terminals let you stake £2 every 2.5 seconds for a jackpot of up to £500. A £2 maximum stake sounds small until you watch it compound. Each machine earns £40,000 a year on average, more than the median pay of a British worker. This is not a luck business; it is a math business. The return to player is fixed, so regular players always lose. That is precisely what makes it a good cash cow: predictable income, like rent collection.

And where is the rent collector installed? Disproportionately in poorer areas, the report says. That is the structural layer moving: not some individual's weak will, but a withdrawal machine placed at the door of people who have no money, fitted with a comfy armchair, a 65-inch screen, free drinks and snacks, prize draws, free spins, and in-shop cash withdrawal so you never have to leave.

Then the cultural layer. Many of these venues open under bingo licences, because "bingo" sounds benign to a local planning committee. In practice, most have one bingo machine that is rarely used, if ever, and some are not in working order. A word gets swapped, and a business gets laundered into community entertainment. This is the cheapest kind of weaponisation: don't change the fact, change its name.

There is a dirtier layer still. The Guardian previously revealed that staff are paid bonuses linked to customers' losses. Put one person in charge of stopping another from losing, and pay the first according to how much the second loses. That is not a regulatory gap. That is design. Merkur was fined £100,000 in 2025 after allowing an older woman with terminal cancer to rack up huge losses over two spells lasting 16 hours. A hundred thousand pounds, for a company whose machines earn £40,000 a year each, is a line item.

The chancellor raised machine games duty from 20% to 25%, saying it would bring in nearly £150m a year, "raising taxes on gambling to support good growth on every high street". It sounds like a crackdown. But the report contains the decisive sentence: shares in the three listed slot machine operators rose sharply after the budget, because investors had feared 40%. The market read it correctly. Twenty-five percent is not a punishment. It is an entry fee. Pay it, and business continues.

So where should this story be read? The direct layer is the woman who played for 16 hours with terminal cancer. The structural layer is the 24-hour cash machine on a poor high street. The cultural layer is the bingo licence and the "safe entertainment" script. And the meta layer is the question of who gets to define the facts: Bacta, the trade body, says its venues are "among the safest places to gamble" and that games cost "as little as 10p a spin". Ten pence a spin is true. A hundred pounds in five minutes is also true. Whoever defines "low risk" defines reality.

This is not a story about personal responsibility. A machine that out-earns a worker does not need you to lose control. It only needs you to walk past.

The budget also excludes AGCs and vape shops from business rates relief, costing them a combined £10m. Ten million, spread across every high street, buys a few new signs. And the report's closing line is that for the slot farms, it is business as usual.

The KitKat is the only thing in this transaction with a clear price tag. What is it worth? A hundred pounds, five minutes.
