从“贱货”到“女老板”：一场关于解释权的夺回战From 'Slag' to 'Boss': A War for the Right of Interpretation

性别 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-05 § 链接
当一个女性决定不再扮演受害者，她就开始夺取定义自己的定价权。
When a woman stops playing the victim, she begins to seize the pricing power of her own identity.

Tulisa的故事不是一个简单的“励志回归”，而是一场典型的存在性战争。在2012年到2013年那个时间点，她遭遇的是一套完整的武器化叙事：前男友泄露私密视频，小报记者用30万英镑的诱饵制造陷阱。在这套叙事里，她被精准地标记为“slag”（贱货）和“rough around the edges”（粗鄙的底层女），这种标记本质上是文化层面的暴力，目的是通过定义她的“低端”身份，让她在社会博弈中彻底丧失票值。

最恶心的是这种共谋机制：媒体制造丑闻，大众消费刻板印象，而法律系统在最初阶段成了这种叙事的执行工具。这种 Meta Violence（元暴力）告诉所有女性，如果你不符合“纯洁”或“得体”的设定，你就不配拥有职业生涯，甚至不配拥有真相。Tulisa在那个阶段的“最优解表达”是崩溃和自杀尝试，因为在那个被男性中心叙事垄断的解释权体系里，她没有一个能让自己生存的合法角色。

现在她启动的 Fix the Press 运动，本质上是在 structural 层面对抗这种暴力。她意识到，单纯的个人疗愈（inner work）只能解决情绪，而真正的胜利必须是夺回解释权。她不再试图证明自己是“好女孩”，而是直接攻击那个制造“坏女孩”定义权的机器——那些自监管的媒体组织。当她把“Female Boss”从一个专辑名称变成一个真实的商业身份，并试图通过立法削弱小报的权力时，她实际上是在缩小 Potential 和 Actual 之间的差额。

但我们要警惕那种“我已经原谅/我爱现在的自己”的叙事陷阱。这种温情主义往往会掩盖结构性暴力的残酷。Tulisa的胜利是基于她拥有版权费等经济资本的支撑，而绝大多数被泄露私密视频或被诬陷的女性，在没有资本支撑的情况下，根本无法启动这场夺权战。她们的主体性在被标记为“贱货”的那一刻起，就已经在社会性意义上被杀死了。

Tulisa’s story is not a simple 'comeback' tale; it is a textbook existential war. Between 2012 and 2013, she was hit by a synchronized weaponized narrative: an ex-boyfriend leaking sex tapes and a tabloid reporter spending £300k to engineer a sting. In this narrative, she was precisely tagged as a 'slag' and 'rough around the edges.' This tagging is cultural violence designed to strip her of any social value by defining her as 'low-class.'

The most sinister part is the complicity: tabloids manufacture the scandal, the public consumes the stereotype, and the legal system initially acts as the enforcement arm of this narrative. This is Meta Violence in action, signaling to women that if they deviate from the 'pure' or 'proper' script, they forfeit their careers and their truth. In that era, Tulisa’s only 'optimal expression' was collapse and suicide attempts, because within a masculine-centric narrative, there was no legitimate role for her to inhabit.

Her current 'Fix the Press' campaign is a strategic move to combat this at the structural level. She realized that personal therapy only heals the emotion; true victory requires seizing the right of interpretation. She is no longer trying to prove she is a 'good girl'; instead, she is attacking the machine that defines 'bad girls'—the self-regulating press. By transforming 'Female Boss' from a marketing slogan into a real business identity and fighting for legislative change, she is narrowing the gap between Potential and Actual.

However, we must be wary of the 'I love who I am now' narrative. This kind of sentimentalism often masks the brutality of structural violence. Tulisa’s victory was made possible by her financial capital—royalties that kept her afloat. For the vast majority of women whose private lives are weaponized without such resources, there is no such war to be fought. Their subjectivity is murdered the moment the 'slag' tag is applied.
