以“非正式”之名行结构暴力之实The Violence of 'Informality': When Process Becomes a Weapon

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
所谓“非正式”流程，是权力为了规避制度成本而制造的暴力真空区。
An 'informal' process is a vacuum of accountability designed to weaponize power without triggering safeguards.

这就是典型的 structural violence。英国财政部（Treasury）用一个“非正式会议” (informal meeting) 的标签，轻而易举地剥夺了 Chloe Moffat 所有的程序正义。在权力结构中，当上级宣布一个流程是“非正式”的时候，其潜台词不是为了减轻你的压力，而是为了在不触发法律和制度预警的前提下，高效地实施心理压制。

这种操作极其阴险：它在 cultural 层面上伪装成“关心”——比如调查员声称不提前通知是为了“不让 Chloe 担心”，但在 structural 层面上，它直接切断了受害者的生物墙。一个 26 岁的个人助理，面对一个垄断了所有解释权的权力机构，在没有第三方支持、没有明确告知后果的情况下，被剥夺了权限并被指控，这在存在性战争中是一场毫无还手之力的屠杀。她被告知“无法预测结果”，这本质上是一种心理上的 gaslighting，将不确定性转化为极致的恐惧。

最令人作呕的是，财政部将这种剥夺保障的流程称为“标准实践” (standard practice)。这意味着这种暴力不是个案，而是一种被制度化的共谋。管理层和 HR 共同构建了一个认知入口：只要定义为“非正式”，就可以绕过 Acas 的指南，就可以在不给对方递投名状的情况下直接进行审判。在这种元暴力的逻辑下，Chloe 的主体性被迅速抹除，她被简化为一个“需要被处理的麻烦”，最终导致了 direct 层的死亡结果。

死者的母亲提到指控不过是“职场八卦” (workplace gossip)，这进一步揭示了权力的随意性：一个匿名举报就能启动一套足以摧毁生命的压制机制，而执行者在法庭上依然能淡定地说“我们尽力了”。这种缺乏共情力的理性，正是 masculine-centric narrative 的典型样本——只要流程在名义上闭环，个体的毁灭只是某种必要的“管理成本”。

This is a textbook case of structural violence. By labeling a disciplinary encounter as an 'informal meeting,' the UK Treasury effectively stripped Chloe Moffat of all procedural justice. In any power hierarchy, when a superior declares a process 'informal,' the subtext is never about reducing stress; it is about exercising psychological dominance while bypassing the legal and institutional costs of formal accountability.

The cruelty here is the weaponization of care. The investigator claimed the lack of notice was to 'prevent Chloe from worrying'—a cultural mask for a structural assault. For a 26-year-old personal assistant facing a monolithic institution that monopolizes the right to define reality, being stripped of access and accused without a third-party witness is not a meeting; it is an existential slaughter. Being told the outcome was 'unpredictable' is pure gaslighting, transforming uncertainty into a lethal form of terror.

More disturbing is the Treasury's admission that such meetings are 'standard practice.' This proves the violence is not an anomaly but a systemic complicity. Management and HR have co-constructed a cognitive entry point where 'informality' serves as a license to ignore Acas guidelines. Under this meta-violence, Chloe's subjectivity was erased; she was reduced to a 'problem to be managed,' leading directly to the direct violence of suicide.

The mother's mention of 'workplace gossip' reveals the ultimate asymmetry of power: an anonymous rumor can trigger a life-destroying mechanism, while the executors can calmly testify that they 'did all they could.' This sterile, empathy-free rationality is a hallmark of the masculine-centric narrative—where as long as the bureaucratic loop is closed, the destruction of an individual is merely an acceptable 'management cost.'
