非洲杯改回两年一届，谁在付账Afcon Goes Back to Two Years. Who Pays?

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
赛程表的每一次改动，都是一次定价。
Every schedule change is a price being set.

非洲杯从四年改回两年一届，Caf 说这是为了非洲足球的财务和竞技发展，是为了“在非洲以外俱乐部效力的非洲球员的利益”。最后这一句是整份声明里最站不住的一句。球员的利益是少飞、少伤、少在赛季中段被抽走，两年一届正好相反。真正被照顾的是 Caf 自己的现金流：声明里写得很直白，替代赛事 Africa Nations League 要“好几年”才能有财务上的可行性，等不起。等不起的是主办方的账本，不是球员的膝盖。

这就是表达被武器化的标准动作：先定义一个词，再让这个词替你干活。“发展”“竞争力”“自给自足”，听起来都是公共利益，落点是赛事收入。Motsepe 把话说得很诚实——新赛事要几年才能“make the substantial financial and football development contributions”，所以还是回到老赛事。翻译过来：我们试过换一个产品，产品回本太慢，于是换回原来那个。球员在这个句子里是宾语，不是主语。

代价的分配也很清楚。俱乐部每两年失去一次主力，俱乐部付工资，Caf 收转播费和赞助。球员每两年多一次高强度赛会制比赛，身体是他们自己的，折旧也是他们自己的。声明里说这是“in the interests of African players”，但整份声明没有一个字提到球员的休息、伤病或合同风险，也没有一个字提到谁为多出来的那一届买单。说替谁好，和让谁付账，是两件事。

同一页新闻里还有另一条线：曼城女足在欧冠联赛阶段三连平，主帅 Jeglertz 说球队“deserved more”，现在大概需要在最后三轮全胜才能避开二月的附加赛。男足的赛程在加码，女足的赛程在挤压，两边都由同一套机构排布，排布的逻辑都是赛事产品的密度，不是人的承受力。女足运动员的赛历这几年被塞得越来越满，理由永远是“发展”，和发展的收益归谁，从来不需要写进新闻稿。

2031 年那一届被推到三年后，因为 2030 年是世界杯年，Fifa 的传统不允许撞车。世界足球的日历由 Fifa 和各大洲联合会协商排定，球员不在谈判桌上。这就是结构层的东西：不是某个人坏，是排赛程的房间里没有身体在场。

非洲杯两年一届本身不是罪，非洲足球需要比赛、需要收入、需要可见度，这些都是真的。问题在于这份声明把商业决策包装成球员福利，而这个包装一旦被接受，下一次加码就不需要解释了。球员的合同年里会多出一行风险条款，俱乐部会多算一笔保险，这些成本会一路传下去，最后落在具体的人身上——一个二十七岁的边锋，在赛季最关键的三周里，为自己的国家队踢满每一分钟，然后回到俱乐部养伤，工资照发，位置没了。

赛程表上没有受害者的名字，只有年份。

Afcon goes back to a two-year cycle, and Caf says it is for African football's finances and development, and for the interests of African players employed by clubs outside Africa. That last clause is the weakest sentence in the statement. A player's interest is fewer flights, fewer injuries, fewer mid-season departures. A two-year cycle delivers the opposite. What it protects is Caf's own cash flow, and the statement says so plainly: the replacement Africa Nations League would need several years before it could make the substantial financial contributions. What could not wait was the ledger, not the knees.

This is the standard move. Define a word, then let the word do the work. Development, competitiveness, self-supporting: all of it sounds like the public good, and all of it lands on tournament revenue. Motsepe is being honest. They tried a new product, the product was too slow to pay back, so they reverted to the old one. The player is the object of that sentence, never its subject.

The cost is distributed just as cleanly. Clubs lose key players every two years and pay their wages; Caf collects broadcast and sponsorship. Players absorb another compressed tournament, and the wear on their bodies is theirs. The statement says this is in the interests of African players, yet not one line mentions rest, injury, or contract risk, and not one line says who pays for the extra edition. Claiming to serve someone and making them pay are two different things.

On the same page: Manchester City's women draw their third consecutive Champions League league-phase game, and Andrée Jeglertz says the side deserved more, with City probably needing to win all three remaining matchdays to avoid the February play-offs. The men's calendar gets denser, the women's gets squeezed, and both are set by the same bodies, on the same logic of product density rather than human capacity. Women's football has been fed an ever-fuller calendar for years, always under the word development, and who collects the returns never has to appear in the press release.

The 2031 edition is pushed three years out because 2030 is a World Cup year and Fifa tradition forbids a clash. The global calendar is negotiated between Fifa and the confederations. Players are not in the room. That is the structural layer: not a villain, just a scheduling meeting with no bodies present.

A two-year Afcon is not a crime. African football needs matches, revenue, visibility, and all of that is real. The problem is that a commercial decision is dressed as player welfare, and once that dressing is accepted, the next increase needs no explanation. Clubs will add a line to their insurance, players will carry a new clause in a contract year, and the cost travels down until it lands on one person: a twenty-seven-year-old winger who plays every minute for his country in the three most important weeks of the season, comes back injured, keeps his wages, and loses his place.

The schedule has no names on it. Only years.
