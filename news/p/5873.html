瘦子变多了，话却变少了Everyone Is Thin, Nobody Says So

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
身体积极死了，留下的是更精密的定价权
Body positivity died and left behind a more precise pricing machine.

这篇报道最狠的一句不是数据，是那位前活动人士说的：身体积极运动"只关注个人和个体"，从没想过"改变或拆解系统"。一句话，这个运动的命门就交代了。

身体积极在2010年代末的高峰期，做的是一件表达层面的事：它改变了语言。2000年代，维多利亚·贝克汉姆可以在直播里被称体重，拉格菲尔德可以说"没人想看曲线女人"，西蒙·考威尔对一个19岁的选秀女孩说你太胖当不了流行歌手，莎伦·奥斯本微笑着补一句"去节食吧"。那时候这些是明面上的规训，粗暴，但看得见。身体积极把这套话术打掉了——胖羞辱成了禁忌，节食闲聊成了失礼。这是真实的胜利，cultural violence 的入口被堵上了一块。

但它堵的是嘴，不是结构。报道里那位模特兼设计师说得很清楚：品牌找大码模特来走秀，却根本不生产那个尺码的衣服。Paloma Elsesser 上了2022年 I-D 封面，穿的是 Miu Miu，而 Miu Miu 不做她的码。走秀是表达，生产是结构；表达改了，结构没动。一个只改表达不改结构的运动，最后能留下的只有语言——而语言是最好被征用的东西。

征用它的第一家公司叫 Eli Lilly。赞助 Vogue World Milano，理由是"关于身体和健康的对话发生在这里"，目标是"把关于体重的对话从审美转向健康"。在一个97.5%的模特穿四码及以下的秀场上，把对话从审美转向健康。这话术的妙处在于：它把"瘦"重新包装成"健康"，于是审美压迫不再需要审美作借口，它有了医学背书。加尔通说文化暴力是让结构暴力看起来不像暴力——这里是让审美暴力看起来像医疗建议。

所以现在形成了一个非常精确的局面：瘦被前所未有地奖励，说瘦却成了禁忌。97%的走秀由直码模特完成，2023到2024是95%，两年涨了两个点；而公开场合没人承认自己在追求瘦。健身网红谈的是宏量营养素不是卡路里，是"紧致"不是"瘦"，是身体成分扫描不是体重秤。语言升级了，目标没变。禁忌没有保护任何人，它只是把定价过程挪到了看不见的地方——挪进了私信、评论区、和每个人对自己身体的沉默审计里。

报道里那些被点名的女人——Lizzo、Meghan Trainor、Oprah、Adele——被当成"原来我们心底都想瘦"的证据。这个归因是错的，也是共谋者理论最典型的操作：把结构性的压力结算到个体身上，让几个女人替一整套机器挨骂。Adele 的回应其实很准确："验证别人对自己身体的感受不是我的工作。"她只是没说出后半句——那也不是她的错。

真正该被追问的是谁在共谋、回报是什么。Eli Lilly 拿到了最有说服力的广告位：高级时装秀场。Vogue 拿到了赞助费和"健康"这层体面的皮。平台拿到了流量——报道里那位教授说得直白，灰区内容被算法推给本来没在找它的人，因为它"触达更大的人群，更赚钱"。禁令禁的是 #SkinnyTok 这种明面上的标签，禁不了灰区，因为灰区才是利润。

身体积极留下的真正遗产，是一个被拆掉了引信的词：它教会了所有人怎么谈论身体，然后这个词被拿去给完全相反的东西做包装。报道结尾说 Joey King 公开自己36小时断食、"我不提倡这个"，网上几乎没人反弹，反而用身体积极时代的语言说她"辟谣"了整容传闻。诚实被奖励了，代价是断食本身没人再问。

这就是差额所在：语言这一层被削掉了一块，结构那一层一寸没动，而结构现在学会了说语言的话。97.5%这个数字不是新闻，它是旧闻——2000年代的秀场也是这样。变的是我们再也不能直接说出来了。

The sharpest line in this piece isn't a statistic. It's a former activist saying body positivity only ever focused on "the personal and individual" and never tried to "change or dismantle systems." That single sentence is the movement's death certificate.

At its late-2010s peak, body positivity did something real at the level of expression: it changed the language. In the 2000s Victoria Beckham could be weighed on live TV, Karl Lagerfeld could say no one wants to see curvy women, Simon Cowell could tell a 19-year-old she was too overweight to be a pop star while Sharon Osbourne smiled and told her to go on a diet. That was discipline out in the open, brutal but visible. Body positivity killed that script. Fat shaming became taboo, diet chat became rude. A real win: one entrance to cultural violence got bricked up.

But it bricked up mouths, not structures. The model and couturier in the piece spells it out: brands cast plus-sized models and then didn't make clothes in their sizes. Paloma Elsesser covered I-D in 2022 wearing Miu Miu, and Miu Miu didn't produce her size. The runway is expression; production is structure. Expression changed, structure didn't move. A movement that only rewrites language ends up leaving behind nothing but language, and language is the easiest thing in the world to requisition.

The first company to requisition it was Eli Lilly. Sponsoring Vogue World Milano because "that is where conversations about bodies and health take place," aiming to shift the conversation "from aesthetics to health." At a show where 97.5% of models were a size four or under. The trick is elegant: relabel thinness as health, and aesthetic coercion no longer needs an aesthetic excuse. It has a medical one. Galtung's point exactly. Cultural violence exists to make structural violence stop looking like violence. Here it makes aesthetic violence look like a prescription.

So we arrive at a very precise arrangement. Thinness is rewarded more than ever; saying so is forbidden. Ninety-seven per cent of looks on straight-size models, up from 95% in 2023-24, two points in two years, while nobody in public admits to wanting to be thin. Fitness influencers talk macros, not calories. Snatched, not skinny. Body-composition scans, not scales. The vocabulary upgraded; the target never moved. The taboo protects no one. It just relocates the pricing into private messages, comment sections, and the silent audit every person runs on their own body.

The women named in the piece, Lizzo, Meghan Trainor, Oprah, Adele, get filed as proof that deep down we all wanted to be thin anyway. That attribution is wrong, and it is the co-conspirator move par excellence: settle a structural pressure onto individuals and let a few women absorb the blame for an entire machine. Adele's reply was actually accurate. It's not her job to validate how people feel about their bodies. She just left out the second half. It isn't her fault either.

The question worth asking is who is conspiring and what they're paid. Eli Lilly bought the most persuasive ad slot there is, a luxury runway. Vogue bought a sponsor and a coat of health paint. The platforms bought traffic. The professor in the piece says it plainly: grey-area content gets funneled to people who weren't looking for it, because it reaches bigger audiences and generates more profit. Banning #SkinnyTok bans the label. It can't ban the grey area, because the grey area is the business.

What body positivity actually left behind is a word with the fuse pulled: it taught everyone how to talk about bodies, and then that vocabulary got repurposed to package the opposite. The piece ends with Joey King disclosing a 36-hour fast, saying she doesn't promote it, and meeting almost no pushback. Headlines framed it in body-positive language, as her shutting down rumours. Honesty got rewarded. The fasting itself stopped being a question.

That's the gap. One layer of language got chipped away. The structural layer didn't move an inch, and the structural layer has now learned to speak the language. 97.5% isn't news. It's old news; the runways looked like this in the 2000s too. What changed is that we can no longer say it out loud.
