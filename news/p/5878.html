鬼故事里关着的是人The Ghost Story Is Hiding the Prisoner

文娱 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把囚禁讲成闹鬼，是替施暴者保管记忆。
Turning imprisonment into a haunting is how the jailer keeps the record.

万圣节榜单上最"闹鬼"的地方，好几处根本不是鬼，是人被关进去过的地方。Dunnottar Castle 的 Whigs Vault，1685 年关过一百六十多名囚犯，条件恶劣到难以想象；Shepton Mallet 监狱 1625 年启用，做过军事监狱，关过 Kray 双胞胎。读者站在黑屋子里说"不可能不去想象他们的恐惧与痛苦"，然后把这份恐惧命名为 spooky，收进节日清单。

这就是文化暴力最经济的一种运作方式：不需要否认苦难，只需要给它换一个用途。苦难本身被承认了，被承认的方式是它成了可供消费的氛围。历史在这里没有被抹掉，被抹掉的是"这是暴力"这个判断。加尔通的公式还在，Potential 减去 Actual 的差额还在，只是差额被重新标价，成了 goosebumps。

更精确的一点：被关进 Whigs Vault 的那些人，是政治与宗教的囚犯，他们被囚禁的原因是权力不允许他们说话。今天他们的牢房成了景点，游客花钱进去感受"阴森"。解释权完成了一次漂亮的转移——从"谁把他们关在这里"转向"这里的风听起来像什么"。前者要求追责，后者只要求想象力。

榜单里也有另一种材料。Nant Gwrtheyrn 的故事是 Rhys 和 Meinir，一场婚前的捉迷藏，新娘躲进树洞再没出来，多年后闪电劈开树干，露出她的骨骸。这个故事被当作浪漫悲剧讲，讲的人自己也说"浪漫"。一个准新娘在婚礼前消失、被找到时是一具骨架，这中间没有浪漫，只有一个人死在了别人看不见的地方。它被写进民俗、写进旅游文案、写成"传统"，于是没人再问：她当时有没有喊。

Cannock Chase 被顺手提了一句"许多谋杀案的发生地"，然后立刻回到酒馆的地窖和阁楼。谋杀案是背景音，鬼才是主角。

我不反对去这些地方，也不反对在秋天想被吓一跳。但"被吓一跳"和"知道这里发生过什么"是两件事，前者需要模糊，后者需要精确。这份榜单从头到尾都在做前一件事：把具体的死者变成气氛，把施暴者的名字省略掉，只留下风、海浪和石头。

Whigs Vault 的墙上写着好几个 Fraser 的名字，读者说因为自己的 Fraser 血统而格外触动。名字还在墙上，但没有人被追究。鬼故事最好用的一点就在这里——鬼不会起诉任何人。

Several entries on this Halloween list are not haunted places. They are places where people were locked up. The Whigs Vault at Dunnottar Castle held more than 160 prisoners in dreadful conditions in 1685. Shepton Mallet prison opened in 1625 and served as a military prison. A reader stands in that dark chamber and says it is impossible not to imagine their fear and suffering — then files the fear under spooky and adds it to a holiday itinerary.

This is cultural violence at its most economical. The suffering is not denied. It is simply reassigned. The suffering is acknowledged, and the acknowledgment is that it makes good atmosphere. Nothing is erased except the judgment that this was violence. Galtung's formula still holds — the gap between potential and actual is still there — but the gap has been repriced as goosebumps.

Be precise about who was in that vault: political and religious prisoners, held because the powers of the day would not let them speak. Their cell is now an attraction where visitors pay to feel eerie. The interpretive authority has shifted cleanly, from who put them there to what the wind sounds like against the stone. The first question demands accountability. The second only demands imagination.

There is other material here too. At Nant Gwrtheyrn the story is Rhys and Meinir: a prenuptial game of hide and seek, the bride hides in a tree hollow and never comes out, and years later lightning splits the trunk and reveals her skeleton. The entry calls it a romantic tragedy. A woman vanishes before her wedding and is found as bones, and the word attached to it is romantic. It has been folded into folklore, into tourism copy, into tradition — so no one asks whether she called out.

Cannock Chase gets one clause as the scene of many murders, then the text moves on to dank cellars and creaking attics. The murders are background noise. The ghosts are the main event.

I am not against visiting these places, or against wanting a scare in autumn. But being scared and knowing what happened here are two different things. The first needs blur. The second needs precision. This list does the first thing from beginning to end: it turns specific dead people into atmosphere, omits the names of those who did the locking, and leaves only wind, waves and stone.

The names of several Frasers are written on the wall of the Whigs Vault, and the reader is especially moved because of Fraser ancestry. The names are still there. Nobody has been held to account. That is the best thing about a ghost story: ghosts never sue anyone.
