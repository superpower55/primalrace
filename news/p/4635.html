所谓的“经济D-Day”不过是一场昂贵的男性自嗨The 'Economic D-Day' is Just an Expensive Masculine Ego Trip

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-30 § 链接
将经济绞杀定义为“胜利”的策略，本质是元暴力的低级重复。
Defining economic strangulation as 'victory' is merely a low-level repetition of meta-violence.

特朗普这次把经济制裁起名为“Operation Economic Outcast”，甚至用到了“D-Day”这种带有浓厚军事色彩的词汇。这种表达方式极其典型：它试图将枯燥的金融封锁包装成一场英雄主义的战争。在男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 中，胜利被定义为对方的“投降” (capitulation) 或“崩溃” (collapse)。在这种逻辑里，过程中的平民苦难——高通胀、生活水平下降——被视为达成目标的必要成本，或者干脆被视为一种隐形的 structural violence，在宏大叙事的掩盖下变得不可见。

这种策略的本质是典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 运作。它不关心一个真实的、可持续的公正表达 (Just Expressions)，而只关心谁在博弈中占据了绝对的支配地位。特朗普认为增加压力就等于拥有战略，这其实是对“力量”最浅薄的理解。他试图通过制造一个极端的生存压力环境，强迫对方在“死亡”与“屈服”之间做选择。但这种博弈忽略了对方同样在寻找自己的最优解表达：伊朗的选择是 endurance（忍耐）加上 reroute（重定向），通过将经济绞杀的成本转嫁给全球金融系统，让这场游戏变得 prohibitively expensive。

最讽刺的是，这场所谓的“经济D-Day”在面对中国时陷入了逻辑死循环。当北京通过法律手段惩罚那些遵守美国制裁的公司时，美国发现自己陷入了另一种武器化叙事的反噬。在这种存在性战争中，美国试图垄断全球经济的解释权和定价权，但当这个权力不再被认同，它的所有威胁都变成了廉价的表演。正如其财政部长所言，他不想“炸掉全球金融系统”，这意味着这场战争的 Potential 远高于 Actual，而其中的差额就是被无数普通人承担的暴力。

Trump’s naming of economic sanctions as “Operation Economic Outcast,” and specifically invoking “D-Day,” is a classic move. It attempts to wrap financial blockade in the cloak of heroic militarism. In a masculine-centric narrative, victory is defined solely by the opponent’s “capitulation” or “collapse.” In this logic, the suffering of ordinary civilians—hyperinflation and collapsing living standards—is dismissed as a necessary cost or a hidden form of structural violence, rendered invisible by the grandeur of the narrative.

This strategy is a textbook operation of meta-violence. It doesn't seek a sustainable Just Expression but only cares about who holds the dominant position in the game. Trump confuses ramping up pressure with having a strategy, revealing a shallow understanding of power. He tries to force a choice between “death” and “submission” by creating an extreme environment of survival pressure. However, he ignores that Iran is seeking its own optimal expression: a combination of endurance and rerouting, making the cost of economic strangulation prohibitively expensive for the global financial system.

The irony peaks when this “Economic D-Day” hits the wall of Chinese resistance. As Beijing penalizes firms that comply with US sanctions, the US finds its own weaponized narrative rebounding. In this existential war, the US attempts to monopolize the interpretation and pricing power of the global economy, but when that power is no longer recognized, the threats become mere performance. As the Treasury Secretary admitted, he doesn't want to “blow up the global financial system,” proving that the gap between the potential and actual state of this conflict is simply a void filled with violence borne by ordinary people.
