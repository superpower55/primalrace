燃烧的警察与失踪的被告A Burning Officer and the Missing Defendants

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
暴力三角里，直接层被看见，结构层被消音。
The direct layer gets a charge; the structural layer gets silence.

法国检方以"蓄意谋杀公职人员"立案，因为一枚燃烧瓶落在了一名宪兵的腿上。这判断是对的，直接暴力就该被这样对待。但同一份新闻稿里，被点名的暴力只有这一种。

数百万人上街，第三次，为的是生活成本上涨和公共服务恶化。这是结构性暴力最标准的形态：没有施暴者，没有血，只有一条缓慢下沉的基线——房租、能源、医院、学校。加尔通的公式里，暴力等于潜在减去实际，差额就是暴力。燃烧瓶被算进了这个差额，几十万人为什么上街则不被算进去。检方给一个词定性："蓄意谋杀"。而让他们走到街上的那个东西，连一个罪名都没有。

然后看谁在收割这场火。勒庞说，肇事者必须"受到最严厉的惩罚"。总统选举还有六个月，她是极右翼的领跑者，承诺恢复"法律与秩序"。一枚燃烧瓶落在宪兵腿上，对她是礼物——她不需要解释为什么公共服务在恶化，她只需要指认街上有暴徒。烧伤是真的，二度烧伤，十三名同单位成员受伤，全国一百五十九名执法人员受伤，七百七十人被捕。这些数字都成立。而它们被组装成的叙事是：问题出在街上，不在预算表上。

共谋不需要密谋。内政部长说暴力程度"非同寻常"，检方立案，极右翼喊严惩，三方各取所需，谁都不必回答那几百万人最初的问题。这就是元暴力在运作：把解释权收归到"秩序"这一侧，让结构性的账目消失在"暴徒"这个词背后。

那名宪兵说他感觉"像是被热度本身裹住了"。他说的是一瞬间。街上的人说的是几年。报道没有写任何一位抗议者为什么站在那儿，没有写公共服务恶化到什么程度，没有写生活成本涨了多少——不是记者漏了，是这条新闻的结构里本来就没有他们的位置。谁的名字没出现，往往就是最该问的地方。

烧伤会愈合，调查会继续，选举会到来。差额还在那儿，一分没少。

French prosecutors opened an attempted-murder investigation because a petrol bomb landed at an officer's feet. That judgment is correct. Direct violence should be treated this way. But only one kind of violence gets named in this story.

Hundreds of thousands of people took to the streets for a third weekend, against soaring living costs and deteriorating public services. That is structural violence in its textbook form: no perpetrator, no blood, just a slowly sinking baseline—rent, energy, hospitals, schools. In Galtung's formula, violence is potential minus actual, and the gap is the violence. The Molotov cocktail gets counted into that gap. Why hundreds of thousands marched does not. The prosecutors supply a phrase: "attempted murder." The thing that put people on the street doesn't even get a charge.

Now watch who harvests the fire. Le Pen says those responsible must be "punished with the utmost severity." The presidential election is six months away; she is the far-right frontrunner, promising to restore law and order. A petrol bomb at a gendarme's leg is a gift to her—she doesn't have to explain why public services are deteriorating, she only has to point at the street. The burns are real: second-degree, thirteen members of the officer's unit injured, 159 law enforcement personnel injured nationwide, 770 arrests. Every number holds. And the narrative they are assembled into says the problem is on the street, not in the budget line.

Conspiracy requires no conspiracy. The interior minister calls the violence "extraordinary," prosecutors open a case, the far right demands severity—three parties each getting what they need, none of them obliged to answer the question those hundreds of thousands started with. This is meta-violence at work: pulling the power of explanation back to the side of "order," letting the structural ledger vanish behind the word "rioter."

The gendarme said he felt as though he had been coated by the heat itself. He is describing a moment. The people in the street are describing years. The report never says why a single protester was standing there, never says how far public services have deteriorated, never says how much living costs have risen—not because the reporter missed it, but because their position was never built into this story. Whose names are absent is usually where the question should go.

The burns will heal, the investigation will proceed, the election will arrive. The gap is still there, not one unit smaller.
