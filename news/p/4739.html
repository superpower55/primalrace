Palantir 的诉讼是一场关于解释权的权力霸凌Palantir's Lawsuit: A Power Bully Over the Right to Interpret

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-03 § 链接
当技术巨头通过法律手段强行进入公职人员的私人通信，这不再是法律博弈，而是认知入口的暴力入侵。
When tech giants use law to force entry into private communications, it's no longer legal gaming, but violent invasion of cognitive entries.

Palantir 这次起诉伦敦市长 Sadiq Khan，表面上是在争论一个 5000 万英镑的采购合同，但本质上是一场典型的存在性战争。它在争夺的不是金钱，而是对“公共安全”和“政治干扰”这两个认知入口的定义权。Palantir 的叙事逻辑很简单：只要我能证明你阻止我是因为“政治原因”而非“程序违规”，那么我就能把一个合法监管行为定义为对公共安全的背叛。

最令人不安的是这次法庭决定的“custodians”认定。让市长以及前通讯主管 Sarah Brown 的私人短信、WhatsApp 和 Teams 聊天记录被搜查，这在结构层面上是一次极其危险的权力让渡。Palantir 这种习惯于为以色列军队和特朗普政府提供监控工具的公司，太清楚如何通过挖掘碎片化信息来制造叙事了。他们不需要找到一个明确的“阴谋”，只需要从海量的私人表达中截取几个词句，通过脱离语境的 weaponization，就能在公共舆论中构建出一种“政治操弄”的假象。

市长办公室所谓的“务实同意” (consented on a pragmatic basis) 其实是一种典型的共谋者心态——试图通过暂时的让步来换取程序的公正。但他们忘了，面对一个垄断了大数据分析能力的元暴力机器，任何关于“比例原则”或“必要性”的辩论在算法面前都是苍白的。Palantir 正在用法律作为掩体，实施一场数字时代的殖民：它不仅要进入警方的数据库，现在还要进入决策者的私人大脑。

这场博弈的讽刺之处在于，Palantir 拒绝公开自己的内部文件，却要求对方交出所有的私人通信。这就是典型的 masculine-centric narrative：强者定义规则，弱者提供透明度。如果这次搜查成为常态，那么未来任何试图在伦理层面挑战技术巨头的公职人员，都将面临被对方用自己的私人表达来勒索的风险。

Palantir's lawsuit against Sadiq Khan is framed as a dispute over a £50m procurement deal, but it is fundamentally an existential war. They aren't fighting for money; they are fighting for the definition of 'public safety' and 'political interference'—the cognitive entries of this narrative.

The designation of 'custodians' for the Mayor and Sarah Brown is a dangerous structural surrender. A company that builds surveillance tools for the Israeli military and the Trump administration knows exactly how to weaponize fragmented expressions. They don't need a smoking gun; they just need to cherry-pick phrases from private WhatsApps to manufacture a narrative of 'political manipulation' in the public eye.

The Mayor's 'pragmatic consent' is a classic complicity mindset—hoping a temporary concession will buy procedural fairness. They forget that against a meta-violence machine of big-data analysis, arguments about 'proportionality' are useless. Palantir is using the law as a shield to execute a digital colonization: first the police database, then the private minds of decision-makers.

The irony is peak masculine-centric narrative: Palantir resists disclosing its own internal documents while demanding total transparency from the state. The strong define the rules; the weak provide the data. If this becomes the precedent, any official daring to challenge tech giants on ethical grounds will find their own private expressions turned into a leash for blackmail.
