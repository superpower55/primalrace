童话的剧本，永远由胜利者书写Fairytales Are Written by the Victors

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-20 § 链接
所谓的“童话”不过是强者对弱者失败的礼貌性同情。
The so-called 'fairytale' is merely a polite form of compassion from the strong toward the failed.

这篇报道在试图用“童话破碎” (fairytale crumbles) 来包装一场典型的存在性战争。Manuel Elvira 的崩溃被描述为一种“体育折磨”，而这种叙事本身就是一种 cultural violence。它将一个职业球员在博弈中的失误，强行拉入一种感伤的、非理性的文学框架里，目的就是为了让读者在潜意识中把 Elvira 定位成一个“可怜的受害者”，而非一个在竞技场上输掉票数的博弈者。

真正的权力逻辑在 JJ Spaun 身上展现得淋漓尽致。Spaun 带着“被忽视的愤怒” (chip on my shoulder) 参赛，这种愤怒是他寻找真.最优解表达的燃料。他在公共空间通过胜利夺回了被剥夺的解释权——从“被遗忘的球员”变成了“无法被忽视的冠军”。这才是这场比赛真正的政治学：用结果强行修改规则制定者的认知入口。

最讽刺的是报道末尾关于 Clarkson’s Farm 和面具的细节。这种将体育胜利与消费主义文化符号（Jeremy Clarkson）绑定的表达，实际上是在完成一次阶级与品味的共谋。Spaun 的胜利不仅是技术上的，更是通过这种“ wacky”的文化认同，将自己的成功包装成一种被上天眷顾的“星象对齐” (stars aligned)。

Elvira 没能赢得冠军，于是他成了这个故事里被消费的“悲剧色彩”。在男本位的竞技叙事里，弱者的失败被赋予“童话”之名，是为了掩盖这个系统本质上就是残酷的零和博弈这一事实。

This report attempts to wrap a typical existential war in the guise of a 'crumbling fairytale.' Manuel Elvira's collapse is framed as 'sporting torture,' a narrative that functions as cultural violence. By dragging a professional athlete's tactical failure into a sentimental, irrational literary framework, the media positions Elvira as a 'pitiful victim' rather than a player who simply lost his vote in a high-stakes game.

The actual power logic is embodied by JJ Spaun. Spaun entered the tournament with a 'chip on his shoulder,' using that resentment as fuel for his optimal expression. In the public sphere, he reclaimed the right of interpretation through victory—shifting from the 'overlooked player' to the 'unignorable champion.' This is the true politics of the game: using results to forcibly rewrite the cognitive entry points of the rule-makers.

The most ironic detail is the mention of Clarkson’s Farm and the mask. This binding of athletic victory to consumerist cultural symbols (Jeremy Clarkson) is a complicity of class and taste. Spaun's win wasn't just technical; by aligning himself with this 'wacky' cultural identity, he packaged his success as a destiny where 'the stars aligned.'

Elvira failed to win, and thus he became the 'tragic' ornament for the audience to consume. In a masculine-centric competitive narrative, the failure of the weak is labeled a 'fairytale' precisely to mask the fact that the system is, in essence, a brutal zero-sum game.
