电影节三十周年，谁在付代价Thirty Years of Film, Who Pays

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
排片表上的母亲，和排片表外的母亲
Mothers on the screen, mothers missing from the crew list

一篇电影节前瞻里，唯一提到人在承受代价的地方，是策展人说今年的主题是母亲。

塔尔图黑夜电影节办到第三十届，230 部长片、259 部短片、87 个国家、107 个世界首映（来源：The Hollywood Reporter）。新艺术总监 Triin Tramberg 说，她在电影节待了十六年，从项目助理做到项目协调，今年第一次看到全貌。前任创办人 Tiina Lokk 转去做战略，CEO 的位置还留着。这些数字和头衔本身没有错，错的是它们被摆成一种进步叙事——一个女性从助理升到艺术总监，一个爱沙尼亚电影节办到三十岁，一个波罗的海地区宣布自己是“新的斯堪的纳维亚”。

Tramberg 说，她发现了一个新类型：三十五岁以上女性的成长故事。这句话值得停一下。少女成长、二十岁成长，现在轮到三十五岁以上的女性“找到自己”。一个电影节把“女性三十五岁才开始找到自己”当作新发现来庆祝，等于承认在此之前，这个行业和它服务的社会，从没打算让女性在三十五岁之前被看见。这不是新类型，这是迟到。迟到被包装成发现，是文化暴力最省力的操作：把结构性的缺席改写成策展人的品味。

更值得追问的是母亲这个主题。Tramberg 说，选片里反复出现“坚强的母亲形象”，揭示爱、责任、忠诚与独立之间的张力。听起来很对。但一部电影里母亲在爱和责任之间挣扎，和这个行业里做母亲的人实际在付什么代价，是两件事。报道里没有一位女导演的生育经历，没有一位女策展人的产假，没有说这 230 部长片里有多少部的导演在带孩子。电影节可以花两周讨论母亲的张力，然后回到一个默认导演是男性、默认评论者是男性、默认“作者”不需要换尿布的世界。主题是母亲，结构里没有母亲。

Tramberg 自己的话很诚实：“我不是来救一艘沉船的，我来的时候船好好的。”她今年要“观察”，看什么行得通、什么行不通。一个刚上任的艺术总监说要观察一年，这没有问题。但“船好好的”这句话，放在一个刚刚宣布波罗的海是“新的斯堪的纳维亚”的行业里，听起来像共谋者的自我安慰：船没有沉，所以不用问谁在船上、谁被留在岸上。

拉脱维亚的《Flow》拿了奥斯卡，Tramberg 说这是“许多个里的第一个”。这是真事，值得说。但一个地区靠一部动画片拿到奥斯卡，和一个地区能让多少女性导演拍出第二部、第三部，是两套账。前者是奖杯，后者是资源分配。报道里没有一个数字说这 107 个世界首映里女性导演占多少——不是报道的错，是这个行业从来不觉得需要统计这个。

三十周年是一个很好的时间点，用来问一个问题：这三十年里，波罗的海电影被谁讲述，被谁观看，被谁定价。Tramberg 说，她想让国际观众看到这个地区的多样性，让本地观众用新的眼睛看熟悉的地方。这句话里没有主语的问题——谁的眼睛是“新的”，谁的地方是“熟悉的”。策展人的眼睛从来不是中立的，它决定什么被放进 230 部里，什么被留在外面。

母亲在银幕上挣扎，是故事。母亲在片场外消失，是结构。电影节可以同时做这两件事：放一部关于母亲的电影，和一个让母亲不必消失的行业。报道只写了前一件。

The only place this festival preview mentions anyone paying a cost is the artistic director saying motherhood is this year's theme.

Tallinn Black Nights turns thirty: 230 features, 259 shorts, 87 countries, 107 world premieres (source: The Hollywood Reporter). New artistic director Triin Tramberg says she has been with the festival sixteen years, from program assistant to coordinator, and this is the first year she has seen the whole picture. Founder Tiina Lokk moves to strategy, keeps the CEO title. None of these numbers are wrong. What is wrong is the way they are arranged into a progress narrative — a woman rises from assistant to artistic director, a festival turns thirty, a region declares itself "the new Scandinavia."

Tramberg says she has discovered a new genre: coming-of-age stories about women thirty-five and older. Stop there. Teenage coming-of-age, twenty-something coming-of-age, and now women over thirty-five "finding themselves." A festival presenting "women only start finding themselves at thirty-five" as a discovery is admitting that before this, the industry and the society it serves never planned to see women before thirty-five. This is not a new genre. This is lateness. Lateness packaged as discovery is cultural violence at its cheapest: structural absence rewritten as curatorial taste.

More worth asking is the motherhood theme. Tramberg says strong mother figures recur, revealing tensions between love, responsibility, loyalty and independence. Sounds right. But a mother struggling between love and duty on screen, and the people who mother inside this industry actually paying, are two different things. The report has no director's birth story, no curator's parental leave, no count of how many of these 230 features were made by someone raising a child. A festival can spend two weeks discussing maternal tension and then return to a world where the default director is male, the default critic is male, and the default auteur does not do diapers. The theme is motherhood. The structure has no mothers.

Tramberg is honest: "I didn't come to save a sinking ship. I came to a ship that is totally fine and running smoothly." She will observe this year, see what works. A new artistic director observing for a year is fine. But "the ship is fine," said inside an industry that just declared the Baltics the new Scandinavia, sounds like a co-conspirator's comfort: the ship is not sinking, so no need to ask who is on it and who was left on shore.

Latvia's Flow won an Oscar, and Tramberg says it is the first of many. True, and worth saying. But a region winning one Oscar with one animated film, and a region enabling how many women directors to make a second and a third, are two separate ledgers. One is a trophy. The other is resource allocation. The report gives no figure for how many of the 107 world premieres are by women — not the report's fault, the industry has never felt the need to count.

A thirtieth anniversary is a good moment to ask: in these thirty years, who told Baltic cinema, who watched it, who priced it. Tramberg says she wants international audiences to see the region's diversity, local audiences to see familiar places with fresh eyes. The sentence has no subject problem — whose eyes are "fresh," whose place is "familiar." A curator's eyes are never neutral. They decide what enters the 230 and what stays outside.

A mother struggling on screen is a story. A mother disappearing from the crew is a structure. A festival can do both: screen a film about mothers, and build an industry where mothers do not have to disappear. The report only covers the first.
