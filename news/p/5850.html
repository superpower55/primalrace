站台不点名，共谋有分寸They Endorsed Him Without Saying His Name

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
民主党拥抱的不是他，是密歇根那50%
The party embraced the 50%, not the man.

这篇报道里最诚实的细节不是特朗普把El-Sayed叫成"圣战分子"，是民主党高层终于站到他身边时，一个个都不肯说出他的名字。

Booker在CNN上谈自己在密歇根的家族根、谈"赢回参议院"，主持人当面点破这不算背书，他面无表情，节目继续。Stevens被问到是否支持他，答"我在队里"，被追问"你刚才没提Abdul El-Sayed"，她转去讲自己曾劝他发一篇安抚黑人选民的演讲、而他没做。Schumer说他在以色列问题上的看法"和我很不一样"。这不是背书，这是共谋者们在算票。

算得没错。El-Sayed八月以不到一个点赢了Stevens，AIPAC为Stevens砸了近3000万美元，是该游说组织在单一选战中的最大投入。密歇根这一席可能决定参议院归谁，Marist上周的民调是50%对48%。所以民主党机器从分裂走向"团结"，靠的不是立场改变，是选举算术。Obama在提前投票第一天站台，是他自八月初选以来第一次公开支持，而他在台上说的是"我们不会在每个议题的每个细节上都一致"，还搬出Michelle Obama来打圆场。

特朗普那边在做什么，报道写得很清楚：这个月反复把他叫成"Mohammed"，共和党参议院委员会在广告里用他的全名Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed，在Port Huron的集会上把他诬成"jihadist"和"穆斯林兄弟会成员"，还翻出9/11。El-Sayed的回应是：他知道我叫Abdul，他每天都这么叫，他是在用我的名字和我的信仰转移我们对油价、菜价和医保的注意力。这是把武器化的身份入口反手拆开——你拿我的名字当武器，我就把名字底下那笔账摊开给你看。

但报道里真正被摆上台面、又始终没被正面回答的，是另一件事。Schumer、Levin、Booker、Stevens的保留意见全都指向同一个坐标：以色列。El-Sayed主张停止对以色列的军援、称加沙的战争是种族灭绝。AIPAC在初选里为他的对手砸了近3000万美元，初选之后，这些钱买来的立场没有消失，只是换成了"我们在队里"这种句子。Stevens在韦恩县以约51%对45%赢了他，那里是黑人占多数的底特律；她现在的说法是他该去回应黑人选民的关切。一个在初选里被本党机器和金主联手阻击的人，如今被同一批人小心翼翼地搂住，搂的姿势还特意避开了他的名字——这就是共谋的日常形态：利益站队一致时合作，合作时留好退路。

Rogers在向犹太选民兜售"El-Sayed反犹"，El-Sayed否认。一个埃及移民之子、可能成为美国第一位穆斯林参议员的人，被两边的叙事同时当作符号使用：一边是"圣战分子"，一边是"需要被谨慎拥抱的进步派"。他自己的政策清单——全民医保、把企业钱赶出政治、对富豪和大公司征税、废除ICE——在这些符号战里几乎没有被讨论。Obama替他挡了一句：想要人人有医保，这有什么激进的？这句话是对的，也是这场竞选里少见的、把人当人说的话。

周二是答案。如果El-Sayed赢了，报道说他将把一台怀疑他的党机器拉到了自己这边。但要注意方向：是机器被拉过来了，还是他被机器收编了，周二晚上分不出来。分得出来的是另一件事——那些至今不肯说出他名字的人，赢了之后会开始说，而且会说得很响。

The most honest detail in this story isn't Trump calling El-Sayed a "jihadist." It's that when the Democratic establishment finally stood beside him, one after another, they wouldn't say his name.

Booker went on CNN and talked about his family roots in Michigan and about "winning back the Senate," never mentioning El-Sayed. When the anchor pointed out this wasn't a ringing endorsement, he kept his face still and the segment moved on. Stevens, asked if she was fully behind him, said she was "on the team"; told she hadn't mentioned Abdul El-Sayed, she pivoted to saying she'd urged him to give a speech addressing Black voters' concerns and that it hadn't happened. Schumer said his views on Israel are "much different than mine." This is not an endorsement. This is co-conspirators doing arithmetic.

The arithmetic is sound. El-Sayed beat Stevens in August by less than a point. AIPAC spent close to $30m backing her — the lobby's largest investment in a single race. Michigan could decide which party controls the chamber, and a Marist poll last week had him at 50% to 48%. So the party machine moved from bitter split to "solidarity," not because anyone changed their mind, but because the seat is winnable. Obama appeared on the first day of early voting, his first public show of support since the August primary, and what he offered on stage was that "we are not going to agree on every detail of every issue," with Michelle Obama hauled out as the punchline.

Trump's side is spelled out plainly: this month he keeps calling him "Mohammed"; the Republican senatorial committee runs ads using his full name, Abdulrahman Mohamed El-Sayed; in Port Huron he branded him a "jihadist" and a "Muslim Brotherhood member" and invoked 9/11. El-Sayed's answer: he knows my name is Abdul, he says it every day, he is using my name and my faith to distract us from the price of gas, groceries and healthcare. That is the weaponized identity entrance turned inside out — you make my name a weapon, I open the ledger underneath it.

But the thing actually put on the table and never squarely answered is Israel. Schumer, Levin, Booker, Stevens — every reservation points at the same coordinate. El-Sayed wants military aid to Israel ended and calls the war in Gaza a genocide. AIPAC put close to $30m into his primary opponent, and after the primary that money's立场 didn't evaporate; it just turned into sentences like "I'm on the team." Stevens beat him in Wayne county, home to majority-Black Detroit, 51% to 45%; now her line is that he should address Black voters' concerns. A man the party machine and its donors ganged up on in the primary is now being carefully embraced by the same people, in a posture that carefully avoids his name. That is what co-conspiracy looks like day to day: cooperate when interests align, and keep an exit.

Rogers is courting Jewish voters by telling the Jewish Telegraphic Agency that El-Sayed is "anti-Jew"; El-Sayed rejects it. A son of Egyptian immigrants who could become the first Muslim US senator is being used as a symbol by both narratives at once — "jihadist" on one side, "the progressive we must embrace cautiously" on the other. His actual platform — Medicare for All, corporate money out of politics, taxing billionaires, abolishing ICE — barely gets discussed inside that fight. Obama threw him one line: what is so radical about wanting everybody to have healthcare? That line is correct, and it is one of the few moments in this race where someone is treated as a person.

Tuesday settles it. If El-Sayed wins, the story says he pulled a skeptical party machine to his side. Watch the direction: whether the machine was pulled, or he was absorbed, won't be visible Tuesday night. What will be visible is that the people who wouldn't say his name will start saying it, loudly, once it's safe.
