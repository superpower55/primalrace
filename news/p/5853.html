肥胖不是意志力问题Obesity Is Not a Willpower Problem

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
把肥胖写成个人选择，政府就能永远只交报告
Call obesity a personal choice, and the state only ever has to deliver reports.

英国议会这份报告说政府的肥胖计划注定失败，理由列了六条：没有指标、没有跨部门方案、没有首相亲自挂帅。六条全是程序问题。三十 percent 的英格兰成年人带着肥胖生活，三分之二超重或肥胖，这个数字从九十年代一路涨上来，现在停在平台期，没有下降的迹象（来源：The Guardian）。报告对一件事只字未提：这个数字是为什么涨上去的。

肥胖的成因被写成了个人意志力，于是解决方案只能是劝人少吃多动，以及等人生病之后发减肥药。这是加尔通那个三角里最典型的操作：把结构性暴力翻译成个人缺陷。食品工业用广告、促销、货架位置、价格把不健康的选项铺满生活，这本身是第三章意义上的表达武器化——认知入口，只不过投放的不是政治立场，是食欲。Moran 说得对，有效的计划意味着在广告、标签、超市配货目标、学校门口的炸鸡店这些点上和食品工业正面开战。但她把这句话放在报告的最后，前面六条程序性要求占满了篇幅。

真正的问题不是政府没能力，是政府没打算。一个由首相亲自挂帅的内阁委员会，能让食品工业少赚多少钱？报告要求三个月内拿出跨部门方案，可方案里要写清楚的是“谁出钱、谁执行、怎么衡量”。这三样一旦写清楚，就等于承认肥胖是分配问题：便宜的、被大规模推销的食物是低质的，能买得起高质量食物、有时间做饭、有安全地方运动的人住在另一个邮编。工党政府已经做的那些——晚上九点前禁垃圾食品电视广告、禁多件优惠、给地方议会权力挡学校附近的新外卖店——方向是对的，但都是边缘操作，没有一条动到价格。

这份报告最该被追问的地方在这里：它把“预防”和“治疗”混为一谈的批评提出来了，却没有问为什么混。预防要动食品工业的利润，治疗要动 NHS 的预算，前者政治上昂贵，后者政治上安全。于是“从疾病转向预防”这句口号在预算表上从来都是往治疗那边倒。

还有一层没人提。肥胖率的分布不是均匀的，女性、低收入群体、少数族裔承受得更多，而她们同时也是被规训得最狠的一群——体型被当成道德评价，被当成阶级表型，被当成可以公开嘲笑的东西。一份只谈“rates”的报告，看不见这些身体上具体的人。

三个月后如果真有一份跨部门方案交到议会，检验标准只有一条：食品工业有没有少赚。没有这一条，再多的首相挂帅也只是把失败从部门级别提升到内阁级别。

The UK parliament's report says the government's obesity plan is doomed to fail, and lists six reasons: no metrics, no cross-departmental plan, no prime ministerial ownership. All six are procedural. Thirty percent of adults in England live with obesity, 66% are overweight or living with obesity, and close to a third of children are estimated to be overweight or living with obesity; the rates climbed from the 1990s and have levelled off without falling (source: The Guardian). The report does not ask why the number went up in the first place.

Obesity gets written as a failure of individual willpower, so the only solutions on offer are telling people to eat less and move more, and handing out weight-loss drugs once they are already sick. This is the cleanest operation in Galtung's triangle: structural violence translated into personal defect. The food industry floods daily life with unhealthy options through advertising, promotions, shelf position and price — the weaponisation of expression through the cognitive entrance, except what is being sold is appetite, not a political position. Moran is right that an effective plan means standing up to the food industry on advertising, labelling, supermarket sales targets and fast food outside schools. She puts that sentence at the end of the report. The six procedural demands fill the front.

The problem is not that the government lacks capacity. It is that the government does not intend to. A cabinet committee chaired by the prime minister — how much less would the food industry earn? The report demands a cross-departmental plan within three months, and what that plan has to specify is who pays, who delivers, and how progress is measured. Specify those three and you have admitted obesity is a distribution problem: cheap, heavily promoted food is low quality, and the people who can afford good food, have time to cook it and have a safe place to move live in a different postcode. What the government has already done — the pre-9pm TV and online junk food ad restrictions, the volume-price promotion ban, stronger council powers to block new hot food takeaways near schools — points the right way, but every measure sits at the margin. None of them touches price.

The report's sharpest blind spot is the one it names and then drops: it says governments have muddled prevention with treatment, and never asks why. Prevention costs the food industry profit. Treatment costs the NHS budget. The first is politically expensive, the second politically safe. So "from sickness to prevention" always, in the actual budget lines, tilts toward treatment.

One more layer goes unmentioned. Obesity rates are not evenly distributed: women, low-income groups and ethnic minorities carry more of it, and they are also the ones most heavily disciplined — body size as moral judgement, as class phenotype, as something that can be mocked in public. A report that only talks about rates cannot see the people inside those bodies.

If a cross-departmental plan does reach parliament in three months, there is one test: did the food industry earn less. Without that, more prime ministerial ownership only upgrades the failure from departmental to cabinet level.
