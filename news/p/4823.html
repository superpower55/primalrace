用“小船”喂养的极右翼性欲Feeding Far-Right Libido with 'Small Boats'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
反移民叙事是典型的男性中心武器化表达，旨在通过制造敌我识别来确立虚假的强权身份。
Anti-migrant narratives are weaponized masculine-centric expressions designed to establish fake power through enemy-identification.

极右翼分子在朴次茅斯（Portsmouth）的聚集，本质上是一场关于“男性气概”的表演性博弈。注意那些细节：140名寻求庇护者被描述为“100多名移民男性”，而极右翼的领头羊是前保镖、自封的“爱国者”。这场冲突的认知入口被精准地设定在“入侵”与“防御”之间，将复杂的全球结构性暴力简化为一场关于领土、肌肉和“保护”的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative)。

这种叙事是极具欺骗性的 weaponized expression。对于这些抗议者来说，真正的敌人不是小船上的难民，而是他们自身在现代社会中主体性的丧失。他们通过扮演“守护者”这个角色，在一个他们无法掌控的经济结构中，试图通过制造一个更弱势的“异类”来换取短暂的权力快感。这是一种典型的假.最优解表达：通过排挤他者来获得群体内部的认同，代价是彻底沦为元暴力的共谋者。

而政府的应对方式则展现了 structural violence 的惯用逻辑：用数据（如“拦截了48,000次”）来掩盖资源分配的不公。无论是法国海滩上的“靴子”，还是曼斯顿（Manston）的处理中心，这些机制并非为了人道救援，而是为了维持一个“受控”的边界。在这种叙事里，难民的身体被客体化为一种“流量”或“威胁”，而极右翼的暴力则被部分地、潜意识地允许，因为他们与权力层在“维持秩序”这一元暴力基准上达成了某种默契的共谋 (complicity)。

这场闹剧最讽刺的地方在于，那些挥舞着旗帜、 slashing dinghies（割破橡皮艇）的男人，以为自己在拯救文明，实际上他们只是在为一个早已将他们视为消耗品的权力结构递交投名状。

The gathering of far-right activists in Portsmouth is essentially a performative game of 'masculinity'. Notice the details: 140 asylum seekers are framed as 'over 100 migrant men', while the leaders are former bodyguards and self-proclaimed 'patriots'. The cognitive entry point is precisely set between 'invasion' and 'defense', simplifying complex global structural violence into a masculine-centric narrative of territory, muscle, and 'protection'.

This is a classic case of weaponized expression. For these protesters, the real enemy is not the refugees on the boats, but the loss of their own subjectivity in a modern society they cannot control. By playing the role of the 'guardian', they attempt to trade the marginalization of a more vulnerable 'other' for a fleeting sense of power. This is a fake optimal expression: gaining internal group validation by excluding others, at the cost of becoming total complicity in meta-violence.

Meanwhile, the government's response reveals the logic of structural violence: using data (e.g., '48,000 attempts prevented') to mask the injustice of resource distribution. Whether it is 'boots on the ground' in France or the processing center in Manston, these mechanisms are not about humanitarian aid, but about maintaining a 'controlled' border. In this narrative, the bodies of refugees are objectified as 'traffic' or 'threats', while far-right violence is subconsciously tolerated because they share a tacit complicity with the power structure in the meta-violence of 'maintaining order'.

The irony is that these men, waving flags and slashing dinghies, believe they are saving civilization. In reality, they are merely handing over a pledge of loyalty to a power structure that has long since viewed them as disposable assets.
