微软的 AI 算力：一场用“电量”掩盖“芯片”的叙事 scamMicrosoft's AI Capacity: A Narrative Scam Masking Chips with Power

中国科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
当现实不配合时，巨头会重新定义衡量现实的尺子。
When reality refuses to cooperate, giants simply redefine the ruler used to measure it.

微软在 AI 竞赛中的姿态极其典型：它在用一种“武器化”的叙事来制造一种不存在的领先。当外界在讨论芯片数量（Actual）时，微软在讨论电力容量（Potential）。这是一个极其阴险的认知入口转换——它试图让你相信，只要我拥有 10GW 的电力容量，我就等同于拥有了能跑 640 万颗芯片的能力。

但这正是典型的 structural violence：通过模糊“电力额度”与“实际算力”的界限，微软在财报和投资者演示中制造了一个巨大的虚假繁荣。电力容量是纸上的数字，而插在机架上的芯片才是真实的存在。Nadella 在播客里承认芯片在仓库里“没地方插”，这直接撕掉了伪装：他们买到了入场券，但没能建成剧场。这种“有电无芯”或“有芯无壳”的脱节，证明了 AI 军备竞赛已经进入了一个极其病态的阶段——公司不再追求真实的性能突破，而是在追求“看起来像是在突破”的指标。

这背后是一场深层的共谋。Nvidia 垄断了供应且拒绝披露细节，微软等巨头则通过不透明的资本支出（CapEx）和模糊的可持续发展报告来维持股价。在这种 masculine-centric 的权力博弈中，真实的数据被视为弱点，而能够被操纵的叙事才是资产。他们不在乎 AI 是否真的在 booming，他们在乎的是能否在认知层面垄断“AI 正在 booming”的解释权。

所谓的 AI 革命，目前看来更像是一场关于“定义权”的战争。如果一个公司能通过重新定义“容量”来掩盖“短缺”，那么它就成功地在资本市场完成了一次存在性战争的掠夺。最讽刺的是，这种用电力指标替代算力结果的逻辑，与某些科技公司宣布新定律来掩盖技术瓶颈的套路如出一辙：现实不配合，就换一把尺子。

Microsoft's posture in the AI race is a textbook case of weaponized narrative. While the world discusses the number of chips (Actual), Microsoft discusses power capacity (Potential). This is a sinister shift in the cognitive entry point—attempting to convince us that possessing 10GW of power capacity is equivalent to the capability of running 6.4 million chips.

This is pure structural violence: by blurring the line between "power quotas" and "actual compute," Microsoft manufactures a facade of prosperity in its annual reports. Power capacity is a number on paper; chips plugged into racks are reality. Nadella's admission that chips are sitting in inventory with "no shells to plug into" rips off the mask. They bought the tickets, but couldn't build the theater. The AI arms race has entered a pathological phase where companies no longer seek real performance breakthroughs, but rather the metrics of "looking like" a breakthrough.

Underneath this lies a deep complicity. Nvidia monopolizes supply and refuses disclosure, while giants like Microsoft use opaque CapEx and vague sustainability reports to prop up stock prices. In this masculine-centric power game, real data is a liability, and a manipulatable narrative is the primary asset. They don't care if AI is actually booming; they care about monopolizing the interpretation that it is.

The so-called AI revolution currently looks more like a war over the right to define. If a company can mask a shortage by redefining "capacity," it wins a predatory victory in the existential war of capital markets. The irony is that this logic—replacing actual results with proxy indicators—is identical to the scam of announcing new "laws" to hide technical bottlenecks: if reality doesn't cooperate, just change the ruler.
