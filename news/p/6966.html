当《蝇王》沦为一张概念图Lord of the Flies, Now a Concept Art Spread

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要《星球大战：曼达洛人与古古》电影今年上映后，Phillip Szostak 所著幕后画册《The Art of Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu》将于 11 月 3 日出版。Polygon 独家披露其中一页概念图，概念艺术家 Brian Matyas 说，解放机器人派系 Droid Gotra 的造型灵感来自 1990 年电影《蝇王》里男孩们逐渐拼凑起来的破败部落式服装和旧车。
The newsThe Art of Star Wars: The Mandalorian and Grogu, a behind-the-scenes book by Phillip Szostak, arrives Nov. 3, following the film's release this year. Polygon previewed a concept-art spread in which artist Brian Matyas says the look of the emancipated-droid faction Droid Gotra drew on the tattered, tribal outfits the boys assemble in the 1990 film adaptation of Lord of the Flies, mixed with junker cars.
被抽掉政治的《蝇王》，正好拿来装饰商品
A story about how civilization eats its weakest, repackaged as a paint job.

《蝇王》讲的是什么？一群男孩流落荒岛，文明的外壳剥落，部落、猎杀、献祭依次登场。它是一则关于「文明」如何自我瓦解的寓言，而其中最先被消耗掉的，总是最弱的那几个。现在这个概念被拿来给一批机器人做造型参考，取的是「破布、部落感、拼凑的零件和油漆」。一个关于暴力的故事，被压缩成一种视觉风格，然后贴到商品上。

这不是孤例，这是文化工业的常规操作。第三章讲的武器化，最省力的一种入口就是审美：把一个概念的来历抽干，只留下它的「样子」，再把这个样子定价。部落纹样、破败感、低科技拼贴，在这里是「酷」的原料。至于部落意味着什么、破败是谁造成的、拼贴在现实里对应哪些人的处境，一概不需要。认知入口被打开，里面装的是品味。

更值得看一眼的是被装饰的对象：Droid Gotra，一支「被解放的机器人」组成的独立派系。被解放者，在造型上被赋予部落与废土的美学。这在叙事上很顺：自由等于野蛮化，脱离主人等于失去秩序，于是「解放」在视觉上先被翻译成「退化」。不需要谁出来说一句反对解放的话，画面已经把话说完了。文化暴力最擅长的事，就是让一个立场看起来只是美术风格。

这篇报道里没有人受伤，没有谁在承受代价，这也是它的问题所在。《蝇王》里被石头砸死的那个男孩，在这本画册里连一个像素都不占。报道写到的是概念艺术家、导演、出版日期和「赶紧入手」，没写到的是：这本书在售卖一种表达，给谁定价，谁付代价。答案是，付代价的人从来不在画册里。

一本幕后画册当然可以只是画册。但当它把一个关于人如何变成野兽的故事，变成机器人身上的做旧涂层时，它就在参与一次很小的、几乎无人察觉的解释权转移：暴力成了风格，风格成了商品。买的人不会觉得自己在买什么别的东西。这才是它成立的原因。

What is Lord of the Flies about? A group of boys stranded on an island, the shell of civilization peeling away, tribe and hunt and sacrifice taking turns on stage. It is a fable about how civilization dismantles itself, and the first thing consumed is always whoever is weakest. Now that concept is used as a style reference for a batch of droids, and what gets taken is the rags, the tribal feel, the mismatched parts and paint. A story about violence compressed into a visual texture, then stuck onto a product.

This is not an outlier; it is the standard move of the culture industry. The cheapest entry point for weaponized expression is aesthetics: drain a concept of its origin, keep only how it looks, then put a price on the look. Tribal motifs, decay, low-tech collage are raw material for cool here. What tribe means, who caused the decay, which real people's circumstances the collage corresponds to — none of it is needed. The cognitive entrance is opened and taste is what gets installed.

More interesting is the object being decorated: the Droid Gotra, an independent faction of emancipated droids. The emancipated get an aesthetic of tribe and wasteland. Narratively this runs smooth: freedom equals savagery, leaving the master equals losing order, so liberation is first translated visually into regression. Nobody has to say a word against emancipation; the image has already finished the sentence. This is what cultural violence does best — make a position look like nothing more than art direction.

Nobody is hurt in this report, nobody bears a cost, and that is precisely its point. The boy crushed by the rock in Lord of the Flies does not occupy a single pixel of this art book. What the report covers is the concept artist, the director, the release date, and the instruction to grab it fast. What it leaves out is that this book is selling an expression: who sets the price, who pays. The ones who pay are never in the art book.

A behind-the-scenes volume can of course just be a volume. But when it turns a story about how people become beasts into a weathering coat on a droid, it takes part in a very small, almost invisible transfer of interpretive power: violence becomes style, style becomes merchandise. Buyers will not feel they are buying anything else. That is exactly why it works.
