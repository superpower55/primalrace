最后一群蚂蚁也被定价了The Last Colony Has a Price Tag Now

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要英国德文郡野生动物信托基金会称，10月中旬例行检查时发现，查德利奈顿希思自然保护区一处窄头蚁巢被连窝挖走，该保护区是这种蚂蚁在英格兰最后的栖息地。一个蚁巢可容纳1000至5000只蚂蚁和一至多只可活25年的蚁后。案件已报德文郡与康沃尔郡警方。
The newsDevon Wildlife Trust said a nest of narrow-headed ants was dug up and taken from Chudleigh Knighton Heath nature reserve in south Devon, the last place in England where the species survives; staff found the hole in mid-October. A single nest can hold 1,000 to 5,000 ants and one or more queens that live up to 25 years. The theft has been reported to police.
偷蚁巢的人知道它值钱，这正是问题所在。
Whoever took the nest knew what it was worth — that is the problem.

一只蚁巢被挖走，英格兰最后的窄头蚁种群少了一窝。偷的人带着铲子，知道该挖哪里——信托会的人说，对蚂蚁没研究的人根本看不出那是个巢。这不是路过顺手，是有人把“稀有”读成了价签。

一只蚁后能活二十五年，一窝从一千到五千只，长得慢。慢的东西在市场上就贵，贵的东西就有人来挖。窄头蚁过去一百年在英格兰南部荒地上到处都是，农业改造和盖楼把栖息地削掉，剩到今天只有查德利奈顿希思这一处。这是结构暴力最安静的一种形态：没有谁拿铲子对着蚂蚁，是整片荒地被换成农田和楼盘，剩下的那一小块就成了孤本，孤本就有了价格。

价格一出现，认知入口就打开了。信托的人猜动机，可能是想“再野化”，把蚂蚁放回旧栖息地；也可能是昆虫收藏者。两种猜法都指向同一件事——有人觉得自己比这片地更懂怎么安排它。再野化听起来是善意的，可善意不需要问过蚂蚁，也不需要问过那个花了多年数巢的人。搬运蚂蚁要专业，要准备新家，信托自己有计划，但那是“大量保护工作”，挪一窝“不太可能成功”。偷的人不关心成不成功，他关心的是自己完成了一次动作。

收藏者是另一条路。昆虫收藏和鸟蛋盗猎是一回事：把活的东西变成藏品，藏品的价值来自稀缺，稀缺来自别人再也拿不到。Balfour 说这案子和非法偷野鸟蛋一样严重，这句话的准确之处在于，两者都不是为了吃、为了用，是为了占有那个“最后一窝”。占有欲不需要理由，只需要一个别人没有的东西。

警方说这处是具特殊科学价值地点，法律保护，故意破坏或干扰是刑事犯罪。法条在那里，可法条管不住一把铲子和一个知道巢在哪的人。信托现在在“审查保护区的安保”——这句话翻译过来就是，之前没有安保，因为之前没人觉得一窝蚂蚁值得偷。

真正被偷走的不是五千只蚂蚁，是那二十五年。蚁后活着的时候，这窝蚂蚁在长；蚁后没了，这个种群在英格兰的账上就少了一笔，且无法快速补回。信托的员工、志愿者、合作方做了多年的事，被一把铲子抹掉一截。

报道里没有偷的人是谁，也没有他打算把蚂蚁卖给谁。这个空白本身就是答案的一部分：在一个把稀有当价格的世界里，最后一群蚂蚁的巢，和最后一只鸟的蛋，是同一种商品。

A nest was dug up and carried off, and England's last population of narrow-headed ants is one colony smaller. The thief brought a spade and knew where to dig; the trust says the nest is not obvious to anyone who does not know about ants. This was not a passer-by. Someone read "rare" as a price.

A queen can live twenty-five years, a colony holds one to five thousand ants, and it grows slowly. Slow things are expensive, and expensive things get dug up. Narrow-headed ants were once widespread across southern English heathland; a century of agricultural change and building developments cut the habitat down to this one heath. That is structural violence at its quietest: nobody swung a spade at the ants, the heath was simply converted until one patch was left, and the last patch became a rarity, and the rarity became a price.

Once there is a price, the cognitive entrance is open. The trust guesses the motive: maybe someone trying to "rewild" the ants back into former habitat, maybe an insect collector. Both guesses point the same way — someone decided he understood this ground better than the people who count the nests. Rewilding sounds kind, but kindness does not consult the ants, or the staff who spent years mapping them. Moving ants takes expertise and preparation; the trust has ambitions to do it, but that is "a great deal of conservation effort," and relocating one nest "isn't likely to be very successful." The thief did not care about success. He cared about completing the gesture.

The collector is the other road. Insect collecting is egg collecting by another name: a living thing turned into a specimen, whose value comes from scarcity, and scarcity comes from everyone else being unable to get one. Balfour says this is a crime as serious as raiding wild bird eggs, and she is precise: neither is done to eat or to use. Both are done to possess the last one. Possession needs no reason beyond someone else not having it.

The police note that the site is legally protected and that damaging or disturbing it is a criminal offence. The statute exists; it does not stop a spade in the hands of a man who knows where the nest is. The trust is now "reviewing the security" of the reserve — which means there was no security, because nobody thought a nest of ants was worth stealing.

What was taken was not five thousand ants. It was twenty-five years. While the queen lived, the colony grew; with her gone, the species' account in England is short one entry that cannot be quickly repaid. Years of work by staff, volunteers and partners were erased in one dig.

The report does not say who took the nest, or who he meant to sell it to. That blank is part of the answer: in a world that prices the rare, the nest of the last ants and the egg of the last bird are the same commodity.
