流亡者的加冕，只维持了58分钟A Coronation in Exile, 58 Minutes Long

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-30 § 链接
自封总统的人，连国徽都还停在1967年
The man who crowned himself couldn't even update the coat of arms.

Flávio Bolsonaro 在迈阿密宣布自己是"巴西真正的当选总统"，58分钟后被改成了"巴西领袖"。改他的不是巴西选民，是华盛顿的官员——他们不想为一个人的虚荣心和巴西现政府闹翻。这就是全部的笑点，也是全部的政治：一个输掉440万票的人，能给自己加冕多久，取决于外国主子愿意容忍他多久。

他把自己比作戴高乐、何塞·马蒂、鲁伊·巴尔博萨。这几个人有一个共同点，他显然没看懂：他们在流亡时手里有组织、有抵抗、有真实的敌人。Flávio 手里有什么？一封据说是 ChatGPT 帮忙写的信，和一枚1967年就作废的"巴西合众国"国徽。连道具都没更新，就想演流亡政府。这不是戴高乐，这是租了个迪士尼城堡办就职典礼的 meme——而那条 meme 已经被人做出来了。

真正值得看的不是他，是那些骂他的人。Estado de São Paulo、O Globo、Veja，全是保守派媒体，骂得比谁都狠。"最高级别的骗子""疯人院里的拿破仑""一群业余白痴"——说这话的 Malafaia 是跟他父亲交好的电视布道家。这不是左右之争，这是自家人在清仓。Estado 的社论说得很直白：保守派该抛弃 Bolsonaro 家族，把力量押到圣保罗州长 Tarcísio de Freitas 身上。骂声是资产重组的前奏，不是道德的苏醒。

所以"Bolsonarismo 已经彻底失败"这句话，是 Amorim 说的，也是 Estado 编辑部说的，但两拨人说的不是一回事。前者在宣布对手死了，后者在宣布该换一个更体面的继承人。一个运动死了，和它的选票死了，是两码事。报道里也写了：另一些人警告，即便家族失去了领导权，这股力量依然是强大的政治势力。谁说得对，不取决于迈阿密那封信写得多蠢，取决于 Tarcísio 能不能把那些票接过去，还不用背这个姓氏。

Trump 拒绝承认选举结果，说那是"巴西人民的极大尴尬"，被问到是否视 Flávio 为合法领袖时答"我不掺和"。一份被 Folha 拿到的美国外交电报，把 Lula 的胜利称作"令人意外但已成定局"。这就是 Flávio 那58分钟的真实长度：不是他自己收回的，是大使馆替他掐的表。他以为自己在领导流亡政府，其实他只是别人外交算计里一个需要被尽快修正的错字。

最刺眼的是那封信的落款被改了，国徽却没改对——因为改的人根本不在乎巴西是哪一年改的国名。一个自称代表国家的人，连国家叫什么都不知道。而真正在讨论他政治前途的，是圣保罗的报社和华盛顿的官员，巴西选民在这场戏里连个配角都没捞上。

Flávio Bolsonaro hoped that by Sunday night people would be calling him the next president of Brazil. Instead he fled to Miami and, for 58 minutes, called himself "Brazil's true president-elect." The correction came not from Brazilian voters but from Trump administration officials who didn't want a diplomatic split with Brazil's actual government. That is the whole joke and the whole politics: how long a man who lost by 4.4m votes can crown himself depends on how long a foreign patron is willing to indulge him.

He compared himself to Charles de Gaulle, José Martí, Rui Barbosa. They share one thing he clearly missed: they had organizations, resistance, real enemies. Flávio had a letter reportedly written with ChatGPT's help and a coat of arms for the "United States of Brazil," a name the country abandoned in 1967. He didn't even update the props before casting himself in a government-in-exile. This isn't de Gaulle. It's a meme about renting Cinderella Castle for an inauguration — and someone already made that meme.

The interesting part isn't him. It's who's denouncing him. Estado de São Paulo, O Globo, Veja — all conservative outlets, and the harshest. "A con artist of the highest order." "A madhouse Napoleon." "A bunch of amateur morons" — that last one from Silas Malafaia, the televangelist close to his jailed father. This isn't left versus right. It's the family clearing inventory. Estado's editorial says it plainly: conservatives should ditch the Bolsonaros and back São Paulo governor Tarcísio de Freitas. The scorn is the overture to a portfolio rebalancing, not a moral awakening.

So when Amorim says Bolsonarismo was "defeated once and for all," and Estado's board says the same, they mean different things. One is burying an opponent; the other is announcing a more presentable heir. A movement dying and its votes dying are not the same event. The report notes others warn the force remains powerful even if the family's leadership is in doubt. Who's right doesn't depend on how stupid the Miami letter was. It depends on whether Tarcísio can inherit the votes without inheriting the name.

Trump won't recognize the result — "a very great embarrassment to the people of Brazil" — and on whether Flávio is Brazil's rightful leader: "I won't get involved." A US diplomatic cable obtained by Folha calls Lula's win "surprising but definitive." That's the true length of Flávio's 58 minutes: not retracted by himself, but timed out by an embassy. He thought he was leading a government-in-exile. He was a typo in someone else's diplomatic arithmetic, to be corrected fast.

The letter's status was amended. The coat of arms wasn't — because whoever fixed it doesn't care what year Brazil renamed itself. A man claiming to speak for a nation didn't know what the nation is called. And the people actually debating his political future are newspaper boards in São Paulo and officials in Washington. Brazilian voters didn't even get a supporting role.
