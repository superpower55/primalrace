恐怖是糖衣，爱情是入口Horror Is the Sugar, Romance Is the Door

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把浪漫爱装进恐怖里，卖的还是同一种东西
Wrap romance in horror and you're still selling the same thing

环球奥兰多“万圣节恐怖夜”三十五周年，创意团队接受采访，讲他们怎么把《养鬼吃人》从克莱夫·巴克的中篇小说搬进鬼屋。导演说，巴克坚持不能删掉原作的性爱层面；鬼屋里十来个钉子头，皮衣、穿孔、拉扯皮肤，配上“愉悦与痛苦”的声音。听起来很硬核。

然后同一篇报道的中段，节目总监查尔斯·格雷开始讲他们今年的原创秀《梦魇燃料：血之黑色》：天使堕落、变成吸血鬼、在超自然情人身边“获得力量”。他说不是所有人都接受这条爱情线，“有时候需要一点说服力”，但他的信心来源是——他是两个年轻女孩的父亲，也有妻子，他知道浪漫是很有力量的东西，不只对女孩，对男孩也是。

这就是第三章那个入口，包装换了一层皮。浪漫爱叙事从来不需要以婚纱和钻戒的形式出场，它可以是哥特的、血腥的、R级的，只要最后落点是“她被诱惑，她和他一起沉进黑暗”，叙事的功能就完成了。格雷自己说得很清楚：最后一刻他咬她，全场沸腾，“他们有时候忘了他是个吸血鬼。是诱惑。她和你一起被诱入黑暗。”观众以为自己在消费恐怖，其实是在消费一场求偶仪式，只不过这次配了火焰表演和空中飞人。

报道里真正值得看的是它怎么描述这门生意的受众。BookTok 读者被请进来，一位读者说她想看《荆棘与玫瑰的王庭》《第四翼》这类书变成鬼屋，因为“读者想觉得自己是故事的一部分，想打扮成喜欢的角色、拍照、吃主题食物”。书店老板说，horrormance 是给那些“还没准备好进入极端区”的人准备的，他们要的是“在恐惧里找安全，在安全里找恐惧”。这句话翻译过来就是：把危险调成可消费的浓度，把亲密关系调成可消费的浓度，两样一起卖。

这里面没有直接的暴力，没有谁被殴打、被囚禁。动的全是文化层：一套关于“爱是什么、女人在爱里是什么位置”的叙事，被做成可以排队进入、可以拍照、可以配鸡尾酒的产品。加尔通的公式在这里算得很清楚：本来可以讲一个别的故事——关于欲望、关于身体、关于权力怎么运作——实际给出的却是“她被咬了一口，然后她获得了力量”。差额就是暴力，只不过它穿着皮衣，打着灯，还带一点草莓酱。

最诚实的一句来自那位节目总监的自我说明：他相信浪漫，因为他是两个女孩的父亲和一个女人的丈夫。这句话把共谋的链条说全了——他不需要论证浪漫叙事对谁有利，他只需要说自己爱女儿。爱女儿和把女儿们训练成同一套叙事的接收端，在他那里是同一件事。

至于巴克坚持保留的那层性，报道里反复出现“愉悦与痛苦”“感官性”“仪式感”这些词，团队把它当作品味来做，做得确实漂亮。但漂亮的东西最容易被吞掉：当痛苦被做成一道叫“愉悦与痛苦之脑”的甜品，牙齿和皮肤是香草蛋糕，血是草莓酱，修道士们那套关于身体、欲望和越界的挑衅就变成了菜单上的一行字。巴克的原作问的是“你到底想要什么”，鬼屋给出的答案是“排队，进来，拍照，买杯子”。

所以这条新闻不脏，也不残忍，它只是把一件很旧的事情做得很新：让女人在故事里被诱惑、被转化、被“赋权”，让读者觉得这就是自己的选择。三十五年的鬼屋经验，最后练出来的不是吓人的手艺，是定价的手艺。

Universal Orlando's Halloween Horror Nights turns thirty-five, and the creative team sits down to explain how they moved Hellraiser from Clive Barker's novella into a haunted house. Barker, they say, insisted they keep the sexual layer. Ten Pinheads in leather and piercings, sounds of pleasure and pain. Hardcore, apparently.

Then, midway through the same piece, show director Charles Gray starts talking about this year's original show, Nightmare Fuel: Blood Noir: an angel falls, becomes a vampire, and finds her "empowerment" beside a supernatural lover. Not everyone is comfortable with a love story in there, he says — sometimes it takes a little convincing — but his confidence comes from being the father of two young daughters and the husband of a wife. Romance, he knows, is powerful. Not just for girls. For guys, too.

This is the Chapter Three entry point with a new coat of paint. The romance narrative never needed weddings and diamonds to do its work. It can be gothic, bloody, R-rated — as long as the landing point is she is seduced, she goes down into the dark with him, the narrative has done its job. Gray says it himself: at the very last moment he bites her and the crowd goes wild, they sometimes forget he's a vampire, it's the seduction, she and you get seduced into this darkness. The audience thinks it is buying horror. It is buying a courtship ritual, this time with fire performers and aerialists.

What is actually worth reading in the piece is how it describes the customer base. BookTok readers are being invited in. One reader wants scare zones built from A Court of Thorns and Roses and Fourth Wing, because readers want to feel like part of the story — dress as their favorite characters, take pictures, eat themed food. A horror bookstore owner says horrormance is for people not ready to dip into the extreme section, people looking for safety in fear and fear in safety. Translated: turn danger into a consumable concentration, turn intimacy into a consumable concentration, sell both together.

There is no direct violence here. Nobody is beaten, nobody is locked up. What moves is the cultural layer: a set of stories about what love is and where a woman stands inside it, turned into something you queue for, photograph, and pair with a cocktail. Galtung's formula does the arithmetic. The story that could have been told — about desire, about the body, about how power actually runs — is replaced by she got bitten and then she got empowered. The gap is the violence. It just happens to be wearing leather, lit beautifully, and served with strawberry sauce.

The most honest line comes from the show director's own justification: he believes in romance because he is a father of two girls and a husband. That sentence completes the chain of complicity. He does not need to argue whom the romance narrative benefits; he only needs to say he loves his daughters. Loving his daughters and training them to be receivers of the same narrative are, for him, one act.

As for the sexuality Barker insisted on keeping: the piece keeps returning to pleasure and pain, sensuality, ritual, sophistication. The team treats it as taste, and the craft is real. But beautiful things are the easiest to swallow. Once pain becomes a dessert called the Pleasure & Pain Brain — teeth and skin as vanilla cake, blood as strawberry sauce — the Cenobites' whole provocation about bodies, desire, and transgression becomes a line on a menu. Barker's novella asks what you actually want. The house answers: queue here, come in, take a photo, buy the cup.

So the story isn't dirty and it isn't cruel. It just does a very old thing very well: it lets women be seduced, transformed, and "empowered" inside the story, and lets readers believe this is their own choice. Thirty-five years of haunted-house experience, and the skill they have really perfected isn't scaring people. It's pricing them.
