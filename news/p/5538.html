特权者的诉讼游戏：用“国家重要事务”作为逃避证据的武器The Litigant's Game: Using 'National Importance' as a Shield Against Evidence

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-13 § 链接
诉讼被武器化为一种权力表演，而法律程序则成了特权者的掩体。
Lawsuits are weaponized as power performances, and legal procedures become bunkers for the privileged.

这是一场典型的关于“解释权”的博弈。特朗普提起 100 亿美元的巨额诉讼，其目的从来不是为了通过法律程序寻求公正的赔偿，而是将司法系统转化为一种武器化的表达（weaponized expression）。通过起诉，他成功地将 BBC 拖入一个由他定义规则的战场，制造出一种“我被不公正对待”的叙事。

但当法律程序进入到最核心的 discovery 阶段——即必须面对事实证据的 deposition 时，特权者的真面目就露出来了。他的律师用“国家重要事务” (matters of national importance) 这种宏大叙事来搪塞法庭。这是一种极其典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 逻辑：通过将自己的身份与“国家”绑定，试图让自己凌驾于公民社会的通用规则之上。在特权者的认知里，法律应该是他攻击他人的剑，而不应该是约束他自身的锁链。

BBC 的处境揭示了结构性暴力 (structural violence) 的一个切面：即便在标榜法治的系统中，当权力足够大时，程序公正也会被这种“身份政治”所稀释。特权者在享受起诉带来的舆论红利的同时，通过拖延战术消解对方的诉讼能力。这种“我可以起诉你，但你不能质询我”的逻辑，本质上是对法律解释权的垄断。

如果法官最终允许他通过这种理由逃避质询，那么这次法律博弈的结果将再次证明：在权力面前，事实是次要的，而能够定义“什么是重要事务”的权力才是决定胜负的唯一尺度。

This is a classic game of fighting for the right of interpretation. Trump's $10bn lawsuit is never about seeking just compensation through legal channels; it is a weaponized expression. By suing, he drags the BBC into a battlefield where he defines the rules, manufacturing a narrative of being 'unfairly treated.'

However, when the legal process reaches the core of the discovery phase—the deposition, where one must face factual evidence—the face of privilege is revealed. His lawyers use the grand narrative of 'matters of national importance' to dodge the court. This is a textbook manifestation of meta violence: by binding his identity to 'the State,' he attempts to place himself above the common rules of civil society. In the mind of the privileged, the law should be a sword to attack others, not a chain to constrain himself.

The BBC's situation reveals a facet of structural violence: even in systems boasting the rule of law, procedural justice is diluted by this kind of identity politics when power is sufficiently concentrated. The privileged enjoy the PR dividends of filing a suit while dissolving the opponent's legal capacity through stalling tactics. The logic of 'I can sue you, but you cannot question me' is essentially a monopoly over the interpretation of the law.

If the judge ultimately allows him to evade deposition based on such excuses, this legal gamble will once again prove that in the face of power, facts are secondary. The only scale that determines victory is the power to define what constitutes a 'matter of importance.'
