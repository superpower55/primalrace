用拳击手的“勇气”掩盖机场的血迹Using a Boxer's 'Courage' to Mask the Bloodstains at the Airport

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-12 § 链接
当死亡成为背景板，英雄主义就成了sportswashing的遮羞布。
When death becomes a backdrop, heroism becomes a shroud for sportswashing.

机场被导弹袭击，13个人死亡，数百人受伤。在这种 direct violence 面前，沙特官员 Turki al-Sheikh 抛出的叙事竟然是：Canelo Álvarez “毫无畏惧” (fears nothing)。这是一个极其典型的 weaponized 表达。它试图将一场关乎生存的恐怖袭击，通过一个体育巨星的“勇气”叙事，转化为一种关于忠诚、友谊和“和平之地”的文化表演。

在 Al-Sheikh 的逻辑里，Canelo 的赴约不是一次商业博弈，而是一次对“文明与历史”的致敬。这正是 meta violence 的运作方式：用一个被神化的男性主体（拳击冠军）的强悍，来抵消结构性暴力 (structural violence) 带来的不安。当民众在废墟中寻找亲人时，权力阶层在社交媒体上庆祝“真朋友”的出现。这种叙事将死亡边缘化，把体育赛事变成了一个认知入口，试图告诉世界：只要最强的男人不害怕，这里就是安全的。

这场比赛本质上是一次昂贵的共谋 (complicity)。Canelo 追求的是在败给 Crawford 后的存在性回归，而沙特追求的是通过 sportswashing 抹除其人权记录和地缘政治危机。双方在金钱和名望的博弈中达成了最优解：拳击手用自己的身体作为担保，为一个处于战争阴影下的政权背书。在这种共谋中，死在机场的平民成了这场盛大演出中被刻意忽略的背景噪音。

最讽刺的是，Canelo 还在谈论需要 21 天来适应时差。在有人因为导弹袭击而永远失去时间的时候，这种对“舒适度”的追求，恰恰揭示了权力和资源分配的极度不对称。这场所谓的“勇气之战”，不过是一场由金钱驱动的表演，其目的是为了证明：只要钱给够，任何血腥的现实都可以被包装成英雄主义的浪漫叙事。

Airports struck by missiles, 13 dead, hundreds wounded. In the face of such direct violence, Saudi official Turki al-Sheikh deploys a narrative that Canelo Álvarez “fears nothing.” This is a textbook example of weaponized expression. It attempts to transform a survival-level terror attack into a cultural performance of loyalty, friendship, and a “land of peace” through the lens of a sports superstar’s courage.

In Al-Sheikh's logic, Canelo's commitment is not a business gamble, but a tribute to “civilization and history.” This is exactly how meta violence operates: using the perceived toughness of a mythologized masculine subject (a boxing champion) to offset the anxiety caused by structural violence. While civilians search for loved ones in the rubble, the power elite celebrates “true friends” on social media. This narrative marginalizes death and turns a sporting event into a cognitive entry point, suggesting that as long as the strongest man is unafraid, the land remains safe.

This fight is essentially an expensive act of complicity. Canelo seeks an existential comeback after losing to Crawford, while Saudi Arabia seeks to erase its human rights record and geopolitical crises through sportswashing. Both parties have reached an optimal expression in a game of money and fame: the boxer uses his body as a guarantee to endorse a regime under the shadow of war. In this complicity, the civilians killed at the airport become the deliberately ignored background noise of a grand spectacle.

The ultimate irony is Canelo discussing the need for 21 days to adjust to the time zone. While some have lost their time forever due to missile strikes, this obsession with “comfort” reveals the extreme asymmetry of power and resource distribution. This so-called “fight of courage” is nothing more than a money-driven performance, designed to prove that with enough cash, any bloody reality can be repackaged as a romantic narrative of heroism.
