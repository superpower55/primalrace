毒物和毒贩睡一张床The Poisoned Sleep With the Poisoners

其他 文化层 · 结构层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要一批公共健康倡导者组成的「让美国再次健康」（MAHA）运动，与长期反对塑料监管的共和党结盟。报道回顾1974年塑料行业内部已得知微塑料可进入血液和器官，却持续压制监管；如今MAHA一边担忧微塑料，一边支持接受石化企业政治捐款的政客。2025年3月，两大行业游说团体向共和党捐款后，于7月获得拜登时期空气污染规则的豁免。
The newsThe MAHA movement, a coalition of public-health advocates, has allied with Republicans who have long fought plastics regulation. The report traces how plastics executives knew since 1974 that microplastics enter blood and organs while suppressing regulation; today MAHA supporters back politicians funded by the same industry. In March 2025 two trade groups donated to Republicans, and by July they had won exemptions from Biden-era air-quality rules.
他们不是没看见毒，是毒贩替他们买了单。
They didn't miss the poison. The poisoners paid for their silence.

1974年，一家德国医生在纽约的 Hotel Delmonico 给塑料和石化公司的 executives 放了一段「恐怖故事」：被吞下的塑料微粒能穿过动物肠壁，进入血液、淋巴，再到大脑和肝脏。五十年后，这些微粒出现在人的血液、大脑、胎盘和睾丸里。中间这五十年，行业没有停下生产，只是停下了承认。

这是文化暴力的标准操作：先制造一个「我们还在活得更久」的叙事，再用它盖住已经被自己人记录在案的事实。1984年 Conoco 化学公司的一位 executive 说，我们活在一堆致癌物里，不但活着，还活得更久。这句话不是无知，是定价——把风险定价为零，把利润定价为全部，差额由所有人的身体支付。

MAHA 的问题不在于它担忧微塑料。担忧是对的。问题在于它把这份担忧交给了一个收钱办事的党。报道写得很清楚：2025年3月19日，代表美国最大塑料和石化制造商的 AFPM 和 ACC 向共和党候选人及共和党相关 PAC 捐了13笔共5万美元，同一天给民主党3笔共1万美元；月底，它们向 EPA 申请豁免拜登时期的空气质量规则；7月，在更多会议和更多捐款之后，它们拿到了想要的。

这不是「被蒙蔽」，这是共谋者理论的教科书案例。共谋不需要说谎，只需要在正确的时刻把支票递到正确的人手里，然后让另一群人继续在集会上喊「让美国再次健康」。回报是豁免，代价是所有人的血液。

更值得盯住的是 MAHA 高层在这场交易里的位置。Zen Honeycutt 在爱荷华州的竞选集会上与 Kennedy 交谈，又与刚辞职的 Calley Means 合影；MAHA 峰会由 Kennedy 的长期出版人 Tony Lyons 运营的机构组织，赞助商包括 Walmart 和靠卖 GLP-1、抗抑郁药、非那雄胺给焦虑的中产千禧一代赚钱的 Hims & Hers。一个反监管议程下的健康运动，赞助商全是靠这个议程获利的人。

报道里最诚实的一句话来自 Heritage Foundation 的 Jennifer Galardi：她警告 MAHA 远离 Bryan Johnson 那种「重新想象人性」、超越死亡的「反乌托邦宗教」，主张用「拥抱挣扎、痛苦甚至死亡」来取代它。一个长期帮企业接管政府的智库，现在来教健康运动接受痛苦。这不是反思，这是换一个更便宜的叙事继续卖。

真正的问题不是 MAHA 虚伪。虚伪太好拆了。真正的问题是：一堆有据可查的事实，为什么这么难变成问责、变成持久的政治多数、变成能扛过换届的政策。Exxon 几十年前就知道化石燃料在让地球变暖，曝光之后它照样赚了十几年的纪录利润。事实不缺，缺的是把事实变成代价的机制。

民主党如果想接住 MAHA 里那些真的担心自己身体的人，起点不该是「企业虚伪」或「政治献金」这种左派老话题，而是一句更基本的话：这东西在让我们生病，它应该停下来。但这句话要成立，得有人为「停下来」付账——报道没有写谁愿意付，也没有写那些被豁免的工厂附近的居民现在在呼吸什么。

In 1974, a German gastroenterologist stood before plastics and petrochemical executives at New York's Hotel Delmonico and showed them a horror story: microscopic plastic particles swallowed by animals crossed the intestinal lining, entered blood and lymph, and reached the brain and liver. Fifty years later, those particles are in human blood, brains, placentas, and testicles. In between, the industry never stopped producing. It only stopped admitting.

This is cultural violence in its standard form: manufacture a narrative—we live in a sea of carcinogens and we're living longer—and use it to cover facts your own people already recorded. A Conoco Chemical executive said exactly that in 1984. That wasn't ignorance. That was pricing: risk at zero, profit at everything, the difference paid by everyone's body.

MAHA's problem isn't that it worries about microplastics. The worry is correct. The problem is that it handed the worry to a party on retainer. The report lays out the sequence: on March 19, 2025, the AFPM and ACC—trade groups for the country's largest plastics and petrochemical manufacturers—made 13 donations totaling $50,000 to Republican candidates and Republican-aligned PACs, and three totaling $10,000 to Democrats. By month's end they had asked the EPA for a two-year exemption from Biden-era air-quality rules. By July, after more meetings and more donations, they had it.

This isn't being duped. This is the co-conspirator theory in plain sight. Conspiracy doesn't require lying; it requires handing the check to the right person at the right moment while another crowd chants Make America Healthy Again. The return is an exemption. The cost is everybody's blood.

Watch where MAHA's leadership stands in the transaction. Zen Honeycutt chatted with Kennedy at an Iowa rally and posed with Calley Means, who had just resigned. The MAHA Summit was organized by a nonprofit run by Kennedy's longtime publisher, Tony Lyons, and sponsored by Walmart and by Hims & Hers—a company that sells GLP-1s, antidepressants, and finasteride to anxious aging millennials. A health movement under a deregulatory agenda, sponsored by everyone who profits from deregulation.

The most honest line comes from the Heritage Foundation's Jennifer Galardi, warning MAHA away from Bryan Johnson's dystopian religion of transcending death and toward embracing struggle, pain, and even death. A think tank that spent decades letting companies run the government now instructs a health movement to accept suffering. That isn't reflection. That's a cheaper narrative.

The real trouble isn't MAHA's hypocrisy. Hypocrisy is easy. The real trouble is how hard it is to turn a well-documented arsenal of facts into accountability, lasting majorities, or policies that survive an administration change. Exxon knew for decades that fossil fuels warm the planet and has posted record profits in the years since. Facts aren't scarce. The mechanism that turns facts into costs is.

If Democrats want to reach the MAHA rank and file who genuinely fear for their bodies, the opening isn't corporate hypocrisy or money in politics. It's something more basic: this stuff is making us sick, and it should stop. But for that sentence to hold, someone has to pay for the stopping—and the report never says who will, or what the people near the exempted plants are breathing now.
