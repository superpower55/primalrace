前排座位的“沉浸感”与被掩盖的剥削The 'Immersion' of the Front Row and the Masked Exploitation

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-08 § 链接
所谓的沉浸感，往往是权力结构中最低端位置的自我合理化。
So-called immersion is often just a self-rationalization of the lowest position in a power structure.

这篇文章试图把电影院前排座位的“糟糕体验”重新定义为一种“极致的沉浸感”。这种论调非常典型：当一个人处于结构性的劣势位置——也就是那个脖子酸痛、视野畸形的 Row A 时，最简单的生存策略就是通过改变叙事，将这种被动地接受低质资源的行为包装成一种主动的、具有审美高度的“选择”。

作者在文中大谈特谈大屏幕如何“浸染”观众，甚至把盯着男演员额头上的毛孔看描述为一种特权。这本质上是一种典型的 cultural violence。它在试图说服那些处于底层的 punters：你们不是被电影院剥削了（在空间布局上被挤到了最差的位置），而是在享受一种“真正的电影体验”。这种叙事逻辑和那些告诉底层劳动者“奋斗才有意义”的 scam 没有任何区别——它通过重新定义“价值”，让受害者在潜意识中完成自我规训。

更讽刺的是，作者提到的那些“优势”：没有被前排挡住、可以随意伸腿、价格便宜。这些所谓的 benefit，其实是结构性匮乏的代偿。当你失去了一个公正的、符合生物力学的观看视角（Potential）时，你只能在“不用挤人”这种微小的 Actual 补偿中寻找心理平衡。这根本不是最优解表达，而是一种在权力等级最低端尝试自救的假.最优解。

电影院的座位分布本身就是一个微小的权力场。电影公司为了 squeeze every last penny，将座位尽可能地向屏幕推进，制造出物理上的压迫感。而评论者则在文化层面上为这种压迫背书。当一个社会开始鼓励人们在“脖子酸痛”中寻找“宏大感”时，这种对身体不适的漠视，正是元暴力在日常生活中的具体投射：只要叙事足够宏大，个体的生物性痛苦就可以被忽略。

This article attempts to rebrand the 'terrible experience' of the cinema's front row as a 'supreme immersion.' This is a classic move: when an individual is placed in a structural disadvantage—stuck in Row A with a distorted view and a cramping neck—the simplest survival strategy is to pivot the narrative, packaging the passive acceptance of low-quality resources as an active, aesthetic 'choice.'

The author raves about how the screen 'drenches' the viewer, describing the act of staring at a male actor's pores as a privilege. This is textbook cultural violence. It seeks to convince the punters that they aren't being exploited by the cinema's spatial greed, but are instead enjoying a 'true cinematic experience.' This logic is no different from the scams that tell laborers that 'struggle is a virtue'—it uses a redefinition of 'value' to facilitate the self-discipline of the victim.

More ironic are the mentioned 'benefits': no obstructions, room to stretch legs, and lower prices. These are merely compensations for structural deprivation. When you are robbed of a just, biologically sound viewing perspective (Potential), you are forced to find psychological balance in the tiny Actual compensation of 'not having to squeeze past people.' This isn't a True Optimal Expression; it's a Fake Optimal Expression attempted by those at the bottom of the hierarchy.

The seating chart of a cinema is a miniature power field. Cinema firms, eager to squeeze every last penny, push seats closer to the screen, creating physical oppression. The critic then provides the cultural endorsement for this oppression. When a society encourages people to find 'grandeur' in 'neck pain,' this disregard for biological discomfort is a concrete projection of meta-violence in daily life: as long as the narrative is colossal enough, individual biological suffering can be ignored.
