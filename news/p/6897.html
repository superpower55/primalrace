一张选票，一场私人恩怨One Ballot, One Personal Vendetta

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要中期选举投票前夜，密歇根州初选落败的民主党人 Haley Stevens 撤回对参议院候选人 Abdul El-Sayed 的支持，转而背书共和党人 Mike Rogers，民主党领导层谴责，特朗普表示欢迎。她未能清楚解释理由，只称自己感到「受伤」。特朗普政府此前在加州和阿拉斯加提出的选票相关诉讼均被法院驳回。
The newsOn election eve, Haley Stevens, who lost Michigan's Democratic Senate primary to Abdul El-Sayed, withdrew her support for him and endorsed Republican Mike Rogers. Democratic leaders condemned the move and Trump praised it; Stevens could not clearly explain her reasoning, saying only that she felt hurt. Courts had already dismissed the Trump administration's pre-election ballot challenges in California and Alaska.
解释权不在投票的人手里，在讲故事的人手里。
The power to explain beats the power to vote.

「美国民主可能取决于谁赢下参议院，而谁赢下参议院可能取决于这一桩小小的私人恩怨。」David Corn 这句话说得很准，但他把它当成荒诞剧的台词，其实它是常规操作。

Haley Stevens 在初选里输给了 Abdul El-Sayed，然后走进底特律的一座黑人教堂，背书共和党人 Mike Rogers。Anderson Cooper 追问了八到十个小时里到底发生了什么变化，她答不出来，只能说「我受伤了」。一个参议院席位的归属，被一个人的情绪定价。这不是民主的背叛，这是民主本来的运行方式——它一直由人的表达驱动，而不是由程序驱动。

值得注意的是她把这场戏放在哪里演。Garrison Hayes 说，Stevens 多年经营的形象是「奥巴马式的黑人选民传声筒」，而她的背书选在黑人教堂里发布，当地黑人选民的反应是困惑和被背叛。她借用的不是自己的信用，是一个她从未属于的群体的信用。这是表达武器化最标准的一手：拿别人的身份当自己的认知入口，用完就丢。

民主党这边的处理也很干净。舒默谴责，密歇根民主党让她把党证撕了，Harris 和 Obama 都在为 El-Sayed 站台。Corn 说得很直白：初选一结束，党只关心多数席位，谁有用就支持谁。所以这不是原则的胜利，是算术的胜利。Stevens 被踢出去，不是因为她背叛了什么价值，是因为她不再能换算成票。

另一半的荒诞在纽约第 21 选区。特朗普两年前在这里赢了 21 个点，共和党候选人 Anthony Constantino 自己砸了 1700 万美元——众议院竞选史上个人投入最多的级别之一——然后还需要副总统在选举前夜飞来救场。他在集会上说记者不是「假新闻」，是「邪恶新闻」，应该被「删除」。一个把新闻业命名为敌人的人，正在竞选制定规则的席位。这不是失态，这是竞选策略：先取消对方说话的资格，剩下的场子就是自己的。

特朗普政府在加州和阿拉斯加提前打的选票官司都被法院驳回了——阿拉斯加那桩因为两个 Dan Sullivan 同名而起，加州那桩要求作废两万张邮寄选票，报道说没有证据。Corn 判断这只是为选后挑战铺地基。铺地基这个词很准确：暴力不需要当场生效，它只需要让结果的可信度先被稀释。等票数出来，争的已经不是谁赢，是谁有资格宣布谁赢。

Constantino 骑着代步车、载着一位老兵的画面被当成这一轮选举的收尾镜头。好笑，但底下是另一件事：一个把「删除记者」当口号的人，拿到了副总统的站台和一千七百万美元的自背书。这套东西不需要赢，它只需要被看见。

「American democracy may hinge on who wins the Senate, and who wins the Senate may hinge on this little personal vendetta.」 David Corn meant that as the punchline of an absurdist drama. It is actually the operating manual.

Haley Stevens lost the Democratic primary to Abdul El-Sayed, then walked into a Black church in Detroit and endorsed the Republican, Mike Rogers. Anderson Cooper asked her what changed in the eight to ten hours between saying she was staying with El-Sayed and announcing the endorsement. She could not say. She felt hurt. The control of a Senate seat got priced by one person's mood. This is not a betrayal of democracy; this is democracy running exactly as it runs, on expression rather than procedure.

Note where she staged it. Garrison Hayes describes Stevens as having spent years cultivating an Obama-adjacent Black-voter-whisperer persona. She released the endorsement inside a Black church, and the Black voters in the room were blindsided. She borrowed credibility from a community she never belonged to. That is the standard move: take someone else's identity as your cognitive entry point, then discard it.

The Democratic response was just as clean. Schumer condemned, the Michigan party told her to tear up her card, Harris and Obama are campaigning for El-Sayed. Corn says it plainly: once primaries end, the party cares about the majority, and whoever delivers it gets support. Stevens was not expelled for betraying a value. She was expelled because she stopped converting into votes.

The other half of the farce is New York's 21st. Trump carried it by 21 points two years ago, and the Republican, Anthony Constantino, put in about $17 million of his own money, among the most ever self-funded into a House race, and still needed the vice president flown in on election eve to bail him out. At the rally he said the press is not the fake news but the evil news, and should be deleted. A man who names journalism the enemy is running for a seat that writes the rules. That is not a gaffe. It is the strategy: disqualify the other side's speech first, and the room is yours.

The administration's pre-election ballot suits in California and Alaska were both dismissed, the Alaska one over two Dan Sullivans on the ballot, the California one seeking to set aside 20,000 mail-in ballots the report describes as claimed without evidence. Corn reads them as laying a foundation for post-election challenges. Foundation is the right word. Violence does not need to land now; it only needs to dilute the credibility of the count in advance. Once the numbers arrive, the fight is no longer who won. It is who is entitled to say who won.

Constantino rolling out of the hangar on a mobility scooter beside an elderly veteran got billed as the closing image of the cycle. Funny, but underneath: a man whose pitch is deleting reporters got a vice presidential visit and $17 million of his own money. The pitch does not need to win. It only needs to be seen.
