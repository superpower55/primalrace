三十年后，烂片改名叫喜剧Thirty Years Later, a Flop Becomes a Comedy

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要1994年电影《街头霸王》的编剧兼导演史蒂文·E·德·索萨近日回应外界对该片喜剧风格的讨论，称影片是「有意搞笑」。他提到当年上映时影评多为差评，如今新一部《街头霸王》本月初上映并夺得票房第一，还包含向1994年版致敬的桥段。
The newsSteven E. de Souza, writer-director of the 1994 Street Fighter film, defended its comedy as intentional, saying reviews at the time were mostly harsh. The new Street Fighter opened No. 1 at the box office this month and includes callbacks to his 1994 version.
评价方式一换，能力就不用改了。
Change the ruler, and the ability never has to change.

一部电影被骂了三十年，现在导演说：你们当年没看懂，它本来就是故意搞笑的。这不是创作，这是重新定义。

1994 年《街头霸王》上映时，评论界的主流说法是这片子蠢得离谱，蠢得意外好笑。德·索萨现在把这句话反过来了：不是意外，是故意。他的证据是两年三十周年时出现的一批修正文章，说其实它本来就该是喜剧。一篇评论改变不了电影，一批评论也改变不了电影——电影还是那部电影。变的是尺子。当年按“正剧”量它，它不及格；现在按“坎普喜剧”量它，它及格了。能力没动，评价方式动了。

这套操作在芯片、GDP 和疫情数据里我都见过：现实不配合，就换一把尺子，然后宣布自己一直站在刻度上。τ law 和 Moore's Law 的区别就在这里——一个是声明出来的，一个是观察出来的。德·索萨声明他拍的是喜剧，观众当年观察到的是尴尬。

有意思的是他抱怨的对象。社交媒体上有人说新片不像 94 版那么端着、更好笑，他听了觉得荒谬：现在 94 版反倒成了严肃正剧。他真正在意的不是好笑不好笑，是谁被记成“故意”的那一个。三十年前观众笑他，是笑他拍砸了；三十年后观众笑他，是笑他拍对了。同样是笑，解释权在谁手里，结论就完全不同。

报道里真正有分量的一句被他顺带说了出来：送审那周俄亥俄州发生了校园枪击，打斗被剪到接近周六早晨卡通的水平。一部改编自格斗游戏、以武术锦标赛为招牌的电影，因为现实里的枪，把自己的招牌拆了。这才是那部片子真正的结构处境——不是导演不懂喜剧，是分级委员会和一场枪击决定了观众能看到多少拳头。这一层他没多谈，因为谈它就不是喜剧问题了。

所以别被“故意”两个字骗了。一部作品是不是喜剧，不由作者事后声明决定，由它在观众那里实际发生了什么决定。三十年前它让人尴尬地笑，三十年后它让人怀旧地笑，两次笑都不是因为导演算准了。把偶然说成设计，是创作者最省力的一种自我修复。

新片票房第一，旧片进了邪典殿堂，导演拿到了三本图像小说的合约。皆大欢喜。只是那场让他剪掉打斗的枪击，没有出现在任何一版“其实它很好”的修正文章里。

A movie got panned for thirty years. Now the director says you missed the point: it was intentionally funny all along. That isn't creation. That's redefinition.

When Street Fighter opened in 1994, the going critical line was that it was so stupid it was accidentally funny. De Souza now flips it: not accidental, intentional. His evidence is a batch of revisionist pieces that appeared around the thirtieth anniversary, arguing it was supposed to be funny. One review can't change a movie. A batch of reviews can't either. The movie is the same movie. What changed is the ruler. Measured as drama, it failed. Measured as camp comedy, it passes. The ability didn't move. The evaluation did.

I've seen this move in chips, GDP and pandemic data: when reality won't cooperate, swap the ruler and announce you were standing on the scale the whole time. That's the difference between a τ law and Moore's Law — one is declared, the other observed. De Souza declares he made a comedy. Audiences observed embarrassment.

What's telling is who he's annoyed at. People on social media say the new film, unlike the '94 one, doesn't take itself seriously. He finds it absurd: now the '94 version is apparently a serious drama. What he actually cares about isn't whether it's funny. It's who gets remembered as having meant it. Thirty years ago people laughed because he'd botched it. Now they laugh because he nailed it. Same laugh, different owner of the explanation, opposite verdict.

The one line in the piece with real weight slips out almost in passing: the week the film went to the ratings board, there was a school shooting in Ohio, and the fights were cut back to almost Saturday-morning-cartoon level. A movie adapted from a fighting game, whose selling point is a martial arts tournament, had its selling point dismantled because of a real shooting. That's the film's actual structural position — not that the director didn't understand comedy, but that a ratings board and a shooting decided how many punches the audience would see. He doesn't dwell on it, because dwelling on it stops being a comedy problem.

So don't buy the word "intentionally." Whether a work is a comedy isn't settled by the author's later statement. It's settled by what actually happened to it in front of an audience. People laughed with embarrassment thirty years ago and with nostalgia now, and neither laugh was engineered. Calling the accident a design is the cheapest form of self-repair available to a creator.

The new film opened at No. 1, the old one has a cult following, and the director just signed for three graphic novels. Everyone's happy. Only the shooting that made him cut the fights doesn't appear in any version of "actually it was good."
