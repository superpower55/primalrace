网易关掉的不是工作室，是账本NetEase Didn't Kill a Studio, It Killed a Line Item

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要由《如龙》创作者名越稔洋创立的 Nagoshi Studio 已正式解散，日本官方公报确认其于10月31日注销。此前彭博社报道称，网易因得知《Gang of Dragon》还需追加70亿日元（4400万美元）开发费用而停止出资，工作室未能找到新投资方。
The newsNagoshi Studio, founded by Yakuza creator Toshihiro Nagoshi, has officially dissolved, Japan's official gazette Kanpo confirmed, with the company dissolved on Oct. 31. Bloomberg reported in March that NetEase cut off funding after learning Gang of Dragon would cost another 7 billion yen ($44 million) to complete, and the studio failed to find another backer.
资本不杀创意，资本只算成本
Capital doesn't kill creativity. It just prices it out.

一家工作室解散，新闻写成"找不到下一个金主"。这句话把主语放错了。真正发生的是：网易算了一笔账，觉得再投70亿日元（约4400万美元，来源：Polygon）把《Gang of Dragon》做完不划算，于是抽走了钱。名越稔洋的团队没有输给创作，输给了一张资产负债表。

这就是文化暴力最日常的形态：它不封杀你，它只是不再给你钱，然后让市场替你解释成"你不够好"。第三章讲表达的武器化，认知入口不只有审查和宣传，还有"什么值得被生产"的定价权。谁掌握发行预算，谁就掌握哪一种叙事能出现在屏幕上。黑帮题材、极道东京、马东锡主演——这些表达本来可以存在，只是没有被允许存在。

网易从2024年起收缩海外游戏投资，关掉的不止这一家，还有Greg Street的MMO工作室。这不是针对某个人的惩罚，是结构性暴力的标准动作：决策发生在离创作者很远的会议室里，代价落在具体的人身上——三十年在世嘉做《Daytona USA》《Shenmue》《Super Monkey Ball》再做出《如龙》的人，最后拿到一纸解散公告。

报道没写的是：那70亿日元的缺口，谁来填？没有答案。工作室没能找到接盘者，于是公司于10月31日解散。这里缺席的声音不是名越的，是那些跟着他从Ryu Ga Gotoku出来、押上职业生涯的中层开发者。他们的名字一个都没出现。

游戏行业管这叫"项目取消"。用暴力三角的尺子量：本可以完成的游戏、本可以延续的团队、本可以存在的表达，全部低于可达状态，差额就是暴力。施暴者没有面孔，只有一份投资组合。

下一次你在预告片里看到一部野心很大的新作，记住它先要通过的不是审查，是财务。而财务从不解释自己为什么说不。

A studio dissolves, and the story gets written as "couldn't find another backer." Wrong subject. What actually happened: NetEase ran the numbers, decided another 7 billion yen (about $44 million, per Polygon) to finish Gang of Dragon wasn't worth it, and pulled the money. Nagoshi's team didn't lose to craft. It lost to a balance sheet.

This is cultural violence in its most ordinary form. It doesn't ban you. It just stops funding you, then lets the market narrate it as "you weren't good enough." Chapter 3's weaponized expression has more entry points than censorship and propaganda — there's also the pricing power over what gets made at all. Whoever holds the production budget decides which narratives reach a screen. A yakuza crime drama set in Kabukicho, starring Ma Dong-seok, could have existed. It just wasn't permitted to.

NetEase has been shrinking its overseas game portfolio since 2024, closing more than this one studio — Greg Street's MMO studio went too. This isn't punishment aimed at a person. It's structural violence doing its standard move: the decision happens in a meeting room far from the creators, and the cost lands on actual people — a man who spent over 30 years at Sega on Daytona USA, Shenmue, Super Monkey Ball, then built Yakuza, receives a dissolution notice.

What the report doesn't say: who fills that 7 billion yen gap? No one. The studio failed to find a backer, and the company was dissolved on Oct. 31. The missing voice isn't Nagoshi's. It's the mid-level developers who left Ryu Ga Gotoku with him and bet their careers on this. Not one of their names appears.

The industry calls this "project cancellation." Run it through the Violence Triangle: a game that could have been finished, a team that could have continued, an expression that could have existed — all below the achievable state. The gap is the violence. The perpetrator has no face, only a portfolio.

The next time a trailer shows you something ambitious, remember what it had to pass first wasn't censorship. It was finance. And finance never explains why it said no.
