别把女性的色彩当成装饰品Stop Treating Femininity as a Decorative Accessory

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-18 § 链接
当“女性化”被简化为一种审美风格时，它就成了抹除主体性的文化暴力。
When 'femininity' is reduced to an aesthetic style, it becomes a cultural violence that erases subjectivity.

很多人看到 France-Lise McGurn 的马卡龙色调，第一反应就是“噢，好女性化”。这种反应本身就是一种典型的 cultural violence。在男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）中，女性的表达被预设为一种“装饰性”的附属品：只要是粉色、柔和、轻盈的，就自动被归类为“女性化”，而这种归类直接导致了深度的缺失。在这种逻辑下，女性化 = 浅薄，而男性化 = 深度。这是一种极其隐蔽的认知入口拦截，它让女性在进入公共讨论空间前，先被贴上一个“装饰品”的标签，从而剥夺了她们定义自身深度的权利。

McGurn 聪明的地方在于她把这种误读变成了博弈的筹码。她使用自己的身体作为参考，但将其抽象化为“符号”（glyphs），这是在对抗被客体化的凝视。她不再扮演一个等待被观看的个体，而是制造一个邀请对方进入的场域。尤其是她对 Katy Perry 亲吻地球那张照片的解读——一个女性在享受太空探索的快感后，面对其带来的破坏而亲吻大地。这种矛盾性精准地捕捉到了现代女性的处境：在获得权力与自由的同时，必须面对结构性破坏的悖论。

最令我感兴趣的是她母亲 Rita McGurn 的那句“不要等待工作室”。这是一种极其激进的表达：绘画不应该被禁锢在被制度化、被男性定义的“艺术空间”里，而应该发生在真实的生活中，发生在沙发上、墙壁上。这本质上是在打破 structural violence 设定的专业壁垒。当艺术不再需要一个昂贵的、被认可的“席位”才能发生，表达就从一种特权变成了生存的本能。这种“画一切”的 ethos，其实就是一种存在性战争的胜利：我不需要你的准入证，我的生活本身就是我的画室。

Many see France-Lise McGurn's pastel palette and immediately think, "Oh, how feminine!" This reaction is a textbook example of cultural violence. Within the masculine-centric narrative, feminine expression is preset as a 'decorative' appendage: anything pink, soft, or light is automatically categorized as 'feminine,' and this categorization leads directly to a perceived lack of depth. In this logic, feminine = superficial, while masculine = profound. This is a subtle hijacking of the cognitive entrance, labeling women as 'decorations' before they even enter the public discourse, thereby stripping them of the right to define their own depth.

McGurn is clever in turning this misreading into a strategic gambit. She uses her own body as a reference but abstracts it into 'glyphs,' resisting the objectifying gaze. She no longer performs as an individual waiting to be looked at; instead, she creates a field that invites others to enter. Her interpretation of the Katy Perry photo—a woman kissing the Earth after the harm caused by her space travel—precisely captures the paradox of modern womanhood: the tension between gaining power and freedom while facing structural destruction.

What fascinates me most is her mother Rita McGurn's maxim: "don't wait for the studio." This is a radical expression: painting should not be confined to 'art spaces' institutionalized and defined by men, but should happen within real life, on sofas and walls. This is essentially breaking the structural violence of professional barriers. When art no longer requires an expensive, recognized 'seat' to exist, expression shifts from a privilege to a survival instinct. This 'paint-everything' ethos is a victory in the existential war: I don't need your permit; my life is my studio.
