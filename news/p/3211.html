被豁免的强奸犯与被出卖的家暴受害者Exempted Rapists and Betrayed Domestic Abuse Survivors

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-03 § 链接
用“性犯罪”做PR遮羞，用“家暴”填补监狱容量的结构性空洞。
Using 'sex crimes' as PR cover while using 'domestic abuse' to plug the structural holes of prison capacity.

这是一场典型的、极其卑劣的政治博弈。PM Andy Burnham 以为通过排除强奸犯和儿童性侵者就能平息愤怒，但这不过是选择了一个最容易被大众感知的“认知入口”来制造一个所谓的胜利。在文化层面上，强奸和儿童侵害被赋予了极高的道德厌恶值，将其作为豁免对象，是成本最低的 PR 操纵，旨在掩盖一个事实：他依然决定让 domestic abusers（家暴者）提前出狱。

这里揭露了极其残酷的 Meta Violence（元暴力）：在男本位叙事中，针对陌生人的性暴力被视为“极恶”，而发生在私密空间的 domestic abuse 却依然被视为“家务事”或“次要矛盾”。将家暴者排除在保护名单之外，本质上是对女性在私域中遭受暴力的结构性漠视。家暴者对受害者的控制是 fixated（执迷）且持续的，将他们提前释放，等同于由国家机器背书，将受害者重新推回施暴者的掌控之下。

谁在共谋？政府在共谋，用一个 £10m 的 victim support 礼包来掩盖制度性地将受害者置于风险之中的决定。而那些庆祝“强奸犯不能早出狱”的公众，在无意识中参与了这次共谋——他们接受了这种被阉割的正义，从而允许了家暴这种结构性暴力继续被当作监狱容量危机的“牺牲品”。

Potential − Actual 的差额不仅没有缩小，反而因为这种选择性的“仁慈”而变得更加讽刺。当国家在定义谁是“不可原谅的罪犯”时，它实际上在定义谁的痛苦是“可以被容忍的”。

This is a classic and despicable political game. PM Andy Burnham believes that by excluding rapists and child abusers, he can quell the public rage. This is nothing more than choosing the most visible 'cognitive entry point' to manufacture a perceived victory. Culturally, rape and child abuse carry the highest levels of moral abhorrence; making them the exempt group is the lowest-cost PR manipulation to mask a grim reality: he still intends to let domestic abusers go free.

This reveals a brutal Meta Violence: in a masculine-centric narrative, sexual violence by strangers is labeled 'extreme evil,' while domestic abuse in private spaces remains a 'domestic matter' or a 'secondary conflict.' Excluding domestic abusers from protection is a structural disregard for the violence women suffer in private. Domestic abusers are often fixated on their victims; releasing them early is essentially the state endorsing the return of the victim to the perpetrator's control.

Who are the complicit parties? The government is complicit, using a £10m victim support package to cover up a decision that systematically places survivors at risk. Meanwhile, the public celebrating that 'rapists won't leave early' unconsciously participates in this complicity—they accept this castrated version of justice, thereby allowing domestic abuse, a form of structural violence, to be the 'sacrificial lamb' for the prison overcrowding crisis.

The gap between Potential and Actual has not shrunk; instead, it has become more ironic due to this selective 'mercy.' When the state defines who is an 'unforgivable criminal,' it is actually defining whose suffering is 'tolerable.'
