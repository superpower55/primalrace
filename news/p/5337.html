品味的定价权与中产阶级的审美诈骗The Pricing Power of Taste and the Bourgeois Aesthetic Scam

哲学 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-30 § 链接
审美不是关于美，而是关于谁有权定义什么是“酷”的筛选机制。
Aesthetics are not about beauty, but a screening mechanism of who defines what is 'cool'.

《卫报》这份秋季美食趋势清单，本质上是一份关于“认知入口”的操盘指南。从 Angel Delight 可颂到老干妈卡门贝尔奶酪，这些所谓的 trend 并不是味觉的进化，而是一次典型的 weaponized expression。它在告诉读者：在这个季度，什么样的组合才代表你处于社交阶层的上游。

最滑稽的是那种对“silly”和“school cake”的刻意追捧。这是一种极其傲慢的审美向下兼容：当中产阶级已经厌倦了精致的 fine dining，他们开始将底层的、廉价的、甚至是粗糙的童年记忆（如学校蛋糕）进行“艺术化”包装。这不是在致敬平民，而是在消费平民。通过将“廉价”定义为“酷”，他们完成了又一次品味上的定价权掠夺——只有足够富有且拥有认知资源的人，才能在吃着像“湿树枝”一样难吃的黄芪根时，依然觉得自己在引领潮流。

这种审美循环是一个闭环的 scam。从“极简主义”到“粗野主义”，再到现在的“波西米亚杂货铺风格”，每一步都在通过制造新的“筛选机制”来排斥异己。当你试图通过购买某种特定的热带醋或尝试“陶艺晚餐”来获得身份认同感时，你其实是在为这套由媒体和商业团队操纵的叙事投票。你以为你在选择生活方式，实际上你只是在扮演一个被定义好的、符合本季趋势的角色。

这种对“真实”的制造，让人们在追求“松弛感”和“原生感”的过程中，反而陷入了最深层的规训。最讽刺的是，当你终于在黑暗餐厅里穿着 Simone Rocha 却无人可见时，你消费的不再是食物或时尚，而是一种极其孤独的、关于“我知道我很有钱且很酷”的自我暗示。

This autumn food trend list from The Guardian is essentially a playbook for manipulating cognitive entry points. From Angel Delight croissants to Lao Gan Ma Camembert, these trends aren't an evolution of palate, but a typical weaponized expression. It tells the reader exactly which combinations signal that you are at the top of the social hierarchy this season.

The most absurd part is the calculated obsession with 'silly' and 'school cakes'. This is an arrogant form of aesthetic downward compatibility: having grown tired of refined fine dining, the bourgeoisie now 'articulates' and consumes the cheap, crude memories of the working class. This isn't a tribute to the common people; it's the consumption of them. By defining 'cheap' as 'cool', they execute another seizure of the pricing power of taste—only those wealthy enough with sufficient cognitive resources can eat something that tastes like 'wet twigs' and still believe they are leading a trend.

This aesthetic cycle is a closed-loop scam. From 'minimalism' to 'brutalism' and now to 'Bric-a-brac Boho', every step is a new screening mechanism designed to exclude others. When you attempt to gain identity by buying a specific tropical vinegar or attending a 'pottery dinner', you are simply voting for a narrative orchestrated by media and commercial teams. You think you are choosing a lifestyle, but you are merely performing a role predefined by the current trend.

This manufacturing of 'authenticity' traps people in the deepest form of discipline while they chase 'effortlessness'. The ultimate irony is dining in the dark wearing Simone Rocha where no one can see you; you are no longer consuming food or fashion, but a lonely self-suggestion: 'I know I am wealthy and cool'.
