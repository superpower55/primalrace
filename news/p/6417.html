万圣节最吓人的是选举周The Scariest Thing This Halloween Is Election Week

文娱 文化层 Vulture ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
老段子重播，是因为观众只记得住重复的脸
Old bits get replayed because audiences only remember repeated faces.

一个段子被重播，是因为它第一次演的时候观众笑了。一个人物被重播，是因为观众第一次看到他的时候记住了他的脸。SNL 昨晚把 David S. Pumpkins 和 Drunk Uncle 一起搬回来，两个都是 2016 年之后的老面孔，报道里自己说了，Pumpkins 和 Trumpkins 都出现在 2016 年，都是橙色的、都很烦人。这句话是玩笑，也是实话：一个国家的政治娱乐工业，靠的是同一批脸反复回来，让观众在熟悉感里确认自己还在原地。

这就是武器化最省力的入口——认知入口不需要新内容，只需要重复。重复制造熟悉，熟悉制造认同，认同制造“这就是我们的时代”的错觉。SNL 拿 Trump 来演，Trump 反过来成了段子的共演者，双方都从中拿到注意力。报道里 Johnson 的 Trump 让其余演员全程冻住，还点名“可怜的 Bobby Moynihan 飞过来就为这个”——这不是讽刺权力，这是把权力当成节目单上的一档固定栏目。

Drunk Uncle 那段更直白。他说自己从酿酒人夺冠那晚开始每晚都喝，之前五十七年也一直喝。五十七年是报道里的数字，不是我的。一个靠酒精撑着的角色，被当成可爱；一个说自己相信 Elizabeth Holmes 的人，被当成勇敢。这两句都是玩笑，但玩笑的方向是：把判断力让渡出去这件事，包装成一种无害的、值得同情的性格。观众笑的是他，不是自己。

真正的暴力不在这一集里。它在于一个国家的选举被剪成万圣节的布景，政治被剪成“橙色、烦人、又回来了”的循环，而观众被训练成每年这个时候等着看同一批人出来吓自己一跳。吓完，关掉，明年再来。差额就在这里：本可以拿这四分钟说点别的，实际用来确认“还是这些人”。

报道没写的是谁不在台上。两个老角色，一个老总统，一个老酒鬼，全是旧脸。新脸在哪里，报道没提，我也不能替它编。

A sketch gets replayed because the audience laughed the first time. A character gets replayed because the audience remembered the face the first time they saw it. SNL brought back David S. Pumpkins and Drunk Uncle last night, both faces from after 2016, and the piece says it outright: Pumpkins and Trumpkins both showed up in 2016, both orange, both annoying. That's a joke, and it's also true. A country's political-entertainment industry runs on the same faces coming back, letting the audience confirm, through familiarity, that it hasn't moved.

This is the cheapest entry point for weaponization: the cognitive gate doesn't need new content, only repetition. Repetition makes familiarity, familiarity makes recognition, recognition makes the illusion that this is our era. SNL performs Trump, and Trump becomes a co-star of the bit; both sides collect attention. In the piece, Johnson's Trump freezes the rest of the cast for his whole monologue and name-checks "poor Bobby Moynihan back there, he flew in for this." That isn't satire of power. It's power placed on the schedule as a recurring segment.

The Drunk Uncle bit is more naked. He says he's been drinking every night since the Brewers won, and for the last 57 years before that. Fifty-seven is the piece's number, not mine. A character held up by alcohol is made cute; a man who says he believes Elizabeth Holmes is made brave. Both are jokes, but the direction of the joke is this: surrendering your judgment gets packaged as a harmless, sympathetic personality. The audience laughs at him, not at itself.

The real violence isn't in this episode. It's that a national election gets cut into Halloween scenery, politics gets cut into a loop of "orange, annoying, back again," and the audience is trained to wait every year for the same faces to jump out and scare them. Then it's over, you turn it off, and next year it happens again. The gap is right here: four minutes that could have said something else were used to confirm "still these people."

What the piece doesn't say is who isn't on stage. Two old characters, an old president, an old drunk, all old faces. Where the new faces are, the piece doesn't mention, and I won't invent them.
