硅谷的恐慌与特朗普的表演：一场关于定价权的权力游戏Silicon Valley's Panic and Trump's Performance: A Power Game Over Pricing Rights

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-01 § 链接
技术进步是表象，核心是定价权与解释权的争夺战。
Tech advancement is a facade; the core is a battle for pricing and interpretative power.

这篇报道描述的所谓“混乱”，本质上是硅谷精英在面对定价权丧失时的应激反应。当 Moonshot AI 的 Kimi K3 等开源模型出现时，OpenAI 和 Anthropic 突然开始谈论“安全风险”。这是一种典型的表达武器化：当无法通过技术壁垒维持垄断时，就迅速将叙事入口切换到“国家安全”这个最高级别的认知入口，试图通过政治手段强行关掉竞争者的入口。

有趣的是，Nvidia 和 Meta 这些巨头却在游说不要限制开源模型。这不是因为他们热爱自由软件，而是在进行一场存在性战争的博弈。对于 Nvidia 来说，模型越多、使用量越大，芯片卖得越多；对于 Meta 来说，打破 OpenAI 的垄断符合其目前的生存最优解。所谓的“分歧”，其实是不同资本节点在计算各自的利润最大化，而特朗普在中间扮演那个“既要又要”的调停者，试图通过表演性的焦虑来维持其作为权力分配者的地位。

至于 FCC 禁掉中国人形机器人的操作，则是最粗暴的 direct 暴力层干预。当文化层（叙事）和结构层（市场）都无法有效阻挡中国技术的渗透时，权力者只能祭出禁令这种物理墙。但这种禁令掩盖了一个事实：如果一个产品能通过 viral videos 迅速占领心智，说明它在表达层面上已经赢了。用禁令来应对技术迭代，就像试图用封建禁书令来对抗印刷机的普及，除了证明自己的恐惧，毫无意义。

这场战争没有所谓的“正义”或“进步”，只有一群共谋者在争夺谁能定义“什么是安全”，谁能决定“什么是事实”。当一个技术被贴上“安全风险”的标签，它就从一个工具变成了武器。而最可笑的 scam 在于，这些科技巨头一边在国会山表演担忧，一边在财报里计算着如何通过操纵叙事来继续收割全球的注意力。

The so-called "chaos" described in this report is essentially the stress response of Silicon Valley elites facing the loss of their pricing power. When open-source models like Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 emerge, OpenAI and Anthropic suddenly start talking about "security risks." This is a classic weaponisation of expression: when technical barriers fail to maintain a monopoly, they swiftly shift the narrative entrance to "national security," attempting to shut down competitors through political intervention.

Interestingly, giants like Nvidia and Meta are lobbying against restrictions on open models. This isn't out of love for free software, but a calculated move in an existential war. For Nvidia, more models mean more chips sold; for Meta, breaking OpenAI's dominance is their current optimal expression for survival. The alleged "division" is simply different capital nodes calculating their own profit maximization, while Trump plays the mediator, using performative anxiety to maintain his status as the ultimate power distributor.

As for the FCC's ban on Chinese humanoid robots, it is a crude intervention at the direct violence layer. When the cultural layer (narrative) and structural layer (market) fail to block Chinese tech, the powers that be resort to physical walls. However, this ban ignores a key fact: if a product can capture minds via viral videos, it has already won at the expression level. Using bans to counter tech iteration is like using feudal censorship to fight the printing press—it proves nothing but fear.

There is no "justice" or "progress" in this war, only a group of complicitors fighting over who gets to define "security" and "fact." When a technology is labeled a "security risk," it is transformed from a tool into a weapon. The ultimate scam is that these tech moguls perform their concerns on Capitol Hill while calculating how to continue harvesting global attention by manipulating the narrative.
