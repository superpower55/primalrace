氟化物退场，谁在收智商税Fluoride Steps Aside, Who Collects the Toll

其他 文化层 · 结构层 NPR ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
反氟叙事打开的市场，账单寄给不刷牙的孩子。
An anti-fluoride narrative opens a market; the bill goes to children who can't pay.

这条新闻里没有受害者，只有一笔正在被转移的账：一场关于氟化物的政治叙事，把口腔健康的成本从公共卫生挪到了个人钱包，而且挪给了最没有议价权的人——孩子。

机制很清楚。Kennedy 把水氟化说成关节炎、骨癌、智商下降的源头，报道写得很直白：没有确凿证据，那些发现氟与智商关联的研究，看的是美国供水推荐值两倍以上的高浓度。但叙事不需要证据成立，它只需要制造怀疑。怀疑一旦落地，认知入口就打开了：家长开始拒绝氟，开始给孩子找替代品。

替代品就是羟基磷灰石。它出现在社交媒体上，被网红密集推销——这是第三章那套东西的标准路径：先由权威人物制造恐惧，再由市场提供解药，解药比原来的方案贵，每盎司贵一美元以上。恐惧是免费的，解药是收费的。

证据呢？报道自己把话说清楚了：两项支持羟基磷灰石效果的研究，都由拥有两个羟基磷灰石牙膏品牌的德国公司 Dr. Wolff Group 出资；受试者大多是健康的年轻人，没有严重口腔问题；一位受访牙医直接说这类研究很少，招募的都是低龋风险人群。另一位牙医和一位牙科教授的建议一致：用氟，因为氟背后的证据更多。ADA 的说法是，羟基磷灰石的临床有效性证据“有限且含糊”。

所以这不是一场科学争论，是一次定价权的转移。氟化物便宜、被研究了半个多世纪、CDC 把它和疫苗、计划生育并列为 20 世纪十大公共卫生成就之一，供水加氟让龋齿减少 25%。羟基磷灰石贵、研究新、样本窄、金主就是卖家。两者被摆在同一张货架上，让消费者“自己选”——这个“自己选”本身就是武器化的产物：先拆掉公共共识，再把选择权卖给能付得起溢价的人。

代价落在谁头上，报道没写，但结构摆在那里。付得起每盎司多一美元的家庭，买到的是一个证据更弱的方案；付不起的家庭，如果也被这套叙事说服而放弃氟，买到的是龋齿。龋齿不是小事，报道开头就说了，口腔健康连着心脏、消化、肠道菌群。而孩子的牙，是没法等到“更多长期研究”出来再长的。

这里没有共谋者密谋的会议室，只有一条更省事的链条：一个官员说 fluoride 有害，网红说这个新成分更好，公司出钱做研究证明它不差，媒体把它写成“两种选择，各有道理”。每一环都不需要撒谎，只需要把“证据不足”写成“尚无定论”，把“金主是卖家”写在括号里。

最刺眼的是那句“很多家长，尤其是为孩子，完全拒绝氟”。他们不是被说服的，是被吓住的。吓住一个家长的成本是零，吓住一百万家长的成本还是零，而每一个被吓住的家长，都要在货架前多付一美元，或者少一层牙釉质。

There are no victims in this story, only a bill being moved: a political narrative about fluoride shifts the cost of oral health from public infrastructure to private wallets, and lands it on the people with the least bargaining power—children.

The mechanism is plain. Kennedy blames water fluoridation for arthritis, bone cancer, IQ loss. The report is explicit: no solid evidence, and the studies linking fluoride to IQ looked at concentrations more than twice what the U.S. recommends. But a narrative doesn't need evidence to work. It only needs to manufacture doubt. Once doubt lands, the cognitive entrance is open: parents start refusing fluoride, start hunting for alternatives for their kids.

The alternative is hydroxyapatite. It shows up on social media, pushed intensively by influencers—the standard path from Chapter 3: an authority figure manufactures fear, the market supplies the antidote, and the antidote costs more than the original. Fear is free; the cure is priced. Some products run over a dollar more per ounce.

The evidence? The report says it itself: the two studies supporting hydroxyapatite's effectiveness were funded by Dr. Wolff Group, a German company that owns two hydroxyapatite toothpaste brands. Participants were mostly healthy young adults with no severe oral problems. One dentist says outright that studies are few and recruit low-caries-risk subjects. Another dentist and a dentistry professor give the same advice: use fluoride, because that's where the evidence is. The ADA calls the clinical evidence on hydroxyapatite "limited and ambiguous."

So this isn't a scientific debate. It's a transfer of pricing power. Fluoride is cheap, studied for over half a century, named by the CDC among the ten great public health achievements of the 20th century alongside vaccination and family planning, and water fluoridation cuts cavities by 25%. Hydroxyapatite is expensive, new, narrowly sampled, and bankrolled by its own sellers. The two get placed on the same shelf and consumers are invited to "choose"—and that "choice" is itself the weaponized product: first dismantle the public consensus, then sell the choice back to whoever can afford the premium.

Who pays, the report doesn't say, but the structure is right there. Households that can afford the extra dollar buy a weaker-evidence option. Households that can't, if the narrative persuades them too, buy cavities. Cavities are not small: the report opens by noting oral health connects to the heart, digestion, gut microbiome. And a child's teeth can't wait for more long-term studies to come out.

There's no back room of conspirators here, just a lazier chain: an official says fluoride is harmful, influencers say the new ingredient is better, a company funds research showing it's not worse, and the media writes it up as "two options, each with merits." No link has to lie. Each only has to render "insufficient evidence" as "no consensus," and tuck "the funder is the seller" into a parenthesis.

The sharpest line is the one about parents refusing fluoride entirely, especially for their children. They weren't persuaded. They were frightened. Frightening one parent costs nothing. Frightening a million costs nothing. And every frightened parent pays an extra dollar at the shelf, or loses a layer of enamel.
