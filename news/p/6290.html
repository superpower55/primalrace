足球的假球在账本上，不在裁判兜里The Fix Is in the Ledger, Not the Referee's Pocket

其他 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要曼联在周六客场负于切尔西后，主帅卡里克预计仍将带队出战罗马。他在赛后表示球队必须继续前进、赢下比赛。同日英超其他场次中，伯恩茅斯取得六连胜，桑德兰与考文垂则陷入困境。
The newsManchester United manager Michael Carrick is expected to stay in charge for the Roma game despite Saturday's defeat at Chelsea. He said the team must move forward and win matches. Elsewhere, Bournemouth won a sixth straight game, while Sunderland and Coventry are in trouble.
球迷忙着抓裁判，老板正在数钱
Fans hunt the referee while the owners count the money.

这篇比赛日直播里最诚实的一句话不是任何教练说的，是 Jonathan Wilson 说的：球迷团体坚信裁判和共济会、黑手党、主业会联手做掉自己的球队，这种偏执分散了注意力，掩盖了足球真正的危机——它已经从一项以竞技成绩和诚信为最高准则的事业，堕落成让本来就很有钱很有权的人更有钱更有权的资本工具。

这就是武器化的标准操作：先给你一个假的敌人。裁判、VAR、Pro Ref、阴谋集团，随便哪个。你所有的愤怒都有了一个具体的、看得见的靶子，而真正在收钱的那一层，永远不在你的视野里。认知入口被占领的标志不是你信了什么假消息，是你把全部的怀疑都花在了一个错误的方向上。

阿尔夫-英格·哈兰德谈曼城那 114/115 项指控的裁决时说，媒体的报道“非常一边倒”，因为上诉期间曼城很难为自己辩护，他希望得到一个公正的听证，看看他们能不能证明自己清白。一个球员替自己的雇主喊冤，这没什么可指摘的。值得注意的是这句话的结构：把一桩关于财务规则的案子，翻译成了“媒体不公”的叙事。谁在定义什么叫“一边倒”，谁就在争夺解释权。

至于 Michael Carrick，Barney Ronay 说得对，他根本不是这件事的重点。曼联自九月初以来一场没赢，输给切尔西之后只因为考文垂和桑德兰当天下午要互相踢，才没在当天结束时坐稳倒数第一。教练是一个可以替换的零件，换掉他，格雷泽和拉特克利夫那一层纹丝不动。把一个结构问题写成一个人的无能，是最省事也最有效的共谋：所有人都有了可以骂的对象，所有人都不必去看账本。

Oliver Glasner 的话倒是把这一层说穿了。他说自己回家还能和家人吃早饭，情况不算最糟，没人需要同情他，他愿意的话两周内就能在别处上岗——但球员和 staff 还留在原地，还是俱乐部去年那个鬼样子，得从底到顶重建，没有妥协，零。一个随时能走的人，在替一群走不了的人说话。这是整篇直播里唯一一段没有把自己放在叙事中心的发言。

女足那边，London City 的 Semmens 说这是“这个周末世界女子足球的第一号比赛”，球队的目标是“稳定在前三”。伦敦城雌狮离切尔西和曼城只差三分。把这句话和男足那一堆关于裁判阴谋的偏执放在一起看，会发现一个不太好笑的对照：一边是把注意力全部投向一个虚构的敌人，一边是一支真的在往上爬的球队，安静地算着自己离榜首还差几分。

所以这篇东西值不值得写，取决于你从哪个入口进。从比分进，它是速报，跳过。从“谁在替这套资本结构维持秩序”进，它是一份共谋者名单的草稿——球迷贡献注意力，媒体贡献偏执叙事，教练贡献可替换的肉身，球员贡献忠诚，而回报，流向那个从来不出现在直播里的层级。

Grealish 在曼城的那条救赎线被掐断了，Maresca 周五还在说他是个好孩子、心大。好孩子和心大，是一个球员在谈判桌上最不值钱的两项资产。

The most honest sentence in this entire matchday live comes not from any manager but from Jonathan Wilson: fan groups are convinced that Pro Ref is conspiring with the Rosicrucians, the Masons, the mob, Opus Dei and the Cubans to deny their club a Europa League spot — and that paranoia distracts from the game's true crisis, its descent from an endeavour where sporting merit and integrity are paramount into a grimly capitalistic vehicle to make very rich and powerful people even richer and more powerful.

This is weaponisation by the book: hand them a fake enemy first. The referee, VAR, Pro Ref, the conspiracy, pick one. Your rage gets a concrete, visible target, and the layer actually collecting the money never enters your field of vision. The sign that a cognitive entry point has been captured isn't that you believed a lie — it's that you spent all your suspicion in the wrong direction.

Alf-Inge Haaland, on the guilty 114/115 verdict, says the coverage has been very one-sided, that it's difficult for City to defend themselves while the appeal is ongoing, and that he hopes they get a fair hearing and can prove their innocence. A player defending his employer — nothing to indict there. What's worth noting is the structure of the sentence: a case about financial rules gets translated into a narrative about media unfairness. Whoever defines what counts as one-sided is contesting the right to interpret.

As for Michael Carrick, Barney Ronay has it right — he is miles away from being the actual story. United haven't won since early September; after the defeat at Stamford Bridge, only the fact that Coventry and Sunderland were playing each other later kept them from ending the day bottom. The manager is a replaceable part. Swap him out and the Glazers and Sir Jim Ratcliffe don't move an inch. Writing a structural problem as one man's incompetence is the cheapest and most effective form of complicity: everyone gets someone to shout at, and nobody has to look at the books.

Oliver Glasner actually says the quiet part out loud. He goes home and has breakfast with his family, so it's not the worst situation; nobody needs to pity him; if he wanted, he could start somewhere else in two weeks. But the players and the staff stay in the same situation — the same shit situation the club had last year — and it has to be rebuilt from the bottom to the top, zero compromises. A man who can leave at any moment, speaking for the people who can't. The only passage in the whole liveblog that doesn't put itself at the centre of the story.

On the women's side, London City's Semmens calls it probably the No 1 game in women's football in the world this weekend, and says the aim is to build a squad consistently in the top three. London City are three points off Chelsea and Manchester City. Put that next to the men's referee-conspiracy paranoia and the contrast isn't funny: one side pours all its attention into a fabricated enemy, the other quietly counts how many points separate it from the top.

So whether this piece is worth writing depends on your entry point. Enter through the scores and it's a ticker — skip it. Enter through who maintains this capital structure and it's a draft of a co-conspirators list: fans supply attention, media supply the paranoid narrative, managers supply replaceable flesh, players supply loyalty — and the return flows to the tier that never appears in the liveblog.

Grealish's redemption arc at City got quashed; Maresca said on Friday he still thinks he's a good boy with a big heart. Good boy and big heart: the two least valuable assets a player can bring to a negotiating table.
