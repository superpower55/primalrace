他靠模仿特朗普买下一张国会门票He Bought a Congressional Seat With a Trump Impression

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
当一个男人把无耻做成品牌，市场会替他定价。
When a man turns shamelessness into a brand, the market prices it for him.

Constantino 的竞选不是政治，是一场表达武器化之后的变现。他把特朗普的全部风格提炼成一个可复制的产品：给总统立巨大雕像、竖一块卫星能拍到的牌子、录一张反复念总统名字的专辑。这套东西在 Sticker Mule 已经跑通过一次——那家公司卖的就是贴纸，一种把态度贴在表面上的商品。现在他把同一种生意搬进国会选区，选票成了新的 SKU。

这套表达之所以有效，是因为它不需要内容。他骂 Ocasio-Cortez「perra falsa」，对保守党主席说要把他「合法地打脸」，在 diss track 里做割喉手势——每一条都在生产同一个信号：我不受规则约束，所以我是真的。特朗普把这种信号变成了政治资本，Constantino 只是照着逆向工程了一遍，他自己说得很直白，「聪明人就是这么做的」。第一章里讲的表达即身份，在这里被压缩成一句口号：我粗鲁，故我在。

值得注意的是他身边那圈人。竞选资金流向 Roger Stone 关联的 LLC，流向一个从青少年棒球联盟偷钱的员工；给他专辑站台的有刚被从迪拜引渡回来面对谋杀未遂指控的 Antonio Brown；两个在他初选获胜几周内因绑架和勒颈一名会计师被捕的人，一个是他的婚礼宾客，另一个是他和相关公司付过五十多万美元的说唱歌手。这不叫用人不察，这叫供应链。他买的不是帮手，是同一个品牌的周边：能打、能吓人、能替他挨官司。

而真正在动的是结构层。纽约州共和党在初选里推的是退役海军上校、州党部背书的 Smullen，Constantino 赢了 19 个百分点，然后州党部转身背书他。当地政治学教授转述长期职业共和党人的话：「我讨厌那个人，我为他感到难堪。」难堪归难堪，票还是他的。这就是第四章说的共谋——不是因为他们相信他，而是因为挡不住他，而挡不住他的原因恰恰是他最能带来注意力和钱。建制派讨厌他的品味，但从不讨厌他的功能。

他公开说要当总统，歌里最后一句就是这个。这句话听起来像玩笑，但它和整篇报道里所有荒诞细节是同构的：在一个把无耻当能力的评价体系里，无耻本身就是简历。

Constantino's campaign is not politics. It is the monetization of a weaponized expression. He took the entire Trump style and compressed it into a replicable product: a giant statue of the president, a sign visible from satellites, an album that mostly repeats the president's name. He had already run this play at Sticker Mule, a company that sells stickers—attitudes made to be stuck on a surface. Now he has moved the same business into a congressional district, and votes are the new SKU.

The expression works because it needs no content. He called Ocasio-Cortez a "perra falsa," told the state Conservative Party chair to get in a boxing ring so he could punch him "in the fucking face legally," and made a throat-slitting gesture in a diss track. Each item produces the same signal: I am not bound by the rules, therefore I am real. Trump turned that signal into political capital; Constantino reverse-engineered it, and says so out loud—"This is what smart people do." Chapter one's claim that expression constitutes identity gets compressed here into a slogan: I am crude, therefore I am.

Then look at the people around him. Campaign money went to a Florida LLC affiliated with Roger Stone, and to an employee who stole from a youth baseball league. His album features Antonio Brown, just extradited from Dubai to face an attempted murder charge. Two men arrested within weeks of his primary win for allegedly kidnapping and strangling an accountant were a wedding guest and a rapper whom Constantino and affiliated companies paid more than $500,000. This is not poor vetting. This is a supply chain. What he buys is not help but merchandise from the same brand: men who can fight, intimidate, and take a charge for him.

The structural layer is where the real action is. New York's Republican Party backed Assemblyman Robert Smullen, a retired Marine colonel; Constantino beat him by 19 points, and then the party backed Constantino. A political science professor relays what longtime professional Republicans in the district say: "I hate that guy. I'm embarrassed by that guy." Embarrassed, and still voting for him. This is the co-conspirator mechanism in plain sight—not belief, but the inability to stop him, and the inability stems precisely from his capacity to generate attention and money. The establishment dislikes his taste. It has never disliked his function.

He says he might be president one day; it is the last line of his song. It sounds like a joke, but it is structurally identical to every absurd detail in the story: in a system that prices shamelessness as competence, shamelessness is the résumé.
