稀土定价权与种族清洗的交易：一场典型的元暴力共谋Rare Earth Pricing vs. Genocide: A Textbook Case of Meta-Violence Complicity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-18 § 链接
人权叙事在资源博弈面前只是可随时抛弃的 PR 插件。
Human rights narratives are merely PR plugins, disposable when resource gaming begins.

Marco Rubio 的转身提供了一个完美的样本：在元暴力（meta violence）的逻辑里，所谓的“人权”从来不是目的，而是一种 weaponized 的叙事入口。2017 年他谈论的是“种族清洗”，2026 年他谈论的是“战略盟友”。这种断裂并非个体的道德崩塌，而是权力逻辑的回归——当缅甸的稀土矿产成为美国与中国博弈的筹码时，罗兴亚人的血就从“必须制止的暴行”变成了“可以协商的成本”。

这是一场典型的共谋（complicity）。特朗普政府、游说客 Roger Stone 以及缅甸军政府，三方在一个极其肮脏的利益点上达成了共识：用对民主运动的背叛换取对资源的控制。在这个博弈中，缅甸民众的生命被彻底客体化，他们不再是拥有主体性的“人”，而成了稀土矿产地上的附属物。军政府通过与美国的接触获得了合法性的 Propaganda 胜利，而美国则试图通过这种交易在稀土供应链上实现所谓的“最优解”。

最讽刺的是，这种“最优解”极其短视。正如文中提到的，矿产控制权在地方民团和中国手中，特朗普政府在认知入口上犯了低级错误：他以为只要通过与独裁者达成协议就能夺取资源，却忽略了结构层（structural layer）的实际控制力。他用掉了一整套西方世界的道德信用，却可能换不回几吨稀土。

对于缅甸的抵抗者来说，这不仅是政治上的背叛，更是一次存在性战争的惨败。当他们将希望寄托于一个将人权视为“可交易资产”的霸权时，他们实际上是掉入了另一个巨大的浪漫主义叙事陷阱——相信某种普世正义能超越资源定价权。在这种元暴力的统治下，弱势种族的生存权永远排在矿产的战略价值之后。

Marco Rubio's pivot provides a perfect specimen: in the logic of meta-violence, 'human rights' are never the goal, but a weaponized narrative entry point. In 2017, he spoke of 'ethnic cleansing'; in 2026, he speaks of 'strategic allies.' This rupture is not a personal moral collapse, but a return to the logic of power—when Myanmar's rare earths became a chip in the US-China game, the blood of the Rohingya shifted from 'atrocities that must be stopped' to 'costs that can be negotiated.'

This is a textbook case of complicity. The Trump administration, lobbyist Roger Stone, and the Myanmar junta have reached a consensus on a filthy interest point: trading the betrayal of democratic movements for resource control. In this game, the Myanmar people are completely objectified; they are no longer 'humans' with subjectivity, but mere appendages to the rare earth mines. The junta gains a propaganda victory of legitimacy, while the US seeks a so-called 'optimal expression' in the rare earth supply chain.

The irony is that this 'optimal expression' is profoundly short-sighted. As noted, control of the mines lies with local militias and China. The Trump administration made a rookie mistake at the cognitive entry point: believing that a deal with a dictator equals resource acquisition, ignoring the actual control at the structural layer. He spent the entire moral credit of the West and may get nothing but a few tons of ore in return.

For the Myanmar resistance, this is not just political betrayal, but a crushing defeat in their existential war. By pinning their hopes on a hegemony that views human rights as 'tradable assets,' they fell into another romantic narrative trap—believing that some universal justice could override the pricing power of resources. Under this meta-violence, the survival rights of a marginalized race will always rank below the strategic value of minerals.
