AI 时代的电影：当表达沦为拼贴的 ScamCinema in the AI Era: When Expression Becomes a Pastiche Scam

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-12 § 链接
缺乏主体性的表达不是致敬，而是对存在性的自我抹除。
Expression without subjectivity is not homage; it is the erasure of one's existential presence.

这部电影被评论者形容为“AI 斯皮尔伯格机器人”的产物，这不仅是一个比喻，而是一个精准的诊断。在存在性战争中，表达是确证身份的唯一方式，但 David Robert Mitchell 在这里选择了一次彻头彻尾的投降。他不再尝试定义自己的现实，而是通过拼贴（pastiche）他人的符号——斯皮尔伯格的氛围、德·帕尔马的分屏镜头、80 年代的怀旧 BGM——来制造一种“像电影的电影”。

最讽刺的细节在于这对夫妇的设定：丈夫 Greg 因为无法承担 breadwinner 的角色而感到羞耻，妻子 Denise 则在秘密写一本关于抛弃家庭的小说。这本该是极佳的、关于私域博弈与主体性觉醒的入口，但导演却将其处理成了廉价的背景板，用来衬托那些毫无威胁感的恐龙。当真实的家庭权力斗争被简化为某种“被压抑的激情”的象征时，这种表达就变成了典型的 weaponized narrative：用一个宏大的、奇观式的外壳，掩盖掉对结构性暴力（如性别分工带来的精神内耗）的真实探讨。

这种“ inert”（惰性）的表达本质上是一种共谋。导演在与商业资本、与大众对“经典好莱坞”的刻板想象共谋，通过扮演一个“合格的导演”来换取短期利益，代价是主体性的死亡。当一个创作者开始“going through the motions”时，他已经从一个定义事实的人，变成了一个被定义的客体。这部电影不是恐龙的生存游戏，而是一个创作者在资本结构中被驯化后的标本。

The critic describes this film as the product of an "AI Spielbergbot," which is less a metaphor and more a precise diagnosis. In the existential war, expression is the only way to confirm identity, but David Robert Mitchell has chosen total surrender here. He no longer attempts to define his own reality; instead, he manufactures a "movie-like movie" by stitching together the symbols of others—Spielberg's atmosphere, De Palma's split-diopter shots, and 80s nostalgia.

The most ironic detail lies in the couple's setup: Greg is shamed by his failure as a breadwinner, while Denise secretly writes a novel about abandoning her family. This should have been a potent entry point for exploring private power struggles and the awakening of subjectivity. Instead, Mitchell treats it as a cheap backdrop for harmless dinosaurs. When real domestic power struggles are reduced to mere symbols of "suppressed passion," the expression becomes a weaponized narrative: using a grand, spectacular shell to mask a genuine investigation into structural violence, such as the mental attrition caused by gendered labor roles.

This "inert" expression is essentially an act of complicity. The director is complicit with commercial capital and the public's cliché of "Classic Hollywood," playing the role of a "competent director" for short-term gain at the cost of his own subjectivity. When a creator begins "going through the motions," they cease to be the one defining reality and become an object defined by others. The End of Oak Street is not a survival game for dinosaurs, but a specimen of a creator domesticated by the capitalist structure.
