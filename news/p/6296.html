圣诞神曲是台印钞机A Christmas Hit Is a Money Printer

文娱 文化层 Entertainment Weekly ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
节日不是她的主题，排名才是。
The holiday isn't her subject. The ranking is.

Mariah Carey 每年十一月一日准时“解冻”，这不是创意传统，是一台按日历启动的印钞机。报道自己把账算得很清楚："All I Want for Christmas Is You" 曾以 22 个非连续周占据 Billboard Hot 100 榜首，是史上在榜首停留最久的单曲；今年 Ella Langley 的 "Choosin' Texas" 以 25 周把它挤了下去，而 Carey 的歌在 2024 和 2025 年各拿了四周冠军。所以今年的 "It's time" 视频第一层意思不是过节，是宣战——报道原文就写了，她“有希望夺回王冠”，前提是 Langley 十一月别再筑防火墙。

这条新闻里唯一有点意思的细节，是她开场唱的那首 "Hermit"。九十年代中期她以乐队 Chick 的名义和朋友 Clarissa Dane 出了一张《Someone's Ugly Daughter》，自己写、自己制作、还在里面唱和声，却把自己的名字藏了起来，直到 2020 年回忆录才承认。藏起名字，是因为一张“丑女儿”式的独立摇滚专辑挂在她头上卖不动；露出名字，是因为这个名字现在能卖圣诞歌。同一个表达，一次是负债，一次是资产——表达的价值从来不由表达本身决定，由谁在定价决定。

然后是那个每年被复述一遍的词：defrost。解冻。一个五十多岁的女人，被写成一台按季节启动的冷冻食品，每年十一月从冰柜里拿出来加热一次，卖完再放回去。这套叙事对她是划算的：它把衰老这件对女艺人最致命的事，包装成了一个可爱的、可循环的梗。代价是她接受了自己作为“季节性商品”的定价——她的身体和声音被允许每年上线四周，其余时间保持冷藏。

报道没写的是这首歌每年十二月替谁赚了多少钱，也没写那些在商场、电梯、外卖骑手耳机里被迫循环它的人。一首歌成为节日基础设施，意味着它不再需要被喜欢，只需要被播放。榜单上的周数是可见的，被它填满的注意力是隐形的。

夺回榜首需要 Langley 配合，需要电台配合，需要算法配合。而真正稳赚不赔的那部分，从来不在榜单上。

Mariah Carey thaws on November 1 every year. This is not a creative tradition. It is a money printer with a calendar trigger. The report does the accounting itself: "All I Want for Christmas Is You" once held No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100 for 22 non-consecutive weeks, the longest run in the chart's history. This year Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas" knocked it off at 25 weeks, while Carey's song took four weeks at the top in both 2024 and 2025. So this year's "It's time" video is not, in the first instance, about the holidays. It is a declaration of war — the report says outright that she has a shot at retaking the crown, provided Langley doesn't build a further firewall in November.

The one genuinely interesting detail is the song she opens with, "Hermit." In the mid-'90s she released Someone's Ugly Daughter under the band name Chick, with her friend Clarissa Dane — she wrote it, produced it, sang background vocals, and kept her name off it until her 2020 memoir. The name was hidden because an album like that wouldn't sell under her name. The name comes out now because that name sells Christmas songs. The same expression, once a liability, now an asset — what an expression is worth was never decided by the expression. It's decided by who sets the price.

Then there's the word the press repeats every year: defrost. A woman in her fifties is written as a frozen food item, taken out of the freezer each November, heated once, sold, put back. The framing pays for her: it turns aging — the deadliest thing for a female performer — into a cute, recyclable bit. The price is that she accepts her own pricing as a seasonal product, permitted to go live for four weeks a year and refrigerated the rest.

What the report doesn't say is how much this song earns each December, or for whom, or anything about the people forced to hear it on loop in malls, elevators, and delivery riders' earbuds. A song that becomes holiday infrastructure no longer needs to be liked. It only needs to be played. The weeks on the chart are visible. The attention it fills is not.

Retaking No. 1 requires Langley to cooperate, radio to cooperate, the algorithm to cooperate. The part that never loses money was never on the chart to begin with.
