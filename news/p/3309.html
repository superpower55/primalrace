用“文明”的坐姿阉割公共空间的随机性Castrating Public Spontaneity with the Posture of "Civility"

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-06 § 链接
将“站立”定义为不文明，本质是通过结构暴力抹除空间的流动性与随机碰撞。
Defining 'standing' as uncivilized is structural violence designed to erase spatial fluidity and random collision.

Westminster council 试图在伦敦 Soho 区禁掉“垂直饮酒”（vertical drinking），理由是鼓励人们坐下来享受服务，减少醉酒和拥挤。这听起来像是一场关于“文明”和“秩序”的温情关怀，但实际上是一次精准的 structural violence。

在 pub 的文化里，站立不仅仅是一个物理姿态，它是一种低门槛的社交表达。站着意味着流动，意味着你可以随时脱离既定的小圈子，与陌生人发生随机的碰撞。而一旦被强制要求“坐下”并接受“桌边服务”，这种随机性就被阉割了。你被固定在预设的座位里，被限制在随行的人群中，社交从一种自由的博弈变成了被制度化管理的就餐行为。

这就是典型的元暴力（meta violence）运作方式：用“管理醉酒”、“提升体验”这种看似中立且正义的叙事，掩盖其对公共空间解释权的垄断。council 想要的是一个可预测、可量化、易于监控的消费场景，而不是一个充满生命力的、不可控的社区空间。他们把“秩序”当作武器，通过修改 licensing policy 这一结构层，强行将人们的身体状态从“流动”调整为“静止”。

Sadiq Khan 的反对虽然在谈论“增长”和“行业支持”，但这依然在商业逻辑内打转。真正的损失在于：当一个城市的公共空间被这种“文明化”的规训填满，人们将失去在非正式场合建立连接的能力。这种对“站立”的禁令，本质上是对个体在公共领域中主体性表达的一种温水煮青蛙式的剥夺。

Westminster council's attempt to ban "vertical drinking" in Soho is framed as a benevolent push for civility and order, encouraging patrons to sit and be served. But this is a textbook example of structural violence.

In pub culture, standing is not just a physical posture; it is a low-threshold expression of sociality. Standing means fluidity—the ability to drift away from predetermined groups and engage in random collisions with strangers. Once you are forced to "sit" and accept "table service," this spontaneity is castrated. You are anchored to a fixed spot, confined to your immediate circle, and social interaction is transformed from a free game into a regulated dining experience.

This is how meta violence operates: using a neutral, "just" narrative of "managing drunkenness" and "enhancing experience" to mask the monopoly over the interpretation of public space. The council doesn't want a living, unpredictable community hub; they want a predictable, quantifiable, and monitorable consumer scene. They weaponize "order" to shift the human body from a state of "flow" to "stasis" through the structural lever of licensing policy.

While Mayor Sadiq Khan opposes this on the grounds of "growth" and "industry support," he is still arguing within a commercial logic. The real loss is the erosion of the ability to form connections in informal spaces. When a city's public realm is filled with this kind of "civilizing" discipline, the individual's subjective expression in the public sphere is slowly boiled away.
