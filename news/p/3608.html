资本的弃子与共谋的闭环The Cast-offs of Capital and the Loop of Complicity

科技 结构层 · 元暴力 Reuters ↗ 2026-08-10 § 链接
所谓的“现代化转型”不过是资本在风险转移后的获利了结。
所谓的 'modernization' is merely a profit-taking exit after a successful risk transfer.

两家加拿大银行把 Moneris 卖给 Francisco Partners，这在商业叙事里被包装成“加速现代化”和“数字化转型”。但剥开这层 PR 外壳，本质是一场典型的风险对冲与获利了结。银行发现数字化支付的迭代速度太快，维持竞争力的 capital expenditure 成了沉没成本，于是决定在估值高点套现。这不是在追求效率，而是在逃离责任。

最精彩的部分在于那段关于“长期推荐协议”的描述。RBC 和 BMO 卖掉了所有权，却保留了 commercial relationships。这意味着它们在结构层（structural layer）完成了资产剥离，在风险层实现了切割，但在利益层依然通过共谋（complicity）维持着对支付入口的控制。它们不再需要为技术的落后买单，却依然能通过推荐协议分享生态红利。

这就是资本世界的 meta-violence：制定规则的人永远在寻找最优解表达。对于银行来说，最优解不是让支付变得更公正或高效，而是将“必须持续投入的压力”转移给私募股权基金，同时将“稳定的客户流量”转化为持续的抽成。这哪里是现代化，这分明是把风险外包给下一任接盘者，而自己拿着 C$20 亿的现金，在股市的涨幅中庆祝这场精准的逃顶。

RBC and BMO selling Moneris to Francisco Partners is packaged in the commercial narrative as 'accelerating modernization' and 'digital transformation.' But stripping away the PR veneer reveals a classic hedge and profit-taking move. The banks realized the pace of digital payment iteration is too fast, and the capital expenditure required to stay competitive became a sunk cost. They chose to cash out at the valuation peak. This isn't a pursuit of efficiency; it's an escape from liability.

The most fascinating part is the mention of 'long-term referral agreements.' RBC and BMO shed ownership but maintained 'commercial relationships.' In the structural layer, they completed asset divestment and risk decoupling, yet in the interest layer, they maintain control over payment gateways through complicity. They no longer pay for technical obsolescence, but they still harvest dividends from the ecosystem.

This is the meta-violence of the capital world: the rule-makers always seek the optimal expression. For the banks, the optimal expression isn't making payments more just or efficient, but transferring the 'pressure of continuous investment' to a private equity firm while converting 'stable customer traffic' into ongoing commissions. This isn't modernization; it's outsourcing risk to the next bag-holder while celebrating a precise exit with C$2 billion in cash and surging stock prices.
