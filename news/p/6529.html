不卖Xbox，也不卖以色列No Xbox, No Israel

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把渠道当成表态，是表达武器化的反向操作
Treating distribution as a statement is weaponized expression run in reverse.

Tali Faulkner 说做《Penguin Colony》是为了"put the games industry on notice"。这句话落到实处的地方不是游戏里那些纳粹和克苏鲁，而是采访最后那段：不上 Xbox，不在以色列的 Steam 上架。一个独立开发者能调动的资源里，渠道名单是他手上少数几个真正有强制力的东西。

他说得很清楚："We are not bringing the game to Xbox. The game is also not available in Israel on Steam." 然后补了一句，联系 Steam 直接说不想在那里卖，对方会劝你，但会允许。这是一个操作细节，不是宣言。宣言谁都会写，操作细节才构成一次真实的表达——它改变了什么能被谁拿到。

按加尔通的算法，这里被削掉的差额很小，但它是具体的：以色列的玩家买不到这款游戏。这不是象征性的谴责声明，是一个可执行的入口被关上了。Faulkner 要求把 No Games for Genocide 的承诺加强，说"一次抵制是不够的"，还说不该给独立开发者开例外。他把自己也算进了要被追究的那一类。

真正刺人的是中间那段关于原住民开发者职业路径的话。他说原住民从来没有第二次机会：一个项目黄了，非原住民成员履历上多一条"和原住民合作过"，职业往上走；原住民成员因为没做完一个游戏，职业往下走。这是结构暴力的标准形态——没有施暴者，只有一条默认的、没人签过字的分配规则。他做这款游戏，一部分目的就是让这条规则没法再被拿来当借口：游戏做出来了，卖得动，9 万条愿望单，"这些游戏卖不动"这句话就失效了。

但注意他付出的代价是什么。他自己说这可能是工作室的最后一个游戏，说自己住在维多利亚，到处是工地，大不了去当学徒，被拉黑就被拉黑。这句话听起来像豪言，其实是成本核算：他清楚表达是有价格的，而且价格由他来付。共谋者理论里那个问题——谁在维护既定秩序、回报是什么——在这里的答案反过来了：拒绝共谋的人，回报是失去渠道。

游戏本身那部分他讲得很实在。PS2 的颗粒感不是怀旧，是让大脑去补全看不见的东西；交互是强制的，所以那些照片、那些循环播放的咀嚼声，你待多久由你决定，逃避不掉。这是把"认知入口"从投放端翻到接收端：不是给你一个结论，是让你自己走进去，走多深随你，但走进去这件事没得选。

结尾那根刺在他自己留下的话里。他说原住民 IP 的版权散在好多家族手里，还有一整套遗产要谈——做"文化内容"被行业当成安全牌，实际上是最难的一件事。那么这款游戏做出来之后，那些家族拿到了什么？报道没写。渠道关上了，叙事夺回来了，可分配的那一头还是空的。

Tali Faulkner says Penguin Colony exists to "put the games industry on notice." The place that sentence actually lands isn't the Nazis or the cosmic horror. It's the last few paragraphs: no Xbox, not for sale in Israel on Steam. Within the resources one indie developer controls, the distribution list is one of the few things with real force behind it.

He's precise about it: "We are not bringing the game to Xbox. The game is also not available in Israel on Steam." Then the operational detail — contact Steam directly, tell them you don't want to sell there, they'll try to talk you out of it, but they'll allow it. That's not a manifesto, it's a procedure. Manifestos are free. Procedures change who can get what.

By Galtung's arithmetic the gap closed here is small but concrete: players in Israel cannot buy this game. Not a statement of condemnation, an entry point shut. Faulkner wants the No Games for Genocide pledge strengthened, says one boycott isn't enough, and refuses an exception for indies. He counts himself among those to be held to it.

The sharpest passage is the one about indigenous careers. Indigenous developers never get a second chance: if a project dies, the non-indigenous team members go up — they have "worked with indigenous people" on the résumé — and the indigenous members go down, because they didn't finish a game. That is structural violence in its standard form: no perpetrator, just a default allocation rule nobody signed. Part of the point of shipping this game was to remove that excuse. It exists, it's selling, 90,000 wishlists, so "these games don't sell" stops working.

But look at what it costs him. He says this could be the studio's last game, that he lives in Victoria where housing developments go up in every direction, that he can get a trainee job, that if he's blacklisted, so be it. It sounds like defiance. It's a cost estimate. Expression has a price, and he's the one paying it. The co-conspirator question — who maintains the order, and what's the payoff — inverts here: the person refusing to conspire loses the channel.

The craft talk is honest too. The PS2 grain isn't nostalgia, it's making the brain fill in what isn't rendered. Interaction is compulsory, so the photographs, the looping chewing sound, how long you stand near them is yours to decide and impossible to avoid. That's the cognitive entry point flipped to the receiving end: no conclusion handed down, just a space you have to walk into, as deep as you choose.

The barb is in what he leaves open. Indigenous IP is owned across many families, with a whole legacy to negotiate — cultural content looks like the safe bet to the industry and is actually the hardest thing to make. So after the game ships, what do those families get? The interview doesn't say. The channel is closed, the narrative reclaimed, and the distribution side is still empty.
