救世主幻想与被定义的“安全感”The Savior Fantasy and the Definition of 'Safety'

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-21 § 链接
所谓的“自然”与“安全”，往往是旧有权力叙事的温情版复刻。
What feels 'natural' and 'safe' is often just a tender remix of old power narratives.

一个前修道士与一个天主教女性的恋爱故事，被包装成一种跨越禁忌的、纯粹的情感连接。但剥开这层浪漫主义的糖衣，你会发现这依然是一场典型的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative) 的共谋。

Orla 提到她过去将 sex 作为换取关注的 bargaining chip，而与 Rory 在一起后她感到了“安全”。这种安全感的来源很有趣：因为 Rory 曾经经历过三年禁欲，他“证明了自己可以没有性”，所以没有压力。这本质上是一种权力的让渡表演——男性通过展示自己对欲望的掌控力，来换取女性的信任。但这依然在维持一个逻辑：安全感是由男性的“自律”或“宽容”赋予的，而非女性主体性的觉醒。

更刺眼的是 Rory 的表达。他被 Orla 抽雪茄、反击男权的自信所吸引，但随后他迅速将这种自信纳入自己的叙事框架。他提到一个“公主在塔中等待救援”的 fantasy，并声称通过 sex 来让 Orla 感到安全，因为“简单的赞美没用”。这是一个极其危险的信号：他将女性的自我认同（beauty/safety）绑定在自己的身体行为和“救援者”角色上。这根本不是什么自然，而是典型的武器化叙事——用性作为一种确认权力的工具，将女性重新客体化为需要被“拯救”的公主。

两人在弥撒中因婚前性行为而无法领圣体，这种对宗教禁忌的共同违背，给他们制造了一种“我们是特殊少数”的亲密幻觉。但这种幻觉掩盖了结构性的事实：他们只是在父权宗教的边缘地带，玩了一场关于“叛逆”的角色扮演游戏。当一个男人认为通过扮演救援者能给女人安全感时，他其实从未走出修道院的权力结构，他只是把神坛换成了卧室。

A romance between an ex-monk and a Catholic woman is packaged as a pure emotional connection transcending taboo. But strip away the romantic glaze, and you find a classic complicity within a masculine-centric narrative.

Orla mentions using sex as a bargaining chip in the past, but feels 'safe' with Rory. The source of this safety is telling: because Rory was celibate for three years, he 'proved he could go without,' thus removing the pressure. This is essentially a performance of power—a man trading his demonstrated mastery over desire for a woman's trust. The logic remains: safety is granted by male 'discipline' or 'tolerance,' not by the woman's own agency.

More glaring is Rory's expression. He was attracted to Orla's confidence and her defiance of male authority, yet he quickly absorbed that confidence into his own framework. He describes a fantasy of a 'princess in a tower waiting for rescue,' claiming that sex is how he makes her feel safe because 'simply telling her rarely works.' This is a red flag: he binds her self-worth (beauty/safety) to his physical actions and his role as the 'rescuer.' This isn't natural; it is the weaponisation of expression—using sex as a tool to re-objectify the woman into a damsel in distress.

Their shared inability to take communion due to premarital sex creates an illusion of 'special' intimacy through shared rebellion. But this illusion masks a structural truth: they are merely playing a role-playing game of 'rebellion' on the fringes of a patriarchal religion. When a man believes that playing the savior provides safety, he hasn't left the monastery's power structure; he has simply moved the altar into the bedroom.
