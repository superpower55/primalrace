监视者被写成守护天使The Watcher as Guardian Angel

The newsRobert Icke's stage adaptation of The Lives of Others is running at London's Adelphi Theatre, with Keira Knightley as Christa-Maria and Stephen Dillane as Stasi officer Wiesler. The review praises Knightley and the revolving set, but says the staging dampens tension in the second half.
把监视者拍成守护天使，是在替监视洗白
Making the Stasi man a guardian angel is how surveillance gets a good name.

这篇剧评最诚实的一句话，是它自己承认维斯勒的转变「像一种浪漫化的愿望满足」。然后它就把这句话放过去了。

《窃听风暴》的原设定是：一个史塔西官员在阁楼里监听一对情侣，最后选择保护他们。这个设定之所以动人，是因为它讲的是共谋者倒戈——一个在暴力机器里领工资的人，把机器对准的对象换成了机器本身。可舞台版把维斯勒放到其他角色中间，坐在他们的沙发上，剧评说这让他「像一个守护天使」。守护天使不需要为监听负责，天使只是恰好在场。

这就是问题所在。监听这个动作被拆成了两层：一层是史塔西的机器，在阁楼里、在耳机里、在打字机上；另一层是维斯勒这个人，被单独拎出来，赋予同情、幽默和良心。观众被允许恨那台机器，同时爱那个操作机器的人。于是结构暴力变成了「个别坏人 + 一个好人」，最需要被追问的东西——为什么监听能持续、谁在供给它、谁在受益——被舞台的旋转带走了。

剧评还提到，部长亨普夫下令监视作家的动机不是意识形态，而是想得到作家的情人。这本来是原片最锋利的一刀：国家暴力可以直接服务于一个男人的性欲，而整个官僚系统会为此运转。舞台版把克里斯塔-玛丽亚从配角提到中心，剧评说她被部长性侵的那场戏「极难观看」，这很好，因为那正是需要被看见的。但剧评紧接着把这份重量记在了奈特莉的表演上，而不是记在「一个男人的欲望可以调动国家机器」这个事实上。表演值得称赞，可如果观众的震动停在演员的演技，那场戏就白演了。

至于「AI 让国家监视变得更可怕」这个开场，是把一个冷战故事接到今天最省力的方式。真正没变的东西剧评没写：监听需要有人坐在耳机前，需要有人写报告，需要有人在事后说「我只是执行命令」。技术换了，坐在耳机前的人没换。

剧评最后说，这是一部关于倾听的作品，本该有更多沉默。它说对了一半。比沉默更缺的，是让观众听见那个监听者打字的声音时，不去想他有多孤独，而去想他每个月领的是谁的钱。

The most honest line in this review is its own admission that Wiesler's turn feels like "a romanticized wish-fulfillment." Then it moves on.

The original premise is a man inside the violence machine who turns the machine on itself. That works because it is a story about a co-conspirator defecting. The stage version puts Wiesler among the other characters, sitting on their sofa, and the review calls him something of a guardian angel. A guardian angel bears no responsibility for the wiretap. He is simply present.

That is the trick. Surveillance gets split in two: the Stasi apparatus, in the attic, in the headphones, in the typewriter; and Wiesler the man, singled out and given sympathy, humor, a conscience. You are allowed to hate the machine and love the man who runs it. Structural violence becomes one bad boss plus one good soul, and the question that matters—why the listening can continue, who supplies it, who profits—gets carried off by the revolving stage.

The review notes that Minister Hempf's motive is not ideology but lust for the playwright's lover. That is the sharpest cut in the original: state violence running directly as a service to one man's desire, with an entire bureaucracy doing the paperwork. The stage version moves Christa-Maria to the center, and the review says her assault is extremely difficult to watch. Good—it should be. But the review then files that weight under Knightley's performance rather than under the fact that one man's desire can summon the state. Praise the acting; just do not let the audience leave thinking the horror was the acting.

The opening gambit about AI making surveillance scarier is the cheapest way to connect a Cold War story to now. What the review leaves out is what has not changed: someone sits at the headphones, someone writes the report, someone later says he was just following orders. The technology changed. The man at the headphones did not.

The review ends by saying this is a piece about listening and could use more silence. Half right. What it needs more than silence is for the sound of the watcher typing to make you ask whose payroll he is on, not how lonely he must be.
