同一个第三号，两个方向Same Amendment, Opposite Direction

性别 结构层 · 文化层 ProPublica ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要密苏里州共和党人将一项名为「第三号修正案」的提案列入11月公投，意在推翻2024年选民以51.6%对48.4%通过、写入州宪法的堕胎权，同时把禁止未成年人性别转换医疗写入宪法。该州2023年8月起已立法禁止此类手术及青春期阻断剂、激素的新处方。民调显示67%的受访者反对未成年人使用性别转换药物、73%反对相关手术，而合并投票时47%支持该修正案。
The newsMissouri Republicans have placed a new Amendment 3 on the November ballot that would repeal the abortion rights voters enshrined in the state constitution in 2024 by 51.6% to 48.4%, while also constitutionally banning gender transition care for minors — care the state already restricted by law since August 2023. Polling found 67% opposed puberty blockers and hormones for minors and 73% opposed surgeries; presented together, 47% backed the amendment.
他们不反对公投，只反对公投赢他们。
They don't oppose ballot measures. They oppose losing them.

2024年投「是」是保住堕胎权，2026年投「是」是拿走它。同一个名字，同一个编号，同一个投票动作，方向相反。这不是懒，是设计。

密苏里共和党在州议会是绝对多数，每一个全州职位都是他们的，可选民一次次用公民创制程序绕过他们：扩大医疗补助、大麻合法化、提高最低工资、恢复堕胎权。本月，超过80%的选民否决了他们让修宪变难的提案；州长取消州所得税的计划，83%投了反对。一个控制一切的党，被投票箱反复打脸。

于是他们学会了不取消公投，而是劫持公投。

手法拆开看很简单：把一件选民强烈反对的小事，绑在一件他们想做成的大事上。禁止未成年人性别转换医疗——这件事密苏里2023年8月就已经立法做了，手术禁了，青春期阻断剂和激素的新处方禁到2027年8月。修正案要做的只是把它写进宪法、去掉到期日，等于把已有的东西再卖一遍。而它真正改变的是那件影响630万人的事：推翻2024年刚写进宪法的生殖权。

这是第三章说的武器化，入口选得极准。广告牌上写「禁止未成年人变性手术」，政治行动委员会的网站写「我们的孩子不是实验室小白鼠」。两句话都没有说这项修正案会废除两年前选民亲手通过的堕胎权。 ballot summary（选票摘要）由议员撰写，写的是「保障急诊」「确保女性堕胎期间的安全」「保护儿童免受性别转换」，只提到强奸和乱伦例外，却不说这两个例外只适用于怀孕前12周。前州最高法院首席法官Michael Wolff说，这段文字看起来是「有意让人以为自己在保护堕胎的女性」。

法院两次驳回，最后上诉法院把摘要改成以「废除2024年选民通过的……」开头。这才是要害：只要如实写，就过不了。

所以整个操作的核心不是说服，是让人不知道自己在投什么。SLU/YouGov 2月的民调把机制量化了：近60%支持怀孕前八周的合法堕胎，67%反对未成年人用性别转换药物，73%反对手术。两项捆在一起，47%支持、40%反对。多数人支持的东西被多数人反对的东西拖下水，靠的不是论点，是选票上不写清楚。

这里要问第四章的问题：谁在共谋，回报是什么。州务卿Denny Hoskins写了一份被法院认定不公的摘要，公开背书这项修正案，接受采访时说自己「没法一下子抹掉在州议会16年的保守派观点」。1年半里，法院驳回或改写了他办公室的五份选票摘要，他还单方面挡下了两项公民提案进入11月 ballot。他说对手是「外州糖爹」资助的自由派特殊利益集团——彭博确实捐了125万美元，但把「外州金主」当罪名，前提是本地选民的钱更干净，而这个州的本地权力正全部握在一党手里。

最值得看的是「保护女性」和「保护儿童」这两个词在这里的分工。它们不指向任何具体的女性或儿童，只负责让一项剥夺医疗选择的提案听起来像防护栏。真正被写进宪法的是：一种已经存在的禁令永久化，一种已经存在的权利被删除。前者是给后者的糖衣，糖衣的成本由一小群未成年人和他们的家庭支付。

2024年那场公投，差额是51.6%对48.4%，大约4.8万张票。四年一次的机会，被压缩成四万八千人的摇摆。如果11月这项修正案通过，被推翻的不只是堕胎权，还有「选民投过的东西算数」这件事本身。而法院能改选票上的字，改不了投票那天选民脑子里被广告牌塞进去的那句话。

In 2024, voting yes on Amendment 3 protected abortion rights. In 2026, voting yes on Amendment 3 takes them away. Same name, same number, same gesture at the ballot box, opposite direction. That is not laziness. That is design.

Missouri Republicans hold a supermajority in the legislature and every statewide office, and voters keep routing around them through the initiative process: Medicaid expansion, legal marijuana, a higher minimum wage, restored abortion rights. This month more than 80% of voters rejected their bid to make amending the constitution nearly impossible; the governor's plan to phase out the state income tax lost 83%. A party that controls everything keeps getting beaten at the ballot box.

So it learned not to abolish the referendum but to hijack it.

The method is simple: strap a small thing voters hate onto a large thing you want. Missouri already banned gender transition surgery for minors in August 2023, and already bars new prescriptions of puberty blockers and hormones until August 2027. The amendment writes that into the constitution and removes the expiration date — selling voters what they already own. What it actually changes is the thing affecting 6.3 million people: it repeals the reproductive rights those same voters wrote into the constitution two years ago.

This is weaponization with a well-chosen entry point. The billboard says ban transgender surgeries for minors. The PAC website says our children are not lab experiments. Neither says the amendment repeals the abortion right voters approved. The ballot summary, written by lawmakers, promised to guarantee emergency care, ensure women's safety during abortions, and protect children from gender transition — mentioning rape and incest exceptions without noting they apply only in the first 12 weeks. A former state Supreme Court chief justice said the language appeared intended to deceive people into thinking they were protecting women having abortions.

Courts rejected it twice; an appeals court finally rewrote the summary to lead with the fact that it would repeal the 2024 voter-approved amendment. That is the tell: told plainly, it loses.

The mechanism is measurable. SLU/YouGov polling in February found nearly 60% support legal abortion in the first eight weeks, 67% oppose transition medication for minors, 73% oppose surgeries. Bundled together, the amendment leads 47 to 40. The majority's support for one thing is dragged under by the majority's opposition to another — not through argument, but through what the ballot does not say.

Then ask the Chapter Four question: who is conspiring, and what do they get? Secretary of State Denny Hoskins wrote a summary a judge called unfair, publicly endorsed the amendment, and said he cannot erase 16 years of conservative views in the legislature. In 1.5 years courts have rejected or rewritten five of his ballot summaries, and he unilaterally blocked two citizen measures from reaching the November ballot. He calls opponents liberal special interest groups funded by out-of-state sugar daddies — Bloomberg did give $1.25 million — but the out-of-state-money charge only lands if local money is cleaner, in a state where local power sits entirely with one party.

Watch what protecting women and protecting children do here. They point at no particular woman or child. They exist to make a measure that removes medical options sound like a guardrail. What gets written into the constitution is a permanent version of a ban that already exists, plus the deletion of a right that already exists. The first is the candy; the second is the medicine. The cost of the candy is paid by a small number of minors and their families.

The 2024 margin was 51.6 to 48.4 — roughly 48,000 voters. A once-every-four-years decision compressed into 48,000 people. If this passes, what falls is not only abortion access but the idea that what voters approved counts. Courts can rewrite the words on the ballot. They cannot rewrite the sentence the billboard put in voters' heads on the way in.
