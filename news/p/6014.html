真人秀的共谋，是观众签的字The Reality Show's Conspiracy Is Signed by the Audience

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 Vulture ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要据Page Six报道，Amanda Batula将不再回归《In the City》第二季。消息传出当天，Kyle Cooke在West Wilson的Instagram帖下留言攻击，随后删除；Batula转发并删除了一条粉丝分析，等于确认退出。
The newsPage Six reported that Amanda Batula will not return for season two of In the City. The same day, Kyle Cooke posted and deleted an attacking comment on West Wilson's Instagram; Batula retweeted and deleted a fan analysis, effectively confirming her exit.
她退出节目，是因为观众终于把票投给了自己
She left the show because the audience finally voted for themselves

Amanda Batula 不回归《In the City》第二季，理由她说得很清楚：网上持续的攻击。一个真人秀演员因为被观众恨到不想再出镜，这在一个把「被看见」当货币的产业里，算是稀有的诚实。

但这条新闻真正值得看的地方不在她，而在那条四千多赞的评论：「我们倒希望你别发，可你什么时候听过。」这句话把整件事的机制说穿了。观众一边消费她，一边投票要她消失，然后在她真的消失时再补一句嘲讽。这不是粉丝失控，这是节目组和观众之间的共谋——节目卖的是冲突，观众付的是注意力，代价由被拍的人单方面承担。

Kyle Cooke 在 West Wilson 的帖子下面骂人，删了，又发声明说尊重她的决定。这一套操作和节目里的叙事完全同构：先制造伤害，再扮演体面。Batula 转的那条分析说得直接——她不再拍，他就失去了控制她的通道，于是崩溃。这条分析被转又被删，删掉的动作本身比内容更说明问题。

真人秀把私人关系做成可定价的产品，参与者在镜头前让渡的不是隐私，是解释权。谁和谁吵架、谁被写成疯子、谁被写成受害者，剪辑台和评论区一起定。Batula 退出，等于从这场博弈里撤回了自己的表达，代价是她的事业，收益是她拿回了对自己叙事的否决权。

至于「Page Six」说的换血——Danielle、Andrea、Lexi 都不回归，两个新情侣加一个单身新人——节目不会因为一个人退出而改变机制，它只会换一批人来付同样的代价。观众下次还是会说「我们倒希望你别发」，然后接着看。

Amanda Batula is not returning for season two of In the City, and she said why: the continued hate from fan accounts and the internet. A reality star quitting because the audience hated her out of wanting to be on camera is a rare honesty in an industry where being seen is the currency.

But the real mechanism here isn't her. It's the comment with 4,000-plus likes: "We'd prefer if you didn't but when have you ever listened." The audience consumes her, votes for her to disappear, then mocks her when she does. That's not fandom out of control. That's a conspiracy between the show and the viewer—the show sells conflict, the viewer pays attention, and the cost is borne by the person filmed.

Kyle Cooke insulted West Wilson in his comments, deleted it, then posted a statement about respecting her decisions. The sequence is structurally identical to the show's own narrative: inflict damage, then perform decency. The fan analysis Batula retweeted said it plainly—once she stopped filming, he lost his channel to control her, so he melted down. She deleted the retweet. The deletion says more than the text.

Reality TV turns private relationships into a priced product. What participants surrender on camera isn't privacy, it's interpretive authority—who fought whom, who gets edited as crazy, who gets edited as the victim, decided jointly by the edit bay and the comment section. Batula exiting means withdrawing her expression from the game. The cost is her career. The return is a veto over her own narrative.

The casting shake-up—Danielle, Andrea, Lexi reportedly out, two new couples and one single newcomer in—won't change the mechanism. The show will just find new people to pay the same price. Next season the audience will still say "we'd prefer if you didn't," and then keep watching.
