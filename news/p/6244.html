一个南瓜头，十年美国梦A Pumpkin Head, Ten Years of America

文娱 文化层 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要汤姆·汉克斯在《周六夜现场》万圣节主题开场中再次出演大卫·S·南瓜，但小品被詹姆斯·奥斯汀·约翰逊饰演的特朗普打断。特朗普在段子里呼吁支持者投票，以防中期选举惨败，并提及得州选情等话题。
The newsTom Hanks returned to Saturday Night Live as David S. Pumpkins in a Halloween-themed opener, but the skit was interrupted by James Austin Johnson's Donald Trump. The Trump character urged supporters to vote to prevent a midterm drubbing and referenced the Texas race.
特朗普和南瓜头同年出道，都靠又响又橙又烦人活着。
Trump and Pumpkins debuted the same year: loud, orange, annoying, and still here.

Tom Hanks 扮 David S. Pumpkins 回归《SNL》，报道点了一个巧合：这个角色第一次出现是 2016 年，就在特朗普第一次当选前几周。十年过去，南瓜头还是那个穿着廉价黑橙套装、带着两个骷髅跳舞的怪人，特朗普也还是那个特朗普。真正的节目不是这段小品，是这十年里美国公共空间被一个表达占满的过程。

南瓜头的笑点在于它毫无意义——出现在鬼屋里，不吓人，只是让人困惑。特朗普的笑点在于它同样毫无意义，却真的在决定谁进参议院。小品里他念叨得州翻紫、念叨 Ken Paxton“丑得像个婊子养的”但还得投他、念叨 Nicki Minaj 当新闻秘书、念叨自己可能是个 gay。这些不是编剧编的，报道说，特朗普最近真在公开谈论男人的屁股，具体到俄亥俄州参议员 Jon Husted 的。

一个掌握核按钮的人，把注意力花在评价男性臀部上，这件事被《SNL》消化成一个段子，观众笑完，继续过。这就是文化暴力最顺滑的运作方式：不是让你相信什么，是让你习惯什么。当“又响又橙又烦人”成为可以被模仿、被消费、被做成万圣节装扮的东西，它就不再是异常，而是气候。

小品里最狠的一句是特朗普说自己和南瓜头有共同点：“我们都出现在 2016 年。”编剧写这句的时候大概只想玩梗。但它准确得可怕。2016 年之后，美国政治的表达被一个真人秀人格接管，不是因为他说得对，是因为他说得响。注意力是定量的，被一个南瓜头占满的十年，就是别的叙事消失的十年。

报道里还有一个细节值得停一下：Katie Zacharia 的新闻秘书任期不到一周。一个真实的白宫新闻秘书，比一段小品里的假特朗普还不持久。这不是讽刺，这是结构的自白——在这个系统里，连“发言人”都只是消耗品，真正稳定的只有那个橙色的人格。

南瓜头十年后还能回来，因为它本来就是空的。特朗普十年后还能回来，因为他也是。空的容器最好用，谁都能往里装东西，包括选票。

Tom Hanks returned as David S. Pumpkins, and the report notes a coincidence: the character first appeared in 2016, a couple of weeks before Trump's first election. Ten years later, Pumpkins is still a strange man in a cheap black-and-orange suit flanked by two dancing skeletons, and Trump is still Trump. The real show isn't the sketch. It's the decade in which one expression filled American public space.

Pumpkins is funny because he means nothing. He shows up in a haunted attraction, doesn't scare anyone, just confuses them. Trump is funny for the same reason, except he's actually deciding who sits in the Senate. In the sketch he mutters about Texas turning purple, about Ken Paxton being an ugly son of a bitch you still have to vote for, about Nicki Minaj as press secretary, about possibly being gay. None of this is invented. The report says Trump has really been talking about men's asses, specifically Ohio Sen. Jon Husted's.

A man with nuclear codes spending his attention on grading male buttocks gets digested into a sketch, the audience laughs, life continues. This is how cultural violence runs smoothest: not by making you believe something, but by making you used to something. Once loud-and-orange-and-annoying becomes imitable, consumable, a Halloween costume, it stops being an anomaly and becomes climate.

The sharpest line is Trump saying he and Pumpkins have a lot in common: both appeared in 2016. The writers probably just wanted a gag. It lands anyway. After 2016, American political expression was taken over by a reality-TV personality, not because he was right but because he was loud. Attention is finite. Ten years occupied by a pumpkin head is ten years of other narratives gone.

One more detail: Katie Zacharia's tenure as press secretary lasted less than a week. A real White House press secretary is less durable than a fake Trump in a sketch. That isn't satire. It's the structure confessing: even the spokesperson is disposable, and the only stable thing is the orange personality.

Pumpkins can come back after ten years because he was always empty. Trump can come back because he is too. Empty containers work best. You can put anything in them, including votes.
