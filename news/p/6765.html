凶器是车，罪名是袭击The Weapon Was the Car

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要据 The Intercept 报道，得州哈里斯县大陪审团上周起诉一名 ICE 探员，指控其驾车撞击正在逃跑的墨西哥籍男子 Miguel Parra Hernandez，罪名是持致命武器袭击，凶器就是车辆。报道另记录 Ricardo Gonzalez、Antonio Moreida 等多起无标识 ICE 车辆撞人事件，并称其常用「攻击性驾驶技术」别车、急刹、冲撞，DHS 否认或把责任推给当事人。
The newsA Harris County, Texas, grand jury indicted an ICE officer last week for striking Miguel Parra Hernandez, a fleeing Mexican national, with his vehicle — assault with a deadly weapon, the weapon being the car. The Intercept documented similar incidents involving unmarked ICE vehicles and so-called offensive driving techniques; DHS denies the accounts or blames the civilians.
车是新的枪，罪名是新的谎。
The car is the new gun. The charge is the new lie.

先把词抢回来。「武器化」这个词是 DHS 自己发明的：他们说移民和抗议者把车武器化了，说 Lorenzo Salgado Araujo 和 Renée Good 开车撞向探员。这些指控没有在刑事起诉里成立，或者被目击证词和视频推翻。而 The Intercept 查到的二十几起案子里，握着方向盘的是 ICE。谁先给「武器化」定价，谁就定义了谁是凶手——这是第三章那套东西，夺取解释权，然后夺取「什么是事实」的制造权。

哈里斯县大陪审团上周给的答案很干脆：持致命武器袭击，凶器是车辆。监控显示 Parra Hernandez 是在背对车辆跑开的时候被撞的，DHS 说他跑到了车前面。同一套剧本在长滩、在 Woodland 又演了一遍：Ricardo Gonzalez 被撞后送医，探员当场拔枪指着他；Antonio Moreida 站在路上记录车牌，黑色 Tahoe 加速撞上去，司机没停。DHS 说他跳上了引擎盖，视频里他站着没动，当地警察和市长公开反驳。视频和官方说法对不上的时候，官方从不改口，只加一句「正在加强培训」。

这套「攻击性驾驶技术」不是失控，是政策。2023 年 CBP 的追车政策基本禁止别车和行驶中接触，2025 年的新指令把裁量权放开了，还允许无视交通法规。也就是说，撞人是被授权的能力，不是意外。而在萨凡纳，当地警察因为风险被限制追车，ICE 却高速追一个只有民事遣返令、没有犯罪记录的人，那人撞死了一位教师。这笔账 DHS 记在被追的人头上。

最脏的一处是：这套暴力被包装成秩序。探员不开警灯、不鸣笛、闯红灯、逆行、在高速上急刹逼后车追尾——急刹在所有五十个州都违法，官方指南里没有它，因为不需要写进去。它的功能不是执法，是让记录消失。被撞的人躺在医院，撞人的机构发一份新闻稿，事实就被重新分配了一次。这是元暴力最标准的样子：不是某个人撒谎，是一整套叙事默认「穿制服的人说的算事实」。

代价落在谁身上，报道写得很清楚：一个墨西哥籍男人、一个美国公民、一个记录车牌的志愿者、一个骑车的父亲、一个带着五岁儿子被撞进水泥护栏的人。他们不是「目标」，他们是可消耗的旁证。而真正被消耗掉的，是普通人相信「报警有用」这件事——当执法者自己成为街上最危险的车，安全就成了一种按身份发放的配额。

大陪审团那一纸起诉是这条新闻里唯一真的东西：direct 层上，一次具体的施暴被指名、被定罪名。但它只覆盖一个人、一辆车、一次撞击。剩下二十几起没有起诉，DHS 的回应是「加强培训」，而培训从来不改变授权。当凶器是公家的车、罪名是公家的解释权，一次起诉不是终点，是给整套机制做的一次公关保养。

Take the word back first. "Weaponized" is DHS's own coinage: they said migrants and protesters weaponized their cars, that Lorenzo Salgado Araujo and Renée Good drove at agents. Those claims never held up in a criminal charge, or were contradicted by witnesses and video. In the two dozen cases The Intercept reviewed, the hands on the wheel belonged to ICE. Whoever prices the word "weaponized" first gets to define who the killer is — that is the Chapter 3 move: seize the interpretation, then seize the right to manufacture what counts as fact.

The Harris County grand jury answered plainly last week: assault with a deadly weapon, and the weapon was the vehicle. Surveillance shows Parra Hernandez running away from the car when he was struck; DHS says he ran in front of it. Same script in Long Beach, same script in Woodland: Ricardo Gonzalez hospitalized, an agent drawing a gun on him; Antonio Moreida standing in the road logging a plate, a black Tahoe accelerating into him, the driver not stopping. DHS said he jumped on the hood. Video shows him standing still, and local police and the mayor said so publicly. When the footage contradicts the statement, the statement does not change — it just gains a line about additional training.

These "offensive driving techniques" are not失控; they are policy. A 2023 CBP pursuit policy largely barred boxing in cars and vehicle contact in motion. A 2025 directive widened discretion and let agents ignore traffic law. Ramming is an authorized capability, not an accident. In Savannah, where local police are restricted from chasing because of the risk, ICE ran a high-speed pursuit of a man with no criminal record and only a civil deportation order; his car struck and killed a teacher. DHS blamed the man being chased.

The dirtiest part: this violence is packaged as order. No lights, no sirens, running reds, driving the wrong way, brake-checking on the freeway — brake-checking is illegal in all fifty states and absent from official guidelines, because it does not need to be written down. Its function is not enforcement. It is making the record disappear. The person hit lies in a hospital; the agency issues a statement; fact gets reallocated. That is meta-violence in its standard form: not one liar, but a narrative in which the uniformed account is the default account.

Who pays is in the reporting: a Mexican national, a U.S. citizen, a volunteer logging plates, a father on a bicycle, a man with his five-year-old in the back whose car smashed into a concrete barrier. They are not targets. They are expendable corroboration. What actually gets spent is the ordinary belief that calling the police helps — when the enforcer is the most dangerous car on the street, safety becomes a quota handed out by identity.

The indictment is the one real thing here: at the direct layer, one specific act of violence got named and charged. It covers one officer, one car, one collision. The other twenty-odd cases have no charge; DHS's answer is more training, and training never touches the authorization. When the weapon is a government car and the charge sheet is a government narrative, one indictment is not an endpoint. It is scheduled maintenance.
