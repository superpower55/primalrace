AO3 停摆四周，谁在付账AO3 Goes Dark for Four Weeks, and Someone Pays

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 404 Media ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要非营利组织OTW运营的同人小说网站AO3于周四因维护下线，预计恢复需约两周，最长可能达四周。OTW表示目前不认为用户数据库被盗，但无法排除这一可能，并称没有数据被删除。截至九月，该网站收录超过1840万部作品。
The newsArchive of Our Own, a fanfiction site run by the nonprofit OTW, went down for maintenance on Thursday. Restoration is expected to take about two weeks and could take up to four weeks. OTW said no data has been deleted and it does not believe the user database was stolen, though it cannot rule it out. As of September, the site hosted over 18.4 million works.
一个由女性无偿劳动撑起来的免费库，停机时连数据库有没有被偷都说不准。
A free library built on women's unpaid labor can't even say whether its database was stolen.

AO3 停了，最多四周。停机通知里最诚实的一句是：我们目前不认为用户数据库被窃，但也无法排除。一个托管着 1840 万部作品、82900 个同人圈的站点，对自家数据到底有没有被拿走，只能给出这种句子。

这不是技术问题，是结构问题。AO3 由 OTW 运营，非营利，内容几乎全部来自无偿写作，读者无偿评论、无偿打标签、无偿做索引。一个体量大到能定义互联网同人文化的库，靠的是大量（历史上以女性为主）不计报酬的表达劳动在支撑。表达在这里不是消遣，是很多人唯一能把自己写进去的地方——谁被写成主角、谁被写成怪物、谁终于不必围着男主角转，这些选择在别处没有入口。这样一个地方，维护预算和维护人力却始终是它最薄的一环。差额就在这里：本来可以达到的稳定，和实际达到的稳定之间，隔着的是没人愿意付钱的那部分劳动。

停机时间点被报道顺手列了出来：hoa hoa hoa season、ACOTAR 新书发布周、那个以前叫 NaNoWriMo 的月份。这三件事的共同点是，它们都是商业出版和平台流量最肥的窗口。同人写作常年给原作和出版业免费做热度、做续命、做用户留存，等到自己家的服务器撑不住，没有任何一方需要为此负责。这不是共谋者理论里那种精心计算的站队，更像是一种默认：反正她们会自己写，会自己修，会自己等。

OTW 建议用户在别处复用同一密码的，去改密码。这条建议是整篇报道里唯一落到具体人身上的动作，而它把风险从机构转移给了个体。数据库有没有被偷，报道没写；谁在维护、为什么一次维护要花四周，报道也没写。缺席的名字往往就是答案的一部分。

真正被这次停机削掉的，不是某个圈子的娱乐时间，而是那批把表达当成存在确证的人，在长达一个月里失去的发布入口。她们会回来，会补档，会重建索引。而让这个入口如此脆弱的那套安排，一秒都没有停。

AO3 is down, for up to four weeks. The most honest line in the whole status update is this: we currently don't believe the user database was stolen, but we can't rule it out. A site hosting 18.4 million works across 82,900 fandoms can only offer that sentence about whether its own data walked away.

This isn't a technical failure. It's a structural one. AO3 is run by OTW, a nonprofit; the writing is unpaid, the comments are unpaid, the tagging and indexing are unpaid. A repository large enough to define internet fan culture rests on a mass of uncompensated expressive labor, historically performed mostly by women. Expression here is not a hobby. For a lot of people it is the only place where they get to write themselves in — who gets to be the protagonist, who gets to be the monster, who finally doesn't have to orbit a male lead. Nowhere else offers that entrance. And the maintenance budget and maintenance hands are always the thinnest part of the arrangement. That's the gap: the stability that was achievable versus the stability that was actually achieved, and what sits in between is the labor nobody wants to pay for.

The timing gets listed almost in passing: hoa hoa hoa season, ACOTAR release week, the month formerly known as NaNoWriMo. What those have in common is that they are the fattest windows for commercial publishing and platform traffic. Fanfic keeps original IP and the publishing industry alive for free — heat, longevity, retention — and when the servers give out, no one on the benefiting side owes anything. This isn't the careful side-picking of co-conspirators. It's a default setting: they'll write anyway, they'll fix it anyway, they'll wait anyway.

OTW tells users who reused their password elsewhere to go change it. That is the only instruction in the report that lands on a specific person, and it moves the risk from the institution to the individual. Was the database stolen? The report doesn't say. Who maintains the site, and why one maintenance job takes four weeks? Also not said. The missing names are part of the answer.

What this outage actually cuts is not leisure time for a fandom. It's the publishing entrance of people for whom expression is the confirmation that they exist, gone for a month. They'll come back, re-upload, rebuild the indexes. The arrangement that made that entrance so fragile will not pause for a second.
