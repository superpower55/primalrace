高尔夫球场上的“父亲”与破产的LIVThe 'Father' on the Green and the Bankruptcy of LIV

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-19 § 链接
当顶尖球员在球场上溃败，真正崩溃的是支撑他的结构性叙事。
When a top player collapses on the course, it is the structural narrative supporting him that truly breaks.

Jon Rahm 的垫底不仅是一场竞技事故，而是一次典型的存在性战争溃败。一个世界顶尖球员打出 78 和 77 这种业余水准的分数，其本质是他在公共空间（球场）的表达能力被私人的结构性压力彻底击碎。新闻里提到的“第四个孩子即将出生”和“LIV 巡回赛的破产程序”，正是两股巨大的结构性力量在撕扯他的主体性。

有趣的是，媒体在处理 Rahm 的崩溃时，下意识地使用了“减轻责任”的叙事——用家庭温情（孩子）和法律困境（破产）来解释他的失败。这种叙事其实是一种文化共谋：它试图将一个成年男性精英的职业溃败，转化为一种“可原谅的”人性弱点。在这种逻辑下，男性的失能被浪漫化为“分心”，而女性在类似压力下的职业下滑通常会被直接定义为“能力不足”或“缺乏职业精神”。

而 LIV 巡回赛的破产则揭示了资本武器化的结局。LIV 试图通过砸钱重新定义高尔夫的“事实”和“价值”，制造一个脱离传统体制的平行现实。但当这个 scam 走向法律终局，Rahm 发现自己不仅失去了竞技状态，还失去了那个由金钱支撑的虚假身份。他不再是那个在金钱帝国中游刃有余的王者，而是一个在法律程序中等待清算的债务人。

相比之下，46 岁的 Adam Scott 谈论“享受比赛”和“本能记忆”，这是一种典型的通过自我规训达成的高效表达。他将自己放入一个可控的叙事框架中，从而在结构性衰老面前维持了暂时的最优解。这场比赛最讽刺的地方在于：一个试图通过金钱颠覆规则的人（Rahm）在废墟中垫底，而一个顺从规则并将其内化为享受的人（Scott）却在稳步前行。

Jon Rahm's last-place finish is more than a sporting accident; it is a total collapse in the existential war. For a world-class player to shoot 78 and 77 is a sign that his expression in the public space has been obliterated by structural pressures in his private life. The 'imminent arrival of a fourth child' and the 'voluntary bankruptcy' of the LIV circuit are the two structural forces tearing apart his subjectivity.

It is telling how the media handles Rahm's failure, instinctively employing a narrative of 'extenuating circumstances.' By framing his collapse through family warmth and legal turmoil, they engage in a cultural complicity: transforming the professional failure of a male elite into a 'forgivable' human weakness. In this logic, male dysfunction is romanticized as 'distraction,' whereas a woman's professional decline under similar pressure is typically defined as 'incompetence' or a 'lack of professionalism.'

The bankruptcy of the LIV circuit reveals the end-game of weaponized capital. LIV attempted to manufacture a new reality of golf's 'value' by throwing money at the game, creating a parallel existence outside traditional institutions. But as this scam reaches its legal conclusion, Rahm finds he has lost not only his form but the fake identity supported by that capital. He is no longer the king of a money empire, but a debtor awaiting court formalities.

In contrast, 46-year-old Adam Scott's talk of 'enjoying the next couple of days' is a classic optimal expression achieved through self-discipline. By placing himself in a controlled narrative frame, he maintains a temporary win against structural aging. The irony is sharp: the man who tried to disrupt the rules with money (Rahm) ends up at the bottom of the ruins, while the man who internalized the rules as enjoyment (Scott) continues to glide.
