用催泪弹谈工资Tear Gas Over a Wage Negotiation

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
三十八亿美元的产业，靠十二万五千塔卡撑着。
A $38bn industry, held up by 12,500 taka a month.

催泪弹和声波手雷落在要求月薪三万塔卡的制衣工人身上，这就是这条新闻的核心。差额摆在那里：产业值三百八十亿美元，工人拿一万两千五百塔卡，约合一百零一美元。Violence = Potential − Actual，这条公式在这里不需要解释，只需要把两个数字并排放。

政府的第一反应不是加工资，是成立一个新的工资委员会来“审查”最低工资。审查是一个时间装置，把当下的要求推迟成未来的流程。老板们的说法更直接：成本高了，能源市场不确定，需求在放缓，所以请工人让流程走完。翻译过来就是：你的工资是我们和国际买家谈价格时的缓冲垫。Babul Akhter 那句话说得比任何分析都准——不能在低薪工人背上建生意。

然后 Mahmud Hasan Khan 补上了真正的底牌：如果抗议继续，“no work, no pay”。这句话的性质要看清。它不是威胁，它是结构本身在说话。工厂主的议价能力从来不来自他多会谈判，而来自工人明天必须吃饭。停工三天，五百家工厂关门，损失落在订单上；工人停一天，损失落在锅里。这个不对称就是结构暴力，它平时不显形，只在工人开口要钱的那一刻露出来。

所以警察在场不是意外。直接暴力在这里的功能是保护结构暴力的正常运转：催泪弹不是冲着某个具体罪犯去的，是冲着“工资不该被重新分配”这个秩序去的。三层联动得很干净——文化层的叙事说这是“审查流程”“行业困难”，结构层的工资委员会把拖延合法化，直接层的声波手雷负责让街头安静。

还有一层没被写进报道：这些衣服卖给谁。国际买家压价，工厂主转手把压力往下压，压到最没有议价能力的那一端。链条上每个环节都声称自己利润薄，只有工人的工资是唯一可以往下调的那一格。报道没有写买家的名字，没有写品牌，没有写那些在达卡谈下低价、在别处卖出高价的公司。缺席的名字本身就是材料。

工资委员会会给出一个数字，大概不会是三万。真正的问题不是这一次涨多少，而是为什么每一次涨薪都要用催泪弹来换。

Tear gas and sound grenades, aimed at garment workers asking for 30,000 taka a month. That is the story. The gap is right there in the numbers: a $38bn industry, a wage of 12,500 taka, about $101. Violence = Potential − Actual. No theory needed, just the two figures side by side.

The government's first move was not to raise the wage but to form a new wage board to review the minimum. Review is a time device: it converts a demand into a process. The owners' version is blunter — higher costs, energy uncertainty, slowing demand, so please let the process proceed. Translated: your wage is the cushion we absorb price pressure with when we negotiate with buyers. Babul Akhter said it better than any analysis could — you cannot build a business on the backs of underpaid workers.

Then Mahmud Hasan Khan laid down the real card: if the protests continue, "no work, no pay." Look at what that sentence is. It is not a threat, it is the structure speaking. The factory owner's leverage never came from negotiating skill; it comes from the fact that the worker has to eat tomorrow. Three days of shutdown costs the owners orders. One day of shutdown costs the worker dinner. That asymmetry is structural violence, invisible until the moment workers ask for money.

Which is why the police are there. Direct violence here exists to keep structural violence running normally: the tear gas is not aimed at a criminal, it is aimed at the idea that wages should be redistributed. The three layers interlock cleanly — the cultural narrative says "review process" and "industry difficulties," the structural layer legalizes delay through the wage board, the direct layer makes the street quiet.

One layer the report leaves out: who buys the clothes. International buyers push prices down, owners pass the pressure along, and it lands on the end with the least bargaining power. Every link in the chain claims thin margins; the wage is the only notch that can be turned down. The report names no buyers, no brands, no companies negotiating cheap in Dhaka and selling dear elsewhere. The missing names are the material.

The wage board will produce a number, probably not 30,000. The real question is not how much this time, but why every raise has to be bought with tear gas.
