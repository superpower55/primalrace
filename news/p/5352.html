房价增速腰斩是结构性暴力的成本转嫁House Price Slowdown as a Cost-Shift of Structural Violence

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-01 § 链接
所谓的“可负担性改善”是用更高的利息成本掩盖资产泡沫的结构性暴力。
The so-called 'improved affordability' is a scam masking asset bubbles through higher interest costs.

这篇报道试图用“地缘政治紧张”和“能源冲击”来解释英国房价增速的腰斩，但这不过是给 structural violence 披上的一层外衣。房价增速放缓并不意味着住房这个基本生存权变得更易获得，相反，它揭示了一个残酷的博弈：当资产泡沫无法通过房价上涨来维持时，系统迅速将成本转嫁给个体。

注意那个所谓的“可负担性改善” (affordability improving) 的叙事，这简直是个 scam。房价涨得慢了，但抵押贷款利率却飙升至 5.9% 以上。这意味着买房者并没有获得真正的“实惠”，而是在一个更高利息的陷阱里进行博弈。对于大多数人来说，这不是 Potential 与 Actual 差额的缩小，而是生存成本的重新定价。

在这种叙事中，金融机构和资产持有者是共谋者 (complicity)。他们通过操纵利率和利用地缘政治恐慌，将住房从一种“居住权利”彻底异化为一种“金融衍生品”。当经济不确定性增加，他们并不降低房价以回归理性，而是通过提高融资门槛来筛选那些能够承受高利息的个体，从而维持资产的底价。

最讽刺的是，首席经济学家 Robert Gardner 还在期待“信心回归”和“利率回落”。这种信心是指什么？是指房价再次上涨、资产再次泡沫化，让持有房产的阶级重新获利？在这种男本位的、以资本增值为核心的经济叙事中，真正需要住房的人永远是那个被牺牲的、不被计算在内的变量。

This report attempts to use 'geopolitical tensions' and 'energy shocks' to explain the halving of UK house price growth, but this is merely a veil for structural violence. A slowdown in price growth does not mean the basic right to housing has become more accessible; instead, it reveals a cruel game: when asset bubbles can no longer be sustained by price hikes, the system rapidly shifts the cost onto individuals.

Note the narrative of 'improving affordability'—it is a complete scam. While prices grow slower, mortgage rates have surged above 5.9%. This means homebuyers aren't getting a 'deal'; they are gambling in a high-interest trap. For most, this isn't a narrowing of the gap between Potential and Actual, but a repricing of the cost of existence.

In this framework, financial institutions and asset holders act as complicity. By manipulating rates and leveraging geopolitical panic, they alienate housing from a 'right to dwell' and turn it into a financial derivative. When uncertainty hits, they don't lower prices to return to rationality; they raise the barrier to entry to filter for those who can stomach high interest, thus protecting the asset floor.

It is peak irony that Robert Gardner expects 'confidence to return.' What 'confidence' is he referring to? The hope that prices will surge again, bubbles will reinflate, and the propertied class will profit once more? In this masculine-centric economic narrative focused on capital appreciation, the people who actually need a home remain the sacrificed, uncalculated variable.
