巴西大选：在两套男性中心叙事的夹缝中Brazil Election: Trapped Between Two Masculine-Centric Narratives

国际 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-17 § 链接
这不是左与右的战争，而是两种元暴力在争夺解释权。
Not a war of Left vs Right, but a clash of two meta-violences fighting for the right to define reality.

卢拉在圣贝尔纳多-多坎波的集会，本质上是一次关于“救世主”叙事的自我复制。一个 80 岁的工会领袖，试图通过唤醒半个世纪前的阶级记忆来对抗右翼。这种叙事在 structural 层面上确实通过社会福利计划缩小了 Potential 和 Actual 的差额，但在 meta 层面上，它依然运行在一种典型的 masculine-centric narrative 之中：一个强有力的男性领袖，通过对抗另一个强有力的男性（博索纳罗）来定义国家的命运。

而博索纳罗之子 Flávio 的出场，则是一场赤裸裸的身份武器化表演。防弹背心、黄色球衣、在 Copacabana 海滩的集会，这些都是精准的认知入口投放。他并不在乎政策，他在乎的是将自己包装成一个“对抗系统”的斗士。这种“斗士”表达是典型的假.最优解，它通过扮演一个被压迫的受害者（尽管他来自权力顶层）来换取底层右翼的认同。这种共谋关系的逻辑很简单：只要有一个强人能带领我们通过暴力获得快感，具体的制度正义并不重要。

最值得警惕的是外部的元暴力介入。特朗普政府将巴西犯罪组织定义为“恐怖组织”，这套叙事逻辑与当年的伊拉克战争如出一辙——通过定义一个“绝对之恶”来夺取军事干预的解释权。当 Pete Hegseth 说要对坏人“做坏事”时，他实际上是在宣布：美国拥有定义谁是“坏人”的定价权。在这种叙事下，巴西的主权被客体化，成为了大国博弈中的一个棋子。

这场选举被冠以“拯救民主”之名，但民主在两套男性中心叙事的碰撞中被简化成了“谁能更有效地使用暴力机器”。在这种博弈中，真正的弱势群体——那些在 favela 中被杀死的女性和贫民，依然只是背景板。他们被卢拉用来证明右翼的残暴，被博索纳罗用来证明系统的腐败，但没有任何一方真正打算将解释权让渡给他们。

Lula’s rally in São Bernardo do Campo is essentially a replication of the 'Savior' narrative. An 80-year-old union leader attempts to combat the right by evoking class memories from half a century ago. While this narrative narrowed the gap between Potential and Actual in the structural layer through social welfare, at the meta level, it still operates within a classic masculine-centric narrative: a powerful male leader defining a nation's fate by opposing another powerful male (Bolsonaro).

Meanwhile, the appearance of Flávio Bolsonaro is a naked weaponisation of identity. The bullet-proof vest, the yellow jersey, the Copacabana beach rally—these are precise cognitive entry points. He doesn't care about policy; he cares about packaging himself as a 'fighter against the system.' This 'fighter' expression is a fake optimal expression, gaining the allegiance of the right-wing underclass by performing as a persecuted victim, despite hailing from the peak of power. The complicity here is simple: as long as a strongman can lead them to the pleasure of violence, institutional justice is irrelevant.

Most alarming is the intervention of external meta-violence. The Trump administration designating Brazilian crime groups as 'terrorist organisations' follows the exact same narrative logic as the Iraq War—using the definition of an 'absolute evil' to seize the right to interpret military intervention. When Pete Hegseth speaks of 'doing bad things to bad people,' he is declaring that the US holds the pricing power to define who the 'bad people' are. In this narrative, Brazilian sovereignty is objectified, reduced to a pawn in great-power games.

This election is framed as 'saving democracy,' but democracy is reduced to 'who can more effectively operate the violence machine' between two masculine-centric narratives. In this game, the truly marginalised—the women and the poor killed in favelas—remain mere background scenery. They are used by Lula to prove the brutality of the right, and by Bolsonaro to prove the corruption of the system, yet neither side intends to cede the right of interpretation to them.
