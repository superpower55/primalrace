体育的皮，色情产业的骨，与被殖民的身体The Skin of Sport, the Bone of Porn, and the Colonized Body

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Athletic ↗ 2026-09-14 § 链接
用性化身体来定义体育，是元暴力对女性主体性的再次殖民。
Defining sports through sexualized bodies is meta-violence recolonizing female subjectivity.

Novig 这场所谓的 "Just Sports" 广告是一次典型的武器化表达。它极其阴险地将体育符号（球类、护具）作为遮羞布，试图在认知入口处建立一个伪逻辑：只要是关于体育的，即便将女性身体物化为产品，也是 "playful"（俏皮）的。这根本不是在推销预测市场，而是在利用女性身体作为流量饵料，通过视觉上的 sexualisation 夺取注意力。

在这里，Sydney Sweeney 扮演的是一个典型的共谋者。她不仅是股权持有者，更是这次叙事设计的参与者。当她用 "simple and playful" 来掩盖这种物化逻辑时，她实际上是在通过扮演一个 "被凝视的客体" 来换取资本收益。这种假.最优解表达的代价，是她将自身的主体性让渡给了父权制的审美定价权，并试图将这种让渡合法化为 "创意"。

而女性运动员们的反击，是一场关于 "表达" 的存在性战争。当 Amy Hunt 强调 "Muscles. Hard work, blood, sweat, tears" 时，她是在试图用真实的生物墙——那些由训练雕刻出的肌肉和汗水——去撞击那个被过滤掉力量感的、纯粹为了满足男性凝视的虚假表型。这不仅仅是关于 "美" 的争论，而是关于 "谁有权定义女性在体育空间中的存在" 的博弈。

最讽刺的是 Novig CEO 的回应："它迫使我们进入国家话语"。这句话撕掉了所有伪装——在他们的逻辑里，女性身体的被物化只是一个 "工具"，用来制造争议，从而在男性中心叙事的舆论场中刷存在感。这就是元暴力的运作方式：将女性的身体作为筹码，在男性的博弈桌上换取商业利益，而女性本身，无论是被物化的模特还是被冒犯的运动员，都只是这场 scam 里的背景板。

Novig's "Just Sports" campaign is a textbook example of weaponized expression. It insidiously uses sports symbols—balls and pads—as fig leaves to establish a pseudo-logic at the cognitive entry point: that objectifying the female body is "playful" as long as it's framed around sports. This isn't about promoting a prediction market; it's about using the female body as bait, seizing attention through systemic sexualisation.

Sydney Sweeney acts here as a clear complicitor. As an equity holder and creative collaborator, she helps craft a narrative where the objectification of women is rebranded as "playful." By performing the role of the "gazed-upon object" for capital gain, she adopts a fake optimal expression, sacrificing her subjectivity to the pricing power of the masculine-centric aesthetic.

The backlash from female athletes is an existential war over expression. When Amy Hunt emphasizes "Muscles. Hard work, blood, sweat, tears," she is using the biological wall—muscles carved by labor—to clash with a fake phenotype designed solely for the masculine gaze. This is not a debate about "beauty," but a struggle over who has the right to define female existence within the sporting space.

Most cynical is the Novig CEO's admission that the ad "forces us into the national discourse." This strips away the mask: in their logic, the objectification of women is merely a tool to generate controversy and visibility within a masculine-centric narrative. This is how meta-violence operates—treating the female body as a chip on a male gambling table to trade for commercial profit, while the women—whether the objectified model or the offended athlete—remain mere background noise in the scam.
