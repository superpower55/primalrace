他倒下之后，枪口还没放下The Guns Stayed Up After He Went Down

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要美国田纳西州孟菲斯市，一段由谢尔比县检方公布的视频显示，今年7月5日国民警卫队成员开枪击中20岁的泰林·约翰逊后，多名警卫队员用手枪指向倒在人行道上、正在死亡的他。家属委托的尸检报告显示子弹从背部射入，与警卫队报告所称约翰逊曾转身举枪指向他们的说法相矛盾。检方尚未决定是否起诉。
The newsVideo released by Shelby County prosecutors shows Tennessee national guard members pointing handguns at 20-year-old Tyrin Johnson as he lay dying on a Memphis sidewalk after a guard member shot him on 5 July. An autopsy shared by his family found he was shot in the back, contradicting guard accounts that he had turned and pointed a gun at them. Prosecutors have not yet decided on charges.
「他转身举枪」是一句叙事，尸检报告是另一句
"He turned and pointed a gun" is a narrative. The autopsy is a fact.

先看画面里最不需要解释的一秒钟：一个人仰面躺在人行道上，已经中枪，几名国民警卫队员的手枪仍然指着他，喊「Hands!」「Turn over!」。他还能把手举起来吗，他还能翻过身吗。视频没有给答案，但枪口的位置给了。

警卫队的报告写得很干净：嫌犯「转过身来，用一把棕褐色的手枪指向他们」，两名队员随即开火。这套句式我们见过太多次——先给死者一个朝向枪口的姿势，开枪就成了自卫。但家属拿到的尸检报告说，子弹是从背后进去的。一个人的背和一个人的正面，是两个方向。这不是记忆偏差，这是两种事实。

哪一种是官方版本，哪一种要由家属自己去找病理医生来做，这本身就是答案。警卫队那份报告是通过公共记录申请拿到的，主动公开；尸检报告是家属自己带去给媒体看的。解释权在谁手上，看一眼谁在发布、谁在申辩就清楚了。

再往外一层。这支队伍不是本地警察，是特朗普派进民主党执政城市的联邦任务组的一部分，田纳西州共和党州长比尔·李把国民警卫队派去「支持」这项行动。这个任务组在五月到七月之间卷入了四起致命枪击。四起。一个以「治安失控」为名被送进城市的武装力量，在三个月里打死了四个人，然后其中一起的现场录像要等四个月才由地方检察官放出来。

「治安」这个词是这套机器的认知入口。它先把一座城市定义成需要被占领的地方，再把住在那里的年轻黑人男性定义成威胁，接着「他转身举枪」这句话就不需要太多证据了——因为前面的定义已经替他完成了举证。约翰逊二十岁，有一个年幼的孩子，正准备接手家里的建筑生意。这些细节在警卫队的报告里一个字都不会有，因为报告要写的不是一个人，是一个目标。

还有一层更冷的。录像里一名孟菲斯警察在电台里叫救护车，然后转头问警卫队员：你们有医疗包吗。至少六名警卫队员在场，其中一人弯腰去动地上的手枪，被警察制止。有人在问医疗包，有人在挪枪。这两件事同时发生在一个正在流血的人旁边，而它们都属于「程序」。

地方检察官史蒂夫·马尔罗伊说，他还没决定要不要起诉。家属律师惠顿的要求很克制：调查应该聚焦在开枪那一刻是否合理。这个要求之所以显得克制，是因为它已经退到不能再退——连录像都没有拍到开枪的那一瞬间，只有枪声，只有模糊的晃动，只有一声喊叫之后的几声枪响。

所以现在能确证的只有一件事：他倒下之后，枪口没有放下。至于他倒下之前那零点几秒里发生了什么，录像没有给，报告给了一个版本，尸检给了另一个。谁有权力决定公众相信哪一个版本，这才是这个案子里真正没有被拍进视频的部分。

Start with the one second in the footage that needs no interpretation: a man is on his back on the sidewalk, already shot, and several national guard members still have their handguns trained on him, shouting "Hands!" and "Turn over!" Can he raise his hands? Can he roll over? The video doesn't answer. The guns do.

The guard report is tidy: the suspect "rounded on them and pointed a tan handgun at them," so both members fired. We have seen this sentence many times before. Give the dead man a posture facing the barrel, and the shooting becomes self-defense. But the autopsy the family obtained says the bullet entered through his back. A back and a front are two directions. This is not a memory lapse. It is two versions of what happened.

Which version is official, and which one required the family to go find a pathologist themselves, is the answer. The guard report was obtained through a public records request. The autopsy was brought to the press by the family. Look at who publishes and who has to argue, and you know who holds the pen.

Zoom out. These troops were not local police. They were part of a federal taskforce Donald Trump sent into Democratic-run cities, with Tennessee's Republican governor Bill Lee deploying the guard to support it. That taskforce was involved in four fatal shootings between May and July. Four. An armed force sent into a city on the grounds that crime was out of control kills four people in three months, and footage from one of those scenes takes four months to surface from the district attorney's office.

"Public safety" is the cognitive entry point for this machine. First define a city as a place that needs to be occupied. Then define the young Black men who live there as threats. After that, "he turned and pointed a gun" barely needs evidence, because the earlier definition has already done the proving. Johnson was 20, had a young child, and was preparing to help run the family construction business. None of that appears in the guard report, because the report is not describing a person. It is describing a target.

There is a colder layer. In the footage a Memphis police officer calls for an ambulance on the radio, then turns to the troops and asks: do you have a medical kit. At least six guard members are on the scene. One of them bends to move the handgun on the ground until an officer tells him to leave it alone. Someone is asking for a medical kit while someone else is repositioning a gun. Both count as procedure.

District attorney Steve Mulroy says he has not yet decided whether to file charges. The family's attorney, André Wharton, asks only that the investigation stay focused on whether the fatal shot was reasonable. That request sounds restrained because it has already retreated as far as it can. The footage does not even show the shot. Only the yelling, a blur, and several gunshots after a single one.

So the only thing confirmed is this: after he went down, the guns stayed up. What happened in the fraction of a second before that is not on the video. The report offers one version. The autopsy offers another. Who gets to decide which version the public believes is the part of this case that was never filmed.
