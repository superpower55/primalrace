用七亿买十六亿，这不叫丑闻$736 Million In, $16.3 Billion Out

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要据 Democracy Defenders Action 报告，马斯克自离开政府17个月以来获得至少100亿美元联邦合同，加上此前38亿美元和任政府效率部期间6.3亿美元。他中期选举花费超4.45亿美元，2024年投入7.36亿美元支持特朗普和共和党。报告称15个联邦部门中至少11个采取过有利于其商业利益的动作。
The newsA Democracy Defenders Action report says Musk won at least $10 billion in federal contracts in the 17 months since leaving government, plus $38 billion earlier and $6.3 billion during his DOGE tenure. He spent over $445 million on the midterms and $736 million backing Trump and Republicans in 2024. The report says at least 11 of 15 federal departments took actions favorable to his businesses.
法律禁的是行贿，资本买的是例外
The law bans bribery; capital buys the loophole instead.

一笔投资，回报率 2,200%。这不是什么硅谷神话，是马斯克花 7.36 亿美元支持特朗普和共和党之后，拿回 16.3 亿美元的联邦合同。报道里那个数字摆得很清楚，清楚到不需要任何修辞：7.36 亿进，16.3 亿出。

法律其实早就料到过这种事。1940 年国会就立了规矩，联邦承包商不得向联邦竞选的政党、委员会或候选人捐款，理由说得毫不含糊——拿政府合同赚钱的人捐政治献金，某些情况下可以被视为行贿。水门事件之后这条禁令还被加强了，2015 年哥伦比亚特区巡回法院仍然维持它。那马斯克为什么没事？因为承包商是公司。SpaceX 是公司，公司的管理层、董事、大股东和政治行动委员会照样可以往联邦选举里砸钱。再加上超级政治行动委员会的“独立支出”不在禁令范围内，而马斯克的 America PAC 占了他说的一半开销。法律把门锁上了，然后在旁边留了一个能开进一辆重型卡车的洞。

真正要看的不是钱，是钱买到了什么。DOGE 名义上是砍政府开支的，结果账单是 5,170 亿美元。同一时间，马斯克从联邦政府手里收走 63 亿美元的合同。他离开政府十七个月，又拿了至少 100 亿美元。而在 2025 年之前，他已经从联邦这口奶里吸了 380 亿美元。这不是监管失灵，这是监管被重新设计。

更精确的一处：联邦政府把最机密的太空追踪数据独家给了 SpaceX——而这发生在 DOGE 把太空交通管制项目的员工几乎全裁掉之后，只留了一个人，大概是防着法院问起来没法交代。先拆掉一个公共职能，再把它私有化，最后交给那个拆它的人。这条路径不需要阴谋论，报道里写的就是这个顺序。

监管上的好处可能比合同更值钱。报道说，15 个联邦行政部门里至少 11 个做了有利于马斯克商业利益的动作。xAI 在孟菲斯的数据中心装了 59 台天然气涡轮机，没拿到联邦清洁空气许可，其中大部分还放在密西西比州一侧，州监管机构说这些涡轮机豁免《清洁空气法》——报道直接说这个说法是错的。全国有色人种协进会代表附近的低收入黑人社区起诉，要求环保署执法。特朗普的司法部站到了 xAI 那边，理由是美国要维持“全球 AI 主导地位”。10 月 19 日，密西西比一位地区法官驳回了这个说法。

谁在为这套东西付代价，报道写得很具体：孟菲斯边上那些低收入黑人社区，呼吸着没有许可的涡轮机排出来的空气。他们不在任何合同里，不在这场 2,200% 的回报里，他们的名字甚至不在标题里。这才是这笔交易的结构——收益被一个人拿走，成本被摊给一群没有谈判席位的人。

还有一处，SEC 本来要马斯克为延迟披露推特持股付 1.5 亿美元罚款，特朗普上台后，和解金额变成 150 万美元。地区法官 Sooknanan 勉强批准时写了一句：法院不是橡皮图章，但也不是监察专员。她把问题交回给选民，说这该由公民在投票箱里决定。这句话听起来像司法的谦逊，实际上是把一个已经查实的证券违规，转手变成了选票问题。

所以这篇报道真正说的不是马斯克贪。贪是结果，不是机制。机制是：当一个人同时是监管的对象、监管的拆解者、以及拆解者的出资人，法律就只剩下措辞。国会 1940 年想禁的那件事，在 2026 年通过一个公司壳、一个超级 PAC、一条“其他交易授权”的快速通道，合法地发生了。

法官说这该由选民决定。那 11 月 3 日的投票，就是关于这笔 2,200% 的账要不要继续记在公共账本上的投票。只是那 59 台涡轮机不会等到投票日才转起来。

A 2,200 percent return. Not a Silicon Valley myth—Musk put $736 million behind Trump and the GOP and got back $16.3 billion in federal contracts. The report lays the numbers out plainly enough that no rhetoric is needed: $736 million in, $16.3 billion out.

The law anticipated this. In 1940 Congress barred federal contractors from contributing to a political party, committee, or candidate for federal office, and the sponsor said why without flinching: people making money off government contracts shouldn't donate, because such donations may in some instances be considered bribery to secure those contracts. Watergate strengthened the ban; the D.C. Circuit upheld it as recently as 2015. So why is Musk untouchable? Because the contractor is a corporation. SpaceX is a corporation, and a corporation's officers, directors, major shareholders, and PACs remain free to spend on federal elections. Add super PAC "independent expenditures," which the ban doesn't cover—and Musk's America PAC accounts for about half his spending. The law locked the door and left a hole you can drive a truck through.

What matters isn't the money. It's what the money bought. DOGE was supposed to cut spending; it ran up a $517 billion bill. In the same period Musk collected $6.3 billion in federal contracts. Seventeen months after leaving government he'd taken at least $10 billion more. Before 2025 he'd already suckled $38 billion from the federal teat. This isn't regulatory failure. It's regulation redesigned.

One detail is exact: the federal government gave SpaceX exclusive access to its most closely held space-tracking data—right after DOGE fired nearly all the staff of the space traffic control program, keeping one, presumably as insurance against a court asking questions. Dismantle a public function, privatize it, hand it to the person who dismantled it. The report gives you that order; no conspiracy theory required.

Regulatory relief may be worth more than the contracts. At least 11 of 15 federal executive departments took actions favorable to Musk's business interests. xAI installed 59 natural gas turbines at a Memphis data center without federal clean air permits, most of them across the state line in Mississippi, where regulators claimed—wrongly, the report says—that the turbines were exempt from the Clean Air Act. The NAACP sued on behalf of nearby low-income Black communities to force EPA enforcement. Trump's Justice Department backed xAI, arguing that America must sustain "global AI dominance." On October 19 a Mississippi district judge rejected that.

Who pays is specific in this report: the low-income Black communities near Memphis, breathing what unpermitted turbines put into the air. They appear in no contract, no 2,200 percent return, not even the headline. That's the structure of the deal—the gain goes to one man, the cost is spread across people with no seat at the table.

And the SEC, which had demanded $150 million from Musk for delaying disclosure of his Twitter share purchases, settled after Trump took office for $1.5 million. Judge Sooknanan approved it reluctantly: a court is not a rubber stamp, but neither is it an ombudsman. She handed the question to the voters. That sounds like judicial humility. It's the transfer of a documented securities violation into a ballot question.

The story isn't that Musk is greedy. Greed is the outcome, not the mechanism. The mechanism is this: when one person is simultaneously the regulated, the dismantler of the regulator, and the funder of the dismantler, the law is reduced to wording. What Congress tried to ban in 1940 happened legally in 2026—through a corporate shell, a super PAC, and a fast-track "other transaction authority."

The judge says voters should decide. Then November 3 is a referendum on whether this 2,200 percent line stays on the public ledger. Except the 59 turbines won't wait for election day to spin.
