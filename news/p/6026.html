战争发财，账单寄给谁War Profits, Family Bills

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
战争的成本被转嫁给家庭，利润归股东。
War costs are shifted onto households; profits go to shareholders.

战争一开始，油价就涨了，利润也跟着涨。这不是市场，这是把战争成本转嫁给普通家庭的机制。

报道里说得很清楚：美国和以色列从二月开始轰炸伊朗，之后美国石油行业赚了将近1300亿美元。去年同期油价便宜的时候，同一批公司赚了不到470亿美元（来源：Mother Jones）。埃克森美孚利润翻倍，雪佛龙涨了180%，BP创下18年新高。运费和保险费因为战争涨了，公司顺势把油价推上去。中位数美国家庭在加油站多花了856美元。

特朗普在佐治亚的集会上把话说得很直白：“我们正在发一笔他妈的大财。”这句话不是失言，是他把结构暴力的分配逻辑说穿了：战争的成本由普通家庭承担，战争的收益归股东。他个人的油气股票今年涨了最多3580万美元。

这不是“国家在赚钱”。国家不赚钱，公司赚钱，股东赚钱，总统赚钱。付钱的是那个在加油站多掏856美元的家庭，是那个因为运费和保险费上涨而生活成本全面抬高的家庭。

这套叙事的武器化在于，它把一场战争的收益包装成“国家的收益”，从而让成本看起来像是全民共同承担、共同受益。实际上，收益是私人的，成本是公共的。这是结构暴力最标准的形态：资源向上分配，代价向下分摊。

报道没有写的是：那些多花856美元的家庭里，有多少人正在为同一场战争支付第二次代价——税收、国债、或者被削减的公共预算。战争的成本从来不只是加油站的账单。

“我们正在发一笔大财”——这句话的主语是谁，报道写得很清楚。但这句话的宾语是谁，报道没有写。

The war starts, oil prices rise, profits rise. This is not a market. It is a mechanism for shifting the cost of war onto ordinary households.

The report is clear: since the US and Israel began bombing Iran in February, the US oil industry has earned almost $130 billion in profits. Over the same span in 2025, when oil was comparatively cheap, the same companies earned less than $47 billion (source: Mother Jones). ExxonMobil's profits doubled. Chevron's grew by 180 percent. BP hit an 18-year high. Shipping and insurance costs spiked because of the war, and the companies raised prices accordingly. The median US household has spent $856 more at the gas pump.

Trump said it plainly at a Georgia rally: "I don't give a damn, I'm going to say it—we're making a freaking fortune." This is not a gaffe. It is the distribution logic of structural violence stated out loud: the cost of war is borne by ordinary households, the gains go to shareholders. His personal oil and gas stocks grew by as much as $35.8 million this year.

This is not "the country making money." The country does not make money. Companies make money. Shareholders make money. The president makes money. The ones paying are the households spending $856 more at the pump, the households whose overall cost of living rises because shipping and insurance costs rise.

The weaponization here is narrative: it packages the gains of a war as "the nation's gains," so that the costs look like something everyone bears and everyone benefits from. In fact the gains are private and the costs are public. This is structural violence in its standard form: resources distributed upward, costs distributed downward.

What the report does not say: among the households paying $856 more, how many are paying a second time for the same war—through taxes, through national debt, through cut public budgets. The cost of war was never just the gas station receipt.

"We're making a freaking fortune"—who the subject is, the report makes clear. Who the object is, the report leaves out.
