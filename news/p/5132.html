“我尽力了”：一种典型的男性中心叙事豁免权“I Did My Best”: The Typical Exemption of Masculine-Centric Narrative

性别 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-19 § 链接
当男性在悲剧后定义“尽力”时，他实际上在完成一次主体性的自我救赎。
When a man defines his 'best' after a tragedy, he is performing a self-redemption of subjectivity.

Patrick Clancy 在 60 Minutes 里的那句 “I think I did the best I could”，是教科书级别的男性中心叙事 (masculine-centric narrative)。在这场由产后精神病 (postpartum psychosis) 导致的惨剧中，死者是三个孩子，施暴者是母亲，而作为父亲的他，通过一次媒体访谈，成功地将自己从“失职的共谋者”转化为一个“努力过但无能为力的受害者”。

请注意这个逻辑：在 structural 层面上，产后精神健康资源的匮乏和医疗系统的失职是元暴力 (meta violence) 的体现，但 Patrick 的表达却将问题个体化。他强调自己“寻求了医疗帮助”，以此证明他在博弈中选择了“最优解表达”——即扮演一个负责任的丈夫和父亲。当他对着镜头说出“我尽力了”时，他实际上在请求公众赋予他一种豁免权：既然我已经执行了社会公约要求的“尽力”，那么结果的惨烈就与我的主体性无关。

而与此同时，Lindsay Clancy 的主体性在法庭上被反复解剖。她的精神状态被简化为一种“辩护理由”或“犯罪计划”。在父权结构的共谋下，一个崩溃的母亲被定义为“疯子”或“杀手”，而一个在旁观察并尝试“管理”这个疯子的男性，却可以通过描述自己的悲伤和瘫痪，在文化层面上获得道德的高地。

最讽刺的是，他的现任妻子，一位不孕症专家，在访谈中通过强调他“无私”和“支持”来为他背书。这种叙事闭环极其完整：一个受创的男性 + 一个专业且温柔的女性支持者 + 一个权威媒体的背书 = 一个完美的、无需被追责的受害者形象。在这种叙事里，真正的暴力——那种将生育和养育压力完全结构化在女性身体上，而男性仅需扮演“监督者”和“救助者”的权力不对等，被彻底掩盖了。

Patrick Clancy’s line in 60 Minutes, “I think I did the best I could,” is a textbook example of masculine-centric narrative. In this tragedy driven by postpartum psychosis, the victims were three children and the perpetrator was the mother. Yet, through a media interview, the father successfully transforms himself from a complicit bystander into a 'victim who tried but failed.'

Notice the logic: on a structural level, the scarcity of postpartum mental health resources and medical negligence are manifestations of meta violence. However, Patrick’s expression individualizes the problem. By emphasizing that he 'sought psychiatric care,' he proves he chose the 'optimal expression'—playing the role of a responsible husband and father. When he tells the camera he 'did his best,' he is essentially requesting a social exemption: since I fulfilled the 'best' required by the social contract, the horrific outcome is detached from my subjectivity.

Meanwhile, Lindsay Clancy’s subjectivity is dissected in court. Her mental state is reduced to either a 'defense' or a 'planned crime.' Under the complicity of the patriarchal structure, a collapsed mother is defined as a 'lunatic' or a 'murderer,' while the man who observed and attempted to 'manage' her gains moral high ground by describing his grief and paralysis.

The irony peaks with his current wife, an infertility specialist, who validates him by calling him 'selfless' and 'supportive.' This narrative loop is seamless: a traumatized man + a professional, gentle female supporter + an authoritative media endorsement = a perfect, unaccountable victim. In this story, the real violence—the structural imbalance where the burden of reproduction and care is placed entirely on the female body while the man merely plays the 'supervisor' and 'rescuer'—is completely erased.
