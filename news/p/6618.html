骂影评人，是导演最后的营销Blame the Critics, Dodge the Numbers

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要塔伊加·维迪提执导、改编自石黑一雄小说的新片《克拉拉与太阳》北美首周末票房仅70.1万美元，成本约4400万美元。维迪提在社交媒体上抨击影评人，称他们是「可悲的迷失灵魂」。该片在烂番茄的观众评分为74%，影评人评分仅26%。
The newsTaika Waititi's new film Klara and the Sun, adapted from Kazuo Ishiguro's novel, took just $701,000 in its North American opening weekend against a reported $44m budget. Waititi attacked critics on social media as "sad lost souls". The film holds 74% with audiences and 26% with critics on Rotten Tomatoes.
票房差不是影评人害的，是没人想看。
The box office didn't fail because of reviews. It failed because nobody came.

Waititi 把票房失败算在影评人头上。这套账算不通：北美首周末 701,000 美元，880 家影院，场均不到 800 美元，排第 16。这个数字不是被骂出来的，是没人买票。影评人没有能力让 880 个厅空着，观众才有。

他发的那段话值得拆开看。“那些没能成为艺术家、只好退而求其次去当影评人的可悲迷失灵魂”——这句话的结构是：批评我的资格需要先验资，你没拍成电影，你就没有评价电影的权利。这是把解释权收回给创作者本人。作品一旦出门，就不再只属于作者。这个道理所有创作者都知道，只是输的时候不想承认。

他搬出 Rotten Tomatoes 的对照：观众 74%，影评人 26%。这个对照被当成民意平反的证据。但同一条数据也说明，买票的人本来就不多——74% 的好评率没有转化成票房，说明连“喜欢的人”这个池子都小得可怜。用观众分给自己辩护，等于用一份样本量极小的问卷证明自己没错。

真正被这套话术遮住的，是钱。4400 万美元成本，第二周北美跌 79%，掉到 145,000 美元。这个跌幅不需要影评人参与，是第一批观众看完之后的口碑在起作用。Peter Bradshaw 给了他一星，说“难以忍受的做作”“难以言说的表演”；Wendy Ide 说它“把原著的深度剥掉，换成自觉的古怪”；Tim Grierson 说那种“精心计算的甜腻”先是让人麻木，然后就让人恼火。三个人的用词指向同一件事：不是主题错了，是处理方式在向观众讨要感情。

讨要感情这件事，在书里叫假的最优解表达。创作者扮演一个被误解的天才，对着“不懂我的世界”发怒，短期能换来同情和话题，代价是把自己的判断力交出去——从此评价体系只剩两种人：懂我的和无关的。他写“我不听任何针对我和我同类的评论，但我听人民的”。“我同类”和“人民”被他分成了两个阵营，中间那批写评论的人被划到阵营外。这不是艺术判断，这是站队。

更值得看的是这条新闻没写的那部分。Klara and the Sun 是石黑一雄的书，讲的是一个被造出来陪伴人的 AI 女孩，讲她被观看、被定价、被使用，讲她怎么学会用讨人喜欢来换取存在。这样一本书被拍成“甜腻”“做作”的片子，本身就是一个讽刺：素材讲的是被消费的表达，成品却在用同样的方式消费观众的情绪。

影评人不是这场失败的原因，也不是这场失败的受害者。真正的差额在这里：4400 万美元、一群演员、一个诺贝尔奖作家的文本，本可以做出点别的东西，现在变成了导演社交媒体上的一条怒气。这笔差额没有人来认领，Waititi 把它记在了影评人的账上，然后宣布自己不听。

Waititi has decided the critics did this. The accounting doesn't work. $701,000 opening weekend across 880 North American venues, just under $800 per theatre, 16th place. That number wasn't written by reviewers. It was written by empty seats. Critics can't empty 880 auditoriums. Audiences can.

Look at what he actually posted. "Sad lost souls who couldn't achieve their dreams of becoming artists, and thus settled, and became critics." The structure of that sentence is a licensing requirement: to review a film you must first have made one. That's an attempt to take the interpretive authority back from the audience and hand it to the author. A work stops belonging only to its maker the moment it ships. Every creator knows this. Losing creators pretend otherwise.

Then the Rotten Tomatoes split: 74% audience, 26% critics. He offers it as a vindication. But the same data says the audience pool was tiny to begin with. A 74% score that produces $701,000 isn't a silenced majority, it's a small room. Using audience scores to defend yourself is citing a survey with almost no respondents.

What the rhetoric covers is the money. A reported $44m budget, a 79% second-weekend collapse to $145,000. That drop doesn't require a critic in the room. It requires the first wave of ticket buyers telling the second wave not to bother. Bradshaw gave it one star: "unbearably mannered," "unspeakably acted." Ide: "a thin and cloying thing" that strips the source of its profundity. Grierson: a "calculated gooeyness" that numbs before it exasperates. Three different reviewers, one diagnosis. Not the wrong subject. The wrong handling. The film asks to be loved rather than earning it.

Asking to be loved is what the book calls a false optimal expression. The creator plays the misunderstood genius raging at a world that doesn't get him. It buys short-term sympathy and attention. The price is his own judgment: from then on there are only two categories, people who understand me and irrelevant people. "I don't read reviews of anything aimed at me or my peers," he writes, "but I do listen to the people." Me and my peers on one side, the people on the other, critics pushed outside both. That isn't aesthetic judgment. That's team selection.

And the part the story leaves out. Klara and the Sun is Ishiguro's novel about an artificial girl built to accompany, watched, priced, used, learning to be likable in exchange for existing. A book about consumed expression, adapted into something critics call cloying and mannered. The material was about being turned into a product for someone else's feeling. The film does the same thing to its audience.

The critics didn't cause this failure and aren't its victims. The real gap is here: $44m, a cast, a Nobel laureate's novel, and what could have been something else, now a social media post. Nobody claims that gap. Waititi has booked it against the reviewers, then announced he isn't listening.
