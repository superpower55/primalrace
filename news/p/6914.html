裁员裁到大动脉，飞轮还在转The Flywheel Spins Because Someone Gets Thrown Off

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要Netflix 10 月中旬裁撤约 1000 名员工（占员工总数 6%），为 2022 年以来最大规模裁员。伦敦办公室约 2% 至 3% 的员工受影响，其中包括负责英国及 EMEA 本地语言内容制作的副总裁 Anna Mallett；她 2020 年 11 月入职，参与过《驯鹿宝贝》《混沌少年时》等项目。美国方面，首席内容官 Bela Bajaria 与电影主管 Dan Lin 也在被裁之列，Bajaria 由 Peter Rice 接替。
The newsNetflix laid off about 1,000 employees, or 6 percent of its workforce, in mid-October, its largest cuts since 2022. In London, roughly 2 to 3 percent of staff were affected, including Anna Mallett, VP of physical production for the U.K. and EMEA local-language content. In the U.S., chief content officer Bela Bajaria and film boss Dan Lin were also cut, with Peter Rice replacing Bajaria.
飞轮转得动，是因为有人被甩出去了
The flywheel still spins — that's what a flywheel does to the people it throws off.

Netflix 说自己的飞轮还在转。飞轮当然还在转，飞轮的定义就是有一部分人被甩出去，剩下的部分继续转。

10 月裁掉约 1000 人，占员工总数 6%，2022 年以来最大的一次。伦敦办公室再削 2% 到 3%，其中包括 Anna Mallett——英国及 EMEA 本地语言内容制作的副总裁，2020 年 11 月入职，手上过的是《驯鹿宝贝》《混沌少年时》和《纸钞屋》后几季。美国那边，首席内容官 Bela Bajaria 和电影主管 Dan Lin 也在名单上，Bajaria 的位置由 Peter Rice 接。

Sarandos 在财报电话上说得很清楚：业务和飞轮都在正常工作，不需要根本性的战略转向，需要的是美国区「更好的执行」。这句话把裁员的性质讲透了。这不是收缩，是重新分配。美国区增长不够快，所以从别处调人调钱过去，伦敦和 EMEA 的本地语言制作就是被调走的那一头。所谓「其他地区指望这些片子成为本垒打，好在全球跑起来」，翻译过来是：你们负责做出能全球流通的内容，但你们自己不在优先序列里。

这里动的是结构层。不是哪个人干得不好，是资源分配的方向变了，而方向由总部定。Mallett 的履历——ITN 的 CEO、BBC Studios 的制作总经理——放在任何一家公司都是硬通货，照样在名单上。结构暴力从来不挑人，它挑位置。

再往下一层是文化层。Netflix 的整个叙事建立在「全球本地化」上：英国的故事、西班牙的故事、韩国的故事，都能被包装成全球产品。这个叙事让各地的内容生产者相信自己是中心，至少是节点。裁员把叙事拆开给你看：本地制作是原料供应地，总部是定价方。原料供应地可以换供应商，定价方不会换自己。

谁在共谋？财报电话上的分析师、把「执行」当作中性词的商业媒体、以及每一个把这次裁员读成「正常业务调整」的人。共谋的回报是股价和叙事稳定。代价由伦敦那 2% 到 3% 的人付，由被裁掉之后不会再被写进报道的那些岗位付。报道只点了 Mallett 的名字，通信和其他岗位的人没有名字，这是新闻业的常规操作，也是结构暴力的常规操作：只让你看见一个够分量的人，剩下的消失在百分比里。

飞轮还在转。转的是谁的轮子，谁在被甩出去，Sarandos 那句话里已经写好了。

Netflix says the flywheel is still working. Of course it is. A flywheel is defined by throwing some people off while the rest keeps spinning.

About 1,000 employees, 6 percent of the workforce, cut in mid-October — the largest round since 2022. London loses another 2 to 3 percent, including Anna Mallett, VP of physical production for the U.K. and EMEA local-language content, hired November 2020, with Baby Reindeer, Adolescence and later seasons of Money Heist on her record. In the U.S., chief content officer Bela Bajaria and film boss Dan Lin were also cut; Peter Rice takes Bajaria's seat.

Sarandos said it plainly on the earnings call: the business and the flywheel are working, no fundamental shift in strategy is needed, what's needed is better execution in the U.S. That sentence explains the layoffs. This is not contraction, it's reallocation. The U.S. isn't growing fast enough, so people and money get pulled from elsewhere — and London and EMEA local-language production are the elsewhere. The line about other territories counting on those titles to be home runs that travel the world translates to: you make content that circulates globally, but you are not yourself a priority.

This is structural. Nobody was bad at their job; the direction of resource allocation changed, and headquarters sets the direction. Mallett's résumé — CEO of ITN, group COO and managing director of production at BBC Studios — is hard currency anywhere, and she was still on the list. Structural violence doesn't pick people. It picks positions.

Below that sits the cultural layer. Netflix's whole story is built on global-local: a British story, a Spanish story, a Korean story, all packaged as global product. That story let producers everywhere believe they were the center, or at least a node. The layoffs take the story apart: local production is the raw-material supplier, headquarters is the pricing party. Suppliers can be swapped. The pricing party does not swap itself.

Who conspires? The analysts on the call, the trade press that treats execution as a neutral word, and anyone who reads this as normal business adjustment. The payoff is a stable share price and a stable narrative. The cost is paid by the 2 to 3 percent in London, and by the roles that stop existing once the story moves on. The report names Mallett; communications and other roles go unnamed. That is standard practice in journalism and in structural violence alike: show one person heavy enough to matter, let the rest dissolve into a percentage.

The flywheel still spins. Whose wheel it is, and who gets thrown off, is already written in Sarandos's sentence.
