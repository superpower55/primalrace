被剔除的厨房劳动力与“极简”的审美骗局The Invisible Kitchen Labour and the Scam of 'Simplicity'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
所谓的“极简烹饪”是建立在对底层劳动力隐形化的结构暴力之上。
So-called 'simple cooking' is a structural violence built upon the invisibility of bottom-tier labour.

一篇典型的 middle-class 审美 scam。这篇文章用“极简”(simplicity) 和“让食材发光”(allowing produce to shine) 这种充满灵性的词汇，把一场极其繁琐的体力劳动包装成了某种轻盈的艺术。这种叙事最阴险的地方在于：它在认知入口处通过“简单”这个词，完成了对实际劳动过程的 erase。

看这个 Risotto 的配方：熬制蔬菜汤、烤制番茄、持续 20 分钟不停搅拌、精准控制米粒的硬度。这在专业厨房里是需要极高专注度和体力投入的 repetitive labour。但当它被 Chris Shaw 这种 head chef 以“简单夏日食谱”的名义输出给大众时，那些在后厨负责洗菜、削皮、盯着火候、洗锅的底层员工被彻底剔除出了叙事。他们是这个“极简”结果背后的共谋者，但这种共谋是不对称的：主厨拿走了“创造力”的定价权，而底层员工承担了所有具体的、琐碎的 structural violence。

这种“极简”其实是一种阶级筛选的审美武器。它告诉读者，只要你拥有正确的食材（datterini tomatoes）和正确的技巧，生活就可以如此 an effortless。它掩盖了这样一个事实：真正的“极简”生活，永远需要一群被隐形的人在后台进行最复杂的劳动。当一个 head chef 在 Guardian 上谈论“minimal intervention”时，他干掉的不是复杂的烹饪步骤，而是那些在厨房里挥汗如雨的、没有名字的劳动力。

A typical middle-class aesthetic scam. This piece uses spiritualized terms like 'simplicity' and 'allowing produce to shine' to package grueling physical labour as a form of airy art. The most insidious part of this narrative is how it uses the word 'simple' at the cognitive entry point to erase the actual process of labour.

Look at the Risotto recipe: simmering stock, roasting tomatoes, 20 minutes of continuous stirring, and precise control over the grain's bite. In a professional kitchen, this is repetitive labour requiring immense focus and physical effort. But when Chris Shaw, a head chef, outputs this as a 'simple summer recipe,' the bottom-tier staff—those washing vegetables, peeling, watching the heat, and scrubbing pots—are completely erased from the narrative. They are the complicity behind this 'simple' result, but it is an asymmetric complicity: the head chef claims the pricing power of 'creativity,' while the staff bears all the concrete, tedious structural violence.

This 'simplicity' is actually an aesthetic weapon for class screening. It tells the reader that with the right ingredients (datterini tomatoes) and the right techniques, life can be effortless. It masks the fact that a truly 'simple' life always requires a group of invisible people performing the most complex labours in the background. When a head chef talks about 'minimal intervention' in The Guardian, he isn't removing complex cooking steps; he is removing the nameless labourers sweating in the kitchen.
