她替女人省下十年，也替自己赚了一笔She Gave Women a Decade. She Also Gave Herself a Position.

性别 结构层 · 文化层 ProPublica ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
黑框警告拆掉了，持股人的利益没拆
The black box came off; the shareholder's stake stayed on.

艾丽西亚·杰克逊站在印着 HHS 标志的讲台后面，说激素替代疗法对绝大多数女性安全有效，说这能给每个你爱的女人多出十年健康寿命。同一天，披露文件显示她还持有至少 11.5 万美元的 Evernow 股票，以及数量不明的期权。Evernow 就是那家把更年期女性对接给开激素的医生的平台，她是创始人兼前 CEO。

这不是巧合，是结构。联邦法律禁止官员“亲自且实质性地”参与可能影响自己财务的事项，连在发布会上说句话、发个新闻稿都算。她说了，也发了。前白宫伦理律师 Virginia Canter 的说法是，这把她放进了“潜在违规的区域”。

要看清这件事，得先看清更年期是什么。它不是一种病，是一种处境——是生物墙在这一段人生里最具体的一次显形：骨密度流失、潮热、失眠、心血管风险变化。而这段处境长期被当成不存在，被当成笑话，被当成“女人到了那个年纪”。黑框警告是 2002 年 WHI 研究之后贴上去的，二十年里把一代女性吓得不敢用药，也把一整块市场冻住了。冻住的市场就是等着被解冻的利润。

所以真正值得看的不是她有没有违规——ProPublica 自己都说，不清楚她在去掉警告这件事里起了什么作用，FDA 的专家小组在她进政府之前几个月就建议取消了。值得看的是，一个持有相关公司股份的人，被放在了那个讲台上，替这项政策做背书。政策的科学依据也许成立，但背书的人带着仓位。这就是共谋的经典形态：不需要她打电话施压，不需要她签一个字，只需要她站在那里，用政府官员的身份说“科学是清楚的”，而她的股票在账户里安静地等着。

更狠的一层是，这套叙事对女性是有吸引力的。二十年的污名确实该被质疑，更年期确实被医疗系统长期忽视，这些都是真的。正因为是真的，“为女性健康发声”才成了一个完美的认知入口——任何质疑都会被读成“你要让女人继续受苦吗”。武器化表达的厉害之处就在这里：它不需要说谎，它只需要挑一个真实的痛点，然后把自己嫁接上去。

再看她的其他持仓。Gameto，一家拿 ARPA-H 两年一千万美元资助做卵巢治疗的公司，她的股份从 1.5 万到 5 万涨到了 5 万到 10 万，而她的办公室正 overseeing 这个项目。她丈夫持有 Palantir 500 万到 2500 万美元的股票，Palantir 在她是 ARPA-H 局长时拿着该机构 1900 万美元的合同，六月又续了一年，最高 1370 万。她名下有 60 多个不向投资人披露持仓的风投和私募基金，其中至少 13 个由她丈夫联合创办的 137 Ventures 管理——而她从未在公开披露里写过丈夫在这家公司的职位。

这些数字摆在一起，问题就不是“她有没有违规”，而是“这套系统还剩下多少执行力”。特朗普政府砍掉了联邦伦理队伍，到 2025 年底少了 274 个全职伦理官员；特朗普任命的人去年漏交了将近 420 份法定的公开财务披露报告，是拜登政府最后一个完整年份的近四倍。杰克逊的两份披露，去年十月和两个月前各一份，至今未获认证。彭博拿到的豁免草案草稿列了 30 项投资，唯独没有 Evernow。

她被安排行政休假了。HHS 说会“在决定她身份是否变化之前评估事实”。注意这个语序：先评估，再决定。而报道里没写的是，谁在评估，评估的标准是什么，以及那个本该认证她披露表的伦理办公室，还剩下几个人。

最后一句留给那些名人天使投资人——格温妮丝·帕特洛、卡梅隆·迪亚兹、黛米·摩尔、德鲁·巴里摩尔。她们的名字印在 Evernow 的网站上，卖的是同一件东西：让更年期女性觉得自己被看见了。被看见是真的，被定价也是真的。

Alicia Jackson stood behind a lectern with the HHS logo on it and said hormone therapy is safe and effective for the vast majority of women, that this could add up to a decade of healthy years to the life of every woman you love. The same day, disclosure records showed she still holds at least $115,000 in Evernow stock and an unknown amount of options. Evernow is the platform that connects menopausal women with doctors who prescribe hormones. She founded it and ran it.

This is not a coincidence. It is a structure. Federal law bars officials from participating "personally and substantially" in matters that could affect their finances — including making statements at a press conference or in a news release. She did both. Virginia Canter, a former ethics lawyer at the White House, Treasury and the SEC, says it puts her "within the zone of a potential violation."

To see this clearly, you have to see what menopause is. Not a disease — a situation. The biological wall showing itself in its most concrete form: bone loss, hot flashes, insomnia, shifting cardiovascular risk. And that situation was treated for years as if it didn't exist, as a joke, as "women of a certain age." The black box warning went up after the 2002 WHI study and spent two decades scaring a generation of women off the treatment — and freezing a market. A frozen market is profit waiting to be thawed.

So the real question isn't whether she broke a rule. ProPublica itself says it's unclear what role, if any, she played in dropping the warning; the FDA's expert panel had recommended ending it months before she arrived. The real question is why someone with a stake in the relevant company was put on that stage to endorse the policy. The science may well hold up. The person endorsing it was holding a position.

This is the classic shape of complicity: no phone call needed, no signature required. Just her standing there, using the authority of government to say "the science is clear," while her stock sits quietly in the account.

And the sharper layer: this narrative is attractive to women. The twenty-year stigma deserves questioning. Menopause has been neglected by the medical system. All true. And because it's true, "speaking up for women's health" becomes a perfect cognitive entry point — any objection gets read as "so you want women to keep suffering?" That's how weaponized expression works. It doesn't need to lie. It just needs a real wound to graft itself onto.

Look at the rest of the portfolio. Gameto, a biotech company working through a two-year, $10 million ARPA-H award to create ovarian treatments, saw her stake grow from $15,001–$50,000 to $50,001–$100,000 while her office oversaw the project. Her husband holds between $5 million and $25 million in Palantir stock; Palantir had a $19 million contract from her agency when she became director, extended in June for up to $13.7 million. She owns more than 60 venture and private equity funds that don't disclose their holdings to investors, at least 13 of them managed by 137 Ventures, co-founded by her husband — whose role at the firm she never listed on her public disclosures.

Put those numbers together and the question stops being "did she violate something" and becomes "how much enforcement is left in this system." The Trump administration has cut the federal ethics workforce by 274 full-time officials by the end of 2025. Trump appointees failed to file nearly 420 legally mandated public financial disclosure reports last year — almost four times the Biden administration's last full year. Jackson's two disclosures, filed last October and two months ago, remain uncertified. The draft waiver Bloomberg obtained named 30 investments. Evernow was not among them.

She's been placed on administrative leave. HHS says it will "assess the facts before determining any change in her status." Note the order: assess first, decide later. What the reporting doesn't say is who is doing the assessing, by what standard, and how many people are left in the ethics office that was supposed to certify her forms.

One last note for the celebrity angel investors — Gwyneth Paltrow, Cameron Diaz, Demi Moore, Drew Barrymore — whose names are on Evernow's website. They're selling the same thing: making menopausal women feel seen. Being seen is real. Being priced is also real.
