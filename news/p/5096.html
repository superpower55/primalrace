用“爱”来掩盖的结构性抛弃Structural Abandonment Masked by the Narrative of 'Love'

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-18 § 链接
将制度崩塌归结为“缺乏爱”，是典型的用文化暴力掩盖结构暴力。
Reducing systemic collapse to a 'lack of love' is a textbook use of cultural violence to mask structural violence.

这封读者来信试图把一个严重的 structural violence 问题，通过叙事转换，降级为一个关于“承诺”和“爱”的文化议题。作者 John Burton 承认非法儿童之家（illegal care homes）的糟糕现状，但他的药方是要求工作人员拥有“不屈的承诺”和“爱”。

这是一个非常典型的 weaponized 叙事陷阱。当地方议会每年花费 1.25 亿英镑将弱势儿童安置在非法场所时，这已经不是个别员工是否“有爱”的问题，而是资源分配、监管失效和成本核算的系统性崩溃。在这种结构下，要求一线员工用个人情感去填补制度的黑洞，本质上是要求弱势者（包括低薪的护理员）通过自我剥削来维持一个病态系统的运转。

最危险的逻辑在于，他将“监管和检查”定义为阻碍，认为真正的家是由“人”而非“规则”创造的。这种叙事在很多领域被反复使用：在家庭暴力中，它叫“为了爱而忍耐”；在职场剥削中，它叫“对事业的激情”。当一个人被告知“爱”高于“规则”时，他实际上是被剥夺了通过规则寻求保护的权利。在这种“爱”的叙事下，任何虐待都可以被解释为“在尝试修复错误过程中产生的阵痛”，而缺乏监管的“爱”往往是直接暴力（direct violence）最好的掩体。

谁在共谋？那些试图通过强调“个人承诺”来减轻政府监管责任的决策者，以及习惯于将体制缺陷浪漫化为“人性缺失”的评论者。他们试图让我们相信，只要人们足够善良，非法且肮脏的收容所也能变成温馨的家。这不仅是 scam，更是对那些被制度抛弃的儿童最残忍的二次伤害。

This letter attempts to downgrade a severe case of structural violence into a cultural issue of 'commitment' and 'love.' While John Burton acknowledges the disgraceful state of illegal care homes, his prescription is for staff to possess 'unflinching commitment' and 'love.'

This is a classic weaponized narrative trap. When local councils spend £125m a year placing vulnerable children in illegal facilities, it is no longer a question of whether individual employees are 'loving'; it is a systemic collapse of resource allocation, regulatory failure, and cost-accounting. In such a structure, demanding that frontline workers fill institutional voids with personal emotion is essentially demanding that the precarious (including low-paid caregivers) sustain a pathological system through self-exploitation.

The most dangerous logic here is the framing of 'regulation and inspection' as hindrances, arguing that homes are created by 'people' rather than 'rules.' This narrative is weaponized across various domains: in domestic abuse, it is 'enduring for love'; in workplace exploitation, it is 'passion for the career.' When an individual is told that 'love' supersedes 'rules,' they are effectively stripped of their right to seek protection through those rules. Under this narrative of 'love,' any abuse can be rebranded as 'growing pains in the process of making things right,' and 'love' without regulation is often the perfect cover for direct violence.

Who are the complicit parties? The decision-makers who use 'personal commitment' to deflect regulatory accountability, and the commentators who romanticize systemic failure as a 'lack of humanity.' They want us to believe that as long as people are kind enough, illegal and filthy shelters can become loving homes. This is not just a scam; it is a cruel secondary trauma for children already abandoned by the system.
