Standing Up is a Political Act: The Pub and the Illusion of OrderStanding Up is a Political Act: The Pub and the Illusion of Order

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-07 § 链接
Order is often just the aesthetic mask for the erasure of spontaneous existence.
Order is often just the aesthetic mask for the erasure of spontaneous existence.

Westminster Council 想禁止在酒吧“站着喝酒” (vertical drinking)，理由是噪音和混乱。这在表面上是城市管理，本质上是一次典型的 structural violence：试图通过修改空间使用规则，将充满随机性的、底层的“液体社交” (liquid sociability) 驯化为一种可控的、安静的、像 wine bars 那样具有阶级区分度的秩序。当一个空间被要求必须“就坐”时，它实际上是在要求你进入一种被定义的角色——一个安静的、等待被服务的消费者，而不是一个拥有主体性的、在碰撞中确证存在的个体。

这种对“混乱”的恐惧，其实是对 meta violence 的维护。在男性中心叙事构建的城市规划中，规划者（通常是权力上位者）将人类视为破坏建筑美感的“杂质”。他们追求的是一种真空般的、静止的秩序感。而站着喝酒意味着什么？意味着打破预设的席位，意味着陌生人之间无需预约的碰撞，这意味着一个不被管理、不被定价的认知入口。这种 spontaneous 的表达，是底层和边缘群体在城市空间中最后的、低成本的存在性博弈。

Simon Jenkins 试图用“孤独”和“社区”来救赎酒吧，这太 naive 了。酒吧的价值不在于它是否能缓解孤独，而在于它提供了一种对抗“被规划生活”的可能性。当政府试图用 table service 取代 open bar，他们其实是在夺取解释权：他们定义了什么是“体面的”社交，从而把那些喧闹的、不体面的、真实的生命状态定义为“噪音”。

这场博弈的共谋者不仅是议会，还有那些追求“安静环境”的中产阶级。他们通过要求秩序，在潜意识中完成了对他者的排斥。所谓的“文明”和“理性”，在这里成了掩盖结构性剥夺的伪装。如果连站着喝酒的权利都被剥夺，那么我们在物理空间中剩下的，就只有被预设好的座位和被算法喂养的沉默。

Westminster Council's move against “vertical drinking” is framed as noise control, but it is a textbook case of structural violence. By mandating seating and table service, the council is attempting to domesticate the raw, unpredictable nature of “liquid sociability” into a controlled, sanitized order—the kind of curated silence found in high-end wine bars. When a space demands you sit, it is demanding you perform a specific role: a passive, predictable consumer rather than a subject asserting existence through spontaneous interaction.

This fear of “boisterousness” is an extension of meta violence. In the masculine-centric narrative of urban planning, humans are often treated as pollutants that spoil the architectural vision. The planners seek a vacuum of order. Vertical drinking, however, represents a rupture in this control. It allows for the collision of strangers without the mediation of a booking or a price tag. It is one of the last low-cost cognitive entries for the marginalized to engage in an existential game within the city.

Simon Jenkins attempts to save the pub by invoking “loneliness” and “community,” which is far too naive. The value of the pub lies not in its therapeutic potential, but in its capacity to resist a programmed life. By replacing the open bar with table service, the state seizes the power of definition: they define what constitutes “decent” socialization, thereby rebranding authentic, messy life as mere “noise.”

The complicity here extends beyond the council to the middle class who crave “quiet.” Through their demand for order, they subconsciously execute the exclusion of the Other. “Civilization” and “rationality” are merely masks for structural deprivation. If the right to stand and drink is erased, all that remains of our physical existence are pre-assigned seats and the silence fed to us by algorithms.
