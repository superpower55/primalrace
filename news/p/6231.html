灾难没有署名，代价有Disaster Has No Signature. The Cost Does.

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要玻利维亚总统罗德里戈·帕斯于周六签署法令，宣布全国进入紧急状态，以应对厄尔尼诺现象带来的影响。干旱已波及该国9个省中的6个，影响7.8万个家庭，并造成超过7.5万头牛死亡。厄尔尼诺预计将于2026年底达到顶峰。
The newsBolivian President Rodrigo Paz signed a decree on Saturday declaring a nationwide state of emergency over the effects of El Nino. Drought is affecting 78,000 families in six of Bolivia's nine departments and has caused the loss of more than 75,000 cattle. El Nino is due to peak in late 2026.
先救牛还是先救人，才是紧急状态的真正内容
Who gets saved first—cattle or families—is what the emergency decree actually decides.

一条全国紧急状态令，读起来像天灾，实际上是资源分配表。玻利维亚宣布进入紧急状态，为的是腾出资源应对厄尔尼诺；报道里给出的数字是：干旱影响九个省中六个省的七万八千个家庭，超过七万五千头牛死亡（来源：Al Jazeera）。总统帕斯签令、呼吁各机构合作、增派救援队——这些都是真的，但真正决定谁先被救的，是那七万八千个家庭和七万五千头牛在预算表上谁的名字排前面。

厄尔尼诺是自然周期，加尔通那个公式在这里要拆开看：Potential 是不发生这场灾害时可达到的状态，Actual 是现在。差额是暴力，但这个暴力不落在天上，落在谁有井、谁有卡车、谁的牧场有保险。报道说干旱影响的是家庭，损失的是牛；牛有价格，家庭没有。这就是结构层在动——灾害本身是自然的，损失怎么分配、救济先到哪，是制度的选择。

再往下一层，文化暴力负责让这一切看起来像天意。"全球气候现象""自然天气周期""有记录以来最强"——这些词都是准确的，也都在把人的决策藏起来。科学家预测这场厄尔尼诺可能造成全球超过45万人死亡，最贫穷的国家受冲击最重（来源：Al Jazeera）。最穷的国家受冲击最重，这不是气象学结论，这是政治经济学结论。谁排放、谁买单，中间那道差额从来不是均匀分布的。

还有一层没人提：七万八千个家庭里，谁在取水、谁在照顾牲畜、谁在洪水来了之后负责把孩子抱上高处。灾害报道通常不写这个，因为写了就得承认，紧急状态里的"救援"和"安置"，大部分劳动是女人在做，而名单和补偿通常登记在男人名下。报道没写，我不替它编；但报道没写这件事本身，就是材料。

紧急状态令解决的是资源能不能动，不是资源往哪动。牛死了七万五千头，会有赔偿方案；家庭受旱七万八千户，会有一句"需要各机构合作"。这两句话之间的距离，就是这条新闻里真正的暴力，而且它不在天气里。

A national state of emergency reads like a natural disaster. It is a resource allocation table. Bolivia declared one to free up resources for El Nino; the numbers given are 78,000 families hit by drought across six of nine departments, and more than 75,000 cattle lost (source: Al Jazeera). President Paz signed the decree, called for institutional cooperation, ordered more rescue teams. All true. What actually decides who gets rescued first is whether those 78,000 families or those 75,000 cattle sit higher on the budget line.

El Nino is a natural cycle, and Galtung's formula has to be taken apart here: the Potential is the state that would exist without this disaster, the Actual is now. The gap is violence, but it doesn't fall from the sky. It falls on who has a well, who has a truck, whose ranch has insurance. The report says drought hits families and kills cattle. Cattle have a price. Families don't. This is the structural layer moving—the hazard is natural, but how losses are distributed and where relief arrives first is an institutional choice.

One layer down, cultural violence makes it all look like fate. "Global climate phenomenon," "natural weather cycle," "possibly the strongest on record"—accurate words, every one, and every one hiding the human decisions. Scientists predict this El Nino could cause more than 450,000 deaths globally, with the poorest countries hit hardest (source: Al Jazeera). The poorest countries hit hardest is not a meteorological finding. It's a political-economic one. Who emits and who pays—that gap was never distributed evenly.

And a layer nobody mentions: of those 78,000 families, who fetches the water, who tends the livestock, who carries the children to higher ground when the flood comes. Disaster reporting usually skips this, because writing it down means admitting that most of the labor inside "rescue" and "resettlement" is women's, while the lists and the compensation usually carry men's names. The report doesn't say it, and I won't invent it for them. But the fact that the report doesn't say it is itself material.

The emergency decree answers whether resources can move. Not where they move. Seventy-five thousand dead cattle will get a compensation scheme. Seventy-eight thousand drought-hit families will get a sentence about institutional cooperation. The distance between those two sentences is the real violence in this story, and it isn't in the weather.
