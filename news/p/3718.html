一次关于“一次性”的权力共谋A Conspiracy of 'Disposability'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-12 § 链接
用局部禁令掩盖结构性危机，是典型的政治PR套路。
Using local bans to mask structural crises is a classic political PR scam.

这是一场典型的关于“认知入口”的博弈。政府试图把野火的罪名全部扣在“一次性烧烤炉”这个具体的、可量化的客体上。在加尔通的暴力三角里，这叫用 cultural layer 的叙事去掩盖 structural layer 的暴力。把野火定义为“不负责任的烧烤者”造成的意外，就成功地把讨论从“气候危机”这个结构性绝望，转移到了“个人行为管理”这个可操作的行政细节上。

Disposable barbecues 确实是直接的 trigger，但真正的暴力是 Potential − Actual 的差额：一个被化石燃料推向极端高温的生态系统，其易燃性（Potential）被无限放大，而政府在气候治理上的实际行动（Actual）几乎为零。这个巨大的差额，就是结构性暴力。禁掉几个烧烤炉，就像是在一座即将坍塌的大厦里禁止抽烟，然后宣布自己采取了“严厉的防火措施”。

更有趣的是政府内部的共谋 (complicity)。Burnham 考虑禁令，而部长们反对 blanket ban，转而推行“产品安全规则”和“局部禁令”。这种拉锯战是完美的政治表演：一方面给公众一个“我们在关注”的姿态，另一方面确保不触动工业资本的利益。他们并不在乎那些被烧掉的 nightjars 巢穴，他们在乎的是如何用一个“部分限制”的 legislation 方案，在不激怒商业共谋者的前提下，完成一次廉价的危机公关。

Greenpeace 试图点破这个 scam，但在这个男性中心叙事的政治场域里，这种声音通常被标记为“激进”而遭到稀释。真正的最优解应该是终结对化石燃料的依赖，但那意味着权力结构的重组，这绝不是这群政客想要的结果。

This is a textbook struggle over the cognitive entry point. The government is attempting to pin the blame for wildfires entirely on the 'disposable barbecue'—a concrete, quantifiable object. In Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is using the cultural layer to camouflage structural violence. By defining wildfires as accidents caused by 'irresponsible grillers,' they successfully shift the discourse from the structural despair of the climate crisis to the administrative detail of 'individual behavior management.'

Disposable barbecues are indeed the direct trigger, but the real violence lies in the gap between Potential and Actual: an ecosystem pushed to extreme temperatures by fossil fuels has its flammability (Potential) infinitely amplified, while the government's actual action (Actual) in climate governance remains near zero. This massive gap is structural violence. Banning a few grills is like banning smoking in a building that is already collapsing, then declaring that 'strict fire safety measures' have been implemented.

Even more interesting is the internal complicity within the government. While Burnham considers a ban, ministers oppose a blanket ban, opting instead for 'product safety rules' and 'local bans.' This tug-of-war is a perfect political performance: it gives the public the impression of 'concern' while ensuring that the interests of industrial capital are not disturbed. They don't care about the destroyed nightjar nests; they care about how to use a 'partial restriction' in legislation to execute a cheap PR exercise without alienating their commercial co-conspirators.

Greenpeace attempts to expose this scam, but in a political arena dominated by a masculine-centric narrative, such voices are usually labeled 'radical' and diluted. The true optimal expression would be ending the reliance on fossil fuels, but that would require a restructuring of power—something these politicians absolutely do not want.
