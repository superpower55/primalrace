育儿账单：一场关于“工作价值”的结构性诈骗The Childcare Bill: A Structural Scam of 'Work Value'

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-25 § 链接
育儿成本的攀升不是经济问题，而是对女性主体性的结构性剥夺。
Rising childcare costs are not an economic issue, but a structural deprivation of female agency.

英国课后托管费用的上涨，在新闻叙事里被包装成一个“预算压力”或“生活成本危机”的经济话题。但如果套用加尔通的暴力三角，这就是典型的 structural violence。当课后托管费用年均突破 2800 英镑，且伴随各种课外活动、校服、电子设备的隐形摊销时，系统实际上在向父母——尤其是母亲——发出一个明确的信号：你的劳动价值在结构面前是廉价的。

新闻中提到的那个 civil servant Anna 及其丈夫的“协商”和“轮班”，被描述成一种应对压力的方法，但这正是存在性战争中的一种被动博弈。在这种博弈中，女性往往是那个被要求做出“最优解表达”的人。当工作收入被高昂的 childcare costs 抵消，女性在面对“是否值得工作”的计算时，很容易被推向那个由父权结构预设的答案：回归家庭。这根本不是自愿的选择，而是一次精准的计算陷阱。系统通过提高公共服务的准入门槛，将女性从公共空间（Public Space）驱逐回私人空间（Private Space）。

这种机制最阴险的地方在于，它利用了“母职”的文化叙事（Cultural Violence）来掩盖结构性的掠夺。它让女性在焦虑中自我审查，认为自己是因为“照顾孩子”而选择放弃事业，而忽略了是制度性地让“全职工作”与“养育孩子”变得互斥。这是一种典型的共谋：政府通过缩减公共资源，市场通过抬高定价权，共同完成了对女性职业生涯的定向爆破。

所谓的“高薪家庭”在账单面前依然感到空虚，说明这套剥削逻辑已经覆盖了阶级。只要养育成本依然被定义为“个人的家庭责任”而非“社会的公共基础设施”，那么每一次账单的上涨，都是在为元暴力的男性中心叙事添砖加瓦——它再次确认了：女性的价值，最终应被锚定在那个被私有化的子宫和厨房里。

The rise in UK after-school care costs is framed as a 'budget pressure' or 'cost-of-living crisis'. But applying Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is textbook structural violence. When childcare bills exceed £2,800 per year, coupled with the invisible attrition of extracurriculars and uniforms, the system sends a clear signal to parents—specifically mothers: your labor value is cheap in the eyes of the structure.

The 'negotiation' and 'shift-swapping' between Anna and her husband are described as coping mechanisms, but they are actually passive gambles in an existential war. In this game, women are the ones pressured to find the 'optimal expression'. When income is neutralized by childcare costs, women are pushed toward a pre-set patriarchal answer: return to the home. This is not a choice; it is a calculated trap. The system uses the high barrier of public services to evict women from the Public Space back into the Private Space.

The most insidious part is how this uses the narrative of 'motherhood' (cultural violence) to mask structural plunder. It forces women into self-censorship, making them believe they 'choose' to leave their careers for their children, while ignoring that the system has made 'full-time work' and 'parenting' mutually exclusive. This is a clear complicity: the state reduces public resources, and the market hikes pricing power, together executing a targeted demolition of female careers.

Even high-earning families feel the void, proving this logic transcends class. As long as childcare is defined as a 'private family responsibility' rather than 'public infrastructure', every price hike reinforces the masculine-centric narrative of meta-violence: confirming that a woman's value is ultimately anchored in a privatized womb and kitchen.
