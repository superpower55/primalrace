断网是账单，不是事故The Outage Was an Invoice

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The New Republic ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要本月，Starlink 在乌克兰的连接突然中断，当时俄罗斯正以导弹密集袭击基辅，造成至少九人死亡。马斯克先归咎于美国军方和特朗普，后承认是公司放缓了服务，称乌克兰违反条款用网络攻击俄罗斯。《大西洋月刊》报道，美方官员询问断网一事时，Starlink 建议升级合约以恢复乌克兰的接入。
The newsThis month Starlink's connection in Ukraine suddenly dropped as Russia pounded Kyiv with missiles, killing at least nine people. Musk first blamed the U.S. military and Trump, then admitted his company slowed the service, claiming Ukraine violated its terms by using it to attack Russia. U.S. officials told The Atlantic that Starlink advised switching to an upgraded contract to restore access.
谁握着开关，谁就握着主权
Whoever holds the switch holds the sovereignty.

断网不是故障，是账单。乌克兰挨导弹的时候，Starlink 把插头拔了；美方来问，回答是升级套餐就恢复。一门生意做到能决定一场战争里谁被炸死，它就已经不是生意了。

马斯克先说是美国军方和特朗普的决定，特朗普不接，最后他自己认了，理由是乌克兰用网络攻击俄罗斯、违反了服务条款。这个顺序本身就值得看：先推给国家，再推给对手，最后承认是自己，每一步都在试探哪一版叙事能站住。等《大西洋月刊》的报道出来，版本又换了——不是惩罚，是推销。

这里动的是结构层。一家私人公司垄断了战场的通信，于是「谁出钱、出多少」直接换算成「谁的防空预警能响、谁的平民能提前躲」。加尔通的公式在这里算得清清楚楚：本可以达到的安全，被一份合约的差价削掉了，差额就是死掉的那九个人。

同一周的另一条线更说明问题：特朗普和普京谈成柴油生意，给俄罗斯续了经济命脉；泽连斯基公开反对，换来的是连续几天被威胁切断情报共享和武器交付。一头是给对手输血，一头是拿援助当缰绳，中间夹着一个被要求升级套餐的乌克兰。这不是失控，这是定价。

把「保护」做成订阅制，是第三章那套东西最赤裸的版本：认知入口不需要篡改事实，只要掌握开关，就能让事实按付款进度发生。自由世界、规则秩序这些词，在一条「请升级合约」的回复面前，一个字都不剩。

至于马斯克说的「服务条款」，条款是他写的，解释权在他手里，什么时候执行也由他定。这不是规则，这是元暴力的私人版：解释权换了个持有者，从国家换成了一个亿万富翁，运作方式一模一样。

还有一层没人问：那九个人死在基辅的住宅楼里，报道里没有他们的名字，也没有人向他们解释过什么叫做「升级合约」。账单寄给了付不起的人。

The outage wasn't a glitch. It was an invoice. Starlink pulled the plug while missiles were falling on Kyiv, and when Washington asked about it, the answer was: upgrade the contract and service comes back. A business that can decide who gets bombed in a war has stopped being a business.

Musk blamed the U.S. military and Trump first. Trump declined to own it. Then Musk admitted it himself, on the grounds that Ukraine violated the terms of service by using the connection to attack Russia. Watch the sequence: blame the state, blame the rival, then confess, testing which version of the story holds. When The Atlantic's reporting surfaced, the story changed again — not punishment, a sales pitch.

This is structural violence. One private company monopolizes the battlefield's communications, so who pays and how much converts directly into whose air-raid warning sounds and whose civilians get to hide in time. Galtung's formula does the arithmetic: the safety that was attainable, minus the safety delivered, equals the gap — and the gap is nine dead.

The other thread that week makes it plainer. Trump brokered a diesel deal with Putin, handing Russia an economic lifeline to keep fighting. Zelensky objected publicly and got a multiday campaign of threats to cut intelligence sharing and weapons deliveries. One hand feeds the adversary, the other holds aid as a leash, and in between sits a country being told to upgrade its subscription.

Turning "protection" into a subscription is the crudest version of the weaponization playbook: you don't need to rewrite the facts if you own the switch. You just make the facts arrive on a payment schedule. "Free world," "rules-based order" — against one reply reading "switch to an upgraded contract," not a word of it survives.

As for the terms of service Musk cites: he wrote them, he interprets them, he decides when to enforce them. That isn't a rule. It's meta-violence in private hands — the monopoly on interpretation moved from the state to a billionaire, and it operates exactly the same way.

One thing nobody asked: the nine people who died in that residential building have no names in this story, and nobody ever explained an "upgraded contract" to them. The bill went to the people who couldn't pay it.
