玩家被迫开枪的那一刻The Moment the Player Is Made to Pull the Trigger

文娱 文化层 · 直接层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要《星球大战 Zero Company》游戏重现了66号指令场景，玩家需操控克隆人士兵Trick攻击包括绝地学徒Tel-Rea在内的盟友。Bit Reactor创意总监Greg Foertsch表示，团队希望从非绝地、非克隆人的视角展现这一事件。该游戏现已登陆PS5、PC和Xbox Series X。
The newsStar Wars Zero Company features its own Order 66 moment, where players control clone trooper Trick in attacking allies including Jedi Padawan Tel-Rea. Creative director Greg Foertsch said Bit Reactor aimed to show the event from a fresh perspective. The game is out now on PS5, PC, and Xbox Series X.
让你亲手扣扳机，是把暴力从剧情变成共谋
Forcing the player to fire turns violence from plot into complicity.

《星球大战：零号连队》做对了一件事：它不给玩家看那段克隆人屠杀绝地的蒙太奇，它让玩家握住扳机。

报道里写得很清楚，玩家被迫操控 Trick 去执行攻击，目标里有队友、有那个可能还活着的学徒 Tel-Rea。这是整篇采访里唯一真正有分量的设计。其余的一切——Hawks 与 Bennic 的友谊、Runa 守着全息桌、演员说这是「克隆人的心碎」——都是包装。核心是把选择权从角色手里拿走，塞进玩家的手柄里。

这个机制值得说，是因为它和暴力三角的关系是反的。通常的叙事暴力是文化层的：让观众在安全距离外消费一场屠杀，哭一下，然后继续。而这里，文化层的背书被拆掉了，玩家被放到直接暴力的执行位上。你不能再对自己说「那是帕尔帕廷干的」，因为按下去的是你。

但别急着给这个设计颁奖。游戏让你「被迫」——被迫这两个字救了一切，也毁了一切。你被按着头开完这一枪，然后系统告诉你，这是命运，这是正传，这是星战宇宙的一部分。玩家的不适感被转化成了对作品的赞美：「太有冲击力了。」冲击力成了卖点，共谋成了体验。

采访里 Foertsch 说，他们想从「不同的视角」展示这个事件，想展示普通人怎么被全息投影里那个新皇帝搞懵。这个视角听着新鲜，其实是把元暴力藏得更深了一层：帝国的暴行被处理成一场「大家都身不由己」的群像戏，每个人都在自己的岗位上做自己该做的事，Trick 执行命令，Bennic 继续为帝国工作，Hawks 选择留下。没有人是坏人，所有人都是系统里的齿轮。这恰好就是共谋者理论最舒服的版本——暴力不需要施暴者，只需要「职责」。

Baker 说，这可能是星战里最重要的时刻，因为它展示了民主怎么垮掉、暴政怎么接管。他说得对。但《零号连队》没有展示暴政怎么接管，它展示的是暴政接管之后，你怎么在任务简报里接受它。

报道没写的是：Tel-Rea 到底死没死，取决于玩家之前的选择。这个「如果她还活着」的括号，才是整个设计里最诚实的地方。星战宇宙里，绝地的命是剧本定的；游戏里，她的命是玩家的操作定的。暴力的差额，就藏在这个括号里。

Star Wars Zero Company gets one thing right: it doesn't show the player the montage of clone troopers gunning down Jedi. It puts the trigger in the player's hand.

The report is explicit — players are forced to control Trick in actually carrying out the attack, and the targets include squadmates, including the Padawan Tel-Rea, if she's still alive. That's the only part of the interview with real weight. Everything else — Hawks and Bennic's friendship, Runa at the holotable, the actors calling it "the heartbreak of the clones" — is packaging. The point is that the choice is taken away from the character and handed to the player.

This mechanic matters because it inverts the Violence Triangle. Narrative violence usually operates at the cultural layer: let the audience consume a massacre from a safe distance, cry a little, move on. Here the cultural alibi is stripped away and the player is placed in the execution seat of direct violence. You can't tell yourself Palpatine did it, because your thumb did.

But don't hand out awards yet. The game makes you "forced" — that word saves everything and ruins everything. You're held down and made to take the shot, and then the system tells you this is fate, this is canon, this is part of the Star Wars universe. The player's discomfort gets converted into praise for the work: "so impactful." Impact becomes the selling point. Complicity becomes the experience.

In the interview, Foertsch says they wanted to show the event from "a different lens," to show ordinary people bewildered by the sudden hologram of their new emperor. That lens sounds fresh, but it buries the meta-violence one layer deeper: the Empire's atrocities get processed as an ensemble piece where everyone is just doing their job. Trick follows orders, Bennic keeps working for the Empire, Hawks chooses to stay. Nobody is the villain; everyone is a cog. This is the co-conspirator theory at its most comfortable — violence needs no perpetrator, only "duty."

Baker says this may be the most important moment in Star Wars because it shows how democracy falls and how tyranny takes over. He's right. But Zero Company doesn't show how tyranny takes over. It shows how, after tyranny has taken over, you accept it in a mission briefing.

What the report doesn't say: whether Tel-Rea lives or dies depends on the player's earlier choices. That parenthetical — "if she's still alive" — is the most honest thing in the whole design. In the Star Wars universe, a Jedi's life is decided by the script. In the game, her life is decided by the player's inputs. The gap in violence lives inside that parenthesis.
