炸弹拆除队的“专业精神”与被掩盖的战争残余The 'Professionalism' of Bomb Squads and the Erasure of War Residue

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-15 § 链接
将战争废料的日常化包装成警务英雄主义，是典型的叙事武器化。
Packaging the banality of war debris as police heroism is a classic weaponisation of narrative.

一个德州居民在二手拍卖会上买到了二战时期的手榴弹，这在新闻里被写成了一个“温馨提醒”的公共安全事件。但剥开这层叙事，这其实是一次极其典型的 cultural violence：它通过赞美拆弹小组的“专业精神”、“稳健神经”和“罕见的专业能力”，将关注点从“战争废料如何如此随意地在民间流通”转移到了“国家暴力机器如何高效地清理现场”。

这种叙事逻辑非常熟悉——它在制造一种认知入口，让你觉得只要有警察和 EOD（爆炸物处理组）在，世界就是安全的。但事实上，这些 Mk 2 碎片手榴弹是杀人机器，是工业化战争的残余。它们在 80 年后依然“活跃”且被随意打包出售，这揭示了 structural violence 的某种荒诞：战争的直接暴力在停止后，其物理残留物变成了某种生活方式（estate sale）中的随机彩蛋。

警察协会的 post 实际上是在 perform 一场关于“秩序”的表演。他们强调“没有犯罪”，试图迅速关闭法律讨论，转而通过赞美自身的 professional 属性来获取社会认同。这种共谋——媒体报道、警方自夸、公众庆幸——共同完成了一次对战争残酷性的稀释。我们不应该只感谢那些稳健的神经，而应该追问：什么样的系统允许杀伤性武器在私人交易市场中像旧书一样被买卖？

当我们将注意力全部投向拆弹队的“英勇”时，我们实际上在认同一种元暴力：即认为安全是由一个强大的、垄断了暴力解释权的机构赋予的，而不是由一个没有战争废料的社会结构保障的。

A Texas resident buying WWII grenades at an estate sale is framed as a 'helpful reminder' for public safety. But strip away the narrative, and this is a textbook case of cultural violence: by praising the bomb squad's 'steady nerves' and 'professionalism,' the focus shifts from the systemic failure of war residue leaking into civilian life to the efficiency of the state's violence apparatus in cleaning up the mess.

This logic is a familiar weaponised expression—creating a cognitive entry point that suggests the world is safe as long as the police and EOD are present. In reality, these Mk 2 fragmentation grenades are killing machines. The fact that they remain 'live' and are casually bundled for sale 80 years later reveals a structural violence: the direct violence of war has evolved into a banal, random 'easter egg' in a middle-class estate sale.

The police association's post is a performance of 'Order.' By declaring 'no crime here,' they shut down legal scrutiny and instead seek social validation through the performance of professionalism. This complicity—between the media's reporting, the police's self-praise, and the public's relief—dilutes the horror of war. We shouldn't just thank the steady nerves; we should ask why a system allows lethal weaponry to be traded like old books in a private market.

When we focus entirely on the 'heroism' of the squad, we succumb to meta-violence: the belief that safety is a gift granted by a powerful agency that monopolises the interpretation of violence, rather than a guarantee provided by a social structure free of war debris.
