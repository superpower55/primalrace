六位数年薪，买的是解释权Six Figures for the Right to Explain

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 semafor.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
AI 数据中心请的不是公关，是叙事雇佣兵。
Data centers finally create jobs: narrative mercenaries, paid by the company.

数据中心终于创造了就业。它创造的岗位叫"社区关系负责人"，六位数年薪，工作内容是让本地人相信那些正在改变他们水、电、地价的机器是好事。

这不是新闻业意义上的招聘信息，这是第三章的原文在招人。微软要的是"主动讲故事"(proactive storytelling)和主持市政厅的能力；谷歌要的是"接到通知即刻出发，前往有争议的市政听证会、规划委员会和社区大会，反击不实信息"。反击不实信息。谁来定义什么是不实信息？出钱的一方。这就是认知入口的招标公告，只不过这次写明了薪水。

Anthropic 给这个岗位开出的底薪是 26.5 万美元，条件是有一半时间在路上，去"越来越不友善的辖区"。不友善，是因为那些地方的人正在为电价、水源、噪音、土地付账。报道里这些代价一个字都没有，只有"本地关切"和"不实信息"两个词把它们打包封存。付账的人没有出现在招聘启事里，他们的反对被预先命名为需要被反击的东西。

注意这条新闻自己是怎么写的："谁说数据中心不创造就业？"——用一个反问把结构问题偷换成就业问题。真正的问题从来不是数据中心有没有工作机会，而是这笔投资的收益归谁、成本落在谁头上、以及谁有权决定。招聘启事回答了第三个问题：出钱的人。

加尔通的三角在这里是三层同时动。结构层是资源分配：电、水、土地从社区流向算力，回报流向股东。文化层是"主动讲故事"，把这场转移包装成地方发展。元层最赤裸：这些岗位的职责就是垄断解释权，把本地人的经验重新命名为 misinformaton，然后由拿六位数的人替他们更正。

所以这份工作市场是真实的，它只是不生产别的东西——它生产同意。

Data centers finally create jobs. The job is called community relations lead, pays six figures, and consists of persuading locals that the machines changing their water, power, and land prices are good for them.

This is not a hiring story. This is Chapter Three running a recruitment ad. Microsoft wants "proactive storytelling" and the ability to run town halls. Google wants people ready to "deploy on short notice to contested municipal hearings, planning commissions, and community town halls to counter misinformation." Counter misinformation. Who defines misinformation? The party paying. This is a tender notice for a cognitive entry point, with the salary printed on it.

Anthropic's floor is $265,000 for half a year on the road, into "increasingly hostile territory." Hostile because the people there are paying for electricity, water, noise, land. The report gives those costs no space at all. It gives them two words instead: local concerns, misinformation. The people footing the bill never appear in the job posting; their objections are pre-labeled as the thing to be countered.

Notice how the story frames itself: who says data centers don't create jobs? A rhetorical question that swaps a structural problem for an employment one. The question was never whether there are jobs. It is who captures the return, who absorbs the cost, and who gets to decide. The job ad answers the third: whoever pays.

All three layers of the Violence Triangle move at once. Structural: power, water, and land flow from the community into compute, and the returns flow to shareholders. Cultural: "proactive storytelling" repackages the transfer as local development. Meta, most nakedly: the job itself is to monopolize interpretation, to rename residents' experience as misinformation and have a six-figure employee correct it for them.

So the job market is real. It just doesn't produce anything except consent.
