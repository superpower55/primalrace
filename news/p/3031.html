所谓的“务实”不过是为共谋寻找的语义掩体Pragmatism as a Semantic Shield for Complicity

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-30 § 链接
Pragmatism is the linguistic camouflage used to legitimize structural violence.
Pragmatism is the linguistic camouflage used to legitimize structural violence.

当政治家开始使用“务实” (pragmatic) 这个词时，通常意味着他们准备在既定利益结构中进行一次低成本的背叛。Andy Burnham 在与 Trump 的通话中暗示将“开放”北海石油，随后在面对公众时将其包装为“务实地看待资源”。这套话术的本质是典型的文化暴力：通过重新定义词义，将对气候崩溃的加速（即对全球原初种族的进一步掠夺）伪装成一种理性的、关怀民众疾苦的行政选择。

这里的博弈逻辑非常简单。Trump 代表的是典型的 masculine-centric 权力逻辑——将自然资源视为可被榨取的客体，将国家财富等同于资源开采的规模。Burnham 的“务实”实际上是在向这种权力逻辑递交投名状。他试图在“选举承诺”与“资本/强权压力”之间寻找一个假.最优解表达：通过引入“tiebacks”（关联井）这种技术细节来钻法律漏洞，试图在不正式撕毁 manifesto 的情况下，完成对化石燃料资本的共谋。

最讽刺的共谋在于，这种“务实”的讨论发生在英格兰半数地区处于干旱、野火肆虐的同一天。结构性暴力 (structural violence) 就在这里闭环了：能源巨头通过文化层面的“务实”叙事获得开采权，导致气候灾难加剧（直接暴力），而政治家则通过这种共谋在权力博弈中换取短期的经济数据或大国关系红利。在这种叙事里，被牺牲掉的永远是那些没有定价权的未来世代和生态脆弱群体。

所谓的“资源”不是救星，而是被武器化的认知入口。当一个领导者需要通过向另一个领导者承诺“开放资源”来证明自己是个“gentleman”时，这种所谓的务实，本质上是对主体性的死亡，是对全球生存权的集体性出卖。

When politicians start using the word "pragmatic," it usually means they are preparing for a low-cost betrayal within the existing interest structure. Andy Burnham’s hint to Trump about "opening up" North Sea oil, subsequently rebranded as a "pragmatic approach to resources," is a textbook example of cultural violence. It is the art of redefining terms to disguise the acceleration of climate collapse—a further plunder of the Primal Race—as a rational administrative choice driven by compassion for the struggling public.

The game logic here is simplistic. Trump represents the quintessential masculine-centric power logic: viewing natural resources as objects to be extracted and equating national wealth with the scale of exploitation. Burnham’s "pragmatism" is effectively a pledge of allegiance to this power structure. He is searching for a fake optimal expression between his election manifesto and the pressure of capital and hegemony, attempting to use technicalities like "tiebacks" to exploit legal loopholes and complete his complicity with fossil fuel capital without formally tearing up his promises.

The most cynical part of this complicity is that this "pragmatic" dialogue occurred on the very day half of England was declared to be in drought and wildfires raged. This is where structural violence closes its loop: energy giants use the "pragmatic" narrative of cultural violence to secure drilling rights, which accelerates climate disaster (direct violence), while politicians reap short-term economic data or diplomatic favors from this complicity. In this narrative, those sacrificed are always the future generations and ecologically vulnerable populations who possess no pricing power.

These "resources" are not saviors; they are weaponized cognitive entries. When a leader feels the need to promise "opening up resources" to another leader just to be recognized as a "gentleman," this so-called pragmatism is, in essence, the death of subjectivity and a collective betrayal of global survival rights.
