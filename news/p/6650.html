国王的团结，没人问谁在买单The King's Unity, and No One Asks Who Pays

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
把袭击者划出"我们"，剩下的"我们"就不用被追问。
Draw the attackers outside "us," and "us" never has to be questioned.

卡拉克的教堂死了九个人，伤了几十个。国王阿卜杜拉二世在议会开幕时说的是："他们不属于我们"。

这句话的功能不是谴责，是划线。线一划，死掉的九个人就成了"我们"的损失，开枪的人就成了"他们"的问题，而"我们"内部——谁让这些人变成"他们"的、谁在南部省份的经济里抽走了可能性、谁在清真寺和教堂之间维持着那套随时会崩的平衡——全都不用被问。加尔通那套算法在这里很好用：九条命是 direct 层的差额，但差额之所以产生，是因为结构层早就把一些人推到了只剩暴力这一种表达的位置上。国王只处理了 direct 层，然后宣布结构层不存在。

"以铁拳打击"是同一句话的延长线。铁拳落下去的对象永远是已经掉到线外的人，而铁拳从来不问线是谁画的。军队和安全部门被点名了，国家团结和公众意识被点名了，唯独没有被点名的是：这个国家一半以上的人口是巴勒斯坦裔，他们住在难民营里，拿的是有限的工作许可，看着约旦河西岸每天发生的事。国王在同一篇讲话里说以色列的扩张主义是地区最大的威胁——这句话是真的，也是安全的。因为它把威胁指向了国境之外，于是国境之内的那套安排就可以继续被称作"团结"。

水的那一段值得单独看。"我们不会允许约旦人的口渴被任何一方挟持"——这句话没有主语。谁在挟持？报道说这反映了对以色列项目的担忧，但国王没说名字。一个君主在议会里说"任何一方"，等于什么都没说；而约旦的水确实在少，少的水要分配给谁、哪个地区先停水、哪座难民营的管道最后修，这些才是结构暴力真正落地的地方，讲话里一个字都没有。

最后那段话最漂亮："法律不认识名字、地位和影响力。"紧接着是"你们的意见分歧是一种力量，只要目标是约旦的利益"。两句话放在一起，意思很清楚——分歧可以存在，前提是它不指向利益本身。议会可以辩论，只要辩论的结果是确认国王划的那条线。这就是元暴力最干净的形态：不是禁止说话，是规定说话必须在"我们"之内进行，而"我们"的定义权不在说话的人手里。

九个人的葬礼已经办完了。亲属坐在议会里听了一分钟默哀。默哀之后，那九个人变成了"国家韧性"的注脚，而让卡拉克的年轻人除了拿起枪之外还有别的表达方式的那些事——工作、水、在国境线两边都算人的身份——一件都没有进入这场讲话。铁拳会落下去，落下去之后，下一批"他们"已经在被制造了。

Nine people died at a church in Karak, dozens wounded. King Abdullah II told parliament: "They are not of us."

The function of that sentence isn't condemnation, it's line-drawing. Once the line is drawn, the nine dead become a loss for "us," the shooters become a problem for "them," and nobody inside "us" has to be asked who made these people into "them"—who drained the possibilities out of the southern governorates, who keeps the balance between mosque and church on a knife's edge. Galtung's arithmetic fits: nine lives is the direct-layer gap, but the gap exists because the structural layer long ago pushed some people to the one expression left to them. The King handled the direct layer and declared the structural layer nonexistent.

"With an iron fist" is the same sentence extended. The fist always lands on those already outside the line, and the fist never asks who drew it. The army and security services get named, national unity and public awareness get named. What doesn't get named: that over half this country is of Palestinian origin, that they live in camps on limited work permits, watching the West Bank every day. The King says Israeli expansionism is the region's greatest threat—true, and safe. It points the threat across the border, so the arrangement inside the border can keep being called "unity."

The water passage deserves its own look. "We will not allow the thirst of Jordanians to be held hostage by any party"—no subject. Who is holding it? The report says this reflects concern over Israeli projects, but the King didn't say the name. A monarch telling parliament "any party" says nothing at all; meanwhile Jordan's water is actually shrinking, and who gets the shortfall, which district goes dry first, which camp's pipes get fixed last—that's where structural violence actually lands, and not a word of it.

The last passage is the finest: "The law knows no name, no position and no influence." Immediately followed by "Your differences of opinion are a strength so long as Jordan's interest is the goal." Together they mean: disagreement may exist, provided it doesn't point at the interest itself. Parliament may debate, as long as the debate confirms the line the King drew. This is meta-violence in its cleanest form—not silencing speech, but ruling that speech must happen inside "us," while the definition of "us" stays outside the speakers' hands.

The nine funerals are over. The relatives sat in parliament for a minute of silence. After the silence, the nine became a footnote to "national resilience," and everything that might have given Karak's young men an expression other than a gun—work, water, an identity that counts as human on both sides of a border—entered the speech not once. The fist will come down. And the next batch of "them" is already being made.
