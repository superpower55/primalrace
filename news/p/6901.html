只认钱的赏金猎人又来了Another Bounty Hunter Who Only Follows the Money

文娱 文化层 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要CBS 剧集《Tracker》第四季将于 11 月 22 日播出一集，乔什·哈洛威客串出演赏金猎人 Luke Kelsol，他只认钱、不问目标是谁，与主角 Colter 被迫合作。哈洛威曾因《迷失》中的 Sawyer 一角知名。
The newsJosh Holloway will guest star in the November 22 episode of CBS' Tracker, playing Luke Kelsol, a rewardist who only follows the money and doesn't care who the target is, forcing an uneasy partnership with Colter. Holloway is best known for playing Sawyer on Lost.
同一个剧本换个频道，照样有人买单。
Same script, different channel. Someone still pays.

一个只认钱、不问目标是谁的赏金猎人，和一个有过去、有原则的赏金猎人，被迫搭档，先互相看不顺眼，再在某个节点上互相救一次。这条新闻里唯一的信息量，是这个模板又开机了一季。

《Tracker》已经拍到第四季。第四季的意思是，前三季的这套东西卖得动。Colter Shaw 是个独狼，靠直觉和野外生存技能找失踪的人，收赏金；上一季结尾他撞上了家族历史的真相，这一季他要把过去甩在后视镜里，专注做他最擅长的事。找人和收钱。这两件事在这部剧里从来不是矛盾的，它们是同一句话的两半。

新来的 Luke Kelsol 被设定成 Colter 的暗面：同样收钱找人，但他的道德指南针永远指向自己。这个对照很省事，因为它不需要真的讨论什么。观众知道 Colter 有底线，因为剧本告诉过我们他有；观众知道 Luke 没有，因为他连问都不问。底线在这里是一种人设配料，不是一种代价。真正的问题是：谁付钱，为什么付钱，被找的人被找到之后会怎样。这些在这类剧里从来不是重点，重点是两个男人之间的紧张感和最后那一下默契。

哈洛威最出名的角色是《迷失》里的 Sawyer，一个自称唯利是图、实际上心软的男人。二十年后他演的还是这个类型：嘴上说只认钱，观众知道他心里有一块地方是软的。这是男性中心叙事里最耐用的一种角色——坏得刚好，好得不彻底，所有的道德挣扎都发生在两个男人之间，女人在这个结构里要么是委托人，要么是被找的人，要么是团队里那个可靠的同事。这部新闻里唯一被提到的女性角色 Reenie，由 Fiona Rene 出演，一句介绍都没有。

这不是说一部周播剧必须承担什么。它承担不了，也没打算承担。但它每晚九点被送进几百万个客厅，一遍一遍地告诉人：正义是一个可以标价的服务，而提供服务的人只需要在最后关头做一次正确的选择。这个叙事不需要武器化，它只需要一直在。

11 月 22 日那一集里，Luke 和 Colter 会从不情愿的搭档变成某种意义上的同伴。至于那个被找的人是谁、为什么被找、找到之后去了哪里——剧本大概不会给答案。观众也不会问。

A bounty hunter who only follows the money and doesn't ask who the target is, paired against a bounty hunter with a past and principles. They dislike each other, then save each other once at the right moment. The only information in this news item is that the template has been renewed.

Tracker is in its fourth season. A fourth season means the first three sold. Colter Shaw is a lone wolf who uses instinct and survival skills to find missing people and collect rewards; last season's finale dropped hard truths about his family, and this season he has to put the past in the rearview and focus on what he does best. Finding people and collecting money. In this show those two things were never in tension. They are two halves of one sentence.

The new arrival, Luke Kelsol, is written as Colter's shadow: same job, but his moral compass always points to himself. The contrast is cheap because it never has to argue anything. The audience knows Colter has a line because the script said so; the audience knows Luke doesn't because he doesn't even ask. A line here is a character ingredient, not a cost. The real questions—who is paying, why, and what happens to the person once found—are never the point. The point is the tension between two men and the nod at the end.

Holloway's best-known role is Sawyer on Lost: a man who claims to be in it for himself and is secretly soft. Twenty years later he is playing the same type. Mouth says money, audience knows there's a soft spot. It's the most durable character in masculine-centric storytelling—bad enough, not fully good, all the moral struggle happening between two men, with women as clients, targets, or the reliable colleague. The one woman named in this piece, Reenie, played by Fiona Rene, gets no description at all.

This isn't to say a weekly procedural must carry anything. It can't, and it isn't trying. But it gets delivered into millions of living rooms at nine every Sunday and tells them, again and again, that justice is a service with a price, and the person providing it only has to make one correct choice at the last minute. This narrative doesn't need to be weaponized. It just needs to keep airing.

On November 22, Luke and Colter will go from reluctant partners to something like comrades. Who the person was, why they were being hunted, where they ended up—the script probably won't say. The audience won't ask.
