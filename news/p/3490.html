爱丁堡边缘节：从“民俗聚会”到企业化生存的共谋Edinburgh Fringe: Complicity in the Shift from Folk Festival to Corporate Survival

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-09 § 链接
当表达被定义为“专业”与“野心”，艺术就成了被定价的商品。
When expression is defined by 'professionalism' and 'ambition', art becomes a priced commodity.

爱丁堡边缘节（Edinburgh Fringe）的演变，是一个典型的 cultural violence 过程。从 Gregor Fisher 描述的“货车后座剧场”到如今被 Stephen K Amos 称为“corporate-y”的企业化景观，这不仅仅是规模的扩大，而是认知入口的彻底更替。在 90 年代，这里是一个 folk festival，表达是存在的确证；而现在，它变成了一个巨大的博弈场，年轻喜剧演员被要求必须拥有“主题”或“剧场元素”，甚至在初次亮相时就递出 business card。这种对“专业性”的追求，本质上是把表达武器化，将其转化为一种可量化的、旨在获取社会阶级跃升的资本。

最耐人寻味的是文中关于“野心”（ambition）的讨论。Frank Skinner 提到 90 年代这个词是禁忌，而现在人们对此坦率得近乎残酷。当“野心”合法化，表达就不再是为了寻找真.最优解，而成了扮演一个“成功喜剧人”的假.最优解。这种转变让很多新人陷入了 ania Magliano 所描述的“恐惧被揭穿”的焦虑中。因为他们不再是在进行艺术探索，而是在参与一场关于“可见度”和“评价权”的存在性战争。在这种结构中，所谓的“生存技巧”其实就是一套关于如何适应企业化规则的共谋手册。

当然，文中也有微小的 structural 突破。比如 Sepideh Kaav 提到的，用英语作为第二语言来讨论波斯文化中难以启齿的性，这是一种利用语言作为生物墙缓冲，从而夺回解释权的尝试。但整体而言，当一个艺术节从“给人们的剧场”变成“给评委和经纪人的橱窗”，它就在削弱那些真正边缘的、不符合商业逻辑的表达。好新闻是多样性依然存在，但坏消息是，这种多样性正被包裹在一种名为“专业”的精致包装纸里，成为一种表演性的让步。

The evolution of the Edinburgh Fringe is a textbook case of cultural violence. Moving from Gregor Fisher's 'theatre out of the back of a van' to the 'corporate-y' landscape described by Stephen K Amos, this isn't just growth—it's a total shift in the cognitive entry point. In the 90s, it was a folk festival where expression was a confirmation of existence. Now, it's a massive game board where newcomers are pressured to have a 'theme' or 'theatrical element', and expected to hand out business cards upon debut. This pursuit of 'professionalism' is essentially the weaponisation of expression, converting it into a quantifiable capital for social climbing.

The most telling part is the discussion on 'ambition'. Frank Skinner notes it was a taboo in the 90s, yet now people are brutally upfront about it. Once ambition is legitimised, expression stops being a search for a true optimal expression and becomes a fake optimal expression—playing the role of a 'successful comedian'. This shift traps newcomers in the anxiety Ania Magliano describes: the 'fear of being exposed'. They are no longer exploring art; they are fighting an existential war over 'visibility' and 'the right to evaluate'. In this structure, 'survival tips' are merely a complicity manual on how to adapt to corporate rules.

There are small structural breakthroughs, such as Sepideh Kaav using English as a second language to discuss sex—a topic taboo in Farsi. This is an attempt to seize the power of interpretation by using language as a buffer against the biological and cultural wall. However, when a festival shifts from 'theatre for the people' to a 'showcase for critics and agents', it erodes the truly marginal expressions that defy commercial logic. The good news is that diversity persists, but the bad news is that this diversity is being wrapped in a polished paper called 'professionalism', becoming a form of performative concession.
