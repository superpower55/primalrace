换把尺子，账单照旧A New Ruler, Same Bill

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
不建不能并网的电，等于不建电
Power that can't connect is power not built.

Healey 这一手不是退缩，是换尺子。他没有说不搞海上风电，他说的是：只买能降账单的项目。听起来无懈可击——谁反对降账单？问题是这个标准由谁定、按什么算。

报道里写得清楚：新项目要投标补贴合约，部长们只有在相信全部发电量能被电网消化、且不推高消费者成本时才签字。可电网能不能消化，不是风电项目自己能决定的，是电缆容量决定的。项目并网不了，风机被付钱关掉，这笔浪费的风电费用由谁来付？Neso 靠能源账单养着。也就是说，风机转不转、关不关，最后都记在家庭账单上。

于是这个逻辑闭环成立了：先不建电缆，再因为没电缆而说风电太贵，再拿“太贵”当理由不批新项目。国家审计署九月警告，到 2030 年这笔账可能一年 78 亿英镑。这个数字被拿来当作风电的罪证，而不是电网欠账的罪证。

能源部门预算砍了 30 多亿，大部分去填国防开支。锅炉升级计划被削 7.41 亿，热泵补贴从今年 4 亿本来要涨到 2029-30 年 7 亿多，现在这个涨幅成了牺牲品。典型家庭能拿 7500 镑，一年装 6 万台，目标是 2030 年 25 万台——砍掉的钱正好砍在这条曲线上。

Healey 说这不是从清洁能源撤退，是“可信的路径”。他还说，想撕毁可再生能源的人会让英国更容易受制于“操纵市场的独裁者”。这话没错，但说这话的人刚刚把装风电的预算挪去买了别的东西。

真正被重新定义的，不是能源政策，是“降账单”这个词。降账单可以靠多发绿电，也可以靠少建绿电、少花钱、把成本推给未来的账单和未来的气候。后者短期数字更好看，因为看不见的部分不算账。

加尔通那个公式在这里很好用：本可以达到的清洁电力，减去实际会建成的清洁电力，差额就是暴力。这个差额不会写进任何一份预算表，它只会出现在 2030 年之后某个家庭的电费单上，以及某个本该并网却被关掉的风机转速表上。

Healey didn't retreat from offshore wind. He changed the ruler. He didn't say no to wind; he said only projects that lower bills get signed off. Who could argue with lower bills? The question is who sets the standard and how it's counted.

The report is explicit: new projects bidding for subsidy contracts will only be approved if ministers believe all the power generated can be absorbed by the grid without pushing up consumer costs. But whether the grid can absorb it isn't up to the wind farm. It's up to cable capacity. When a project can't connect, turbines get paid to switch off — and who pays for that wasted wind? Neso is funded through energy bills. So whether the turbines spin or stop, it lands on household bills.

The loop closes neatly: don't build the cables, then call wind expensive because there are no cables, then use "expensive" as grounds to reject new projects. The National Audit Office warned in September that by 2030 this could cost £7.8bn a year. That figure gets used as evidence against wind, not as evidence of a grid debt.

The energy department's budget was cut by more than £3bn, much of it to pay for higher defence spending. The boiler upgrade scheme lost £741m. Heat pump grants — £7,500 for a typical household, 60,000 installations a year, a target of 250,000 by 2030 — had their funding path from £400m this year to just over £700m by 2029-30 cut right where the curve was supposed to rise.

Healey says this is not a retreat from clean power but "the credible route to it." He says those who want to rip up renewables would leave Britain more exposed to volatile markets and "the dictators who exploit them." True enough. But the man saying it just moved the money for building renewables somewhere else.

What's actually being redefined isn't energy policy. It's the phrase "lower bills." Bills can fall by generating more clean power, or by generating less, spending less, and pushing the cost onto future bills and future climate. The second looks better in the short term, because what you can't see doesn't get counted.

Galtung's formula fits: potential clean power minus actual clean power equals violence. That gap won't appear in any budget table. It will appear on a household bill after 2030, and on the tachometer of a turbine that should have been connected but was switched off.
