无偿照护：一场被浪漫化叙事掩盖的结构性掠夺Unpaid Care: A Structural Plunder Masked by Romanticized Narratives

性别 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-16 § 链接
所谓的“家庭责任”是国家通过文化暴力将公共开支转嫁给个体的scam。
The so-called 'family responsibility' is a scam used by the state to privatize public expenditure through cultural violence.

每天1500人离职成为无偿照护者，这在主流叙事里会被包装成“亲情”、“奉献”或“家庭责任”。但如果用加尔通的暴力三角来看，这就是典型的 structural violence。国家通过削减社会护理服务，人为制造了一个巨大的 Potential（应有的社会保障）与 Actual（实际获得的资源）之间的差额。这个差额没有消失，而是被精准地转嫁到了个体，尤其是女性身上。

虽然报道中提到的是“Britons”，但我们可以预见，在这百万离职者中，女性占据绝对多数。这再次验证了“母职惩罚” (Motherhood Penalty) 的变体——“照护惩罚”。在父权结构中，女性被预设为天然的、具有“滋养属性”的容器。这种文化暴力 (cultural violence) 让女性在面对亲人病榻时，更容易内化一种“我必须牺牲”的责任感，从而在存在性战争中选择了最糟糕的假.最优解：用主体性的死亡（放弃职业、养老金和社交）来换取一种道德上的自我认同。

最讽刺的是政府的回复：用“非凡的贡献”这种廉价的赞美来抵消数以十亿计的经济损失。这是一种极其阴险的共谋：政府通过口头表扬，将一种结构性的剥削转化为一种个体性的荣誉。当 Jacqui Hylton 这样一名资深助产士不得不放弃职业时，损失的不仅是她的养老金，更是整个公共医疗系统的专业能力。但这正是元暴力的运作方式——它并不在乎具体的个体是否被榨干，它只在乎这套“家庭内部消化”的低成本运行机制是否能继续维持。

所谓的“加强就业权利”或“10天带薪假”不过是表演性的让步。只要照护的定义依然绑定在“家庭成员”的道德绑架上，只要国家依然把社会护理视为一种可以被削减的开支而非基本人权，这种对个体的掠夺就永远不会停止。

1,500 Britons quitting their jobs daily to become unpaid carers is framed in mainstream narratives as 'devotion' or 'family duty.' However, through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this is textbook structural violence. By slashing state-funded social care, the government creates a massive gap between Potential (the social security citizens should have) and Actual (the resources they actually receive). This gap isn't eliminated; it is precisely transferred onto individuals, predominantly women.

While the report says 'Britons,' we know women will be the vast majority of this million-strong exodus. This is a variation of the 'Motherhood Penalty'—the 'Caregiving Penalty.' In a masculine-centric narrative, women are preset as natural, 'nurturing' vessels. This cultural violence tricks women into internalizing a 'duty to sacrifice,' leading them to choose a fake optimal expression in their existential war: sacrificing their subjectivity (career, pensions, social life) for a crumb of moral validation.

The government's response is the height of irony: using cheap praise like 'extraordinary contribution' to offset billions in economic loss. This is a sinister form of complicity. By framing structural exploitation as individual honor, the state ensures the system keeps running. When a veteran midwife like Jacqui Hylton is forced out of her profession, it's not just her pension that vanishes, but the professional capacity of the entire public health system. This is how meta-violence operates—it doesn't care if individuals are drained, as long as the low-cost mechanism of 'internal family digestion' persists.

Proposing 'enhanced employment rights' or '10 days of paid leave' is mere performative concession. As long as the definition of care remains bound to the moral blackmail of 'family,' and as long as the state views social care as a cuttable expense rather than a fundamental human right, this plunder of the individual will never end.
