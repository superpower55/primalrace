穷人是她的道具The Poor as Her Prop

文娱 文化层 The Cut ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要万圣节周末，Martha Stewart 在 Instagram 发布装扮成无家可归者的照片：脏脸、破洞牛仔裤，手拿写字板写着「I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!」。遭到批评后她删除帖子，先称本意是「唤起对无家可归问题的关注」，随后在评论区道歉，周日晚又发截图声明承认「不得体、不好笑」。
The newsOver Halloween weekend, Martha Stewart posted Instagram photos of herself dressed as an unhoused person: dirty face, ripped jeans, and a clipboard reading “I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!!” After backlash she deleted the post, first saying she meant to “bring more awareness” to homelessness, then apologizing in a comment and finally in a Notes-app screenshot admitting the costume was “insensitive and not funny.”
把别人的绝境穿在身上，再宣布这是关怀。
She wore someone’s worst day as a costume, then called it care.

Martha Stewart 把无家可归穿成了一套衣服：脏脸、破洞牛仔裤、一块写着「I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!!」的写字板。这不是表达，是征用——把别人的绝境从人身上剥下来，做成一件可以拍照、可以配蜡烛广告的道具。

她的第一反应最能说明问题。她说这套装扮是为了「唤起对无家可归这一极其严肃议题的更多关注」。这套句式我们太熟了：先制造一次伤害，再宣布伤害的动机是关怀。真正需要被看见的人没有出现在她的照片里，出现的只有她自己，以及她那些「非常适合万圣节」的 QVC 蜡烛。

第二反应更直接。她在评论区写，这套装扮「确实让所有人都想到了无家可归者和饥饿的人！！！」然后反问：你们呢？道歉的句子里夹着一个对公众的考核。这是共谋者最标准的动作——把一次冒犯改写成一次道德动员，自己在动员里继续当主角。

直到第三份声明，她才说出「不得体、不好笑」。三天、三个场合、三次表态，道歉的力度是被舆论一步步逼出来的，不是她自己走到的。

报道里没有出现任何一个无家可归者的名字，也没有出现任何一家她声称长期支持的机构的名字。这不是报道的疏漏，这是这件事的形状：从头到尾，穷人都只是背景板，是让一套服装显得「有议题」的素材。

真正的不适不在那身破洞牛仔裤，在于她穿完就脱掉了，而有人脱不掉。

Martha Stewart wore homelessness as an outfit: a dirty face, ripped jeans, a clipboard reading “I AM HUNGRY HELP ME!!!” This isn’t expression. It’s requisition — stripping someone’s worst day off their body and turning it into a prop that can be photographed next to a candle ad.

Her first response is the tell. The costume, she said, was intended to “bring more awareness to the very serious subject of homelessness.” We know this sentence. Cause the harm first, then announce that the harm was care. The people who actually need to be seen appear nowhere in the photos. Only she does, alongside her QVC candles that are “perfect for Halloween!!!!!”

Her second response is even more direct. In a comment she wrote that the costume “certainly did” bring the homeless and the hungry to everyone’s mind — then asked, “Do all of you??” An apology with an exam attached to the audience. This is the standard co-conspirator move: convert an offense into a moral mobilization in which you remain the protagonist.

Only in the third statement does she say it plainly: “insensitive and not funny.” Three days, three venues, three statements — the force of the apology was extracted by backlash, not reached on her own.

Nowhere in the report does a single unhoused person appear by name. Nowhere does one of the charities she says she has spent “a significant amount of time and effort supporting” appear by name. That isn’t a gap in the reporting. It’s the shape of the thing: from start to finish, the poor are scenery, the material that makes an outfit “about something.”

The real discomfort isn’t in the ripped jeans. It’s that she took them off afterward, and some people can’t.
