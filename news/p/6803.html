电先断，人后死Cut the Power First, the Dead Come Later

国际 结构层 · 直接层 Al Jazeera ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要苏丹东部和北部的三座变电站于周一遭无人机袭击，导致多地大面积停电。过去一周，苏丹电网遭袭次数已达18次。苏丹军政府称埃塞俄比亚协助准军事组织快速支援部队（RSF）实施袭击，已向联合国安理会和非洲联盟提出申诉。军方同日称，有RSF成员在青尼罗州达马津投降。
The newsDrone strikes hit three electricity substations in eastern and northern Sudan on Monday, causing widespread power outages. The attacks brought the number of strikes on Sudan's power network to 18 in the past week. Sudan's military government accuses Ethiopia of aiding the paramilitary RSF and has filed complaints with the UN Security Council and the African Union.
先炸变电站，是让死亡显得像意外
Bombing substations makes death look like an accident

炸变电站是战争里最干净的一步棋。没有人当场流血，新闻里只有「大面积停电」，听上去像天气。但医院的手术台、保温箱、透析机、水泵，全靠电。18次袭击打在一个国家的电网上，这不是军事目标，这是把一整个平民的日常变成待机状态。直接暴力还没落到谁头上，结构暴力已经先动手了。

苏丹军政府的说法是埃塞俄比亚在帮RSF。这个指控有没有证据，报道没写，我不替它补。但值得看的是它被送到哪里去了：联合国安理会，非盟和平与安全理事会。这两个机构的记录摆在那里，申诉递上去，通常换来的是声明、关切、再一轮声明。把一个邻国告到安理会，等于把停电的账单转交给一个专收账单不付款的机构。

同一篇报道里，军方还宣布有RSF成员在达马津投降，并说一名指挥官提供了关于「埃塞俄比亚支持」的情报。投降的消息和外国干预的指控在同一天发布，这个时间点本身就在说话。战况不利的时候，敌人的敌人就成了叙事里的主角。谁在共谋、回报是什么，报道没给；但谁在借这场停电讲一个对自己有利的故事，报道给了。

真正付代价的人没有名字。报道写了停电范围，没写停电的医院里有没有人死，没写水泵停了多少小时，没写哪一户人家的孩子在黑暗里断了氧。这不是记者的失职，战时核实本来就难，但这也正是这类袭击被选中的原因：伤亡不进入统计，暴力就不进入账本。炸电网的人比谁都清楚这一点。

18次，一周。这个数字还会涨，直到某一座城市彻底黑下来，那时候新闻会写「人道主义危机」，好像危机是突然发生的。它是一周一周、一座变电站一座变电站被制造出来的。

Bombing substations is the cleanest move in a war. Nobody bleeds on camera; the headline just says power outage, which reads like weather. But operating tables, incubators, dialysis machines, water pumps all run on electricity. Eighteen strikes on a national grid in one week is not a military target. It is a whole population's daily life put on standby. Direct violence hasn't landed on anyone yet; structural violence got there first.

Sudan's military government says Ethiopia is helping the RSF. The report doesn't give evidence, and I won't invent any. What's worth watching is where the accusation was sent: the UN Security Council and the African Union's Peace and Security Council. We know how those institutions behave. A complaint goes in, statements come out, then more statements. Taking a neighbor to the Security Council is how you hand the blackout's bill to an office that collects bills and never pays.

In the same report, the army announces RSF members surrendering in Damazin, and says a commander supplied intelligence on Ethiopian support. The defection and the foreign-interference claim were released the same day. That timing is doing work. When the battlefield goes badly, the enemy's enemy becomes the star of the narrative. Who is conspiring and what they get for it, the report doesn't say. Who is using this blackout to tell a story that suits them, it does.

The people paying the price have no names. The report gives the scope of the outage, not whether anyone died in a dark hospital, not how many hours the pumps stopped, not which child lost oxygen in the night. That isn't the reporter's failure — verification in wartime is hard — but it is exactly why these strikes get chosen. If casualties never enter the count, the violence never enters the ledger. Whoever is hitting the grid knows this better than anyone.

Eighteen strikes, one week. The number will keep climbing until some city goes fully dark, and then the coverage will say humanitarian crisis, as if the crisis just happened. It is being manufactured, one substation at a time.
