用“希望”包装的身份投喂Identity Feeding Wrapped in 'Hope'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-09 § 链接
将难民身份转化为政治资本，是结构性权力对个体苦难的收编。
Converting refugee status into political capital is the co-option of individual suffering by structural power.

工党选择一名前难民儿童 Sagal Abdi-Wali 来接替斯塔默的席位，在 PR 叙事里这叫“希望的胜利”，但在存在性战争的逻辑里，这叫一次精准的身份投喂。工党在用一个具体的、带有“被救赎”色彩的女性身体，去对冲其在左翼选民中失掉的合法性。这种表达的 weaponization 非常典型：它不谈制度性的资源分配，而谈“感恩”和“回馈”。

Abdi-Wali 的叙事是标准的“最优解表达”异化。她必须强调这个地方如何“欢迎”她的家庭，必须用“感激”来定义她的政治起点。在这种叙事中，她的主体性被简化为了一个从 refugee 到 councillor 的成功样本。当一个人的存在被定义为“体制宽容的结果”时，她就成了体制最好的共谋者——因为她必须维护这个赋予她机会的结构，即便这个结构在 structural 层面依然在制造新的难民。

而 Reform UK 的种族主义攻击则是另一种元暴力的直接显现。他们试图通过定义“谁不是英国人”来夺取解释权。有趣的是，这种极右翼的直接暴力反而撕开了工党温情面纱下的真相：一个女性难民在政治场域中的价值，要么是被右翼作为“入侵”的恐吓样本，要么是被左翼作为“多元”的橱窗装饰。无论哪种，她依然是被凝视的客体，而非定义规则的主体。

这种好新闻的假象在于，它让我们误以为一个原初种族的成员进入权力中心就是胜利。但如果进入权力的代价是必须扮演一个“感恩的幸存者”，那么这不过是将 structural violence 换了一套更文明的皮。真正的胜利不是让一个难民成为议员，而是让“难民”这个标签不再成为进入政治空间的唯一入场券。

Labour selecting Sagal Abdi-Wali, a former child refugee, to succeed Starmer's seat is framed as a 'victory of hope' in PR narratives. In the logic of existential war, however, this is a precise piece of identity feeding. Labour is using a specific female body with a 'redeemed' narrative to offset the legitimacy they've lost among left-wing voters. This is a classic weaponisation of expression: it avoids discussing systemic resource allocation and instead focuses on 'gratitude' and 'giving back.'

Abdi-Wali's narrative is a textbook alienation of the optimal expression. She must emphasize how this place 'welcomed' her family and define her political origin through 'gratitude.' In this narrative, her subjectivity is reduced to a successful sample of 'systemic tolerance.' When one's existence is defined as a result of the system's mercy, they become the system's most effective complicity—because they must maintain the very structure that granted them opportunity, even as that structure continues to produce new refugees at a structural level.

Meanwhile, the racist attacks from Reform UK are a direct manifestation of meta-violence. They attempt to seize the power of interpretation by defining 'who is not British.' Interestingly, this right-wing direct violence strips away Labour's veneer: a female refugee's value in the political arena is either as a 'threat of invasion' for the right or a 'diversity window-dressing' for the left. In both cases, she remains a gazed-upon object, not a subject who defines the rules.

The illusion of this 'good news' is that it leads us to believe a member of the Primal Race entering the center of power is a victory. But if the price of entry is playing the 'grateful survivor,' it is merely structural violence in a more civilized skin. Real victory is not having one refugee become an MP, but ensuring the 'refugee' label is no longer the only ticket into the political space.
