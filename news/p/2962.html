用建筑标准掩盖的结构性暴力Structural Violence Masked by Building Codes

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-07-29 § 链接
技术上的“抗震”不能抵消制度对生存权的漠视。
Technical 'earthquake resistance' cannot offset the systemic neglect of basic survival rights.

报道在赞美日本严格的建筑标准和滚轮地基技术，试图将这次灾难定义为一次“损失较小”的自然事件。这是典型的 cultural violence：用技术叙事掩盖 structural violence。当人们在讨论大楼是否摇晃时，忽视了 14 万人失去水源、5 万户失去电力，以及在 30 度高温高湿环境下，大量民众被迫在车内度过惊恐之夜的 Actual 状态。

这种“相对成功”的防灾叙事，实际上是在为既定秩序提供 PR。真正的 Potential 状态不应该是“死的人比较少”，而应该是“没有任何人因为基础资源缺失而在灾后陷入生存危机”。当医疗设备因断电失效、医院内部渗水，而政府依然在通过发言人重复“全力搜救”的模板化表达时，这种 gap 就是暴力。

注意到报道中提到的两名 20 多岁女性在商场爆炸中死亡。在男本位的灾难叙事中，个体往往被简化为“伤亡数字”，而那些在避难所中需要面对的、关于生理需求与隐私权的博弈，永远不会出现在主流新闻的认知入口里。这就是元暴力的运作方式：它定义了什么是“有效的救援”，而将那些不可见、不方便的生存困境定义为“必要的代价”。

The report praises Japan's strict construction standards and roller-foundation technology, attempting to frame this disaster as a 'relatively minor' natural event. This is classic cultural violence: using a technical narrative to mask structural violence. While the discourse focuses on whether buildings swayed, it ignores the actual state of 140,000 people without water, 50,000 without power, and masses of citizens forced to sleep in cars under 30°C humidity and fear.

This narrative of 'relative success' in disaster prevention is mere PR for the established order. The true Potential state should not be 'fewer deaths,' but a system where no one falls into a survival crisis due to a lack of basic resources. When medical equipment fails due to power outages and hospitals leak water while government spokespeople repeat templated phrases about 'going all out,' that gap is the violence.

Note the two women in their 20s confirmed dead in the mall explosion. In a masculine-centric disaster narrative, individuals are reduced to 'casualty counts.' The existential wars fought in evacuation centers—over physiological needs and privacy—never enter the cognitive entry points of mainstream news. This is how meta-violence operates: it defines what constitutes 'effective rescue' and dismisses the invisible, inconvenient struggles as 'necessary costs.'
