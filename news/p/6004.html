这不是新闻，这是新闻业的操作手册This Isn't News. It's a Manual for the Trade.

暴力最隐蔽的一层，是让你以为它不存在
The most hidden layer of violence is making you believe it isn't there.

ProPublica 这篇不是新闻，是一份爆料指南。它告诉你什么样的信息值得递出去、怎么递、怎么不被抓。它自己不是事件，它是事件发生之前的那个通道——而通道能不能通畅，本身就是 violence 三角里那个差额的一部分。

看它列的条件：信息必须是新的，必须有证据，必须指向有权势的人做了不该做的事，并且造成了显著伤害。这四条合起来，就是加尔通那个公式的另一种写法。它要的是 potential 和 actual 之间的裂缝，是那些本来可以不被造成、却被造成了的伤害。ProPublica 不报道天气，它报道差额。

然后是防务部分。加密表单、Signal、平信、SecureDrop、Tor、Tails。它甚至提醒你别用公司的电脑、公司的邮箱、公司的 Wi-Fi，因为公司会记录流量。这一段读起来像间谍手册，其实是一份结构暴力的地形图：它默认了告密者会付出代价，默认了权力会追查来源，默认了在某些机构里，说出真相本身就是一种风险。这些默认不是 ProPublica 编的，是它从现实里学来的。

最有意思的是那句"我们不会未经你同意就公布你的名字"。这句话是承诺，也是定价——它承认了名字一旦公布，人就可能被报复。新闻业的这个通道之所以需要这么多层加密，是因为在通道的另一头，有一整套共谋在等着。谁在共谋？那些记录员工流量的公司，那些用 warrant 才能拦下的邮件，那些需要 Tor 才能绕开的追踪。它们不杀人，它们只是让说话变得昂贵。

所以这篇东西真正在做的，是把"说真话"这件事的成本摊开给你看。它没有缩小那个差额，它只是把差额的位置标了出来。标出来之后，能不能缩小，取决于有没有人愿意从那个标好的入口走进去。

结尾的刺在这里：这份指南保护的是递出信息的人，不保护被信息指向的人。ProPublica 的报道能改变现实，但改变的方向不由递信息的人决定。你递出去的证据，最后写成什么故事，是编辑和记者的事。通道是双向的，但定价权不在你手里。

This ProPublica page isn't a news story. It's a tip sheet. It tells you what information is worth handing over, how to hand it over, and how not to get caught. It isn't the event; it's the channel that exists before the event—and whether that channel stays open is itself part of the gap in the violence triangle.

Look at the criteria: the information must be new, it must come with evidence, it must point to powerful people doing something they shouldn't, and it must be causing significant harm. Those four conditions are just another way of writing Galtung's formula. What ProPublica wants is the crack between potential and actual—the harm that could have been avoided but wasn't. It doesn't report the weather. It reports the gap.

Then comes the defense section. Encrypted forms, Signal, snail mail, SecureDrop, Tor, Tails. It even warns you not to use your work computer, your work email, your work Wi-Fi, because companies log traffic. This reads like a spy manual, but it's really a map of structural violence: it assumes whistleblowers will pay a price, assumes power will trace its sources, assumes that inside certain institutions, telling the truth is itself a risk. Those assumptions aren't ProPublica's invention. They learned them from reality.

The sharpest line is the promise not to publish your name without consent. That's a commitment, but it's also a price tag—it admits that once a name is out, a person can be retaliated against. The reason this channel needs so many layers of encryption is that on the other end, a whole apparatus of complicity is waiting. Who's complicit? The companies logging employee traffic, the emails that need a warrant to intercept, the tracking that requires Tor to bypass. They don't kill anyone. They just make speaking expensive.

So what this page actually does is lay out the cost of telling the truth. It doesn't close the gap. It just marks where the gap is. Whether it gets closed depends on whether anyone is willing to walk through the marked entrance.

The sting at the end: this guide protects the person handing over information, not the person the information points to. ProPublica's reporting can change reality, but the direction of that change isn't decided by the tipster. The evidence you hand over becomes whatever story the editors and reporters write. The channel runs both ways, but the pricing power isn't yours.
