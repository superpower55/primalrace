唱完歌，毒水还在They Sang, the Water Stayed Poisoned

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
和谐叙事是共谋者的最优解表达
Harmony talk is the co-conspirator's optimal expression.

Rob Sand 的竞选集会以全体合唱《America the Beautiful》开场，他管这叫“证明我们能一起做事”。我看这是共谋者理论的标准操作：把结构暴力包装成“大家不够友好”。

爱荷华的水被硝酸盐和永久化学品污染，癌症增长率全国第二，共和党州长和议会砍掉了州立大学的监测经费——测不到硝酸盐，就没有硝酸盐。这是 structural violence 的教科书案例：不是谁往你杯子里下毒，是预算表上的一行被划掉。Sand 对此的回应是给农民更多参与湿地保护项目的激励。合作。报道写得很清楚：他一年打磨的水质政策不点名大型农业，也不跟农场局开战。而那位被他称为极端 MAHA 的共和党对手 Zach Lahn 反而直接把癌症和垄断性企业农业挂钩。

这里的关键不是谁更左，是谁在替谁维持现状。Sand 的竞选资金主要来自妻子家族——Lauridsen 集团，做汤料和宠物食品添加剂，妻子任 CEO，家族已投入 1150 万美元，其中 700 万在他正式宣布参选前就到账。2022 年他以 2893 票之差连任，家族资金让他的花费是对手的 40 倍。对手在州博览会上说，这家公司“生产很多最终流进我们溪流、让我们生病的 product”。Sand 的回应是：这家公司把废料变成商品，减少了行业的过度行为。至于能否把家族和责任分开，他说：“Watch me.”

这就是假.最优解表达：扮演一个双方都认可的和气角色，换取短期选票，代价是把解释权让给造成伤害的那一方。他自己说过，有些人不喜欢他谈论体制的方式，希望他“更像一个民主党人”。他回答：我就是我。听着很硬气，但“我”的边界恰好停在妻子家族的生意和大型农业的门口。

九个小时的演讲里他一次没提特朗普。他说，人们知道总统是谁，不需要他提醒。可报道里写着：联邦政府调查民主党州长、卡他们的项目、拒批救灾、往他们的社区派蒙面探员。Sand 被问到会不会配合 ICE，回答是“乐于配合做好事，不配合做坏事”，再问伊利诺伊和明尼苏达的部署，他说不想基于那边发生的事预判爱荷华。这不是克制，这是把最烫的那块留给别人。

他真心想推的激进改革只有选举制度：开放式初选、前五名晋级、认可投票。这套东西确实能松动两党垄断，我承认它有价值。但选举制度是入口，不是出口。换了投票规则，硝酸盐还在水里，癌症还在涨，产科医生还是全国最少——爱荷华每 capita 产科医生垫底，最严堕胎法让它继续垫底。

合唱很好听。报道里那位穿“Iowa: We all are going to die”T恤的老人说他和妻子和声了 43 年。Sand 问：如果政治一直像这样该多好。可政治一直不像这样，恰恰因为有人靠它不像这样获利——包括那些把监测经费砍掉的人，也包括那些不愿点名他们的人。歌停之后，水还是那杯水。

Rob Sand opens every campaign stop by having the room sing “America the Beautiful.” He calls it proof that we can actually work together. What it is, is the co-conspirators' theory in performance: structural violence dressed up as a shortage of friendliness.

Iowa's water carries nitrates and forever chemicals. The state is No. 2 for cancer-rate growth. The governor and legislature cut funding to the University of Iowa's monitoring system, so nonprofits and counties had to pick up the bill. Test no nitrates, see no nitrates. That is structural violence in its purest form: nobody poisons your glass by hand, a line gets struck from a budget.

Sand's answer is more incentives for farmers to join conservation programs. Cooperation. The reporting is explicit: after a year developing a water policy, he does not call out Big Ag and does not pick a fight with the Iowa Farm Bureau. His ultra-MAHA opponent, Zach Lahn, is the one who explicitly links cancer to monopolistic corporate agriculture.

The question is not who is further left. It is who is holding the status quo in place, and for whom. Sand's campaign is heavily financed by his wife and her family, the Lauridsens, who own an Iowa conglomerate making additives for soup stock and pet food; Christine Lauridsen Sand is its CEO. The family has put $11.5 million into the race, $7 million before he officially announced. In 2022 he survived by 2,893 votes while outspending his opponent 40 to 1 on family money. Lahn told voters at the state fair that the company makes many of the products ending up in our streams. Sand's reply: the firm turns waste into commodities and reduces the industry's excesses. Asked whether he can separate family from duty, he said, “Watch me.”

This is the false optimal expression: play the agreeable role both sides approve of, collect the short-term payoff, and pay with the interpretive authority that should have gone to the people being harmed.

Across ten events and more than nine hours, he mentioned Trump zero times. People know who the president is, he says. But the administration has subjected Democratic governors to federal investigations, blocked their projects, denied disaster relief, sent masked agents into their communities. Asked about ICE cooperation, Sand says he is happy to cooperate in good things and won't in bad things. Asked about the deployments to Illinois and Minnesota, he says he doesn't want to prejudge what might happen in Iowa. That is not restraint. That is leaving the hottest part of the stove to someone else.

His one genuinely radical proposal is electoral: open primaries, top-five advancement, approval voting. It has real value. But the voting system is an entrance, not an exit. Change the rules and the nitrates are still in the water, the cancer rates still climbing, the state still last in OB-GYNs per capita, with one of the nation's most restrictive abortion laws keeping it there.

The singing is lovely. The man in the “Iowa: We all are going to die” shirt has been harmonizing with his wife for 43 years. Sand asks: what if politics felt like that all the time? It doesn't, precisely because someone profits from it not feeling that way, and that includes the people who cut the monitoring budget and the man who won't name them. When the song ends, the water is still the water.
