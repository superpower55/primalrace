战争叙事是元暴力的最高形式：将肉体降格为工具，以保护资源为名实施性别暴力。
War narratives are the peak of meta-violence: degrading bodies into tools to secure resources through gendered violence.

这场所谓的“防御性攻势”不过是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。沙特在组织海陆联军、在与特朗普和万斯密谋，其核心逻辑不是为了所谓的“地区稳定”，而是为了夺回被胡塞武装掐住的油路定价权。在男权主义的博弈场上，资源（石油）的流动高于人的生命。无论是被空袭的也门平民，还是在伊拉克被击毙的 20 人，在这些决策者的眼中，他们不是主体，而是可被消耗的 an expenditure，是达成战略目标的 cost。

这就是元暴力的运作方式：将“保护工业”和“维护航行自由”武器化，使其成为实施直接暴力（direct violence）的 cultural cover。这种叙事让人们习惯于将战争看作是两个政治实体或两个男人（沙特与伊朗）的权力游戏，而刻意抹去了在这场游戏底层被剥削、被屠杀的原初种族——女性与儿童。他们是这场存在性战争中最彻底的 loser，因为他们被排除在决策桌之外，却在物理层面上承担所有代价。

所谓的“外交解决”或“多国联盟”，不过是共谋者们在分配利益时的礼貌用语。当沙特国防部长与美国政客在会议室讨论“下一步”时，他们实际上是在共谋一套将身体客体化的逻辑：只要能保证石油顺利出口，多少个也门家庭被撕裂在 structural violence 中并不重要。这种对生命权的极端轻视，正是男性中心叙事中“强权即真理”的生物学投射。

This so-called 'defensive offensive' is a textbook example of a masculine-centric narrative. Riyadh is organizing naval coalitions and plotting with Trump and Vance, not for 'regional stability,' but to reclaim the pricing power of oil routes choked by the Houthis. In the gambling den of patriarchy, the flow of resources (oil) always outweighs human life. Whether it is the civilians in Yemen or the 20 killed in Iraq, they are not subjects to these decision-makers; they are merely an expenditure, a cost to achieve a strategic goal.

This is how meta-violence operates: weaponizing 'industrial protection' and 'freedom of navigation' as a cultural cover to legitimize direct violence. This narrative trains the public to view war as a power game between political entities or 'strongmen' (Saudi vs. Iran), while systematically erasing the Primal Race—women and children—who are slaughtered at the bottom of this game. They are the absolute losers in this existential war, excluded from the decision-making table yet bearing the full physical brunt of the conflict.

Terms like 'diplomatic resolution' or 'multinational coalition' are merely polite euphemisms used by co-conspirators while dividing spoils. When the Saudi Defense Minister discusses 'next steps' with US politicians, they are essentially conspiring to uphold a logic of objectification: as long as oil flows, the structural violence tearing apart Yemeni families is an acceptable externality. This extreme disregard for life is the biological projection of 'might makes right' inherent in the masculine-centric narrative.
