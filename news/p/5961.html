她拆掉的是计票，不是舞弊She Tore Down the Count, Not the Fraud

国际 元暴力 · 文化层 · 结构层 ProPublica ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
把选举变成圣战，是解释权最后的武器化
Turning elections into holy war is the last weaponization of interpretation.

Cleta Mitchell 从来没有正式职位，却进出白宫如入无人之境。这不是什么幕后操盘手的传奇，这是元暴力最标准的运作方式：谁垄断了「什么是事实」的制造权，谁就不需要头衔。

ProPublica 的报道里有一个细节值得停三秒：特朗普那场黄金时段讲话里超过 27.5 万非公民在选民名册上的说法，源头是她一个门生在国土安全部主导的工作，报道说这个数字后来被证伪。一个被证伪的数字，经由一个没有官职的人的网络，变成了总统对全国说的「事实」。这就是第三章说的：夺取话语权，夺取解释权，夺取「什么是事实」的制造权。她不需要赢下法庭，她只需要让足够多的人相信法庭不可信。

再看她的路径。七十年代她是俄克拉荷马州的民主党人，推动投票权扩展，参与创立全国妇女政治党团，在 1972 年民主党全国代表大会上当代表。九十年代转投保守派，自称「庞大右翼阴谋的军师」。同一个人，两套叙事，中间隔着的不是理念转向，是权力的入口换了方向。报道里她当年的同僚说，一生中没见过政治转变比她更大的人。这不是背叛理想的故事，理想从一开始就是博弈里的筹码。

真正要看清的是她建的东西。2020 年之后，别人散了，她加倍下注，建起 Election Integrity Network，从 2022 年七个州的分会扩到今天的至少 26 个。ProPublica 分析的数据显示，2021 到 2025 年间她推动超过 7000 万美元流入这项工作，其中至少 1100 万来自她监管的非营利组织。报道说 2020 年后新成立的约 30 家保守派选举诚信非营利组织里，超过一半拿过她的钱或与她关系密切。

这不是一个律师在打官司，这是一个运动在建造。她给参与者发 20 页手册，教他们去掌控地方选举委员会的席位。2022 年她在密歇根对着一屋子人说「欢迎，各位可悲者」，说「作为一个老南方浸信会信徒，我喜欢传福音，所以这是你们的圣经」。把选举程序当成需要皈依的信仰，这是第三章宗教入口的教科书用法：制造神圣性，让信徒自己把叙事内化成自我审查。她不用鞭子，她让地方选举工作人员自己相信，不查非公民就是在渎职。

报道里最锋利的不是她的成功，是她的失败。SAVE Act 和 SAVE America Act 被参议院温和派挡住，行政令被法院拦下，佐治亚的认证规则被法院推翻，俄亥俄她坚持的强硬版本被州长否决。她自己的盟友、在选举诚信运动里干了几十年的 Ken Blackwell 说，「这是她的路或高速公路」，说有时她为了不让步，反而让选举更不安全。

失败之后她做了什么？在 Bannon 的节目上，Bannon 建议宣布国家紧急状态，她说不用那么麻烦，总统已经有权力依据保护关键基础设施的法律单方面执行法案的要求。「我们有蓝图，我们会帮忙，我们只需要一个有权力的人说，干吧。」十月六日那期节目里，她七次重复「今天」这个词。

这就是报道里 ProPublica 点出的那个模式：把冲突升级成赢家通吃的战争。当制度不配合，就绕过制度。当法院说不，就找一个不问法院的人。她推荐 Dan Bishop 去接手佐治亚的案子，Bishop 上任后试图传唤数千名富尔顿县计票人员的个人信息，法院驳回了。

这里要问的是共谋。谁在和她共谋，回报是什么。至少 25 名政府任命人员与她关系密切，Heather Honey 从她的宾州分会负责人变成国土安全部处理选举事务的高级顾问，一位了解招聘过程的联邦高官说，Honey 唯一的资格就是她跟谁共事过。这不是阴谋论，这是第四章说的：共谋不需要秘密会议，共谋只需要利益站队一致，回报是职位、是资源、是运动的方向盘。

而付代价的是谁？报道写得很清楚：投票权倡导组织说 SAVE 类法案可能剥夺数百万合格选民的投票权，尤其是女性、穷人和少数族裔。报道也写了北卡分会有人试图强行进入投票办公室，分会负责人被指控开车跟踪选举工作人员。这些是直接层的暴力，落在具体的计票员身上。

最后回到那个词。她说「我们做的没有任何事情是为了『左右』任何选举结果」，说「唯一的目的」是让选民对结果有信心。这句话本身就是武器化的样本：把「让选举更可信」变成「让选举按我的标准可信」，再把「我的标准」变成「唯一的标准」。反对她的人不是有不同意见，是「反对诚实的选举」，是「堕落」。

一个 76 岁的人，把这场运动说成「运动就是我」。这不是自嘲，这是她对自己位置的准确描述。问题不在于她还能推多远，问题在于：当一个人把「什么是事实」的制造权攥在手里，而制度一次次只是挡住她、却没有拆掉她建的那台机器，下一次她不需要总统的耳朵，她需要的是一个不问问题的人。她已经在找了。

Cleta Mitchell holds no formal position in the Trump administration, yet she moves in and out of the White House at will. This is not the legend of a shadow operator. This is meta-violence in its standard form: whoever monopolizes the manufacture of "what is fact" does not need a title.

One detail in ProPublica's reporting deserves three seconds of pause. The claim in Trump's primetime address that more than 275,000 noncitizens were on state voter rolls originated from work led by one of her proteges at the Department of Homeland Security — a claim the report says was later debunked. A debunked number, routed through the network of a woman with no official role, became a "fact" the president told the country. That is Chapter 3 exactly: seize discourse, seize interpretation, seize the manufacture of "what is fact." She does not need to win in court. She only needs enough people to believe the courts cannot be trusted.

Look at her path. In the 1970s she was a Democrat in Oklahoma, spearheading voter drives and helping launch the state chapter of the National Women's Political Caucus, a delegate at the 1972 Democratic National Convention. By the 1990s she had switched parties and was joking that she was the "consigliere to the vast right-wing conspiracy." Same person, two narratives. What changed between them was not conviction but the entrance to power. A colleague from her Democratic years said no one in his lifetime had changed more politically. This is not a story of betrayed ideals. The ideals were chips in the game from the start.

What matters is what she built. After 2020, while others walked away, she doubled down and founded the Election Integrity Network, growing it from seven state chapters in 2022 to at least 26 today. ProPublica's analysis of data shows she helped direct more than $70 million into this work between 2021 and 2025, at least $11 million of it from nonprofits she oversaw. Roughly 30 conservative election-integrity nonprofits were started after 2020; well over half have received money from her or have close ties to her.

This is not a lawyer litigating. This is a movement being built. She handed participants a 20-page manual and told them to master the rules and take seats on local election boards. In Michigan in 2022 she opened with "Welcome, fellow deplorables!" and "As an old Southern Baptist, I like evangelizing. So, this is your Bible!" Treating election procedure as a faith to be converted to is the textbook use of the religious entry point from Chapter 3: manufacture sacredness, and let the believers internalize the narrative as self-censorship. She does not need a whip. She needs local election workers to believe that not hunting noncitizens is dereliction.

What cuts sharpest in the reporting is not her success but her failure. The SAVE Act and its successor were blocked by moderate Republicans in the Senate. Executive orders were blocked by courts. Georgia's certification rule was struck down. In Ohio, the tougher version she insisted on was vetoed by the governor. Ken Blackwell, who has worked with her for decades, said, "It was her way or the highway," and that sometimes she makes elections less secure by torpedoing practical solutions.

So what does she do after failure? On Bannon's show, when he suggested declaring a national emergency, she went further: the president already has authority under a law protecting critical infrastructure to unilaterally enforce the bill's requirements. "We've got the blueprint. We'll help. We just need someone in authority to say, 'Let's do it.'" On the October 6 episode she repeated the word "today" seven times.

This is the pattern ProPublica names: escalate conflict into winner-take-all battles. When institutions do not comply, route around them. When courts say no, find someone who does not ask courts. She recommended Dan Bishop to take over the Georgia case; Bishop then tried to subpoena personal information for thousands of Fulton County poll workers, and a court quashed it.

Then ask who is complicit, and what the payoff is. At least 25 administration appointees have close ties to her. Heather Honey went from leading her Pennsylvania chapter to a senior counselor post at DHS touching almost everything the administration does on elections. A former federal official said Honey's "sole qualification was who she worked with." This is not conspiracy theory. This is Chapter 4: complicity does not require secret meetings. It requires aligned interests. The payoff is positions, resources, the steering wheel of the movement.

And who pays? The report is clear: voting advocacy groups say SAVE-style bills could disenfranchise millions of eligible voters, particularly women, the poor and minorities. It also reports that a North Carolina chapter participant tried to force his way into a voting office and that the chapter's leader was accused of following election workers in his car. That is direct violence, landing on specific people who count ballots.

Return to the word. She says nothing she does is designed or intended to "sway" any election outcome, that her only aim is to give voters confidence that outcomes are correct. That sentence is itself a specimen of weaponization: turn "make elections trustworthy" into "make elections trustworthy by my standard," then turn "my standard" into "the only standard." Those who resist are not people with a different view. They are "opposed to honest elections." They are "depraved."

A 76-year-old woman says the election-integrity movement is her: "c'est moi." That is not self-deprecation. It is an accurate description of her position. The question is not how much further she can push. The question is this: when one person holds the manufacture of "what is fact," and the institutions only block her without dismantling the machine she built, next time she will not need the president's ear. She will need someone who does not ask questions. She is already looking.
