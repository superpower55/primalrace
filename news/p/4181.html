性价比的陷阱：被‘廉价’掩盖的结构性抛弃The Value Trap: Structural Abandonment Masked by 'Bargains'

科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-19 § 链接
用价格锚点制造的‘性价比’，本质上是计划报废的文化暴力。
Value propositions based on price anchors are often cultural violence in the form of planned obsolescence.

这篇关于 Ninja 咖啡机的评测，精准地展示了现代商业如何通过 weaponized pricing（武器化定价）来操纵消费者的认知入口。当 Sage 的机器被标价在 1200 到 2500 英镑时，Ninja 549 英镑的价格就成了一个巨大的‘诱饵’，让消费者在潜意识里把‘省钱’等同于‘聪明’，从而心甘情愿地接受一个充满 quirk（怪癖）且难以维修的半成品。

最令人不安的不是机器本身的缺陷，而是那种 structural violence（结构性暴力）的闭环：Ninja 不向第三方销售内部零件，这意味着一旦过了保修期，这台机器就从‘性价比之选’变成了‘电子垃圾’。这种‘计划报废’的逻辑是典型的共谋场域——厂商通过降低耐用性来提高复购率，而消费者在‘低价’的幻觉中，成了自己被剥削的共谋者。

评测者提到的‘手动麻烦’（faff）其实是另一种表达的博弈。厂家将部分自动化流程剔除，却将其包装成‘手动完美的微光’，试图让用户在扮演‘业余咖啡师’的过程中获得某种主体性的错觉。但真正的最优解表达应该是：一个工具应当在保证功能公正的同时，不对使用者的生命周期（维修权）进行掠夺。

当一个产品在设计之初就决定了它的‘死亡日期’，且封锁了所有自救路径时，这已经不是一个简单的商业选择，而是一场关于控制权的 scam。

This review of the Ninja espresso machine perfectly illustrates how modern commerce uses weaponized pricing to hijack the cognitive entry point. By anchoring Sage machines at £1,200 to £2,500, Ninja’s £549 price tag becomes a lure, tricking consumers into equating 'saving money' with 'being smart,' making them complacent about a glitchy, unrepairable semi-product.

More disturbing than the technical flaws is the closed loop of structural violence: Ninja refuses to sell internal parts to third parties. This ensures that once the warranty expires, the machine transforms from a 'bargain' into 'e-waste.' This logic of planned obsolescence is a field of complicity—manufacturers boost repurchase rates by sacrificing durability, while consumers, blinded by the illusion of low cost, become accomplices in their own exploitation.

The 'faff' or manual effort mentioned by the reviewer is another game of expression. The company strips away automation but packages it as a 'glimmer of manual perfection,' attempting to give users a false sense of agency by letting them play the role of an 'amateur barista.' However, the true optimal expression would be a tool that ensures functional justice without plundering the user's lifecycle (the right to repair).

When a product is designed with a predetermined 'death date' and blocks all paths to self-rescue, it is no longer a simple business decision—it is a scam of control.
