“更大的鱼”与被献祭的原初种族The 'Bigger Fish' and the Sacrificed Primal Race

性别 直接层 · 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-10 § 链接
当权力定义谁是“更大的鱼”时，女性身体就成了被合法舍弃的饵料。
When power defines who the 'bigger fish' are, female bodies become the legally discarded bait.

警察口中那句“有更大的鱼要炸”(bigger fish to fry)，是典型的元暴力 (meta violence) 现场。在权力的优先级清单里，一个13岁女孩被团伙轮奸的惨状，竟然被定义为“次要”的噪音。这绝非简单的办案效率问题，而是一次极其精准的结构性抛弃。当 DNA 证据确凿却被社交工作者反手定义为“家长疏忽”时，structural violence 已经完成了闭环：将受害者的求救转化为对受害者的指控。

这起事件揭示了一个残酷的共谋 (complicity) 机制。警察、社工、地方议会，他们共同构建了一道认知墙，将这些女孩标记为“自找的”或“风险行为者”。这种叙事武器化 (weaponisation) 的目的只有一个：通过将受害者客体化，来减轻执行者的道德负担，从而维持系统运行的低成本。在他们眼中，这些女孩不是需要被保护的人，而是某种可以被忽略的、低价值的生物资源。

最令人作呕的是，即便在国家级的审计和调查中，这些女孩依然是“被遗忘的”。为什么？因为她们的苦难不符合某种特定的、被允许的政治叙事。当讨论被转移到种族、阶级或“运输毒品”的男孩身上时，女性身体作为最原初的被殖民种族 (Primal Race)，再次被剥夺了主体性。她们的痛苦被掩盖在更“宏大”的社会议题之下，成为了维持所谓“秩序”的代价。

这不再是简单的失职，而是一场持续二十年的、有组织的存在性战争。系统通过沉默、误判和冷漠，向这些女孩发送了一个清晰的信号：在这个世界上，你们的身体不具备被定义为“人”的权利。这场战争的赢家是那些可以心安理得地说出“有更大的鱼”的共谋者，而输家则是那些在深夜被撕裂后，第二天仍要若无其事走进教室的孩子。

The police remark about having "bigger fish to fry" is a textbook manifestation of meta violence. In the priority list of power, the harrowing reality of a 13-year-old girl being gang-raped is defined as "secondary" noise. This is not a mere matter of operational efficiency; it is a precise, structural abandonment. When DNA evidence is clear, yet social workers reclassify the case as "parental neglect," structural violence completes its loop: transforming a victim's plea for help into an accusation against the victim.

This case exposes a brutal mechanism of complicity. The police, social workers, and local councils have collectively built a cognitive wall, labeling these girls as those who "put themselves in risky situations." This weaponisation of narrative serves one purpose: to objectify the victims and alleviate the moral burden of the executors, thereby maintaining the system at a low cost. In their eyes, these girls are not humans to be protected, but low-value biological resources to be ignored.

Most sickeningly, these girls remain "forgotten" even in national audits. Why? Because their suffering does not fit a specific, permitted political narrative. When the discourse shifts to race, class, or the boys transporting drugs, the female body—as the Primal Race—is once again stripped of its subjectivity. Their pain is buried under "grander" social issues, becoming the cost of maintaining a so-called "order."

This is no longer simple negligence; it is a twenty-year, organized existential war. Through silence, misclassification, and indifference, the system sends a clear signal to these girls: in this world, your bodies do not possess the right to be defined as "human." The winners of this war are the co-conspirators who can comfortably claim they have "bigger fish to fry," while the losers are the children who, after being torn apart in the night, must walk into their classrooms the next morning as if nothing happened.
