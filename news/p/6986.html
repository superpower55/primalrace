他们怀念的是一个杀人的布景They're Nostalgic for a Killing Floor

国际 文化层 · 结构层 Mother Jones ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要美国佐治亚州共和党众议员迈克·柯林斯、德州铁路委员会共和党候选人博·弗伦奇、西德州众议院选区共和党候选人布兰登·埃雷拉等人近年公开引用或扮演津巴布韦前身、白人少数统治的罗得西亚；小唐纳德·特朗普的品牌售卖罗得西亚迷彩服饰，马斯克今年至少四次转发或评论相关内容。
The newsGeorgia Republican Rep. Mike Collins, Texas Railroad Commission GOP nominee Bo French and West Texas House GOP nominee Brandon Herrera have publicly referenced or cosplayed Rhodesia, the former white-minority state now Zimbabwe. Donald Trump Jr.'s brand sells Rhodesian-camo apparel, and Elon Musk has shared or discussed Rhodesia posts at least four times this year.
罗得西亚不是乡愁，是给不平等重新定价的广告
Rhodesia isn't nostalgia. It's an ad that reprices inequality.

罗得西亚在右翼那里回潮，靠的不是历史，是布景。短裤、头带、长枪、灌木丛，一半兄弟会一半兰博。这套画面卖的是感觉，感觉背后是一句报价：平等是自杀，等级才是文明。

这就是文化暴力在干活。加尔通的三角里，文化层的功能是让另外两层看起来不像暴力。罗得西亚十五年的存在，核心是白人少数对黑人多数的统治，报道里写得很清楚：白人政府以种族优越的名义抵抗黑人的自决。现在它被重新包装成一个「失落的伊甸园」，一个关于「选择平等而非择优的危险」的警示故事。把殖民统治说成择优，把多数人夺回权力说成文明的黄昏——这一句话就把结构暴力翻译成了审美。

武器化的入口在这里是审美和品味，第三章那一套。埃雷拉的YouTube视频里穿着罗得西亚迷彩开枪，背台词；小唐纳德·特朗普的Field Ethos卖149.99美元的「Rhodie camo」人字拖，还有那件「gin」衬衫，公司说是「向这个传奇图案和RLI的男人们致敬」。CEO的辩解很典型：迷彩「颜色温暖，笔触随意，气质休闲」，还说罗得西亚是种族和谐的 unlikely story，因为一半士兵是黑人——报道顺手点破了：黑人在政府里没有权力，在怀旧表情包里也集体缺席。被统治的人既不在决策里，也不在画面里，只在「和谐」的修辞里当背景板。

再往下一层，是共谋。柯林斯发帖，他有个住隔壁、在网上发纳粹内容的女婿；赫拉在2024年竞选时跳着鹅步配纳粹歌曲，自己说是讽刺，「funny as hell」；兰姆的儿子把「BE A MAN AMONG MEN」印上T恤，那是罗得西亚军队的招募海报；国土安全部发ICE招募广告用「BE A PATRIOT AMONG PATRIOTS」，被问到时既不承认也不否认，反手说批评者是「共产主义活动人士」，还说这类指责让联邦官员面临暴力。这一串人共享同一个利益站队：把白人男性气概重新定价为一种被围困的、值得捍卫的东西，而他们自己就是那个定价的人。回报是选票、流量、 merch 销量、以及一种「我们才是真正的受害者」的道德资本。

马斯克是这个共谋网络里最大的节点。他今年至少四次转发或评论罗得西亚内容，其中一次配的是「平等 doctrine 毁掉了一个天堂」的帖子，他回「嫉妒驱动了人类太多行为」；另一次他转发给2.46亿粉丝，说「很不幸，这就是美国要去的方向」。一个拥有平台的人，把「美国正在变成津巴布韦」当成警告发出去，这不是怀旧，这是在给一个种族等级秩序做预售。

报道里最锋利的一句来自历史学家伊丽莎白·施密特：罗得西亚白人把歧视性的隔离政策学自南非，南非学自美国，「现在绕了一圈回来了」。这句话把「非洲的遥远故事」拉回本土——美国右翼在罗得西亚身上照镜子，镜子里是他们自己想要的东西。

还有一层没人问：这些怀旧画面里的黑人去哪了？报道给了答案——他们不在表情包里，不在「和谐」的叙述里，不在「择优」的定义里。一个族群被从自己的土地上抹掉，然后被抹掉这件事本身被做成了一件149.99美元的衬衫。

真正的问题不是这些帖子有多疯，而是它们为什么现在能公开。2018年《纽约时报》还说公开支持罗得西亚在道德上站不住脚，八年后一个在任国会议员发罗得西亚梗，一个党内提名人在视频里扮演罗得西亚士兵，总统的儿子在卖迷彩拖鞋，世界首富在转发「美国正在变成津巴布韦」。道德门槛没有移动，是被搬走了。搬走它的人，正是那些从「平等是自杀」这句话里拿到选票、流量和销量的人。

The Rhodesia revival on the right isn't history. It's set design. Short shorts, headbands, long guns, the bush — half frat house, half Rambo. What's being sold is a feeling, and behind the feeling is a price quote: equality is suicide, hierarchy is civilization.

This is cultural violence doing its job. In Galtung's triangle, the cultural layer exists to make the other two layers stop looking like violence. Rhodesia's fifteen years were, at the core, white-minority rule over a Black majority — the report says it plainly: a white government resisting Black self-determination in the name of racial superiority. Now it's repackaged as a lost Eden, a cautionary tale about "the suicidal danger of choosing equality over merit." Calling colonial rule meritocracy and majority rule civilizational twilight — that one move translates structural violence into aesthetics.

The weaponized entry point here is taste. Herrera cosplays a Rhodesian soldier on YouTube, reciting Blood Diamond dialogue. Donald Trump Jr.'s Field Ethos sells $149.99 "Rhodie camo" flip-flops and a "gin" shirt it calls "our toast to this legendary pattern and the men of the RLI." The CEO's defense is textbook: the camo is "a very cool retro pattern," the colors warm, the brushstrokes haphazard, the vibe casual. He even calls Rhodesia an unlikely story of racial harmony because half its soldiers were Black — and the report quietly notes that Black Africans had no power in the government and are conspicuously absent from the nostalgic memes. The ruled appear neither in the decisions nor in the frame, only in the rhetoric of "harmony," as backdrop.

One layer down is complicity. Collins posts the meme; he has a Nazi-posting son-in-law living next door. Herrera goose-stepped to a Nazi song and called it "funny as hell." Cade Lamb put "BE A MAN AMONG MEN" on a T-shirt — a Rhodesian army recruiting slogan. DHS ran an ICE recruitment ad with "BE A PATRIOT AMONG PATRIOTS," and when asked, neither confirmed nor denied, instead calling critics "communist activists" and saying such smears expose officers to violence. These people share one interest: repricing white masculinity as something besieged and worth defending, with themselves as the appraisers. The payoff is votes, traffic, merch sales, and the moral capital of being the real victims.

Musk is the biggest node in that network. He has shared or discussed Rhodesia at least four times this year — replying "yes" to a post warning that "race communism" is coming for America, answering a post about "the doctrine of equality" destroying a paradise with "Envy drives so much of human behavior," and sharing another to 246 million followers with the warning: "Unfortunately, this is where America is headed." A man who owns the platform is not reminiscing. He's pre-selling a racial order.

The sharpest line in the piece belongs to historian Elizabeth Schmidt: Rhodesian whites modeled their segregation on South Africa, South Africa modeled itself on us, "and now we've come full circle." That pulls the "distant African story" back home. The American right is looking in a mirror, and the mirror shows what it wants.

And the question nobody asks: where are the Black people in these nostalgic images? The report answers — not in the memes, not in the "harmony" narrative, not in the definition of "merit." A people were erased from their own land, and the erasure itself became a $149.99 shirt.

The real story isn't how crazy the posts are. It's why they can be public now. In 2018 the New York Times said open support for Rhodesia was "morally untenable." Eight years later a sitting congressman posts Rhodesia memes, two GOP nominees cosplay Rhodesian soldiers, the president's son sells the camo, and the world's richest man shares "this is where America is headed." The moral threshold didn't move. It was carried away — by the same people who cash in on "equality is suicide."
