福利账单砍向年轻人之前Before the Welfare Bill Cuts the Young

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
账单是真的，砍的对象是挑过的
The bill is real. The target was chosen.

Healey 说要把福利账单降下来，方式是对年轻人“tough”。这句话里没有任何一个数字指向年轻人。OBR 给出的账是：福利总支出从去年的 3340 亿英镑涨到 2030 年的 4310 亿，其中大约一半给养老金领取者，四分之一给疾病和残疾福利（来源：The Guardian）。年轻人不在那四分之一里，也不在那二分之一里。真正在涨的两块，一块是老人，一块是病残，而 Healey 挑出来示众的是第三块——没接受工作或培训 offer 的年轻人。

这不是财政算术，是叙事的选角。养老金是选票，动不得；疾病和残疾福利上一次被 Starmer 碰过，被工党议员摁住了，Healey 自己说要避免“crude cuts”。剩下能拿来表演强硬、又不会立刻得罪任何有组织的选民群体的，就是年轻人。福利国家的账本上有一栏叫“不可持续”，但不可持续的部分被小心地绕开了，被点名的是最没有还手能力的那一栏。

他举的例子很诚实：90 年代末的工党政府，南约克郡五人里一人失业，那时候也是这么干的。他把这当成方法论的传承，其实是把二十多年前的剧本原样搬过来。那时的“tough”配上的是“real support and advice”，现在这条新闻里 support 和 advice 的具体数额、名额、由谁执行，一个字都没有。承诺在前，机制在后，机制还没写，强硬已经上了头条。

更值得看的是 No.10 随后的澄清：首相府说财政大臣没有设定削减福利账单的时间表，也不设“arbitrary target”。同一件事，上午是“会有削减”，下午是“没有时间表”。这不是口径混乱，这是把一句强硬的话放出去试水，看反应再决定它算不算数。真正要动的结构，从来不需要在广播采访里预告；需要预告的，是那种要拿去换政治分数的姿态。

预算里给每户的那 100 英镑能源折扣，Resolution Foundation 算过：2026-27 年典型年度能源账单在政府支持之后仍将到 1750 英镑，比 2022-23 年能源价格冲击时的 1685 英镑还高（来源：The Guardian）。也就是说，生活成本的支持被摊薄到几乎看不见，而账单本身比上一次危机时更贵。一边是给两千万户发一百镑，一边是告诉年轻人不接 offer 就别怪我们。钱没有变多，只是被重新分配了说法。

这套操作在暴力三角里落在结构层，靠文化层供弹药。“不可持续”“tough”“real potential”——这些词把一次资源分配的取舍包装成对年轻人潜力的信任。被削减的人如果抗议，就是不接受帮助、不愿工作；削减的人则是在负责任地理财。谁在共谋？每一个默认养老金不能碰、病残福利碰了会翻车、于是把刀口转向最安静那一群的人。回报是选票和“敢做事”的形象，代价由还没进入牌桌的人先付。

报道没有写的是：那些被认定“不接受支持”的年轻人里，有多少是照护者、有多少住在没有公交的镇上、有多少在等一个根本不存在的培训名额。这些名字没有出现在采访里，也没有出现在 OBR 的表格里。账单会降，降在哪一栏，等 spending review 出来就知道了——而到那时，强硬的话已经说完了。

Healey says he will bring the welfare bill down, with a "tough" approach to young people. Not one number in that sentence points at young people. The OBR's arithmetic: total welfare spending rises from £334bn last year to £431bn by 2030, about half of it going to pensioners and a quarter to sickness and disability benefits (source: The Guardian). Young people are in neither the half nor the quarter. The two lines actually growing are the old and the sick. Healey picked the third line to put on display.

This is not fiscal arithmetic. It is casting. Pensions are votes and cannot be touched. Sickness and disability benefits were touched once by Starmer and stopped by Labour MPs; Healey himself promises no "crude cuts." What remains is the group that can be made to look tough without immediately offending any organised constituency: the young. The ledger has a column labelled "unsustainable," but the unsustainable part is carefully stepped around, and the part named out loud is the one with the least capacity to hit back.

His example is honest to a fault: the Labour government of the late 90s, one in five unemployed in South Yorkshire, and they did exactly the same there. He offers this as lineage. It is a script from twenty-five years ago, reused. Back then "tough" came paired with "real support and advice." In this report, the size, the slots, and the administrators of that support are absent. The promise comes first, the mechanism later, and the toughness makes the headline.

Then No. 10 clarifies: the chancellor has set no timetable, no "arbitrary target." Same policy, morning and afternoon. This is not confusion. It is a hard line released to test the water, to be counted or uncounted depending on the response. Structural change does not need a radio interview to announce itself. What needs announcing is the posture that will be traded for political credit.

The £100 energy discount sits next to it. The Resolution Foundation calculates typical annual bills will reach £1,750 after government support in 2026-27 — higher than the £1,685 paid during the 2022-23 price shock (source: The Guardian). Cost-of-living support spread so thin it disappears, while the bill itself is larger than last crisis. A hundred pounds for twenty million households on one side; on the other, a warning to young people who decline an offer. The money did not grow. The wording was redistributed.

In the violence triangle this lands on the structural layer, fed by the cultural one. "Unsustainable," "tough," "real potential" — these words turn an allocation choice into faith in the young. Those cut are told they refused help. Those cutting are told they are being responsible. Who conspires? Everyone who accepts that pensions are untouchable, that disability benefits cost too much politically, and that the knife therefore goes to the quietest group. The return is votes and the look of decisiveness. The cost is paid first by people not yet at the table.

What the report does not say: how many of those "not taking up the offer" are carers, how many live in towns with no bus, how many are waiting for a training place that does not exist. Those names are not in the interview and not in the OBR tables. The bill will come down. Which column it comes down in will be clear at the spending review — by which time the tough words will already have been said.
