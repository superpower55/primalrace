用恐怖主义叙事为政治特权修补围墙Using Terrorism Narratives to Patch the Walls of Political Privilege

国际 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-02 § 链接
当暴力被定义为“恐怖主义”，它就从个体犯罪变成了加固权力阶层的武器。
When violence is defined as 'terrorism', it shifts from an individual crime to a tool for fortifying the power class.

一个 28 岁的男人用锤子击打一名 78 岁女性 21 次，这在 direct layer 是极其残暴的直接暴力。但有趣的是，当这个嫌疑人被指控计划攻击 Nigel Farage 时，这起案件迅速从一起血腥的谋杀案被升级为“恐怖主义准备行为”。

注意这种叙事的切换。一旦被打上 terrorism 的标签，整个国家的反恐机器（CTP）便可以合法地启动最高级别的资源投入。这种升级不仅仅是为了正义，而是一种典型的武器化表达（weaponized expression）。它将一个具体的暴力个案，迅速转化为一次针对“政治阶层”的集体威胁。内政大臣 Shabana Mahmood 的反应极其精准：她没有过多讨论受害者的惨状，而是迅速将话题导向“议员安全”和“加强保护”。

这就是典型的元暴力（meta violence）运作方式：将政治人物的特权安全定义为“民主的基石”。在这种叙事下，对政治精英的潜在威胁被等同于对民主制度的攻击。这种逻辑在 structural layer 创造了一个特权闭环——权力持有者通过定义“谁在被攻击”，来合法地申请更多资源、更多监控权力、更厚实的保护围墙。

我们看到一种奇怪的共谋（complicity）：无论政治立场如何，从内政大臣到 Jo Cox 基金会的执行官，都在迅速达成共识——政治人物需要被特殊保护。而这种保护的代价，往往是进一步强化政治阶层与普通大众之间的生物墙。当暴力被定义为恐怖主义，它就不再是关于一个死去的女性，而是变成了权力阶层在面对不确定性时，一次成功的 PR 升级和资源扩容。

A 28-year-old man hitting a 78-year-old woman 21 times with a hammer is, in the direct layer, an act of extreme brutality. But it is fascinating how, the moment this suspect was linked to a planned attack on Nigel Farage, the case was rapidly upgraded from a bloody murder to 'preparation for acts of terrorism'.

Notice this shift in narrative. Once the label of terrorism is applied, the state's entire counter-terrorism machinery (CTP) can legitimately mobilize maximum resources. This upgrade is not merely about justice; it is a classic weaponized expression. It transforms a specific act of violence into a collective threat against the 'political class'. Home Secretary Shabana Mahmood's reaction was surgically precise: she spent little time on the victim's suffering, instead pivoting immediately to 'MP security' and 'strengthening protections'.

This is exactly how meta violence operates: defining the privileged security of political figures as the 'bedrock of democracy'. In the structural layer, this logic creates a privileged loop—those in power define 'who is being attacked' to legitimately claim more resources, more surveillance powers, and thicker protective walls.

We see a clear complicity here: regardless of political leaning, from the Home Secretary to the Jo Cox Foundation, there is a rapid consensus that politicians require special protection. The cost of this protection is the further reinforcement of the biological and social wall between the political elite and the general public. When violence is rebranded as terrorism, it ceases to be about a dead woman and becomes a successful PR upgrade and resource expansion for the power class in the face of uncertainty.
