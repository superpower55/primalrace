伊甸园东边的贫民窟：美国梦的定价权骗局The Slum East of Eden: The Pricing Scam of the American Dream

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-03 § 链接
所谓的“中产阶级”不过是结构性暴力中被精准定价的缓冲垫。
The so-called 'middle class' is merely a buffer zone precisely priced by structural violence.

当 Netflix 把《伊甸园东边》重新搬上屏幕，萨利纳斯谷（Salinas Valley）再次成为了一个浪漫的文化符号。但现实是，这个被誉为“世界沙拉碗”的地方，正在上演一场极其残酷的 existental war。当市长还在用文学叙事承诺一个“快乐结局”时，60% 的当地中产阶级甚至无法负担基本生活必需品。这就是典型的 cultural violence：用一个关于“希望”和“奋斗”的宏大叙事，掩盖 structural violence 正在对个体进行地毯式收割的事实。

最讽刺的是关于“中产阶级”的定义。在这个谷地，年入 19 万美元依然可能被定义为“难以生存”。这意味着所谓的 middle class 已经失去了其原本的阶级属性，变成了一个被资本市场精准定价的缓冲垫——他们太“富”而无法获得低保救济，又太“穷”而无法在湾区经济的溢出效应中买到一张入场券。这是一种极其高效的剥削：让一个群体在“即将跨入中产”的幻觉中，心甘情愿地支付掉他们绝大部分的生命时间去填补房租的黑洞。

萨利纳斯的租金管制之争，本质上是共谋者的博弈。房东担心房产维护成本，租客担心被扫地出门。而真正的元暴力（meta violence）隐藏在更高层：硅谷的经济逻辑像寄生虫一样吸干了周边卫星城的生存空间。当地政府在 2008 年计划建设 11,500 套住房，结果在金融危机和疫情的反复蹂躏下变成了空谈。这种“计划中”的救济，不过是 structural violence 的 PR 版本，是用一个未来的 promise 来换取当下的顺从。

当一个博士学位持有者在 75 岁时还得计算炖菜能吃几天，当消防员被告知必须拥有“双薪”才能在这个城市生存，所谓的“美国梦”就成了一个巨大的 scam。它不再是关于阶级跃迁的可能，而是一套被武器化的认知入口，诱导人们在一个名为“伊甸园”的陷阱里，通过扮演一个勤奋的受害者来维持一个已经崩塌的系统。

As Netflix brings *East of Eden* back to the screen, the Salinas Valley is once again treated as a romantic cultural symbol. In reality, this 'salad bowl of the world' is a site of brutal existential war. While the mayor uses literary narratives to promise a 'happy ending,' 60% of the local middle class cannot even afford basic necessities. This is textbook cultural violence: using a grand narrative of 'hope' and 'striving' to mask the fact that structural violence is conducting a carpet-bombing of individual lives.

The most cynical part is the definition of the 'middle class.' In this valley, earning $190,000 can still mean struggling to survive. The middle class has lost its class attribute and become a buffer zone precisely priced by the market—too 'rich' for low-income subsidies, yet too 'poor' to buy a ticket into the overflow of Silicon Valley's economy. It is a highly efficient form of exploitation: keeping a group in the illusion of 'almost making it' while they spend the vast majority of their life-time filling the black hole of rent.

The battle over rent stabilization in Salinas is essentially a game of complicity. Landlords fear maintenance costs; tenants fear eviction. But the meta violence is hidden higher up: the economic logic of Silicon Valley acts like a parasite, draining the living space of its satellite cities. The local government's 2008 plan for 11,500 housing units became a joke after the Great Recession and the pandemic. Such 'planned' relief is merely the PR version of structural violence, trading a future promise for current submission.

When a PhD holder at 75 must calculate how many days a stew will last, and when a firefighter is told they need 'two incomes' just to stay in town, the 'American Dream' is revealed as a massive scam. It is no longer about the possibility of social mobility, but a weaponized cognitive entry, inducing people to play the role of the 'diligent victim' to sustain a collapsed system in a trap called 'Eden.'
