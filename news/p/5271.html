牛津大学的“数字化”骗局与认知入口的终极收割The Oxford Digitization Scam and the Ultimate Harvest of Cognitive Entry

科技 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-26 § 链接
用“普及知识”掩盖“喂养机器”，是最高级的文化共谋。
Masking 'feeding the machine' as 'democratizing knowledge' is the highest form of cultural complicity.

这是一场典型的 weaponized 叙事。牛津大学和 OpenAI 把这次交易包装成“让历史文献更易获取”的公益之举，试图用一个 a priori 的正义感来掩盖其本质：对人类最后一块未经数字化处女地的掠夺。当 OpenAI 说他们要“保护世界历史知识”时，这简直是一个巨大的 scam。保护知识的方式是让人们阅读它，而不是将其转化为权重参数，然后通过一个付费订阅的聊天框，把经过过滤、阉割且带有算法偏见的结论重新卖给用户。

这里的共谋机制非常阴险。牛津大学在内部会议中担心“名誉风险”和“环保承诺”，这说明他们很清楚自己在做什么——他们在出卖解释权。通过将 Bodleian Library 的海量文本交给 OpenAI，牛津实际上是在帮一个私人公司构建一个关于“历史”的认知入口。未来的学生不再需要走进图书馆去面对原件的复杂性，而只需要询问“Ask the Bod”机器人。这意味着人类对历史的理解，将从“面对证据的思考”降级为“对模型输出的认同”。

更令人作呕的是这种对物理世界的物理性掠夺。Anthropic 把绝版书切掉书脊然后 pulped（纸浆化），这已经不是文化暴力，而是 direct violence。而牛津虽然保留了原件，但在 structural 层面上，它完成了同样的交接：将公共知识产权转化为私有公司的竞争壁垒。当互联网被 AI 生成的垃圾信息填满，这些物理书籍成了唯一的“新鲜数据”，此时它们不再是文化遗产，而变成了 AI 时代的“矿产”。

这场交易的本质是元暴力的升级。男性中心叙事在历史上垄断了书写权，而现在，硅谷的资本垄断了对这些书写内容的解释权。所谓的“让更多人接触”，不过是给这台巨大的收割机贴上了一张人道主义的标签。

This is a textbook case of weaponized narrative. Oxford University and OpenAI have packaged this deal as a philanthropic effort to make historical texts 'more accessible,' attempting to use a pre-packaged sense of justice to mask the reality: the predation of the last untapped virgin land of human data.

When OpenAI claims they are 'preserving the world's historical knowledge,' it is a complete scam. Preserving knowledge means enabling people to read it, not converting it into weights and parameters, only to sell back filtered, castrated, and biased conclusions through a paid subscription chatbox.

The mechanism of complicity here is insidious. Oxford's internal concerns about 'reputational risk' and 'environmental commitments' prove they know exactly what they are doing—they are selling the right of interpretation. By handing the Bodleian's archives to OpenAI, Oxford is helping a private corporation construct a cognitive entry point for 'history.' Future students will no longer face the complexity of original documents; they will simply ask the 'Ask the Bod' chatbot. Human understanding of history is thus downgraded from 'thinking via evidence' to 'agreeing with model output.'

Even more repulsive is the physical predation. Anthropic slicing off book spines and pulping them is no longer just cultural violence; it is direct violence. While Oxford keeps its physical copies, it has achieved the same result at the structural level: converting public intellectual heritage into a private company's competitive moat. As the web becomes saturated with AI-generated sludge, these physical books are no longer heritage—they are 'minerals' for the AI era.

This is an escalation of meta-violence. If masculine-centric narratives once monopolized the act of writing, Silicon Valley capital now monopolizes the interpretation of those writings. The claim of 'wider access' is nothing more than a humanitarian label slapped onto a massive industrial harvester.
