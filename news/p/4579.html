失踪名单里的国籍与被抹除的底层Nationalities in the Missing List and the Erased Underclass

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
关注点的国籍化，是结构性暴力对生命价值的二次定价。
The nationalization of attention is a secondary pricing of human life by structural violence.

新闻的标题精准地捕捉到了一个 13 岁的英国女孩，以及 12 名英国公民的失踪。在 384 个失踪者和 95 具尸体的血腥背景下，这种叙事方式极其典型：它将生命的价值与 passport 绑定。当一个英国女孩失踪，这成了一则国际新闻；而当 100 多个国籍尚未确认的失踪者在泥石流中被抹除时，他们只是一个统计数字。

这不仅是新闻业的惯例，更是 structural violence 的具体呈现。在尼泊尔这个被殖民历史和经济依附定义的空间里，西方游客的“冒险”被包装成探索，而当地民众在同一片土地上遭遇的灾难则被视为“自然现象”。同一个 flash flood，对英国公民是突发的 tragedy，对当地被冲毁房屋和道路的贫民来说，则是生存空间的日常崩塌。

最讽刺的是那些使馆的 joint statement，用“深感悲痛”和“团结”这种 weaponized 情感词汇来掩盖权力不对等。在这种叙事里，救援的优先级、信息的透明度、甚至被报道的频率，都遵循着一套隐形的定价权。生命的 Potential 被 Actual 的国籍等级给截断了，差额就是暴力。

我们不该只问那个 13 岁女孩在哪里，而该问：那些没有国籍确认、没有使馆发声的失踪者，在谁的眼中才是“人”？

The headline precisely captures a 13-year-old British girl and 12 missing British citizens. Against a backdrop of 384 missing and 95 recovered bodies, this narrative is textbook: it binds the value of life to a passport. When a British girl goes missing, it is international news; when over 100 people of unconfirmed nationality are erased by mudslides, they are merely a statistic.

This is more than just journalistic convention; it is a manifestation of structural violence. In Nepal, a space defined by colonial history and economic dependency, the "adventures" of Western tourists are framed as exploration, while the disasters hitting local populations on the same soil are dismissed as "natural phenomena." The same flash flood is a sudden tragedy for British citizens, but for the locals whose homes and roads were swept away, it is the routine collapse of their survival space.

Most ironic are the joint statements from embassies, using weaponized emotional terms like "deeply saddened" and "solidarity" to mask power imbalances. In this narrative, the priority of rescue, the transparency of information, and even the frequency of reporting follow an invisible pricing power. The Potential of life is truncated by the Actual hierarchy of nationality; the difference is violence.

We should not only ask where the 13-year-old girl is, but ask: for those without confirmed nationalities or embassy voices, in whose eyes are they actually human?
