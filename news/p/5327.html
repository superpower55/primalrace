奥斯本的“尺度”与乡村的“代价”Osborne's Scale and the Rural Cost

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-29 § 链接
所谓“阻碍进步”的 NIMBY，本质上是结构性暴力对局部生存权的掠夺。
The so-called NIMBYism is essentially the plunder of local survival rights by structural violence.

George Osborne 把反对 AI 数据中心的居民定义为 NIMBY (Not In My Back Yard)，这套叙事是典型的武器化表达：通过将具体的生存焦虑标签化为“狭隘的自私”，从而在文化层面上抹除对方的合法性。在奥斯本的逻辑里，AI 基础设施的扩张是宏大的“进步”，而乡村居民对噪音、光污染和生态破坏的恐惧则成了阻碍国家竞争力的“噪音”。

这其实是一场关于“尺度”的博弈。当一个比城镇本身还大的数据中心被强行植入德文郡的农田，这种 structural violence 表现为资源分配的极度不对称：AI 巨头拿走了计算力带来的指数级利润，而当地居民承担了物理层面的环境损耗。奥斯本在公共空间定义的“进步”，是建立在将乡村客体化、工具化基础之上的。对于他来说，那 850 英亩土地只是地图上的一个坐标，但对于当地人，那是他们的 biological wall，是生活质量的底线。

最讽刺的共谋在于，这类政治精英习惯于在宏观叙事中扮演“文明的推动者”，但正如读者信中所揭露的，他们对“后院”的定义具有极强的阶级排他性。当权力掌握在手中时，他们定义什么是事实，什么是进步；而当这种“进步”真正触碰到他们自己的草坪时，他们才会发现，原来被剥夺主体性的痛苦并不是通过一个标签就能消解的。这场战争不是关于 AI，而是关于谁有权决定谁的家园被牺牲。

George Osborne labels those opposing AI datacentres as NIMBYs, a classic weaponisation of expression. By tagging concrete survival anxiety as 'narrow selfishness,' he erases the legitimacy of the opposition at a cultural level. In Osborne's narrative, AI expansion is a grand 'progress,' while the fear of noise, light pollution, and ecological collapse in Devon is merely 'noise' hindering national competitiveness.

This is a game of scale. When a datacentre larger than the town itself is imposed on farmland, the structural violence manifests as a gross asymmetry in resource distribution: AI giants reap exponential profits from computing power, while locals absorb the physical environmental costs. The 'progress' Osborne defines in the public sphere is built upon the objectification and instrumentalisation of the countryside. To him, 850 acres are just coordinates on a map; to the residents, it is their biological wall and the baseline of their quality of life.

The most cynical complicity lies in how these political elites perform as 'promoters of civilisation' in macro-narratives, while maintaining a class-based exclusivity regarding their own 'back yards.' When they hold power, they define what is fact and what is progress. Only when this 'progress' actually touches their own lawns do they realise that the pain of losing one's agency cannot be dissolved by a simple label. This war is not about AI; it is about who holds the power to decide whose home must be sacrificed.
