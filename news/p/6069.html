处决失败，国家想的还是再杀一次The State's Plan After a Failed Killing: Kill Again

国际 结构层 · 文化层 Truthout ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
她活下来，不是正义到场，是国家失手
She survived not because justice arrived, but because the state missed.

Christa Pike 活下来了，这不是赦免，是田纳西州没杀成。两次注射戊巴比妥都没能把她杀死，她被救护车送进范德堡大学医学中心。一个以“司法”名义执行的程序，最后靠急救兜底——这已经不是执行失败，而是国家在展示它连杀人都不专业。

真正说明问题的是 Bill Lee 的表态。这位州长在处决失败后叫停了田纳西州今年剩下的死刑，转头却说，Pike“仍然依据田纳西州法律在押”，他猜“她的刑期会和之前一样，除非法院另有裁定”。翻译过来就是：我们还会再试一次。一个已经用两次失败证明自己无法“人道处决”的机构，给出的方案是再来一遍。这不是正义，这是流程不肯认输。

Pike 的陈述里有一句被所有废死派引用的话：“自那晚起，我没有一刻不在疼痛。”手臂、手、喉咙，还有精神和灵魂。她在死牢单独监禁了 28 年，如今又被隔离在监狱医疗病房——她说，这和当年那种状态是重复。国家先把她从人群里拿走二十八年，再在她身上试了两次针，最后把她放回同一间隔离病房，然后说这叫服刑。

这里动的不是直接暴力那一层——直接暴力是 1995 年 Colleen Slemmer 的死，19 岁，Pike 因此被判死刑。动的也不是“她是否无辜”这一层：报道没有替她翻案，她的律师打的是她青年时期长期遭受的虐待。真正在动的是结构层：一个州拥有合法杀人的权力，执行失败后，权力不检讨自己是否该有这项权力，只检讨针头插得对不对。Commissioner Frank Strada 宣布辞职，审查启动，Amnesty International USA 说这证明了死刑是“终极的残忍、不人道和有辱人格的惩罚”。都对，但都绕开了那个更简单的问题：如果国家连“干净地杀一个人”都做不到，它凭什么保留杀人的资格？

还有一层没人愿意写进去。Pike 说“我继续想到受害者家属承受的痛苦和失去”。这句话在报道里只占一行，但它把整件事的账目摆平了：一个女人的身体被扎了两次没死，另一个家庭失去的 19 岁女儿不会因为这次失败回来。死刑号称替后者讨还，实际做的是在前者身上再制造一个受害者，然后让两个家庭一起等下一轮。

Lee 说“除非法院另有裁定”。法院已经裁定过一次，让她去死。现在的问题是，同一套机器准备再裁定一次。Pike 说她不知道那晚为什么活下来，也不知道接下来是什么。田纳西州知道：下一针。

Christa Pike is alive. That is not clemency. Tennessee failed to kill her. Two injections of pentobarbital, and she had to be taken by ambulance to Vanderbilt University Medical Center. When a procedure carried out in the name of justice ends with emergency care, it is no longer an execution gone wrong. It is the state demonstrating it cannot even kill competently.

What actually explains the situation is Bill Lee's statement. The governor halted executions in Tennessee through the end of the year after the failure, then said Pike "is still incarcerated under the laws of the state of Tennessee" and that he suspects "her sentence will be the same as it was before unless determined otherwise by the courts." Translated: we will try again. An institution that has just proven, twice, that it cannot carry out a "humane" execution proposes to repeat it. That is not justice. That is a process refusing to admit defeat.

One line in Pike's statement gets quoted by every abolitionist: "There hasn't been a moment since that night when I haven't been in pain." Arms, hands, throat, mind, soul. She spent 28 years in solitary confinement. Now she is isolated in the prison's medical ward, and she says it is a repeat of that condition. The state removed her from human contact for twenty-eight years, put two needles in her, returned her to the same kind of isolation, and calls this serving a sentence.

The layer in motion here is not direct violence. Direct violence is the 1995 murder of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, for which Pike was sentenced to death. Nor is it the question of innocence: the report does not exonerate her; her legal fight stems from the abuse she suffered throughout her youth. What is moving is the structural layer. A state holds the legal power to kill. When the killing fails, the power does not ask whether it should exist. It asks whether the needle went in right. Commissioner Frank Strada has announced he will step down; a review is underway; Amnesty International USA says the case shows the death penalty is "the ultimate cruel, inhuman, and degrading punishment." All true, and all sidestepping the simpler question: if the state cannot cleanly kill a person, why does it keep the right to try?

There is one more entry in the ledger that nobody wants to write down. Pike says she continues to think about "my victim's family and the terrible pain and loss they have endured." That is one line in the report, and it balances the whole account. One woman's body was pierced twice and did not die. Another family's 19-year-old daughter does not come back because of that failure. The death penalty claims to settle that debt. What it actually does is manufacture a second victim out of the first, then leave both families waiting for the next round.

Lee says "unless determined otherwise by the courts." The courts already determined once that she should die. The question now is whether the same machine is preparing to determine it again. Pike says she does not know why she survived that night, or what comes next. Tennessee knows: the next needle.
