石油换算力，谁在给谁定价Oil for Compute, and Who Prices Whom

科技 结构层 · 元暴力 semafor.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要OpenAI CEO 萨姆·奥尔特曼在阿布扎比签下四项协议，最大一项与 ADNOC 及其 G42 子公司 Inception42 签署，多年期，将 OpenAI 的 Frontier 平台用于油田开发、生产、炼油和交易决策，运行在 G42 的阿联酋基础设施上，财务条款未披露。阿布扎比政府将让部分政策可被 AI 代理读取，Mubadala 用 OpenAI 模型分析潜在交易。包括 Mubadala 支持的 MGX 在内的阿联酋投资方预计成为 OpenAI 最新一轮融资最大投资方，并获得该地区新算力容量的租赁选择权。
The newsOpenAI CEO Sam Altman signed four agreements in Abu Dhabi, the largest a multi-year deal with ADNOC and G42 subsidiary Inception42 to run OpenAI's Frontier platform across oilfield development, production, refining and trading on G42's UAE infrastructure; financial terms were not disclosed. Abu Dhabi's government will make some policies readable by AI agents, and Mubadala will use OpenAI models to analyze potential deals. A UAE consortium including Mubadala-backed MGX is expected to be the largest investor in OpenAI's latest funding round, with an option to lease new computing capacity in the region.
融资的尽头是主权基金，算力的尽头是石油。
Funding ends in sovereign wealth; compute ends in oil.

奥尔特曼在阿布扎比签下四份协议，最大的一份是把 OpenAI 的 Frontier 平台接进 ADNOC 的油田开发、生产、炼油和交易决策。同一天，包括 MGX 在内的阿联酋财团被报道将成为 OpenAI 最新一轮融资的最大投资方。这两件事放在一起看，就不是「AI 落地能源行业」的产业新闻，是一笔用算力换石油、用石油换股权的对敲。

先看谁在买什么。ADNOC 买的是把上游决策交给模型；阿布扎比政府买的是让自己的政策变成 AI 代理可读的格式；Mubadala 买的是用 OpenAI 的模型筛交易。三笔都是同一个方向：把判断权从人手里挪进一个由 G42 的基础设施托着的黑箱。而 G42 是谁的基础设施，协议写得很清楚。

再看谁在付什么。财务条款未披露，但答案在融资那一头：阿联酋财团拿到的不只是股份，还有该地区新算力容量的租赁选择权。石油美元买下模型公司的一部分，换回来的是算力的优先使用权。这是第三章那套东西的当代版本——认知入口不再靠广告和叙事去买，直接靠资本买断。谁出钱，谁就先拿到「什么是事实」的制造工具。

奥尔特曼在台上被问最大的挑战，一个字：能源。这句话放在 ADIPEC 说，放在埃克森和雪佛龙高管因安全顾虑缺席的会场说，意思很直白：模型再便宜，电不够就长不大。于是 AI 扩张的瓶颈从芯片挪到了电网，而电网背后是化石能源，化石能源背后是主权基金，主权基金现在又是 OpenAI 的股东。一个闭环，每一环都在给自己定价。

这里没有受害者出镜，代价是弥散的：阿联酋的公共政策被做成机器可读的接口，油田的决策链被一个不公开条款的模型接管，而这些模型的电力成本最终由谁的土地、谁的电价、谁的气候来承担，报道一个字没写。没写，就是材料。

真正值得盯的不是这四份协议，是那个「政策可被 AI 代理读取」的条款。它把政府的解释权外包给了一个模型，而模型的股东里坐着同一个政府的主权基金。元暴力从来不需要鞭子，它只需要让所有人相信，决策本来就该这么下。

Altman signed four agreements in Abu Dhabi. The largest plugs OpenAI's Frontier platform into ADNOC's oilfield development, production, refining and trading decisions. The same day, a UAE consortium including MGX was reported to be the largest investor in OpenAI's latest round. Read together, this is not an industry story about AI meeting energy. It is a swap: compute for oil, oil for equity.

Look at what is being bought. ADNOC buys the handover of upstream decisions to a model. Abu Dhabi's government buys having its policies made readable to AI agents. Mubadala buys using OpenAI's models to screen deals. All three point the same way: judgment moves out of human hands into a black box sitting on G42 infrastructure, and the agreement says whose infrastructure that is.

Look at what is being paid. Financial terms were not disclosed, but the answer sits on the funding side: the UAE consortium gets not only shares but an option to lease new computing capacity in the region. Petrodollars buy a piece of the model company and get back priority access to compute. This is the weaponization playbook in its current form. You no longer buy the cognitive entry point through advertising and narrative. You buy it outright with capital. Whoever pays first gets the machine that manufactures facts.

Asked onstage about his biggest challenge, Altman gave one word: energy. Said at ADIPEC, at a conference Exxon and Chevron executives skipped over security concerns, it means something plain. Models can get cheaper, but without power they stop growing. The bottleneck moves from chips to the grid, the grid runs on fossil fuel, fossil fuel runs on sovereign funds, and the sovereign funds now sit among OpenAI's shareholders. A closed loop, each link pricing itself.

No victim appears on camera. The cost is diffuse: public policy turned into a machine-readable interface, an oil decision chain handed to a model with undisclosed terms, and the question of whose land, whose electricity prices and whose climate carry the power bill, which the report does not touch. What is missing is the material.

The clause to watch is not the oil deal. It is the one making government policy readable by AI agents. That outsources the state's interpretive authority to a model whose shareholders include the same state's sovereign fund. Meta-violence never needs a whip. It only needs everyone to believe decisions were always meant to be made this way.
