食谱里的“空白画布”与中产阶级的殖民快感The 'Blank Slate' in Recipes and the Colonial Pleasure of the Middle Class

哲学 文化层 The New York Times ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
将他者文化简化为可随意涂抹的“画布”，是典型的文化暴力。
Reducing another's culture to a 'blank slate' for personal creativity is a form of cultural violence.

纽约时报的一份墨西哥风味食谱，本质上是一次关于“认知入口”的微型殖民。它把一种具有深厚地缘背景和历史沉淀的 Guisado 简化成了 35 分钟的快速工业流程。最耐人寻味的是评论区里那些中产阶级的狂欢：他们把这道菜称为“blank slate”（空白画布），兴奋地讨论着如何用烟熏红椒粉、乔利佐香肠甚至随便什么冰箱剩余物去“创造”和“改良”。

这种“创造力”的背后是极度的傲慢。当一个文化被剥离了其原生的结构层，被简化为一组可替换的 Ingredients 时，它就变成了某种可以被随意消费的“审美符号”。在这些评论者眼中，墨西哥饮食不再是一种生存状态或身份表达，而是一个可以被随意涂抹的底色。这种将他者文化“工具化”的行为，正是文化暴力 (cultural violence) 的典型样本——它让这种剥削看起来像是“热爱”和“创意”。

这种叙事逻辑与大公司垄断认知入口如出一辙：定义什么是“正宗”，然后定义如何“改良”它。当一个群体掌握了定义“好品味”的定价权，他们就可以在享受他者文化带来的异域快感的同时，通过“优化”对方的表达来确立自己的优越感。这根本不是在烹饪，而是在进行一场关于文化解释权的微型博弈。

A Mexican-inspired recipe from The New York Times is essentially a micro-colonization of cognitive entry points. It reduces the deeply rooted historical and geographical identity of a Guisado into a 35-minute industrial process. The most telling part is the middle-class euphoria in the comments: they describe the dish as a "blank slate," excitedly discussing how to "create" or "improve" it using smoked paprika, chorizo, or whatever leftovers happen to be in the fridge.

This perceived "creativity" is rooted in profound arrogance. When a culture is stripped of its structural layer and reduced to a set of interchangeable ingredients, it becomes a mere aesthetic symbol for consumption. To these commenters, Mexican cuisine is no longer a state of existence or an expression of identity, but a backdrop to be painted over. This instrumentalization of another's culture is a textbook example of cultural violence—it makes exploitation look like "love" and "innovation."

This narrative logic mirrors how large corporations monopolize the right to define reality: first, they define what is "authentic," and then they define how to "optimize" it. When a group holds the pricing power over "good taste," they can enjoy the exotic pleasure of another culture while asserting their own superiority by "improving" the other's expression. This isn't cooking; it is an existential war over the right of interpretation.
