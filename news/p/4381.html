在‘幸福婚姻’的叙事里，谁在支付隐形成本The Hidden Cost of a 'Perfect Marriage'

性别 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
所谓‘完美的陪伴’，往往是女性在结构性牺牲中完成了对男性的共谋。
What is framed as 'perfect companionship' is often a woman's complicity in her own structural sacrifice.

这篇文章读起来像是一篇标准的、充满温情的退休生活特写，但如果用加尔通的暴力三角去审视，这种‘温馨’恰恰是 cultural violence 的高级伪装。Russ Abbot 谈论他的成功，谈论他 1984 年一年工作 363 天的勤奋，而他口中那个‘支持他的一切’的妻子 Trish，在叙事中被简化成了一个提供咖啡、饼干和偶尔咯咯笑的背景板。

请注意这个细节：Abbot 坦然地承认自己在事业巅峰期‘总是外出’，而 Trish 则‘独自抚养四个孩子’。在 masculine-centric narrative 中，这被描述为一种默契的家庭分工，甚至被包装成一种‘伟大的支持’。但本质上，这是一种 structural violence：女性的生命时间被强制性地分配给了无偿的养育劳动，从而在公共空间彻底消失，以换取男性在事业上的 ‘soar to success’。

最令人不安的是 Trish 本人的表达。她用一种近乎自嘲的幽默说，自己当年是‘认错了人’才嫁给了 Abbot。这种幽默不是主体性的觉醒，而是一种在长期共谋中习得的生存策略。在父权结构下，女性通过扮演‘贤内助’、通过将自己的需求最小化，来换取在家庭内部的相对稳定。Trish 的‘幸福’是建立在对自身主体性让渡的基础之上的，她成为了 Abbot 成功叙事中的一个注脚。

这种叙事最危险的地方在于，它让这种掠夺看起来像是一种‘爱’。当 Abbot 在葡萄牙的别墅里回忆往昔荣光时，他并未意识到，他所享受的这种‘富足的退休’，很大程度上是由 Trish 几十年前在私人领域里被剥夺的 Potential 支付的。这种差额，就是暴力。

This piece reads like a standard, heartwarming retirement profile, but through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this 'warmth' is a sophisticated camouflage for cultural violence. Russ Abbot speaks of his success and his diligence—working 363 days in 1984—while his wife Trish, who 'supported everything,' is reduced to a background character providing coffee, biscuits, and occasional giggles.

Notice the detail: Abbot casually admits he was 'always away' during his peak, while Trish 'raised them all.' In a masculine-centric narrative, this is framed as a tacit family division of labor or even 'great support.' In reality, it is structural violence: a woman's life-time is forcibly allocated to unpaid care work, causing her to vanish from the public sphere so the man can 'soar to success.'

Most disturbing is Trish's own expression. She uses a self-deprecating humor, claiming she 'got the wrong bloke.' This isn't an awakening of subjectivity, but a survival strategy learned through long-term complicity. Within the patriarchal structure, women obtain relative stability by playing the 'supportive wife' and minimizing their own needs. Trish's 'happiness' is built upon the surrender of her own agency; she has become a footnote in Abbot's success story.

The danger of this narrative is that it frames exploitation as 'love.' As Abbot reminisces about his glory days in a Portuguese villa, he fails to realize that his 'richly deserved retirement' was largely paid for by the Potential stripped from Trish in the private sphere decades ago. That gap is exactly where the violence lies.
