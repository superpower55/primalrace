他造了传送门，又亲手拆了He Built the Warp, Then Tore It Out

其他 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要任天堂在《塞尔达传说：时之笛》重制版发售前发布开发团队访谈。制作人青沼英二说，他曾要求团队加入原版没有的快速旅行传送点，但在试玩后认为传送点削弱了途中「等等，这是什么」的发现时刻，于是让团队全部撤掉。重制版将于 11 月 5 日在 Switch 2 发售，仍会加入冲刺按键。
The newsNintendo shared a developer Q&A ahead of the Nov. 5 release of its Ocarina of Time remake on Switch 2. Producer Eiji Aonuma said he asked the team to add warp points absent from the original, then had them removed after playtesting showed the fast travel erased the 「wait, what's this?」 moments of discovery along the way. The remake will still add a sprint button.
设计一个让人绕路的系统，比让人直达更难。
Designing a system that makes people take the long way is harder than letting them skip.

青沼英二让团队做了一套快速旅行系统，试玩之后又让他们全部拆掉。这条新闻表面上是「开发者尊重原作精神」的温情小故事，实际上是一次关于「绕路」的定价：他把玩家省下的时间，换成了玩家在路上撞见意外的时间。

这个交换值得说清楚。快速旅行不是中性的便利功能，它是一种对空间的重新定义。Breath of the Wild 里几十个传送点把海拉鲁切成一个个可跳转的节点，地图从「要走过的地形」变成「要解锁的菜单」。玩家得到的是效率，失去的是被迫经过——而被迫经过，恰恰是发现发生的唯一条件。青沼自己说的那句话很准：传送点让他们错过了「等等，这是什么」的时刻。

所以这不是怀旧，是一次判断：在这个游戏里，绕路本身是内容，不是成本。重制版仍然加了冲刺键，也保留了伊波娜，说明他并不反对让移动变快，他反对的是让移动变「可跳过」。这两件事经常被混为一谈，一个是在路上跑得更快，一个是根本不在路上。

值得留意的是这套逻辑的边界。它成立，是因为时之笛的世界密度撑得起绕路——每一段路都有东西可撞见。换一个世界密度不够的游戏，同样的决定就只是折磨玩家。青沼能拆掉传送点，是因为他先确认了路上有值得停下的东西；很多号称「鼓励探索」的作品只学了后半句，把空白塞进地图，再管它叫发现。

报道里没有写的是：这套传送点做了多久，拆掉之后团队怎么处理已经为它调整过的地图结构。开发者的取舍被写成了一个漂亮的顿悟时刻，但真正的代价在工时里，在那些被删掉又需要重新缝合的关卡里。玩家最后拿到的是一段更长的路，没人拿到的是那段被抹掉的开发时间。

Eiji Aonuma asked his team to build a fast-travel system, playtested it, and then had them rip it all out. On the surface this is a warm little story about developers respecting the spirit of the original. It is actually a pricing decision about detours: he traded the time players would save for the time players would spend stumbling into things.

That trade deserves to be stated plainly. Fast travel is not a neutral convenience. It redefines space. The dozens of warp points in Breath of the Wild chop Hyrule into jumpable nodes; the map stops being terrain you cross and becomes a menu you unlock. Players gain efficiency and lose being forced to pass through — and being forced to pass through is the only condition under which discovery happens. Aonuma's own line is precise: the warp points cost them the moments of stopping and wondering, wait, what's this?

So this isn't nostalgia. It's a judgment that in this game the detour is the content, not the overhead. The remake still adds a sprint button and still has Epona, which means he isn't against moving faster; he's against movement becoming skippable. Those two things get conflated constantly. One is running faster along the road. The other is never being on it.

The logic has an edge, though. It holds because Ocarina's world is dense enough to justify the walk — there is something to hit on every stretch. Apply the same decision to a world with nothing in it and you're just torturing the player. Aonuma could remove the warp points because he first confirmed there was something worth stopping for. Plenty of games advertising exploration learn only the second half of that lesson: fill the map with emptiness and call it discovery.

What the report doesn't say is how long the warp system took to build, or how the team re-stitched the level structure it had already shaped around it. The tradeoff is written up as a tidy moment of insight, but the real cost sits in labor hours, in levels deleted and sewn back together. Players end up with a longer road. What nobody gets back is the development time that was erased to give it to them.
