公共空间的“商业化”陷阱与行政暴力的共谋The 'Commercialization' Trap of Public Space and Administrative Complicity

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-06 § 链接
将社区互助定义为“商业活动”是典型的用行政定义篡改事实的元暴力。
Defining community mutual aid as 'commercial activity' is a meta-violence that uses administrative definitions to hijack reality.

这起事件揭示了一个极其阴险的逻辑：当权力机构想要榨取资源时，它首先会通过重新定义“事实”来制造合法性。英格兰和威尔士的这些地方议会将捐赠制（donation-based）的瑜伽课、气功课甚至母亲与婴儿合唱团定义为“商业活动” (commercial activity)。这是一个典型的认知入口武器化——通过强行将“社区互助”划入“商业运营”的范畴，议会成功地将原本属于公民基本权利的公共空间使用权，转化为了一个可以定价、可以收税的商品。

按照加尔通的暴力三角，这属于典型的结构层暴力 (structural violence)。议会利用维多利亚时代的公园法规或过时的商业许可制度，制造了一套极其冗长的 paperwork 和高昂的 fees。这种制度设计的目的根本不是为了所谓的“公共安全”或“维护公园”，因为一个 7 人的气功课对公园草坪的损耗几乎为零。真正的目的在于通过行政手段建立一个筛选机制，将那些无法支付“入场费”的底层社区组织驱逐出公共空间。

最令人作呕的是这种行政逻辑中的共谋 (complicity)。议会声称费用用于“改善公园”，而执法则外包给像 Parkguard 这样的私人承包商。这形成了一个闭环：私人承包商通过开罚单获利 $\rightarrow$ 议会通过许可费获利 $\rightarrow$ 双方共同维护一套将“自发表达”刑事化的规则。在这种叙事下，一个带领新母亲唱歌的女性被定义为“违规经营者”，而这种定义权本身就是一种元暴力 (meta violence)——它垄断了对“什么是公共空间用途”的解释权。

当一个母亲在公园里为其他母亲唱歌被视为“商业行为”时，公共空间已经死了。它不再是公民确证身份、建立连接的场所，而变成了一个巨大的、由行政指令驱动的收费站。这种对“微小表达”的围剿，本质上是在清除社区中那些不被体制掌控的、自发的互助节点。

This incident reveals a sinister logic: when power structures seek to extract resources, they first manufacture legitimacy by redefining 'facts'. By categorizing donation-based yoga, qigong, and mother-baby choirs as 'commercial activity', councils in England and Wales are weaponizing the cognitive entry point. They are forcibly shifting the use of public space—a basic civic right—into a priced commodity.

In terms of Galtung's Violence Triangle, this is textbook structural violence. By leveraging outdated Victorian bylaws and cumbersome paperwork, councils have created a filter to purge grassroots community gatherings. The claim that these fees ensure 'public safety' or 'park maintenance' is a scam; a qigong class of seven people has zero impact on turf wear. The real goal is to exclude those who cannot afford the 'entry fee' from the commons.

More repulsive is the complicity inherent in this administrative machinery. Councils claim fees are for 'improvement', while enforcement is outsourced to private contractors like Parkguard. This creates a closed loop: private contractors profit from penalties, councils profit from licenses, and both collaborate to criminalize spontaneous expression. Defining a woman leading a choir for new mothers as an 'unlicensed trader' is an act of meta-violence—a monopoly over the interpretation of what 'public space' is for.

When a mother singing in a park is rebranded as a 'commercial operation', the public space is dead. It is no longer a site for the establishment of identity or community connection, but a giant toll booth driven by administrative decrees. This crackdown on 'micro-expressions' is, in essence, the systematic erasure of autonomous mutual-aid nodes that the state cannot control.
