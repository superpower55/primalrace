换一把尺子，就有了政绩Change the Ruler, Keep the Record

中国科技 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
能力没变，评价方式变了，差额就消失了
The capability didn't change. The metric did. The gap disappeared.

NHS England 说 29 家信托「正在受益」，这 29 家自己说：系统没上线，或者一个应用都没用。中间那个差额不是沟通误会，是有人把尺子换了。

这就是第三章那个机制：现实不配合，就重新定义现实。做不出能力，就宣布一套新的评价方式——「live」的定义从「医生在用」改成「基础设施在位、能提交数据」；「realising benefits」的定义从「病人受益」改成「用平台做过后台数据分析」。特雷贝和南德文说得很清楚：不是运营使用，只是「支持数据提交」。换个尺子，能力没变，数字全变了。τ law 的 NHS 版本。

更值得看的是那个 117,539 台手术。一个具体到个位的数字，用来给一份 3.3 亿英镑的合同背书，然后 NHS England 自己承认证明不了因果，统计监管机构 OSR 介入调查。数字越精确，越像在制造事实，而不是在报告事实。

Palantir 的反击方式同样标准：雇了工党前副党魁汤姆·沃森做全职说客，在 X 上发视频「辟谣」，还给批评他们的议员写信，信里数着对方批评过公司多少次。一位收信的议员说这「非常老大哥」。这就是武器化表达的第二层——不只是投放叙事，还要让批评者先算一算自己说话的次数。Mosley 把质疑称作「Palantir 妄想症」，把终止合同说成「把政治置于病人之上」。谁在定义「病人利益」，谁就赢了这一局。

共谋的位置也很清楚：NHS England 用这份账目去 defend 合同，部长们可能拿这份账目做决定，Palantir 拿到 3.3 亿英镑和后续锁定。代价由谁付？报道里没有病人倒下，没有医生被起诉，代价是那些本该被这套系统加速的出院的病人——他们等的时间，被换成了网站上的一个「benefits」标签。

这条不是好新闻，但里面有一件事是真的：一百多份信息公开申请，一家医疗慈善机构一份一份地问，最后把 29 家信托的原话摆在了台面上。差额被具体地量出来了。剩下的问题是，量出来之后，2027 年的退出条款会不会真的被用。

如果不会，那这套系统最大的「benefit」，是教会了所有人怎么用一把新尺子量旧能力。

NHS England lists 29 hospital trusts as "realising benefits" from Palantir's platform. Those 29 trusts say the system isn't live, or they aren't using any of its applications. That gap isn't a communication glitch. Someone changed the ruler.

This is the mechanism from Chapter 3: when reality won't cooperate, redefine reality. Can't deliver capability? Announce a new evaluation standard. "Live" quietly shifts from "clinicians are using it" to "infrastructure is in place to support data submission." "Realising benefits" shifts from "patients benefited" to "the trust ran some back-office analysis." Torbay and South Devon spells it out: not operational use, just data submission. Same capability, new ruler, brand-new numbers. The NHS edition of the τ law.

Then there's the 117,539 additional operations. A figure precise to the single digit, used to back a £330m contract — and NHS England itself concedes it cannot prove cause and effect, with the OSR now taking it up. The more exact the number, the more it looks like manufacturing a fact rather than reporting one.

Palantir's counterattack is textbook. Hire Labour's former deputy leader Tom Watson as a full-time lobbyist. Post videos on X debunking "myths." Write to critical MPs and count how many times they've spoken against the company — one recipient called it "very Big Brother." This is the second layer of weaponized expression: don't just push a narrative, make the critics tally their own words first. Mosley calls the scrutiny "Palantir Derangement Syndrome" and ending the contract "putting politics above patients." Whoever defines "patient interest" wins the round.

The co-conspiracy is visible: NHS England uses this ledger to defend the contract, ministers may use it to decide, Palantir collects £330m and the lock-in. Who pays? No patient collapses in this article, no doctor is prosecuted. The cost is the discharge that should have been faster — that waiting time converted into a "benefits" tag on a website.

This isn't good news. But one thing in it is real: over a hundred FOI requests, filed one by one by a medical charity, put 29 trusts' own words on the record. The gap got measured. The open question is whether the 2027 break clause gets used.

If it doesn't, the platform's greatest benefit will have been teaching everyone how to measure old capability with a new ruler.
