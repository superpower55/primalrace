生孩子的戏，签字的是男人In This Birth Scene, the Man Signs Off

文娱 文化层 · 结构层 Deadline ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
她的子宫被摘掉，消息先送到丈夫手里。
Her uterus is gone, and the news goes to her husband first.

《镀金时代》第四季第一集让 Gladys 在产床上大出血，为了止血，她的子宫被摘除。医生把这件事先告诉了公爵——她的丈夫——然后由丈夫决定什么时候告诉她。Bertha 给公爵的建议是：等她养好身体再说。

这不是剧情漏洞，这是剧本写对了。它把一件所有人都在假装不存在的事摆到了台面上：生育这件事从头到尾发生在女人身上，可解释权、知情权、决定权，一路向上传递，最后停在男人手里。产房里那个可能要在母亲和孩子之间二选一的人，是 Duke of Buckingham。

扮演 Gladys 的 Taissa Farmiga 说，她读剧本时的反应是“这可怜的姑娘一刻都不得闲”。她说得对，但说得太轻了。Gladys 的处境不是运气不好，是结构。第三季她被母亲安排进一场她以为会是噩梦的婚姻，第四季她生下一个孩子、失去一个子宫，而这一切的起因，是 Bertha 要一个公爵的头衔。婚姻在这里是交易，孩子是交易的产物，子宫是交易的成本。

有意思的是，真正救了 Gladys 命的不是医生，是她的贴身女仆 Adelheid Weber——她给公爵夫人掖被子的时候发现她还在流血。一个没有头衔、没有话语权的女人，靠的是“掖被子”这种最不被计入价值的劳动，捡回了一条命。然后呢？然后她被请出了叙事，去通知 Mrs. Russell 和医生，接下来出场的是医生、公爵、母亲，全是能对着别人宣布消息的人。

Carrie Coon 说 Bertha 在这一季“优先级从没这么清楚过”，她差点失去女儿、差点失去外孙、一年之内还差点失去丈夫。请注意这个排序，也请注意 Bertha 做的事：她安慰公爵，劝他等 Gladys 身体好一点再告诉她。这句话听起来是体贴，实际上是把 Gladys 的身体当成一个需要被管理的容器——先养好，再告知，至于她本人什么时候想知道自己再也不能生育，由别人替她决定。

剧集把这一切拍得很体面：城堡、礼服、母亲含泪的安慰。这就是文化暴力最擅长的活——它不否认痛苦，它把痛苦装进一个漂亮的盒子里，再配上一句“他是个好人，他选了你”。Gladys 的丈夫确实在母亲和孩子之间选了她，剧集把这一点当作温情的落点。可一个女人的生死要等一个男人来做选择，这件事本身就是暴力，选对了也只是暴力的温柔版本。

Farmiga 说 Gladys 最终“找到了一个爱她的伴侣，一个选择她的人”。选择她。你听出来了吗，连爱都被写成了施予。

这一集真正让人不舒服的地方，不是子宫被摘掉，是摘掉之后，第一个被通知的人不是她。

In the Season 4 premiere of The Gilded Age, Gladys bleeds out on a childbed, and to stop the hemorrhage her uterus is removed. The doctor takes this news to the Duke—her husband—who then decides when to tell her. Bertha's advice to the Duke: wait until she's stronger.

This isn't a plot hole. The script got it right. It puts on the table something everyone pretends isn't there: birth happens entirely inside a woman's body, but the right to know, the right to explain, the right to decide, all travel upward and come to rest with a man. The person who may have to choose between mother and child in that room is the Duke of Buckingham.

Taissa Farmiga, who plays Gladys, said her reaction to the script was that this poor girl never gets a break. She's right, but she's being generous. Gladys's situation isn't bad luck, it's structure. In Season 3 her mother arranged her into a marriage she expected to be a hellish nightmare; in Season 4 she bears a child and loses a uterus, and the whole thing starts because Bertha wanted a ducal title. Marriage here is a transaction, the child is the product, the uterus is the cost.

What's telling is that the person who actually saves Gladys's life isn't the doctor—it's her lady's maid, Adelheid Weber, who finds her still bleeding while tucking her in. A woman with no title and no voice, doing the kind of labor that never gets counted, is what pulls her back. And then? Then she's escorted out of the narrative to fetch Mrs. Russell and the doctor, and the room fills up with people who get to announce things to other people.

Carrie Coon says Bertha's priorities have never been clearer this season—she nearly lost her daughter, nearly lost her grandson, and within a year nearly lost her husband. Note the ordering. Note also what Bertha does: she comforts the Duke and advises him to let Gladys heal before telling her. It sounds like tenderness. It's the management of a body—first restore it, then disclose—and the question of when a woman learns she can never bear another child is answered by someone else.

The show keeps it all very polished: the castle, the gowns, the mother's tears. This is cultural violence doing what it does best. It doesn't deny the pain; it puts the pain in a beautiful box and ties it with the line that he's a good man, he chose her. The Duke did choose her over the baby, and the episode treats this as the warm landing. But a woman's life hinging on a man's choice is violence, and choosing correctly only makes it the gentle version.

Farmiga says Gladys ends up with a loving partner, a human who chooses her. Chooses her. Even love gets written as a bestowal.

The truly uncomfortable thing here isn't the hysterectomy. It's that after the hysterectomy, the first person told is not her.
