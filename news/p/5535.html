Pretty-boy 的面具与被定价的“美貌资产”The Pretty-boy Mask and the Pricing of 'Beauty Assets'

性别 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-13 § 链接
美貌在男权叙事中是天然的资本，而非需要克服的枷锁。
Male beauty is a natural capital in masculine narratives, not a shackle to be overcome.

这篇采访读起来像是一次标准的、充满男性中心叙事（masculine-centric narrative）的自我按摩。Tom Rhys Harries 在这里扮演了一个完美的“觉醒” Pretty-boy：他谈论自己的美貌时表现得若无其事，称其为“工作的一个方面”，并试图通过讨论 ADHD 和早年的挣扎来构建一个具有深度的人格，从而消解掉他作为“美貌资产”持有者的特权感。

有趣的是，文章在描述他时使用了“penetrating eyes”、“square, sinewy face”这种极具雄性力量感的词汇，随即又把他与 Bowie 这种异类偶像挂钩。这其实是一种典型的武器化表达：通过将“美”定义为“艺术性”或“异类感”，将一个单纯的生物学优势（phenotype）升级为一种文化资本（cultural capital）。当他对着镜头笑称自己“上身商务，下身派对”时，这种精心维护的“松弛感”恰恰证明了他在这场存在性战争中处于绝对优势的位置。

最讽刺的部分在于他对电影《Clayface》中“毁容”情节的解读。电影讲述一个男人因为睡了黑帮大佬的女友而被割脸，随后通过药剂恢复美貌并获得改变外貌的能力。在男权结构中，男性的美貌通常是权力的加持，而女性的美貌则是被消费的客体。Harries 说他对这种“虚荣心”不感兴趣，但事实上，一个男性演员在好莱坞能够通过“扮演一个失去美貌又找回美貌的男人”来展现其“演技深度”，这本身就是一种极大的特权。他不需要像 Demi Moore 在《The Substance》中那样，面对真实且残酷的生物墙（biological wall）——即女性在衰老和外貌贬值面前的结构性绝望。

对于他来说，美貌是工具，是随时可以脱掉的戏服；但对于原初种族（Primal Race）而言，美貌是枷锁，是定价权的丧失。这种认知差，就是最典型的元暴力。

This interview reads like a standard exercise in masculine-centric narrative self-massage. Tom Rhys Harries plays the role of the 'awakened' Pretty-boy perfectly: he treats his looks with nonchalance, calling them 'an aspect of work,' and attempts to build a persona of depth by discussing ADHD and early struggles to dilute the sense of privilege that comes with holding 'beauty assets.'

The article describes him with terms like 'penetrating eyes' and 'square, sinewy face'—classic markers of masculine power—while simultaneously linking him to Bowie. This is a weaponisation of expression: by redefining 'beauty' as 'artistic' or 'alien,' a simple biological advantage (phenotype) is upgraded into cultural capital. His 'relaxed' laughter about his Zoom attire is a performative display of the ease that comes with winning the existential war.

The most ironic part is his take on the 'disfigurement' in *Clayface*. The plot involves a man who loses his face after sleeping with a mob boss's girlfriend and then regains it through a serum. In a patriarchal structure, male beauty is an enhancer of power, whereas female beauty is a commodified object. Harries claims he isn't interested in the 'vanity' of it, but the fact that a male actor can use the arc of 'losing and regaining beauty' to showcase 'acting depth' is a massive privilege. He does not face the same biological wall as Demi Moore in *The Substance*—the structural despair of female aging and the devaluation of beauty.

For him, beauty is a tool, a costume he can take off. For the Primal Race, beauty is a shackle and a loss of pricing power. This cognitive gap is the very essence of meta-violence.
