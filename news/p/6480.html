二十五周冠军是尺子换来的25 Weeks at No. 1 Is a Ruler Change

文娱 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要Ella Langley 的《Choosin’ Texas》在 2026 年 9 月 5 日当周的 Billboard Hot 100 上拿下第 20 周冠军，成为非节日歌曲中登顶最久的一首；截至 10 月 31 日的榜单，它以 25 周刷新总纪录，超过 Mariah Carey《All I Want for Christmas Is You》的 22 周。
The newsElla Langley's "Choosin' Texas" logged its 20th week at No. 1 on the Sept. 5, 2026-dated Billboard Hot 100, the longest reign for a non-holiday hit. By the Oct. 31 chart it held the overall record at 25 weeks, passing Mariah Carey's 22-week "All I Want for Christmas Is You."
榜单变长了，不是歌变强了。
The chart got longer, not the songs stronger.

Ella Langley 的《Choosin' Texas》在 Hot 100 上坐了 25 周冠军，超过 Mariah Carey 的《All I Want for Christmas Is You》。这不是一首歌打败了另一首歌，是尺子换了。报道自己写了：1991 年底 Hot 100 改用电子追踪数据之后，冠军停留的时间才开始变长。前半段的历史是 Debby Boone 1977 年的 10 周、Olivia Newton-John 1981-82 年的 10 周，十几年没人破；后半段是 16 周、19 周、20 周、25 周，一路往上。这不是音乐史，是计量史。

改变的是评价方式，不是能力。这一点跨领域通用：做不出更小的芯片，就宣布一条新定律；增长不动了，就改 GDP 的算法；歌没法让更多人同时真的在听，就让“在榜周数”这个指标自己膨胀。榜单本来是对注意力的粗糙测量，现在测量方式精密了，被测量的东西反而更稀薄。25 周第一名的意思是，25 周里没有别的歌能顶上来——这既可以读成统治力，也可以读成流通的停滞。

再看这份名单的构成。25 周、22 周、19 周、19 周、16 周、16 周、16 周……最长的那些席位里，女性名字不少：mariah、Whitney、Taylor、Adele、Beyoncé。但她们的位置是唱将、是圣诞、是情感叙事；而 2019 年之后把周数纪录一路推上去的，是乡村和“cowboy”这套男性气质的符号——《Old Town Road》《A Bar Song (Tipsy)》《Choosin' Texas》。这不是巧合，是产业在给一种表达定价：牛仔、德州、酒、男人，这套符号好卖，好卖到可以连续霸榜。Langley 说“fans can change your career”，说得对，只是 fans 不是自己聚起来的，是算法和播放列表聚起来的。

真正值得问的是：谁在为这 25 周付代价。报道没写。没写 Langley 本人被这套“德州牛仔”人设绑定了多少，没写那些被 25 周压在第二名、连名字都没进这份名单的歌，没写榜单背后的流媒体分成怎么分。榜单越长，越像一场共谋：平台需要长尾数据，厂牌需要纪录叙事，艺人需要“现象级”的头衔，粉丝需要为偶像的排名打仗。所有人都有回报，代价是“一首歌到底有多好”这个问题被彻底替换成了“一首歌在榜上待了多久”。

所以这份名单最讽刺的地方在于，它把自己写成了荣誉榜，实际上是一份测量工具的校准记录。1977 年的 10 周和 2026 年的 25 周，中间隔着的不是音乐，是 1991 年的一次技术升级。下一次有人再破纪录，先问一句：这次换的是哪把尺子。

Ella Langley's "Choosin' Texas" spent 25 weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100, passing Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You." This is not one song beating another. This is a ruler change. The report says it itself: songs began logging longer No. 1 stays after the Hot 100 adopted electronically tracked data in late 1991. The first half of the history is Debby Boone's 10 weeks in 1977, Olivia Newton-John's 10 weeks in 1981-82, and then more than a decade with no one breaking it. The second half is 16 weeks, 19, 20, 25, climbing. This is not music history. It is measurement history.

What changed is the evaluation method, not the capacity. The same move travels across fields: can't make a smaller chip, announce a new law; growth stalls, change how GDP is calculated; can't get more people actually listening at once, let the metric of "weeks on chart" inflate itself. A chart is a rough measure of attention. Now the measurement is precise and the thing measured is thinner. Twenty-five weeks at No. 1 means that for twenty-five weeks nothing else could rise. That reads as dominance, or as a stalled circulation.

Look at who fills the list. 25, 22, 19, 19, 16, 16, 16... plenty of women near the top: Mariah, Whitney, Taylor, Adele, Beyoncé. But their slots are diva, Christmas, feeling. What has pushed the record upward since 2019 is country and the "cowboy" signifier: "Old Town Road," "A Bar Song (Tipsy)," "Choosin' Texas." Not a coincidence. The industry is pricing a kind of expression: cowboy, Texas, whiskey, man. It sells well enough to hold the top for months. Langley says fans can change your career. True, except the fans did not assemble themselves. Algorithms and playlists assembled them.

The real question is who pays for these 25 weeks. The report does not say. It does not say how tightly Langley is now bound to the Texas-cowboy persona, does not name the songs held at No. 2 for those 25 weeks and left off this list, does not say how streaming revenue splits behind the chart. The longer the chart runs, the more it looks like a conspiracy: platforms need long-tail data, labels need record narratives, artists need the "phenomenon" title, fans need a ranking to fight over. Everyone gets a return. The cost is that the question "is this a good song" has been fully replaced by "how long did it stay."

The most ironic thing about this list is that it presents itself as an honor roll while actually being a calibration log for a measuring tool. Ten weeks in 1977 and 25 weeks in 2026 are separated not by music but by a technical upgrade in 1991. Next time someone breaks the record, ask first: which ruler got swapped this time.
