她留着他的信，他替国王去送死She Kept His Letters, He Died for the King

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-29 § 链接
一场帝国远征，被写成一段爱情救赎
An imperial voyage sold to us as a love story

这封信能留下来，不是因为爱情伟大，是因为她把它藏进了丈夫看不见的地方。报道说得很清楚：收信人 Louise Victoria Le Cloustier 1821 年去世，一辈子不知道 Mongez 的结局，但信被读过、重读过，很可能必须瞒着丈夫。一个已婚女性保存另一个男人的私人来信几十年——这件事本身比信里的内容更值得看。

Mongez 在信里反复写“我会回来，我会变成另一个人，一个更好的人”。拍卖行的 Miran 说，他大概是之前对她做过什么不好的事，被她摆回了原位，于是决定去参加这趟远航。这个解释很动人，也很方便：一段私人关系里的亏欠，被兑换成一次国家级的科学远征。个人情感成了宏大叙事的燃料，而宏大叙事从来不需要向个人交代。

那趟远征是什么？路易十六派 La Pérouse 出去，是要跟库克抢，是要摸清英国是不是真打算在 Botany Bay 建永久基地，是要开贸易路线。报道顺带提了一句：那片土地上的原住民已经住了几万年。225 名军官、科学家、船员，从布雷斯特出发，去替一个帝国丈量别人的海岸。

然后这些人死在了 Vanikoro 的珊瑚礁上，死于一场台风。Mongez 最后一封信说，这趟远航已经转向了悲伤，他现在只想回家，因为这点微薄的科学成果补偿不了这样的不幸。这句话是整篇报道里最诚实的一句，也是最没人会引用的那一句。

路易十六上断头台前问的是“有 la Pérouse 的消息吗”。一个国王在死前惦记的是他的船队，不是他的妻子，不是他的孩子。这套价值排序，和把男人送上船的那套排序是同一套：荣誉、知识、帝国的版图，排在活着的人前面。Mongez 说他是“为了对名声的爱和求知的欲望”走的，Miran 说，他们出发前都读过库克的书，知道有多危险。知道，还是去了。

现在这些信估价三万到五万欧元，11 月 17 日在巴黎 Hôtel Drouot 拍卖。Miran 说这是一场“奇迹”，说这些信“让人完全重新理解”这件在法国非同寻常的科学与历史事件。一件被藏了两百年的私人物件，进入市场的那一刻，就变成了民族叙事的一块新拼图。

Le Cloustier 没有烧掉这些信。这是她做过的最有主体性的一件事，而报道只把它当作一个浪漫的注脚。她瞒着丈夫保存的那些纸，最终被陌生人翻出来，标上价格，摆上拍卖台。她当年要躲开的那个人，和今天给她定价的那些人，是同一个结构的两端。

These letters survived not because love is great, but because she hid them where her husband couldn't see. The report is clear: Louise Victoria Le Cloustier died in 1821, never knowing what became of Mongez, but the letters were read and reread, and she most likely had to keep them from her husband. A married woman preserving another man's private letters for decades — that fact tells you more than anything inside them.

Mongez writes repeatedly: I will come back, I will be another man, a better man. Miran, the auction house expert, suggests he had behaved badly toward her at some point and she put him in his place, and that this may be why he joined such a long and perilous journey. It's a moving explanation, and a convenient one: a private debt gets converted into a state scientific expedition. Personal feeling becomes fuel for a grand narrative, and the grand narrative never has to answer to the person.

What was that expedition? Louis XVI sent La Pérouse out to rival Cook, to find out whether Britain really meant to establish a permanent base at Botany Bay, to open trade routes. The report notes in passing that the land was inhabited by Indigenous people for tens of thousands of years. Two hundred and twenty-five officers, scientists and crew sailed from Brest to measure other people's coastlines for an empire.

Then they died on the reefs at Vanikoro, in a tornado. Mongez's last letter says the expedition has taken a sad turn, that he now only wants to come home, because such unhappiness is not compensated for by the meagre scientific results. That is the most honest sentence in the whole report, and the one nobody will quote.

On his way to the guillotine, Louis XVI asked whether there was news of La Pérouse. A king facing death thinks about his ships, not his wife, not his children. That ranking of values is the same one that put these men on the ships: glory, knowledge, imperial maps, ahead of the people who are alive. Mongez says he left a bit for the love of fame and the desire for new knowledge; Miran notes they had all read Cook and knew the dangers. They knew, and went.

The letters are valued at thirty to fifty thousand euros and go under the hammer at the Hôtel Drouot in Paris on 17 November. Miran calls the find a miracle, says it throws a completely new light on an extraordinary scientific and historical event. A private object hidden for two centuries becomes, the moment it enters the market, a new piece of national narrative.

Le Cloustier did not burn the letters. That was the most autonomous thing she ever did, and the report files it as a romantic footnote. The paper she kept behind her husband's back ends up pulled out by strangers, priced, and put on an auction block. The man she had to hide from then and the people pricing her now are two ends of the same structure.
