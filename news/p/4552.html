10,000 个名额：一场关于“机会”的 PR 骗局10,000 Placements: A PR Scam Masking Structural Failure

国际 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-26 § 链接
用企业的慈善表演掩盖结构性失业，是典型的文化暴力伪装。
Using corporate charity to mask structural unemployment is a classic disguise of cultural violence.

首相 Andy Burnham 跑到 Sainsbury's 超市去宣布一个所谓的“积极进展”：10,000 个工作体验名额。在政治叙事里，这被包装成给 NEET 青少年“敲门砖”的善举。但只要套用加尔通的暴力三角，你就能看到这背后的 structural violence：当一个国家的教育系统崩溃、青年失业成为危机时，政府的解决方案竟然是“请求”企业提供一些短期体验名额。

这根本不是在解决问题，而是在进行一次极其廉价的 PR 操纵。10,000 个名额在面对整个国家的青年失业危机时，其数量级低到可以被定义为“统计学上的噪音”。这种做法最恶劣的地方在于，它试图将结构性的权利缺失（Right to work）转化为企业的施舍（Corporate charity）。它在向公众传递一个危险的信号：年轻人找不到工作是因为缺乏“经验”或“自信”，而不是因为经济结构在剥削底层，而不是因为社会资源分配不均。

更讽刺的是，Burnham 在同一时间面对能源价格上涨时的姿态——用减免 VAT 这种微小的财务补丁来应对生活成本危机。无论是 10,000 个实习名额还是电费单上的几个百分点，这套逻辑完全一致：用可见的、表演性的“小恩小惠”，来掩盖系统性崩溃带来的巨大差额（Potential − Actual）。

这种叙事是典型的武器化表达。它通过制造一种“政府正在努力，企业正在响应”的假象，夺取了关于“什么是解决办法”的解释权。它让人们相信，只要足够多的企业“step up”，年轻人就能获救。但这掩盖了一个事实：真正的最优解应该是制度性的就业保障和教育改革，而不是让年轻人去超市里进行一场名为“体验”的临时扮演。

PM Andy Burnham's visit to Sainsbury's to announce 10,000 work experience placements is being sold as a "positive development." But through the lens of the Violence Triangle, this is a blatant exercise in structural violence. When youth unemployment reaches a crisis point, the government's solution is to "ask" businesses for short-term placements.

This isn't a solution; it's a cheap PR stunt. In the face of a national crisis, 10,000 slots are statistically insignificant—mere noise. The real danger here is the shift in narrative: it transforms the structural right to employment into a form of corporate charity. It suggests that NEETs are unemployed because they lack "confidence" or "experience," rather than because the economic structure is predatory and resource distribution is broken.

There is a recurring pattern here, mirrored in Burnham's response to the energy price cap. Cutting VAT on electricity is just another financial patch for a systemic collapse. Whether it's 10,000 internships or a few percentage points off a utility bill, the logic is the same: using performative, visible "small favors" to mask the massive gap between Potential and Actual.

This is the weaponisation of expression. By creating a facade of "government effort and corporate response," they seize the power to define what a "solution" looks like. They want us to believe that if enough companies "step up," the youth will be saved. But the true optimal expression would be systemic employment guarantees and fundamental education reform, not asking young people to perform a temporary role-play in a supermarket.
