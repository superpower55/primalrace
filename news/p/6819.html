当官方的车成为凶器When the State's Car Becomes the Weapon

国际 直接层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Intercept ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要得克萨斯州哈里斯县一个大陪审团起诉了一名ICE官员，指控其去年10月驾车冲撞徒步逃跑的墨西哥籍男子Miguel Parra Hernandez，罪名是持致命武器袭击，武器就是车辆。The Intercept梳理了约二十起类似事件：ICE车辆冲撞、急刹逼停、高速追车，导致多名平民受伤住院，佐治亚州一次追车还撞死了一名教师。国土安全部否认部分指控，把多起事故归咎于被追捕者逃跑。
The newsA Harris County, Texas, grand jury has charged an ICE officer with assault with a deadly weapon for striking Miguel Parra Hernandez, a Mexican national who was fleeing on foot; the weapon was the vehicle. The Intercept reviewed roughly two dozen cases of ICE vehicles ramming cars, brake-checking, and running high-speed chases that injured civilians and killed a teacher in Georgia. DHS disputes some accounts and blames fleeing targets for the crashes.
定义谁在施暴的人，往往就是施暴的人。
Whoever defines who is violent is usually the one being violent.

先把词抢回来。「将车辆武器化」是国土安全部用来指控移民和活动人士的说法：他们说 Lorenzo Salgado Araujo 和 Renée Good 用车攻击联邦探员，但这些指控没有在刑事起诉里成立，或者被目击证词和视频推翻。真正把车当武器用的是开车的探员。得州哈里斯县的大陪审团上周起诉了一名 ICE 官员，罪名是持致命武器袭击——武器是一辆没有标识的政府车，受害者是徒步逃跑的 Miguel Parra Hernandez。录像显示他在被撞前正背对车辆奔跑，而国土安全部说他跑到了车前面。

这不是一两起失控的个案。The Intercept 梳理了约二十起 ICE 用车对付平民的事件，手段有名字，探员内部管它叫「offensive driving techniques」：撞目标的车，急刹逼停跟在后面的观察志愿者，有时直接开向行人。加州 Ventura County 的志愿者 Leo Martinez 被撞过两次，第二次是三辆无标识政府车一起撞，车窗被砸，人被拖出来，两次都抓了又放、不予起诉。志愿者说，当地的无标识车太好认了——看车身上的撞痕。

「急刹逼停」在五十个州都违法，属于鲁莽驾驶一类；它不出现在官方指南里，理由很充分。但同一批人可以在高速公路上、在居民区里做这件事，因为规则不约束他们。2023 年 CBP 的政策基本禁止用车围堵、禁止行驶中接触其他车辆，2025 年的新指令放宽了裁量权，允许使用这类技术并忽略交通法规。研究追车政策的犯罪学教授 Geoffrey Alpert 说，多数联邦机构在警察与公民互动、交通拦截和追车方面训练很差。规则松了，训练没跟上，车就成了钝器。

代价落在谁身上，报道写得很清楚。佐治亚州萨凡纳郊外，探员高速追捕只有一张 2024 年民事驱逐令、没有犯罪记录的 Oscar Vasquez Lopez，他的车撞死了一名教师——而当地警察局恰恰因为风险限制追车。芝加哥的居民区里，探员闯过路口撞上目标的车，邻居说自己两岁的孩子天天在那条街上走。明尼阿波利斯的美国公民 Christian Molina 靠边停车时被从后面撞上，背部受伤，探员说会赔，没留联系方式就走了。Elgin 的州调查人员认定探员很可能做了不熟练的 PIT maneuver 并失控。每一起，国土安全部的说法都差不多：是对方逃跑造成了不安全状况。逃跑的人当然有责任，但决定用一辆两吨重的车去追一个没有犯罪记录的人，这个决定是探员做的。

这里有一层更冷的东西。追捕、冲撞、逼停都是可见的直接暴力，但让它们能反复发生的，是那层把「谁在施暴」的解释权攥在手里的东西。政府先宣布平民把车武器化了，再让自己的车去撞人；等视频出来，再说是对方跑到了车前面。这套顺序不是失误，是元暴力在运作：先定义事实，再让事实去追。地方警察和市长公开反驳 ICE 关于 Moreida 的说法，说明录像还能起点作用——但前提是有人拍到，有人愿意放出来，有地方官员愿意站出来。纽约的快速响应者说得直白：探员高速冲进逆行车道、闯红灯、不打灯，就是为了不被记录；照这个频率，撞死一个骑车人或行人只是时间问题。

还有一件事报道没写：那些被撞的人后来怎么样了。Parra Hernandez 的伤情、Gonzalez 住院多久、Martinez 的伤有没有后遗症，都不在这篇报道里。被撞的人进了医院，撞人的机构在发声明，起诉书只针对一个开车的官员。一个被起诉的探员，和二十起没人被追责的事件放在一起，比例本身就是答案。

Take the word back. 「Weaponized vehicles」 is the Department of Homeland Security's phrase for immigrants and activists: it said Lorenzo Salgado Araujo and Renée Good used their cars to attack federal agents, claims that never held up in criminal charges or were contradicted by witnesses and video. The ones weaponizing vehicles are the agents driving them. Last week a Harris County, Texas, grand jury charged an ICE officer with assault with a deadly weapon; the weapon was an unmarked government car, and the victim was Miguel Parra Hernandez, who was fleeing on foot. Surveillance footage shows him running away from the car moments before he is hit. DHS says he ran in front of it.

This is not a handful of失控 incidents. The Intercept reviewed roughly two dozen cases of ICE using vehicles against civilians, with tactics agents call 「offensive driving techniques」: ramming the cars of targets, brake-checking volunteers who follow them, and in some cases driving straight into pedestrians. Leo Martinez, a volunteer in Ventura County, California, was rammed twice; the second time three unmarked government vehicles hit his car, agents broke his window and dragged him out, and he was released without charge both times. Volunteers there identify the unmarked cars by their body damage.

Brake-checking is illegal in all 50 states, covered by reckless driving and anti-road-rage statutes. It does not appear in official guidelines for good reason. Yet the same agents can do it on freeways and in residential streets because the rules do not bind them. A 2023 CBP policy largely barred boxing in vehicles or making contact with a moving car; a 2025 directive widened agents' discretion to use these techniques and ignore traffic laws. Geoffrey Alpert, a criminologist who helped rewrite CBP's chase policy, says most federal agencies are poorly trained in police-citizen interactions, traffic stops, and pursuits. Loosen the rules, skip the training, and the car becomes a blunt object.

The costs are specific. Outside Savannah, Georgia, agents chased Oscar Vasquez Lopez, who had no criminal record and only a 2024 civil deportation order; his car struck and killed a teacher, in a place where local police restrict pursuits precisely because of that risk. In a Chicago residential neighborhood, agents blew through intersections and rammed their target's car; a neighbor said her two-year-old walks that street. Christian Molina, a U.S. citizen, was pulling over for an ICE stop in Minneapolis when agents rammed the back of his car, injuring his back; they said they would pay for the damage and left without contact information. In Elgin, Illinois, state investigators found agents likely performed an unskilled PIT maneuver and lost control. Each time, DHS blames the fleeing target for creating unsafe conditions. Fleeing is a choice; so is chasing a man with no criminal record through traffic in a two-ton vehicle.

There is a colder layer here. The chases and rammings are direct violence, but what lets them repeat is the power to define who is violent. The government declares that civilians weaponized their cars, then sends its own cars into people; when the video surfaces, it says the victim ran in front of the vehicle. That sequence is not a mistake. It is meta-violence at work: define the fact first, then let the fact catch up. Local police and a mayor publicly disputing ICE's account of Moreida shows video still counts for something—but only when someone films it, someone releases it, and a local official is willing to say so. A New York rapid responder put it plainly: agents accelerate into oncoming traffic, run red lights, turn without signaling, all to avoid being documented. At that frequency, it is only a matter of time before an agent kills a cyclist or pedestrian.

One thing the report does not say: what happened to the people who were hit. Parra Hernandez's injuries, how long Gonzalez was hospitalized, whether Martinez recovered—none of it is here. The hit go to the hospital, the agency issues a statement, and the indictment names one driver. One charged agent against two dozen incidents with no accountability is the ratio that answers the question.
