她求来的角色，被叫做恩赐The Role She Begged For, Called a Favor

文娱 文化层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要海伦·米伦近日接受采访时表示，希望自己能出现在最后一部《速度与激情》电影中。她自2017年《速度与激情8》起已参演该系列四部影片，并称能参演是因为范·迪塞尔对她非常照顾。
The newsHelen Mirren said in a recent interview that she hopes to appear in the final Fast and Furious film. She has appeared in four films in the series since 2017's The Fate of the Furious, crediting Vin Diesel's kindness for her roles.
一个演员求了十年的角色，被说成别人好心塞给她的
An actor spent a decade asking for a part. The story calls it kindness.

Helen Mirren 想演《速度与激情》最后一部。她说的是“in my dreams”。这四个字不是客套，是一个七十几岁的演员在镜头前把姿态放到最低：希望他们能把我塞进去，给我留一点地方。

她进这个系列，是她自己求来的。2017 年《The Fate of the Furious》之前，她在派对上遇到 Vin Diesel，据她对 People 的说法，她几乎是求他给自己找个角色。此后四部，她的出场是“briefly, very, very briefly”。她把这解释成 Vin 对她“incredibly kind”，感谢他给的机会。一个演了半世纪戏的演员，在别人的系列里拿到几分钟戏份，要归功于男主角的善意。这就是表达被定价的样子：谁有权力决定谁出现在画面里、出现多久，谁就是发善心的那一方，而被塞进来的人负责感恩。

这不是她一个人的处境，是整条产业链的默认设置。系列片的权力集中在男主角身上，连选角都成了他个人慷慨的证明。Mirren 说的每一句话都准确、得体、无可挑剔，正因为无可挑剔，才看得见那个结构：她不能说“我值得一个更大的角色”，她只能说“希望他们能把我塞进去”。假的最优解表达在这里运转得极其顺滑——扮演一个感激的、不添麻烦的客人，换取下一次被塞进去的可能。代价是她自己也知道，这个角色本来可以是别的样子。

报道里唯一没被追问的是：为什么一个 militia leader、Statham 角色的母亲，在四部电影里只能“briefly, very, very briefly”。这个系列里女性角色的位置一直是功能性的——母亲、爱人、需要被保护或被纪念的人。Mirren 的角色有名字、有权力、有枪，然后被剪成几分钟的客串。这不是选角问题，是叙事分配的问题：谁的故事值得被完整讲，谁的出现只是为了给男主角的世界添一笔。

Ehrenreich 在旁边问“你在里面吗”，这句话比任何分析都直白。连同剧组的演员都不知道她演过四部。她的表达在这个系列里被压到几乎不可见，而她还在说“in my dreams”。

如果这条新闻消失，差额不会变大——她本来也没拿到那个角色。真正的问题是：最后一部会不会真的把她塞进去，塞进去之后，她能不能不只说一句“谢谢”。

Helen Mirren wants to be in the last Fast and Furious movie. What she said was "in my dreams." That's not politeness. That's a woman in her seventies putting herself as low as the frame allows: hoping they might squeeze her in, give her a little space somewhere.

She got into this franchise by asking. Before 2017's The Fate of the Furious, she met Vin Diesel at a party and, by her own account to People, practically begged him to find her a part. Four films later, her appearances are "briefly, very, very briefly." She frames this as Vin being "incredibly kind" to her, and thanks him for the opportunity. An actor with half a century of work gets a few minutes in someone else's series, and the credit goes to the leading man's generosity. That is what it looks like when expression gets priced: whoever decides who appears on screen and for how long is the one doing the favor, and the person squeezed in is the one who says thank you.

This isn't her private predicament. It's the default setting of the franchise machine. Power concentrates in the male star, and even casting becomes proof of his personal largesse. Every sentence Mirren says is precise, gracious, unimpeachable — and because it's unimpeachable, the structure shows through. She can't say she deserves a bigger role. She can only say she hopes they squeeze her in. The false optimal expression runs perfectly here: play the grateful guest who causes no trouble, in exchange for the chance to be squeezed in again. The cost is that she knows the part could have been something else.

The one thing the piece never asks: why a militia leader, the mother of Statham's character, can only be "briefly, very, very briefly" across four films. Women in this series occupy functional slots — mother, lover, someone to be protected or mourned. Mirren's character has a name, power, and a gun, and gets cut down to a cameo. This isn't a casting problem. It's a problem of narrative allocation: whose story gets told in full, and whose appearance exists to add a brushstroke to the hero's world.

Ehrenreich, sitting beside her, asks, "Are you in them?" That line is more damning than any analysis. A co-star doesn't even know she's been in four of these films. Her expression has been compressed to near-invisibility, and she's still saying "in my dreams."

If this story disappeared, the gap wouldn't grow — she never had the role anyway. The real question is whether the last film actually squeezes her in, and whether, once she's in, she gets to say more than thank you.
