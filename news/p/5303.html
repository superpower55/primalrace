图赫尔的“全速前进”与足球世界的元暴力Tuchel’s 'Full Throttle' and the Meta-Violence of Football

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-27 § 链接
所谓的“竞技精神”往往是掩盖结构性剥削的文化伪装。
The so-called 'competitive spirit' is often a cultural disguise for structural exploitation.

图赫尔把轮换球员称为“madness”，这种叙事极其典型：将身体的极限损耗包装成某种崇高的、不可妥协的“胜利意志”。在足球这个高度男性中心的场域里，这种“全速前进”（full throttle）的表达不仅是战术选择，更是一种文化暴力。它在潜意识中定义了什么才是“真正的球员”——即一个可以被无限榨取、不计代价、将身体作为消耗品投入战争的客体。

这种逻辑是典型的 masculine-centric narrative。图赫尔提到贝林厄姆的“愤怒”和“火热”能让他变得更好，这实际上是在神化一种破坏性的、不计后果的雄性特质。在这种叙事下，球员的身体不再是生物学意义上的肉身，而成了实现国家荣耀或教练战绩的工具。当图赫尔说“不跟俱乐部经理打电话”时，他是在维护一种绝对的权力等级，这种等级制度确保了在国际比赛日这个特定的共谋场域中，国家队的权力高于一切，而球员的生物墙（身体耐受力）被视为可以被意志力强行突破的障碍。

最讽刺的是，这种“不妥协”的姿态被视为一种职业精神。但按照加尔通的暴力三角，当 Actual（球员的实际身体状态）低于 Potential（健康的生理需求）时，这个差额就是暴力。图赫尔口中的“理想场景”是 go again and go again，这本质上是对身体的殖民。在这种共谋中，教练、足协、甚至崇拜这种“硬汉”文化的球迷，共同完成了对球员主体性的抹除。他们庆祝的是一种由于过度损耗而产生的壮烈感，而这种壮烈感正是元暴力最完美的伪装。

Tuchel labels player rotation as “madness,” a classic narrative move: packaging physical exhaustion as a sublime, uncompromising “will to win.” In the hyper-masculine domain of football, this “full throttle” expression is more than a tactic; it is cultural violence. It defines the “true player” as an object to be infinitely extracted, a disposable asset cast into a war of attrition.

This is a textbook masculine-centric narrative. By praising Bellingham’s “rage” and “fire,” Tuchel sanctifies a destructive, reckless masculine trait. In this framework, the athlete's body is no longer a biological entity but a tool for national glory or a coach's record. When Tuchel refuses to “take calls from Premier League managers,” he is upholding an absolute power hierarchy. In the complicity of the international window, the national team's authority overrides all, and the biological wall of the player's physical limit is treated as a mere obstacle to be crushed by sheer will.

The irony is that this “uncompromising” stance is celebrated as professionalism. However, according to the Violence Triangle, when Actual (the player's physical state) falls below Potential (biological health needs), the gap is violence. Tuchel’s “ideal scenario” to “go again and go again” is essentially a colonization of the body. In this complicity, the coach, the federation, and the fans who worship this “tough guy” culture collectively erase the player's subjectivity. They celebrate a tragedy of attrition, and this perceived grandeur is the most perfect camouflage for meta-violence.
