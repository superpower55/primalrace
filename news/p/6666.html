换教练救不了西汉姆Sacking Nuno Won't Fix West Ham

文娱 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要西汉姆联本赛季开局不佳，周六1-3负于西布罗姆维奇后跌至英冠第八，主帅努诺·埃斯皮里托·桑托的帅位受到威胁。球队已连续两个主场失利，目前仅占据最后一个附加赛席位，落后第二名斯旺西4分。努诺预计仍会获得时间扭转局面，但俱乐部关键人物已日渐失去耐心。
The newsWest Ham's poor start has put manager Nuno Espírito Santo under pressure after Saturday's 3-1 loss to West Bromwich Albion dropped them to eighth in the Championship. With two straight home defeats, they hold the final playoff spot, four points behind second-placed Swansea. Nuno is expected to be given time, but key figures are growing impatient.
下课声里没人问：谁把球队造成这样
Everyone asks who's to blame. Nobody asks who built this squad.

西汉姆联在英冠排第八，努诺的位置开始晃。报道写得很清楚：俱乐部内部觉得「我们有英冠最好的阵容，应该踢得更好」。这句话本身就是问题所在——它把一支刚降级的球队的困境，全部记在主教练账上。

降级之后，俱乐部卖了萨默维尔和费尔南德斯，留下了鲍恩和绍切克，又花2200万英镑从凯尔特人买来恩格尔斯，创下英格兰足球联赛的转会纪录。这些决定是谁做的？报道说得很清楚：任何关于努诺去留的决定都是董事会集体决定，临时CEO卡里姆·维拉尼和招募总监尼尔斯·科彭都有发言权。也就是说，买人的人和评判买人效果的人是同一批人。

这就是共谋者理论最朴素的形态：权力结构把责任向下转移，自己保留解释权。沙利文上赛季降级后就想换掉努诺，克雷廷斯基主张保持连续性，两人博弈的结果不是「谁对球队更好」，而是谁在董事会里股份更大。克雷廷斯基九月买下46%的股份成为最大股东，沙利文辞去联合主席去应对「不实指控」，股份从38%涨到40%。这些数字和足球无关，和权力有关。

努诺52岁，去年九月接替波特，没能救球队于降级。报道说他「没能确定最佳11人」，13场只零封3次，6次先丢球。这些都是真的。但一支球队先丢球六次、中场两个防守型球员马加萨和阿尔瓦雷斯都失宠、三名前锋加起来只进五球——这些是教练一个人的问题，还是招募、阵容构建、赛季规划的问题？董事会说「我们有最好的阵容」，这句话的功能是：如果阵容最好而成绩不好，那一定是教练的错。解释权在谁手里，责任就在谁身上。

球迷在西布朗那场比赛里表达了不满。球迷的不满是真的，但球迷的不满被董事会用来当作换帅的合法性来源。这是文化暴力的经典操作：把结构问题翻译成个人问题，然后让愤怒的群众去攻击那个最容易被替换的零件。

西汉姆周三要去加的夫城。如果努诺赢了，董事会松一口气；如果输了，国际比赛日就是动手的窗口。无论哪种结果，董事会都不用回答那个真正的问题：一支球队从英超降到英冠，是谁在决定它该是什么样子。

换教练是最便宜的公关。真正贵的，是承认自己就是那个把球队造成这样的人。

West Ham are eighth in the Championship and Nuno Espírito Santo's job is under threat. The report is clear: the feeling inside the club is that they have the best squad in the Championship and should be playing better. That sentence is the problem. It assigns the entire failure of a relegated club to one man.

After going down, the club sold Crysencio Summerville and Mateus Fernandes, kept Jarrod Bowen and Tomas Soucek, and broke the EFL transfer record paying £22m for Arne Engels. Who made those decisions? The report says any decision on Nuno's future will be a collective board decision, with interim CEO Karim Virani and director of recruitment Nils Koppen having their say. The people who bought the players are the same people judging whether the players work.

This is the co-conspirator theory in its plainest form: the power structure transfers responsibility downward and keeps the interpretive authority for itself. David Sullivan wanted Nuno gone after relegation last season; Daniel Kretinsky argued for continuity. The outcome wasn't about who was better for the club. It was about who held more shares. Kretinsky bought 46% in September to become the largest shareholder; Sullivan resigned as joint-chair to fight what he called false allegations about his personal conduct, and his stake rose from 38% to 40%. None of these numbers are about football. They're about power.

Nuno is 52. He replaced Graham Potter in September 2025 and failed to keep the club up. The report says he has struggled to settle on his best 11, kept three clean sheets in 13 games, conceded first on six occasions. All true. But six times conceding first, two holding midfielders out of favour, three strikers with five league goals between them — is that one coach's failure, or recruitment, squad-building, season planning? The board says they have the best squad. The function of that sentence is: if the squad is best and the results are bad, it must be the coach. Whoever holds the explanation holds the blame.

Supporters showed unrest during the West Brom game. Their anger is real. But that anger is being used by the board as legitimacy for a sacking. This is cultural violence's classic move: translate a structural problem into an individual one, then let the angry crowd attack the most replaceable part.

West Ham visit Cardiff on Wednesday. Win, and the board breathes. Lose, and the international break is the window. Either way, the board never has to answer the real question: who decided what this club should look like after relegation.

Sacking the manager is the cheapest PR. The expensive part is admitting you're the one who built it this way.
