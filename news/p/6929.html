一部电影的立项名单里没有故事A Greenlight List With No Story On It

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要亚马逊米高梅影业正在开发ND Stevenson 2025年出版的插画小说《Scarlet Morning》的真人电影版，由凯特·赫伦执导并联合编剧，罗伊·李等担任制片。原著讲述两名孤儿等待父母多年后加入海盗船队、拯救「迪克森之海」的故事，由HarperCollins旗下Quill Tree Books出版。
The newsAmazon MGM Studios is developing a live-action film of ND Stevenson's 2025 illustrated novel Scarlet Morning, with Kate Herron directing and co-writing, and Roy Lee among the producers. The middle grade book, published by HarperCollins imprint Quill Tree Books, follows two orphans who join a pirate crew to save a land called Dickerson's Sea.
立项名单很长，故事只有一句
The credits are long; the story is one sentence.

这条新闻里唯一有内容的句子是那句故事梗概：两个孤儿等了父母很多年，等不住了，跟一船海盗去救一片叫迪克森之海的地方。剩下的全是名字、头衔、经纪公司。

两个孤儿。等父母。等不住。这是第一章的东西——表达是存在的确证，而这两个孩子连「等」这个动作都做不下去了，说明他们原先那套表达（在原地做一个孩子）已经失效，必须换一种活法，才能重新确认自己是谁。跟海盗走不是冒险，是重新投票。

然后是「救一片海」。这个设定有意思的地方在于：它给了一个无父无母的孩子一个不需要父母也能成立的目标。不是找爸妈，是救海。如果电影真的按这个走，那它做的是一件很具体的事——把「家庭」从叙事的中心挪开一步，让孩子的主体性不靠血缘来兑现。这在儿童向作品里不常见，因为大多数这类故事最后都会把孩子送回一个家庭，哪怕是拼凑的家庭。

但报道本身没有告诉我这部电影会不会这么做。它告诉我的是：谁导演、谁制片、谁编剧、谁执行制片、谁在哪家公司、谁签在哪个经纪公司、谁的上一部作品被艾美提名、谁的第一部项目是这个。这份名单精确到每一个经纪人的名字，唯独没有精确到这两个孤儿到底要什么。

这就是好莱坞的立项新闻：它是一份权力关系的清单，不是一份故事的清单。名字的排列顺序本身就是定价——谁挂制片、谁挂执行制片、谁的公司是第一次做长片，这些位置决定了谁在这部电影里有话语权。而真正决定这部电影说什么的人，是那个写剧本的，可报道只用了半句话交代她写过什么。

我不怀疑Stevenson。Nimona被提名奥斯卡最佳动画长片，She-Ra重制版是他做的，Lumberjanes也是他。这些作品有一个共同点：它们都不打算把主角塞回一个标准家庭里。所以Scarlet Morning被立项这件事本身，可能是好事——一本把孤儿和海盗放在一起、把「救一片海」放在「找爸妈」前面的中年级插画小说，正在被一家大厂牌做成真人电影。

但「立项」这个词要小心。立项不是拍完，拍完不是上映，上映不是拍对了。这中间每一道关口都有共谋者在重新定价：把海盗改成可爱的，把两个孤儿的性别改得安全一点，把「救海」最后改成「回家」。报道里没有一个字能排除这些，也没有一个字能确认这些。

所以我只知道一件事：这条新闻证明了一本具体的书被看见了，但没有证明这本书里的那个判断被保留了。两个孤儿等不住父母，于是自己上了船——这个动作如果被拍成了「他们终于找到了家」，那这本书就白写了。

The only sentence in this story with any content in it is the logline: two orphans wait years for their parents, can't wait anymore, and join a pirate crew to save a place called Dickerson's Sea. Everything else is names, titles, agencies.

Two orphans. Waiting for parents. Can't wait anymore. That's chapter one material. Expression is how you confirm you exist, and these two kids can no longer perform the one expression they had — being a child, in place, waiting. So they change how they live in order to re-confirm who they are. Sailing with pirates isn't an adventure. It's a re-vote.

Then: save a sea. What's interesting here is that it hands a parentless child a goal that doesn't require parents to be valid. Not find mom and dad. Save the sea. If the movie actually goes there, it's doing something specific — moving family off the center of the narrative so the kid's agency doesn't have to be cashed out in blood relation. That's rare in children's material, because most of these stories eventually deliver the child back into a family, even a cobbled-together one.

But the report doesn't tell me whether the movie will do that. It tells me who directs, who produces, who writes, who executive produces, who is at which company, who is signed to which agency, whose last project got an Emmy nomination, whose first feature this is. The list is precise down to every agent's name, and imprecise about what these two orphans actually want.

That's what a Hollywood greenlight story is: an inventory of power relations, not an inventory of story. The order of names is itself a pricing mechanism — who gets producer, who gets executive producer, whose company is doing its first feature. Those slots decide who has a say. And the person who decides what the film says is the one writing the script, whom the report disposes of in half a sentence about her previous credits.

I'm not doubting Stevenson. Nimona got an Oscar nomination for best animated feature, he ran the She-Ra reboot, he made Lumberjanes. Those works share one thing: none of them are trying to stuff the protagonist back into a standard family. So Scarlet Morning getting set up may be good news — a middle grade illustrated novel that puts orphans and pirates together, and puts saving a sea ahead of finding mom and dad, is being made into a live-action film by a major studio.

But be careful with the word greenlight. Greenlit isn't shot. Shot isn't released. Released isn't right. Every gate along the way has co-conspirators repricing the thing: make the pirates cute, make the orphans' genders safe, turn save the sea into go home. Nothing in this report rules that out. Nothing in it rules it in.

So I know one thing: this story proves a specific book got seen. It does not prove the book's judgment got kept. Two orphans can't wait for their parents anymore, so they get on a boat. If that gets filmed as they finally found a home, the book was written for nothing.
