一张专辑的排名，和排名里的那套爱情剧本An Album Ranking, and the Love Script Inside It

文娱 文化层 Billboard ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
排行榜给歌排名，歌给女人排位置
Charts rank songs; songs rank where women stand.

Billboard 给 Doja Cat 新专辑《Vie》的十五首歌排了个名次，从垫底到最佳。这不是新闻，这是产品说明书。但说明书里写着的东西值得看一眼：这十五首歌里，绝大多数在唱同一件事——追一个男人、留住一个男人、问那个男人快不快乐、跟那个男人去做伴侣咨询。

专辑的收尾曲《Come Back》被描述成她在这张唱片里追了一整张的那个人，歌词里她算了一笔账：“如果你继续这样，为我开门、替我拿包、多抱抱我，我觉得那也补不回我失去的希望。”这句词写得很诚实，诚实到把浪漫爱叙事的定价机制直接摊开了：门、包、拥抱，是可以标价的服务，而希望是收不回来的成本。Billboard 的乐评人注意到了这句，然后说结局“悬而未决”。悬而未决的是她有没有追到人，不是这笔账划不划算。

《Silly! Fun!》里她问对方要不要生个孩子，乐评人形容这首歌“异想天开、惹人喜爱”。生育在这张专辑里是一个撒娇式的问句，和“我们要不要去个派对”并排出现，中间没有逗号以外的任何东西。这不是 Doja Cat 的错，这是浪漫爱叙事最标准的语法：把一件改变女人身体、职业轨迹和余生现金流的事，写成一个可爱的小提议。

《Acts of Service》更直接，歌名就是爱的五种语言里的那一条，她一本正经地念着自己偏好的“爱的语言”，乐评人说她唱到结尾“对爱这个概念本身都有点困了”。这句评价比整首歌都准。

有意思的是《Gorgeous》和《AAAHH MEN!》。前者是她在歌里欣赏自己和身边女人的美，后者被形容成一首“对男人保持警惕的、复杂又上头的颂歌”，乐评人说它既能填满体育场，也能塞进《芭比2》的原声带。这两首是这张专辑里唯二没有把镜头对准男人的歌，而它们在榜单上一个排第三、一个排第四——不是垫底，但也没拿到第一。第一名给了《Jealous Type》，一首关于嫉妒的、控制感十足的歌。

这不是在骂一张流行专辑。流行专辑就是干这个的：它把一整套关于爱、关于关系、关于女人应该追着谁跑的脚本，包装成八十年代的迪斯科节奏，让你在通勤路上跟着哼。哼的时候脚本就进去了。Billboard 的这份排名做的也是同一件事——它不评价这套脚本，它只评价这套脚本的编曲好不好听。

所以真正被排名的不是十五首歌，是十五种表达方式，以及它们各自被允许占据多少注意力。而这份榜单里，一个女人的独立、自足、对同性的欣赏，排在嫉妒和追逐的后面。这不是乐评人的恶意，这是市场的品味，而品味从来都是被定价的。

这张专辑里没有一首歌问：如果她追不到那个人，她失去的那些希望，谁来赔。

Billboard ranked all fifteen tracks on Doja Cat's new album Vie, from worst to best. This isn't news, it's a product manual. But the manual says something worth reading: most of these fifteen songs are about the same thing — chasing a man, keeping a man, asking whether that man is happy, going to couples therapy with that man.

The closing track, "Come Back," is described as the person she's chased through much of the record. In it she does the math out loud: if he keeps holding doors, carrying her bag, holding her more — "I don't think that would make up for the hope I lost." That line is honest enough to lay the pricing mechanism of the romance narrative right on the table: doors, bags, hugs are services with a price, and hope is a cost you don't recover. Billboard's critic noticed the line, then called the ending "ambiguous." What's ambiguous is whether she got the guy, not whether the deal was worth it.

On "Silly! Fun!" she asks whether they should have kids, and the critic calls the song whimsical and lovestruck. In this album, having a child is a coy question mark placed right next to "should we go to a party," with nothing between them but a comma. That's not Doja Cat's fault. It's the standard grammar of the romance narrative: take something that rewrites a woman's body, career trajectory and cash flow for the rest of her life, and phrase it as a cute little proposal.

"Acts of Service" is even more direct — the title is literally one of the five love languages, and she recites her preferred one matter-of-factly. The critic says she sounds "kind of sleepy at the idea of love in general" by the end. That review is more accurate than the song.

The interesting ones are "Gorgeous" and "AAAHH MEN!" The first has her reveling in her own beauty and the beauty of the women around her; the second is described as a complex, man-wary anthem that could fill stadiums and fit snugly on the Barbie 2 soundtrack. These are the only two tracks on the album that don't point the camera at a man — and they land third and fourth on the ranking. Not bottom, but not first. First place went to "Jealous Type," a slick, controlling song about jealousy.

This isn't a attack on a pop album. Pop albums do exactly this: they take a whole script about love, relationships, and who women should be chasing, wrap it in '80s disco, and let you hum along on your commute. The humming is how the script gets in. Billboard's ranking does the same thing — it doesn't evaluate the script, it evaluates whether the script's arrangement sounds good.

So what's really being ranked isn't fifteen songs. It's fifteen modes of expression, and how much attention each one is allowed to occupy. And in this list, a woman's independence, self-sufficiency, and appreciation of other women rank behind jealousy and pursuit. That's not the critic's malice. That's the market's taste — and taste has always been priced.

Not one song on this album asks: if she never catches him, who pays back the hope she lost.
