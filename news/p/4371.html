足球被定价权绑架：一场关于“所有权”的共谋战争Football's Price Tag: A War of Complicity over Ownership

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-24 § 链接
当运动被视为可出售的资产，规则就成了权力者的定价单。
When sport is treated as a saleable asset, rules become nothing more than a price list for the powerful.

Infantino 试图通过 FFE 计划出售世界杯股份，这本质上是一次极其粗暴的 weaponization。他试图将足球这个属于全球共识的 cultural 资产，转化为一个可以被私募股权和资本操纵的商业 product。在 Infantino 的逻辑里，足球不再是关于竞技的表达，而是一个可以被切分、定价并出售的认知入口。

Ceferin 将此比作比超级联赛（Super League）更糟糕的计划，因为前者是俱乐部在抢地盘，而后者是直接把整个运动的“所有权”给卖了。这揭示了足球管理层内部的一场存在性战争：一方试图维持一种名为“体育精神”的伪装，而另一方则试图撕掉伪装，直接进入资本的 meta-violence 阶段——即定义谁拥有这个运动的最终解释权。

最令人作呕的细节是 Trump 试图通过私人关系干预红牌裁决。这证明了在 Infantino 的统治下，足球的 rules 已经沦为了权力交换的筹码。当一个政治人物可以影响场上的判罚，足球就从一项运动变成了权力的表演场。所谓的“规则”在这些共谋者面前毫无意义，只有利益的对齐才是唯一的真实。

北欧足协要求第三方审查并禁止私人所有权，这在 structural 层面上是一次反击。但我们要警惕：这场战争是两个权力中心（UEFA vs FIFA）的博弈，而非底层球迷的胜利。当精英们在争论足球应该由谁来“管理”时，他们依然在共谋一件事——足球的定义权永远不在球员和球迷手中。

Infantino's attempt to sell stakes in the World Cup via the FFE scheme is a crude act of weaponisation. He sought to transform football—a global cultural asset—into a commercial product manipulatable by private equity and capital. In Infantino's logic, football is no longer an expression of athletic competition, but a cognitive entry point to be sliced, priced, and sold.

Ceferin describes this as worse than the Super League because while the latter was about clubs grabbing territory, the former was about selling the very ownership of the sport. This reveals an existential war within football's governing elite: one side clings to the facade of "sportsmanship," while the other attempts to strip that mask away and enter a stage of meta-violence—defining who holds the ultimate power of interpretation over the game.

The most repulsive detail is Trump's attempt to influence a red card via personal ties. This proves that under Infantino, the rules of football have devolved into bargaining chips for power exchanges. When a politician can sway a referee's decision, the game ceases to be a sport and becomes a theatre of power. Rules are meaningless to these co-conspirators; only the alignment of interests is real.

The Nordic associations demanding a third-party review and a ban on private ownership is a structural counter-attack. However, we must remain vigilant: this is a clash between two power centres (UEFA vs FIFA), not a victory for the grassroots. While elites argue over who should "manage" football, they remain in complicity over one thing—the power to define the game will never belong to the players or the fans.
