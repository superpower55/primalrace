用程序正义打程序正义Fixing the Court by Breaking It

国际 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
新闻摘要波兰总理图斯克周六晚间发表电视讲话，誓言推进宪法法院改革，称「在某些问题上没有妥协余地」。此前周五，政府支持的法官暂停了法院院长和副院长的职务，引发抗议和防暴警察部署。图斯克称该法院在PiS执政期间已瘫痪十余年。
The newsPolish PM Donald Tusk vowed in a TV address on Saturday to push through a contested overhaul of the constitutional court, saying there can be no compromise. On Friday, government-backed judges suspended the court's chief and deputy, prompting protests and riot police deployment. Tusk said the court had been crippled for over a decade under PiS rule.
没人提法院里那些案子，也没人提谁在为这场拉锯付代价。
Nobody in this story mentions the cases inside the court, or who pays for the standoff.

图斯克说"某些事情上没有妥协余地"。这句话本身没错，问题是：谁被排除在"妥协"之外，谁又在为"不妥协"付账。

报道里的机制很清楚：法院瘫痪了十多年，PiS 八年执政期间把它变成了党争的一部分，欧盟法庭也判过它不算真正的法院。图斯克2023年回朝，想拆掉 PiS 的司法改造，被总统纳夫罗茨基的否决权卡死——今年年初，五个议会通过的法官，总统拒绝宣誓。于是周五，政府支持的法官直接动手，暂停了 PiS 任命的首席法官和副手，程序控制权被夺。

这一手，靠的不是立法，不是谈判，是程序上的突袭。赫尔辛基人权基金会说这个临时停职的法律依据"可疑"，连自己阵营的外长西科尔斯基都承认法院外的事"不优雅"。"不优雅"是个很体面的词，翻译过来是：我们没走完程序，但我们认为对方更没走完。

图斯克那句小偷抢老太太包的比喻，很妙，也很危险。妙在于它把对方描述成先动手的人；危险在于，这个比喻里老太太是被抢的对象，不是有权决定自己包里装什么的人。法院在这套叙事里就是那个包——谁抢到算谁的。而真正在法院里等判决的人，那些离婚、劳动纠纷、宪法申诉的普通人，从头到尾没出现在总理的电视讲话里。

再看这条新闻怎么收尾的：梅德韦杰夫也出来骂这是"宪法政变"，图斯克说"一切就清楚了"。一个俄罗斯前总统的表态，被用来给波兰国内的反对派定性。这是第三章那套东西：把对手和外部敌人焊在一起，认知入口一关，剩下的事就不用讨论了。

真正的问题不是图斯克对不对，PiS 该不该被清理。真问题是：波兰的法治之争已经变成两个阵营轮流用程序漏洞打程序正义，每次都说自己是在修复，每次修复都留下新的伤口。专家到现在还在争这次程序操作合不合法——一个连合法性都要专家吵的事，它修复的到底是法院，还是选举前的民调？

明年议会选举，民调咬得很紧。图斯克需要一场"我在动手"的表演，纳夫罗茨基需要一场"我在抵抗"的表演，两边都有观众。法院楼外有防暴警察，楼里有被暂停的法官，而那个老太太的包，还在半空中。

Tusk says that "in certain matters, there can be no compromise." Fine. The question is who gets excluded from the compromise, and who foots the bill for the refusal.

The mechanism here is plain. The court has been paralysed for over a decade; under eight years of PiS it became an arm of party politics, and European tribunals said as much. Tusk returned in 2023 to unwind that, and got stuck on President Nawrocki's veto — earlier this year Nawrocki refused to swear in five judges parliament had approved. So on Friday, government-backed judges simply moved: they suspended the PiS-appointed chief judge and deputy and took procedural control.

That is not legislation and not negotiation. It is a procedural raid. The Helsinki Foundation for Human Rights calls the legal basis for the suspension "questionable"; even Tusk's own foreign minister concedes the scene outside the court was "not elegant." Not elegant is a polite word for: we skipped the process, because we think they skipped it worse.

Tusk's mugger-and-old-lady analogy is clever and dangerous. Clever because it casts the other side as the one who struck first. Dangerous because in that image the old woman is the one being robbed, not the one who decides what goes in her bag. The court becomes the bag — whoever grabs it, holds it. And the people actually waiting on rulings, the divorces and labour disputes and constitutional complaints, never appear in the prime minister's address.

Then watch how the story closes: Medvedev also denounced the move as a constitutional coup, and Tusk said, "and everything is clear." A former Russian president's statement gets used to label the domestic opposition. That is Chapter Three exactly — weld your opponents to a foreign enemy, shut the cognitive entrance, and the argument is over.

The real issue is not whether Tusk is right or whether PiS deserved cleaning out. It is that Poland's rule-of-law fight has become two camps taking turns exploiting procedural loopholes in the name of procedural justice. Each side calls it repair; each repair leaves a new wound. Experts are still arguing over whether this move is even legal — and a move whose legality is still under expert dispute is not repairing the court. It is repairing the polls.

Next year's parliamentary election looks close. Tusk needs a performance of doing something; Nawrocki needs a performance of resisting. Both have audiences. Outside the building there is riot police, inside there are suspended judges, and the old woman's handbag is still in mid-air.
