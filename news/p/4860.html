用“术语”掩盖的权力傲慢与阶级清洗Power Arrogance and Class Cleansing Masked by 'Terminology'

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-08 § 链接
将公共生活定义为“问题”，是权力通过定义事实来实施结构暴力的典型手段。
Defining public life as a 'problem' is a classic structural violence used by power to control reality.

Westminster council 试图用一套所谓的 licensing policy 扼杀 Soho 的 nightlife，最荒诞的切入点竟然是“vertical drinking”——站着喝酒。在英国 pub 文化中，站着喝酒是典型的公共社交表达，而议会试图通过强制要求“table service”和“seated”来将其取缔。这根本不是在讨论酒精管理，而是在进行一场关于“谁有权出现在公共空间”的筛选。

最令人作呕的 meta-violence 在于 council 的回应：他们声称 81% 的反对者是因为“不理解术语 (terminology)”才反对的。这是一个极其阴险的逻辑：当你不同意我的权力扩张时，不是因为我的政策有错，而是因为你的认知水平不足以理解我的“专业术语”。通过垄断解释权，council 将公民的合法诉求定义为“认知缺失”，从而将权力傲慢包装成专业主义。

这背后是典型的 NIMBY (Not In My Backyard) 心理与阶级共谋。Soho Society 这种打着“保护特色”旗号的组织，本质上是在通过限制营业时间和空间形态，将 Soho 从一个充满生命力的公共博弈场，改造为一个安静、可控、符合中产审美且排除“低端”社交的私人花园。他们不需要 nightlife thrive，他们只需要 nightlife 处于一种被阉割的、可被管理的状态。

Sadiq Khan 获得的新权力或许能提供 structural 层的救济，但这场战争揭示了一个残酷的事实：当权力掌握了定义“什么是事实”和“什么是专业”的认知入口，它就可以在不流血的情况下，通过修改几行 licensing document，就把一个社区的灵魂给杀掉。

Westminster council is attempting to strangle Soho's nightlife through a licensing policy, with the most absurd target being 'vertical drinking.' In British pub culture, standing and drinking is a fundamental expression of public socialization. By mandating 'table service' and 'seating,' the council isn't managing alcohol; they are filtering who is allowed to exist in public space.

The most sinister meta-violence lies in the council's response: claiming that 81% of respondents opposed the plan because they 'did not understand the terminology.' This is a predatory logic: if you disagree with my power grab, it's not because the policy is wrong, but because your cognitive capacity is insufficient to grasp my 'professional jargon.' By monopolizing the power of interpretation, the council redefines legitimate civic opposition as 'ignorance,' masking breathtaking arrogance as professionalism.

This is a classic collusion between NIMBYism and class interests. Groups like the Soho Society, under the guise of 'preserving character,' are actually trying to transform Soho from a vibrant public arena of existential war into a quiet, controlled, middle-class garden. They don't want nightlife to thrive; they want it castrated and manageable.

While Sadiq Khan's new powers might offer structural relief, the event reveals a grim truth: when power controls the cognitive entry points of 'what is fact' and 'what is professional,' it can kill the soul of a community by simply editing a few lines in a licensing document, all without spilling a drop of blood.
