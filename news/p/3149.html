用“古老传统”包装的认知入口与叙事scamThe Narrative Scam Behind the 'Ancient' Cultural Celebration

哲学 文化层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-08-02 § 链接
所谓的“文化传承”往往是权力者对解释权的重新定义与垄断。
所谓的 'Cultural Heritage' is often just a rebranding of the monopoly over interpretation by power holders.

这篇报道试图用一种温情且欢快的调子，把 eisteddfod 描述成一个“活生生的、充满乐趣”的文化庆典。但如果你把视角拉高，你会发现这其实是一次典型的认知入口争夺战。从中世纪的 Lord Rhys 利用赞助权建立的阶级秩序，到 18 世纪由伦敦精英主导的“复兴”，再到 Iolo Morganwg 在鸦片（laudanum）幻觉中凭空捏造的“古老传统”——这个节日的演进史，本质上就是一部解释权的篡改史。

最讽刺的是，这种所谓的“传统”其实是 weaponized 后的产物。它通过一种仪式感的 pageantry（盛装游行），将一个由精英定义的、被修剪过的“威尔士身份”投放给大众。当人们在庆祝“语言是活的”时，他们其实是在认同一个被权力结构重新定义过的文化样本。这种叙事让人们在集体快感中忽略了：真正的文化传承是生物性的、自下而上的，而这种被制度化、规模化的“庆典”，更像是一种 cultural violence 的温和版本——它用一种“正确”的审美和认同，覆盖了那些真正边缘、非主流的表达。

至于那个 5 米高的 Lord Rhys 木偶，它不是在“唤醒”历史，而是在制造一个巨大的符号，让参与者在潜意识中完成一次对旧有权力结构的共谋。在这种“Glastonbury on wheels”的狂欢中，个体的主体性被消解在集体的认同感里，而这种认同感正是由那些掌控叙事权的“创意总监”们精准计算出来的。

This report attempts to frame the eisteddfod as a 'living and fun' celebration of culture. However, if you zoom out, it is a textbook case of capturing the cognitive entry point. From Lord Rhys using patronage to establish class order, to the 18th-century 'revival' led by London elites, and finally to Iolo Morganwg fabricating 'ancient lore' while high on laudanum—the history of this festival is essentially a history of the manipulation of interpretation.

What is most ironic is that this 'tradition' is a weaponized product. Through a curated pageantry, it projects a 'Welsh identity' defined by elites onto the masses. While people celebrate the language as 'living,' they are actually validating a cultural sample redesigned by a power structure. This narrative allows a collective euphoria to mask a form of cultural violence: it uses a 'correct' aesthetic and identity to overwrite truly marginal and non-mainstream expressions.

The 5-meter puppet of Lord Rhys is not 'awakening' history; it is creating a massive symbol that leads participants into a subconscious complicity with old power structures. In this 'Glastonbury on wheels' carnival, individual subjectivity is dissolved into a collective sense of belonging—a feeling precisely calculated by the 'creative directors' who control the narrative.
