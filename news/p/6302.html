史努比不需要第六个排名Snoopy Doesn't Need a Sixth Place

文娱 文化层 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
把漫画排成榜单，是给品味定价的生意
Ranking comics is the business of pricing taste.

Polygon 这篇把舒尔茨1960年代的六则《花生》排了个名次，第一名、第二名、第六名，配一句“他真正在做饭”。这不是漫画评论，这是把品味变成可流通商品的第一步：先排序，再定价。

舒尔茨的漫画里没有排名。露西不会因为自己是最佳露西而获得奖杯，查理·布朗写圣诞贺卡时铅笔断了，这件事本身没有名次，它只是发生了。文章自己也承认这一点——它说这些漫画“捕捉了生活的小瞬间”，然后转身给这些瞬间打了分。捕捉小瞬间和给小瞬间排名，是两件互相拆台的事。

第三章讲审美入口：有话语权的人给事物贴标签、定价值，标签的贵贱就是定价权。这篇文章的标签是“genius”“perfect”“nothing tops”。它没有说这些漫画好在哪里的具体机制，它说的是“你该觉得好”。读者被邀请进入一个品味共同体，进入的方式是点头。

更值得看的是它引用的商品化痕迹。“Happiness is a warm puppy”这句话被印在无数周边上，文章特意说“所有这些产品并没有让这句话变廉价”。这句话本身就在承认：它已经被廉价化过一轮了，现在需要一篇评论来把它重新洗回真诚。评论在这里的功能不是分析，是给商品做二次包装。

舒尔茨1965年画史努比看雪，四个格子，最后一句“Actually, they all look alike to me!”。这句话放在这篇榜单里正好：六个格子里的漫画，在“genius”这个标签下，看起来都差不多。真正被排掉的是那个安静看雪的下午——它本来不需要被证明是第一名。

Polygon ranks six Peanuts strips from the 1960s, first through sixth, and adds that Schulz was really cooking. This is not comics criticism. This is the first step in turning taste into a tradable good: sort first, price second.

There is no ranking inside Schulz's strips. Lucy does not win a trophy for being Best Lucy. Charlie Brown's pencil breaks while he writes a Christmas note, and the event has no place in a hierarchy. It simply happens. The article admits this itself — it says these strips capture life's little moments, then turns around and scores the little moments. Capturing them and ranking them are two acts that undermine each other.

Chapter Three describes the aesthetic entrance: those with discursive power label things, assign value, and the label's rank becomes the pricing power. This article's labels are genius, perfect, nothing tops. It does not explain the mechanism of why a strip works. It says you should feel that it works. The reader is invited into a taste community, and the way in is to nod.

The commercialization is right there in the text. The line Happiness is a warm puppy has been printed on countless merchandise items, and the article takes care to say all that product hasn't cheapened the sentiment. That sentence admits the cheapening already happened, and now a piece of criticism is needed to wash it back to sincerity. Criticism here does not analyze. It repackages the product a second time.

In 1965 Schulz drew Snoopy watching snow in four panels, ending with Actually, they all look alike to me! That line belongs in this list: six strips under the label genius, and they all look alike. What gets ranked out is the quiet afternoon of watching snow — it never needed to be proven first.
