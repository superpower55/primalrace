大学的“死亡螺旋”与被当成筹码的学生The University Death Spiral and Students as Bargaining Chips

哲学 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-30 § 链接
当教育被异化为资产负债表上的数字，知识的传递就成了博弈的筹码。
When education is reduced to a balance sheet, the transmission of knowledge becomes a mere pawn in a power game.

爱丁堡、诺丁汉、邓迪，英国高校的罢工潮不是简单的薪资纠纷，而是一场关于“教育”定义权的 structural violence。大学管理层在谈论 4 亿英镑的缺口和“结构调整”，而教授在谈论“大屠杀” (carnage)。这种叙事差额揭示了一个残酷的事实：在管理者的认知入口里，大学不再是学术共同体，而是一家面临现金流危机的公司。当教育被量化为 tuition fees 和 international recruitment 的 KPI，那些不产生即时商业价值的现代语言、音乐和人文学科就成了首先被剔除的“冗余资产”。

最讽刺的博弈在于，学生被推到了存在性战争的最前线，成了 UCU 工会手中唯一的 bargaining chip。学生在同情教授的同时，也在承受着一种被动且不可逆的损失——被抹去的学期和破碎的教学计划。这种结构极其恶劣：管理层通过制造 deficit 来逼迫裁员，工会通过牺牲学生的教学时间来向管理层施压。在这个 closed-loop 里，真正拥有解释权的 administrator 们在表演“遗憾”，而实际的代价由最底层的 staff 和 students 分担。

这种“死亡螺旋” (death spiral) 是典型的元暴力运作：它用“经济必然性”和“理性管理”作为掩体，将学术理想主义包装成一种奢侈的、可以被牺牲的情怀。当一个 elite institution 真的崩塌之前，这种 structural violence 会被伪装成“必要的阵痛”。所谓的“建设性对话”不过是 PR 版本的缓兵之计，因为在权力不对等的情况下，没有利益让渡的对话只是在浪费时间。

The strike waves at Edinburgh, Nottingham, and Dundee are not mere salary disputes, but a manifestation of structural violence regarding the definition of 'education.' While university leaders speak of £400m shortfalls and 'restructuring,' professors describe it as 'carnage.' This narrative gap reveals a grim reality: in the cognitive entry point of administrators, the university is no longer an academic community, but a company facing a liquidity crisis. Once education is quantified by tuition fees and international recruitment KPIs, arts and humanities—which offer no immediate commercial return—become 'redundant assets' to be purged.

The most cynical part of this game is that students have been pushed to the frontlines of an existential war, serving as the only bargaining chip for the UCU. Students find themselves in a precarious position: sympathizing with their professors while suffering an irreversible loss of their own education. This structure is predatory: management uses the deficit to force redundancies, while unions leverage the disruption of students' learning to pressure management. In this closed-loop, the administrators—who hold the power of interpretation—perform 'disappointment,' while the actual costs are borne by the lowest tiers: the staff and the students.

This 'death spiral' is a classic operation of meta-violence. It uses 'economic necessity' and 'rational management' as cover to frame academic idealism as a luxury that can be sacrificed. Until an elite institution actually collapses, this structural violence will be disguised as 'necessary pain.' The so-called 'constructive engagement' is merely a PR version of stalling, because in a state of absolute power asymmetry, dialogue without a transfer of interest is simply a waste of time.
