被否决的法案也是资产A Veto Is a Campaign Asset

国际 结构层 · 文化层 semafor.com ↗ 2026-11-02 § 链接
新闻摘要美国众议员格雷格·卡萨尔在中期选举前接受采访，主张民主党若夺回多数党地位，应推动针对哄抬物价、限制公用事业费率上涨、禁止政客利用职位牟利的法案投票，哪怕被特朗普否决，也可作为2028年的竞选纲领。他反对参议员布莱恩·沙茨将重大政策议题推迟到2028年的主张，并称若拿下参议院，应推动绕过冗长辩论的预算调解法案。
The newsUS Rep. Greg Casar said Democrats, if they win the majority, should force votes on bills targeting price gouging, rising utility rates and politicians profiting from office — even if Trump vetoes them, framing the vetoes as a 2028 platform. He rejected Sen. Brian Schatz's call to defer big policy fights until 2028, and said if Democrats take the Senate, reconciliation bills bypassing the filibuster should be on the table.
他把否决权当竞选素材，账单留给选民。
He treats vetoes as campaign footage and sends voters the bill.

卡萨尔这套话术里最诚实的部分，是他自己说漏的那句：这些法案「有些可能成为法律」。也就是说，其余的大概率不会。不会成为法律的法案，投完票之后去了哪里？去了竞选广告里。

这是一次非常干净的表达武器化操作，入口是「价格」——哄抬物价、电费、政客中饱私囊。这三个议题的共同点是：它们都是真的，选民每天都在付账。用真问题做弹药，比用假问题有效得多，也更难被反驳。卡萨尔不需要法案通过，他需要的是投票记录和特朗普的否决印章，两样东西拼起来就是2028年的海报。

问题在于，被武器化的表达有一个固定成本：它必须停在能被否决的位置上。真要让公用事业费率降下来，需要的是监管机构的人事、州层面的电价机制、反垄断执法，这些不进否决程序，也不上广告。所以「大胆想」的实际含义是：想到刚好够被拦下的地方。

沙茨说的其实是同一件事的另一面——特朗普还在白宫，法案过不了，不如等。两个人争的不是政策，是 timing：一个要把筹码现在兑现成叙事，一个要留到2028年再兑现。选民的账单在这两套算法里都不是变量，是常数。

至于那句「我不想把多数党浪费在和特朗普讨价还价上」——谈判本来就是立法机构的职能。把拒绝谈判包装成骨气，是把结构层的失能翻译成文化层的姿态。姿态免费，费率不免费。

还有一处值得记下：有进步派新议员扬言，除非杰弗里斯承诺进步派政策目标，否则不给议长票。记者问卡萨尔怎么看，他说每个人的票当然是自己做主，然后让每位议员自己去回答这个问题。这是一句标准的共谋者语言——立场站在进步派这边，动作留给别人做，代价留给别人付。

真正被削掉的差额在哪里，报道里一个字没提：那些现在就在为电价发愁的人，在这套「大胆想」里拿到的是2028年的一张选票承诺，而不是这个月的账单减免。差额没缩小，只是被重新分期了。

The most honest line in Casar's pitch is the one he lets slip: some of these bills "might become law." Which means most probably won't. And a bill that doesn't become law doesn't vanish — it goes into an ad.

This is a clean piece of weaponized expression, and the entry point is price: gouging, utility rates, politicians profiting off office. All three are real, and voters pay for them every month. Real grievances make better ammunition than invented ones, and they're harder to argue against. Casar doesn't need the bill to pass. He needs the roll-call vote and Trump's veto stamp, and the two together are a 2028 poster.

Weaponized expression has a fixed cost: it has to stop exactly where it can be blocked. Actually lowering utility rates takes regulators, state-level rate mechanisms, antitrust enforcement — none of which go through a veto, and none of which go on a poster. So "think big" in practice means: think up to the point of being stopped.

Schatz is arguing the same thing from the other side — Trump is still in the White House, the bills won't pass, wait. What the two of them are fighting over isn't policy, it's timing: cash the筹码 into narrative now, or hold it until 2028. Voters' bills are a constant in both calculations, not a variable.

And "I don't want to waste our majority by negotiating down to Trump's level" — negotiation is the legislature's job. Reframing a refusal to negotiate as backbone translates structural failure into cultural posture. Posture is free. Rates aren't.

One more thing worth noting: some incoming progressives have threatened to withhold their speaker votes unless Jeffries commits to progressive goals. Asked about it, Casar said every member's vote is their own, and let each member answer for themselves. That is textbook co-conspirator language — the position is with the progressives, the action is someone else's, the cost is someone else's.

The gap that actually gets closed is nowhere in this report. The people worrying about their electric bill right now are being handed a 2028 campaign promise instead of this month's relief. The gap didn't shrink. It got refinanced.
