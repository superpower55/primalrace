当生理需求被制度性剥夺，所谓的“文明社会”只是结构暴力的PR版本。
When biological needs are systemically denied, 'civilized society' is merely a PR version of structural violence.

In the UK's rail and road transport sectors, drivers are forced to use bottles for excretion or endure dehydration to avoid the risk of having no facilities. This is not mere mismanagement; it is a textbook case of structural violence. When regulators describe welfare facilities as a "basic requirement in any civilized society," the phrasing itself acts as cultural violence—using the word "civilized" to mask the fact that the basic biological needs of frontline workers have been priced at zero in institutional design.

The most repulsive complicity lies with the operators who, a year after signing a "welfare charter," continue to maintain the status quo using bureaucratic excuses like "coordination barriers." This cycle of "promise-failure-repromise" is a mechanism for the center of power to extract maximum utility from the bodies of the marginalized. By manufacturing an illusion of progress, they lure workers to sustain the system while their subjectivity is eroded.

Female drivers bear a disproportionate burden. The biological wall of menstruation and menopause makes the lack of facilities a severe health risk. The industry's attempt to recruit more women into the cab, without solving the toilet crisis, is nothing more than performative political correctness. Inviting women into a system designed by a masculine-centric narrative without basic dignity is simply inviting them into a predatory trap.

Human rights are women's rights, and the first step of human rights is the right to a dignified toilet. If a nation's transport infrastructure relies on drivers urinating in bottles, its "civilization" is merely a collective erasure of biological necessity.
