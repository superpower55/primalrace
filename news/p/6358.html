监控的枪口从来先对准女人The Surveillance Scope Finds Women First

国际 结构层 · 文化层 · 元暴力 WIRED ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
把反监控写成反暴政，漏掉了谁最先被看住
Calling it anti-left misses who gets watched first

ICE 这次被曝出来的东西，规模上确实少见。15 名明尼苏达居民被联邦起诉，辩护律师在证据开示里挖出了一整套针对左翼社群的监控机器：Project Whipple Shield、Operation Puppet Master、Operation Keyhole，120 名 HSI 探员、11 个卧底、Reaper 无人机、爱国者法案的金融追踪工具、15 TB 被渗透的 Signal 聊天记录。上一次美国见到这个量级，是 1960 到 70 年代对 New Left 和反战运动的那一轮。

值得先看清楚的是这套机器的入口在哪一层。它不靠打人，靠的是把“组织社区”重新定义成“犯罪阴谋”。明尼苏达 15 人被控的罪名是串谋阻碍移民执法、非法识别和跟踪联邦探员，而他们的实际行为是志愿者用加密群组记录 DHS 车辆、建立车牌数据库、互相通报。辩护律师 Kevin Riach 那句话很准：他第一次见到被控串谋的当事人还在跑演讲巡回。行为够不上罪名，就把行为周围的那圈组织关系写成罪名——这是文化层的操作，但落下来是结构层的：NSPM 7 在 Charlie Kirk 遇刺后发布，授权联邦执法对所有批评政府政策、持有“反基督教”观念的组织和个人动用一切工具。一份备忘录改写了“什么算威胁”，剩下的只是执行。

这里必须点出共谋者。不是抽象的“体制”，是具体的人在具体的位置上把工具转了个方向。Operation Keyhole 2008 年就存在，本来是圣保罗办公室用来资助毒品、儿童性虐待、金融犯罪卧底调查的项目，2026 年 6 月续期时它资助的六项调查里就有一项是 Whipple Shield。HSI 把反洗钱和反恐的金融工具——财政部 314(a) 可疑活动报告程序、商业数据经纪商、Thomson-Reuters CLEAR——拿来拼一个普通活动人士的驾照号、社保号、住址史、共同登记车辆。有探员甚至用海关税务的行政传票去调记者的通话记录。工具是中性的，转向是人为的，每一次转向都有签字、有续期、有委员会审核。

再看谁在这套机器里被特别看住。报道里提到的名字不多，但每一个都值得停一下：SEIU 加州主席 David Huerta，58 岁，2025 年 6 月抗议中被 HSI 探员 Ryan Ribner 逮捕并被指控制造袭击，7 月视频出来、指控撤销；Ribner 本人现在出现在明尼苏达 15 的案卷里。前 CNN 主播 Don Lemon 因为报道一场教堂抗议被立案，那座教堂的牧师是 ICE 探员。Renee Good 被杀时，SOG 探员 Yue Her 就在现场。把这些点连起来，监控的瞄准镜里站着的，很多是替别人挡枪的人——工会的、报道的、在街头记录执法的人。

这套东西的本质不是“反左”，是重新分配谁有资格被看见。被渗透的教堂、被拍照抄牌的每辆车、被无人机跟拍的活动人士，构成一张名单。名单上的人不一定被起诉，但从此知道自己被看着。这就是元暴力最经济的一种用法：不需要说服你闭嘴，只需要让你相信开口会被记录。

报道里有一个数字对比很说明问题：CIA 当年调查新左翼和反战运动是否受外国影响，高峰期 42 名官员；明尼苏达 15 这一个案子，119 名 HSI 探员。规模变了，但结构没变——国家机器把“批评”翻译成“威胁”，再把“威胁”翻译成“可调查对象”，这个翻译动作从 COINTELPRO 到今天，语法一模一样。

真正没被写出来的是这些被监控者里的女性。反 ICE 的社区响应网络里，做记录、做通报、做后勤、做法律观察的，大量是女性；被无人机跟拍、被卧底混进互助会、被翻出住址史和共同车辆的人里，她们的名字在案卷里，在报道里却只剩数字。监控机器从来不是均匀撒网，它优先罩住那些已经在结构里更暴露的人——她们的住址更容易被查到，她们的经济记录更少被保护，她们被起诉后的代价更高。这层不对称，报道没写，但它是这套机器能运转的润滑油。

What ICE has been caught running in Minnesota is genuinely large. Fifteen residents face federal conspiracy charges, and their attorneys, working through discovery, pulled out a full surveillance apparatus aimed at left-wing organizing: Project Whipple Shield, Operation Puppet Master, Operation Keyhole, 120 HSI agents, 11 undercovers, Reaper drones, Patriot Act financial tools, 15 terabytes of infiltrated Signal chats. The last time the US saw anything at this scale was the 1960s and 70s campaign against the New Left and the anti-war movement.

The entry point matters. This machine doesn't work by hitting people. It works by redefining "organizing your community" as "criminal conspiracy." The Minnesota 15 are charged with conspiring to obstruct immigration enforcement, unlawfully identifying and stalking federal agents. What they actually did was use encrypted groups to log DHS vehicles, build a license-plate database, and dispatch volunteers. Defense attorney Kevin Riach puts it plainly: this is the first time he's had a client accused of conspiracy who went on a speaking tour. When the conduct won't sustain a charge, you write the organizing around the conduct into the charge. That's a cultural-layer move, but it lands structurally: NSPM 7, issued after Charlie Kirk's assassination, authorizes federal law enforcement to use every tool available against anyone who criticizes the administration and espouses "anti-Christian" ideas. One memorandum rewrites what counts as a threat. The rest is execution.

Name the co-conspirators. Not "the system" in the abstract—specific people in specific seats who turned the tools. Operation Keyhole has existed since 2008, funding undercover work on narcotics, child sexual abuse, financial crime. When it was renewed in June 2026, one of its six active investigations was Whipple Shield. HSI took anti-money-laundering and counterterrorism instruments—Treasury's 314(a) suspicious activity reporting, commercial data brokers, Thomson-Reuters CLEAR—and fused them to produce a single activist's driver's license number, Social Security summary, years of address history, jointly registered vehicles. Agents used a customs-and-taxation administrative summons to obtain phone records of journalists. The tools are neutral. The turn is deliberate. Every turn has a signature, a renewal, a committee review.

Look at who gets watched hardest. The names in the piece are few, and each one is worth pausing on: David Huerta, 58, president of SEIU California, arrested at a June 2025 protest by HSI agent Ryan Ribner, charged with assault—charges dropped in July after video contradicted Ribner's account. Ribner now appears in the Minnesota 15 case file. Former CNN host Don Lemon was investigated for reporting on a protest at a Minneapolis church whose pastor was an ICE agent. SOG agent Yue Her was on scene when Renee Good was killed. Connect the dots: the scope is trained on people who stand in front of others—union leaders, reporters, the ones documenting enforcement on the street.

The point isn't "anti-left." It's redistributing who gets to be seen. Infiltrated churches, photographed license plates, drone footage of activists moving through the Twin Cities—together they make a list. Being on the list doesn't require indictment. It requires only that you know you're on it. That's the cheapest use of meta-violence: you don't have to convince anyone to shut up. You just have to make sure they believe speaking will be recorded.

One comparison in the piece is telling. The CIA's investigation into foreign influence on the New Left and anti-war movement peaked at 42 officers. The Minnesota 15 case alone has 119 HSI agents. The scale changed. The structure didn't. The state translates "criticism" into "threat," then "threat" into "investigable subject." That translation has the same grammar from COINTELPRO to today.

What the reporting doesn't say is who inside those networks is most exposed. Community response work—logging, dispatching, logistics, legal observing—is largely done by women. When drones photograph a meeting, when undercovers sit in a know-your-rights training, when financial records get fused into a profile, the people whose names surface in the case file are disproportionately the ones already more legible to the state: thinner financial buffers, less legal protection, higher cost after indictment. The surveillance machine doesn't cast an even net. It settles first on those the structure has already made visible. That asymmetry isn't in the story. It's the oil that keeps the machine running.
