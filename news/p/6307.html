谁在替辛普森数钱Who's Still Cashing In on Simpson

文娱 文化层 The Hollywood Reporter ↗ 2026-11-01 § 链接
新闻摘要两集纪录片《O.J.'s Accomplice》将于11月1日在Peacock上线，重新审视1994年妮可·布朗·辛普森和罗恩·戈德曼谋杀案，探讨O.J.辛普森并非单独作案的可能。该片在Peacock独家播出，可通过DirecTV套餐获得两个月免费观看。
The newsThe two-part docuseries "O.J.'s Accomplice" premieres Nov. 1 on Peacock, reexamining the 1994 murders of Nicole Brown Simpson and Ron Goldman and exploring whether O.J. Simpson acted alone. It streams exclusively on Peacock, with two months free available through DirecTV packages.
三十年后，尸体还在被拿去换订阅。
Thirty years on, the bodies are still being sold for subscriptions.

这篇报道里唯一被反复定价的东西，是两具尸体。

Nicole Brown Simpson 和 Ron Goldman 在 1994 年被杀，报道通篇只把他们当作一个纪录片的钩子，剩下的篇幅全在教你怎么免费看：DirecTV 的 Signature 套餐送两个月 Peacock，Prime Video 有七天试用，试用结束按标准价自动续费。文章开头就写明，通过链接购买，The Hollywood Reporter 可能拿到联盟佣金。

这不是一篇新闻，这是一张优惠券，包着谋杀案的外壳。

把这条放进暴力三角里看，动的是最上面那层。直接暴力三十年前就发生过一次，凶手是谁、有没有第二个人，法庭已经吵完了。现在动的是文化层：一个女人的死被重新包装成“未解之谜”，好让她的名字继续生产注意力。Nicole Brown Simpson 在这篇报道里出现的唯一方式是“1994 年的谋杀案”，她不是一个被杀死的人，是一个可点击的入口。

“辛普森不是一个人干的”——这个说法被当作卖点抛出来，报道没有给出任何证据，也没有交代是谁提出的、依据是什么。它只需要足够耸动，能让人点开、能让人订阅。至于真相，那是纪录片自己的事，反正订阅费已经收下了。

这里有个共谋结构很清楚：平台要新用户，媒体要佣金，制作方要话题。三方分账，成本由谁承担？由那两个已经不能开口的人承担。他们的死被反复取用，取用者不需要征得同意，也不需要给出任何东西作为交换。

报道里没写 Nicole Brown Simpson 的家人怎么想，没写 Ron Goldman 的父亲是否被问过。这不是疏漏，是这类文章的常态——死者的亲属在这套生意里没有位置，他们既不是买家也不是卖家。

三十年前那场审判让全世界看了一个女人怎么在死后被拆解成证据、动机和疑点。三十年后，同一件事被拆解成套餐、试用期和续费价格。形式变了，逻辑没变：她的身体从来不属于她自己。

The only thing repeatedly priced in this article is two dead bodies.

Nicole Brown Simpson and Ron Goldman were killed in 1994. The piece treats them purely as a hook for a docuseries, then spends the rest of its length telling you how to watch for free: two months of Peacock with any DirecTV Signature package, a seven-day Prime Video trial, auto-renewal at the standard rate. The opening line discloses that The Hollywood Reporter may earn an affiliate commission on purchases made through its links.

This is not a news article. It is a coupon wrapped around a murder case.

Put it through the Violence Triangle and the layer that moves is the top one. The direct violence happened thirty years ago; who did it, and whether anyone helped, has already been litigated. What moves now is the cultural layer: a woman's death repackaged as an unsolved mystery so her name can keep generating attention. Nicole Brown Simpson appears here in exactly one form — "the 1994 murders." Not a person who was killed. A clickable entry point.

"O.J. didn't act alone" is thrown out as a selling point. The article offers no evidence, no attribution, no indication of who is making the claim or on what basis. It only needs to be lurid enough to get a click and a signup. The truth is the documentary's problem. The subscription fee is already collected.

The co-conspiracy is legible: the platform wants new users, the outlet wants commission, the producers want buzz. Three parties split the take. Who bears the cost? The two people who can no longer speak. Their deaths are drawn on repeatedly, without consent and without anything given in return.

The article does not say what Nicole Brown Simpson's family thinks. It does not say whether Ron Goldman's father was asked. That is not an oversight; it is standard for this genre. The bereaved have no position in this economy — they are neither buyer nor seller.

Thirty years ago, a trial let the world watch a woman get disassembled after death into evidence, motive, and doubt. Thirty years later, the same case gets disassembled into bundles, trial periods, and renewal prices. The form changed. The logic didn't: her body was never hers.
