死者有基础病，奶牛没有The dead had conditions; the cows didn't

其他 结构层 · 文化层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-11-03 § 链接
新闻摘要华盛顿州亚基马县一名居民在接触受感染奶牛后感染禽流感，因重症住院并于周日死亡，生前有基础疾病。这是美国今年首例禽流感死亡病例。自10月初以来，该县已有33个奶牛群检测出高致病性禽流感阳性，共记录3例人感染病例。
The newsA Washington state resident died Sunday after being hospitalized with severe bird flu, contracted at a Yakima county dairy with infected cattle. The person had underlying health conditions. It is the first US bird flu death this year. Since early October, 33 dairy herds in the county have tested positive, with three human cases recorded.
风险被写成「低」，代价写在一个人的病历上。
Risk is written as "low"; the cost is written on one person's chart.

一个在亚基马县奶牛场接触了病牛的人死了。卫生官员的通报里，这件事被处理成两句话：一，死者有基础疾病；二，对普通公众的风险仍然很低。两句都是真的，两句合起来完成了一次转移——把一场产业里的传染病，改写成一个个人的健康问题。

加尔通的算式在这里很好用：33个奶牛群阳性，3例人感染，1例死亡，这是实际。可能是什么？是奶牛场的工人被纳入常规检测、口罩和防护装备被强制执行、阳性牛群被隔离而不是继续挤奶。这个差额不是天灾，是结构层的选择。风险从来不是均匀分布的，它沿着谁必须靠近病牛、谁没有带薪病假、谁不敢拒绝上班这条线落下去。

「对普通公众风险低」这句话的修辞功能，是让「普通公众」安心地不去看那些不普通的人。真正的高风险人群被通报点名列出来了：奶牛工人，以及与受感染动物长期密切接触的人。名单都写好了，然后是防护、检测、补偿——报道一个字没提。死者的身份没有公布，他的工作条件、是否拿到防护装备、出现症状后多久才就医，全部缺席。缺席的部分正好是结构暴力发生的地方。

还有一层更安静的操作：把死因归到「基础疾病」上。这在公共卫生里是标准表述，在叙事里却是一次定价——它把死亡从「工作环境的产物」划归为「个人体质的意外」。于是没有人需要为那33个阳性牛群负责，也没有人需要回答为什么一个人要在病牛旁边工作到被感染。

CDC记录了2024年以来72例人感染，并坚持没有已知的人传人。这个说法目前站得住。但「尚未人传人」是一句关于昨天的陈述，不是关于明天的保证；病毒每多感染一个哺乳动物宿主，就多一次掷骰子。把这句话当作不需要加强监测的理由，是在用过去的运气给未来的风险定价。

报道里没有出现的名字，比出现的更值得看：没有农场主的名字，没有奶牛场的名字，没有州农业部门关于牛群处置的说明。一个死了人、33个牛群带毒的地方，公共讨论的重心是「公众风险低」。

那句「风险低」对说它的人是准确的：说这话的人不在牛棚里。

A man who worked around sick cattle in Yakima county is dead. The official notice handles it in two moves: he had underlying health conditions, and the risk to the general public remains low. Both are true. Together they perform a substitution — an outbreak inside an industry becomes a personal medical event.

Galtung's arithmetic fits: 33 positive dairy herds, three human cases, one death. That is the actual. The potential is routine testing of dairy workers, enforced protective equipment, isolation of positive herds instead of continued milking. The gap is not weather. It is a structural choice. Risk is never distributed evenly; it follows the line of who has to stand next to the sick animal, who has no paid sick leave, who cannot refuse a shift.

"Low risk to the general public" exists to keep the general public from looking at the people who are not general. The notice even names them: dairy workers and others in close or prolonged contact with infected animals. The list is written. What follows the list — protection, testing, compensation — the report does not mention. The dead person's identity is withheld, and with it his working conditions, whether he had gear, how long he waited before seeking care. The absence sits exactly where structural violence does its work.

Then the quieter move: attributing death to underlying conditions. Standard public-health phrasing, and also a pricing decision — it reclassifies a death from product of a workplace to accident of a body. Nobody then has to answer for the 33 positive herds, or for why a person worked beside infected cattle until he was infected.

The CDC counts 72 human cases since 2024 and maintains there is no known person-to-person spread. That holds for now. But "no transmission yet" is a statement about yesterday, not a guarantee about tomorrow; every additional mammalian host is another roll. Treating that sentence as a reason to skip stronger surveillance is pricing future risk with past luck.

What the report leaves out matters more than what it prints: no farm name, no owner, no word from the state agriculture department on herd disposal. A place with one death and 33 infected herds is having a public conversation about how low the risk is.

"Low risk" is accurate for the people saying it. They are not in the barn.
