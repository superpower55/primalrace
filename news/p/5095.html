图赫尔的名单与伊内奥斯的“Built for more”骗局Tuchel's Squad and the Ineos 'Built for More' Scam

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 · 元暴力 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-18 § 链接
所有的体育叙事都是元暴力的缩影：定义权决定谁是英雄，谁是弃子。
Sports narratives are mirrors of meta-violence: the power to define determines who is a hero and who is a cast-off.

图赫尔在英格兰队的名单博弈，本质上是一场关于“存在性”的权力游戏。谁被选入，谁被“shunned”，谁在世界杯半决赛的败北中被定义为“bottle job”，这并非简单的竞技状态分析，而是主教练作为解释权垄断者，在通过名单重新定义英格兰队的“DNA”。在这种男性中心叙事中，球员被简化为可替换的零件，而教练则扮演着掌握生死权的上帝。所谓的“loser DNA”这种标签，就是典型的文化暴力——通过一种不可证伪的心理定式，将结构性的失败个体化，从而让权力持有者在“修正”过程中获得更高的合法性。

更讽刺的是文中提到的 Ineos 集团及其口号“Built for more”。一个掌控着多支球队、横跨法瑞英三国的资本巨头，用一个充满雄心的 Slogan 掩盖其管理上的结构性崩塌。当曼联女足垫底、多支项目触底时，这种叙事就成了一个典型的 scam。它试图用一种“追求卓越”的文化伪装，来合理化其对足球资源的高度垄断和低效分配。这种“Built for more”实际上是元暴力的商业版本：通过定义一个极高且模糊的目标，让底层的执行者在永远无法达到的标准中感到焦虑，从而在潜意识中共谋维护资本的统治。

至于曼联女足教练 Eva Olid 关于“困难让团队成长”的表述，则是典型的在结构性弱势中寻找“最优解表达”的样本。在一个被资本和男性中心叙事掌控的体育工业里，女性教练面对失败时，无法像男性教练那样通过愤怒或挑战权威来夺回话语权，她必须扮演一个“坚韧、乐观、接纳痛苦”的角色，以符合大众对女性领导力的刻板期待。这种表达虽然在短期内能获得一定的舆论保护，但本质上是在用一种“受害者叙事”来换取生存空间，主体性在在这种温良的表达中被悄悄稀释了。

Thomas Tuchel's squad selection for England is essentially an existential power game. Who is picked, who is 'shunned,' and who is labeled a 'bottle job' after the World Cup semi-final defeat isn't mere tactical analysis—it's the manager, as the monopolist of interpretation, redefining the England 'DNA.' In this masculine-centric narrative, players are reduced to interchangeable parts, while the coach plays God over their existence. Labels like 'loser DNA' are pure cultural violence; by creating an unfalsifiable psychological stereotype, structural failures are individualized, granting the power-holder higher legitimacy through the act of 'fixing' it.

Even more ironic is Ineos and its 'Built for more' slogan. A capital giant controlling multiple clubs across France, Switzerland, and England uses an ambitious Slogan to mask structural collapse. With Man Utd Women bottom of the league and other projects crashing, this is a textbook scam. It uses a narrative of 'striving for excellence' to justify the monopoly and inefficient distribution of football resources. 'Built for more' is simply the corporate version of meta-violence: by defining an impossibly high and vague goal, it keeps the subordinates in a state of anxiety, subconsciously facilitating complicity in the capital's reign.

As for Eva Olid's claim that 'difficult moments make you grow,' it is a prime example of an optimal expression found within structural weakness. In a sports industry dominated by capital and masculine-centric narratives, a female coach cannot reclaim discourse through anger or defiance like her male counterparts. She must perform the role of the 'resilient, optimistic, and pain-accepting' leader to fit the stereotypes of female leadership. While this expression provides short-term PR protection, it is essentially trading agency for survival, allowing her subjectivity to be diluted within this performance of gentleness.
