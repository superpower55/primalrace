136年的亚军：一种被浪漫化的结构性失败136 Years of Runner-up: The Romanticization of Structural Failure

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-23 § 链接
将长期的失败叙事化为“情怀”，是掩盖系统性无能的文化共谋。
Turning chronic failure into a narrative of 'passion' is a cultural complicity that masks systemic incompetence.

这篇文章读起来像是一首关于失落的挽歌，充满了典型的英式体育叙事：对传统（Victoriana）的迷恋，对“亚军”这种宿命论的自嘲，以及一种“只要还在痛，就说明还在意”的自我感动。但如果剥离这些感性的外壳，你会发现这其实是一个关于结构性失败（structural failure）的典型样本。

Somerset 136年没拿冠军，这在体育博弈中已经不是偶然，而是一种惯性。当一个组织在同一个维度上连续数十年、甚至一个世纪地扮演“陪跑者”时，这种“亚军身份”就成了一种文化层面的 weaponized narrative。它把竞技体育中残酷的 Potential − Actual 差额，通过“悲剧英雄”的叙事包装成了某种高尚的忠诚。这种叙事让支持者和参与者在潜意识里达成了一种共谋（complicity）：我们不追求真正的胜利，我们追求的是“追求胜利而不得”的这种纯粹感。

更讽刺的是文中提到的 Tom Banton。一个球员在去年还口喊红球赛（长赛制）至关重要，今年就直接 quit 了。这揭示了当前板球生态中的存在性战争：传统的、需要六个月苦旅的冠军赛（Championship）正在被快餐化的 The Hundred 和 T20 蚕食。红球赛变成了某种“秘术”（arcana），而球员们为了寻找个人表达的最优解（optimal expression），选择了那些能带来更快回报、更多注意力的短赛制。在这种结构性坍塌面前，Somerset 的“心痛”其实是一种廉价的文化装饰品。

这种“心痛”之所以能持续136年，是因为它不需要承担胜利后的责任，只需要在每年九月的末端，通过一次集体性的叹息来完成身份的确立。这根本不是什么体育精神，而是一场关于“失败”的定价权游戏：只要把失败定义为“宿命”，那么无能就变成了某种浪漫。

This article reads like an elegy for loss, saturated with typical British sporting narratives: a fetish for tradition (Victoriana), a fatalistic mockery of being the 'eternal runner-up,' and a self-indulgent belief that 'as long as it hurts, it matters.' But strip away the emotional veneer, and you find a textbook case of structural failure.

Somerset failing to win a title for 136 years is no longer an accident; it is inertia. When an organization consistently plays the role of the 'sidekick' for decades, this 'runner-up identity' becomes a weaponized narrative. It packages the brutal gap between Potential and Actual in the Violence Triangle as a kind of noble loyalty. This narrative fosters a complicity among supporters and players: they stop pursuing actual victory and start pursuing the 'pure' feeling of longing for a victory that never comes.

Even more ironic is the case of Tom Banton. A player who championed the vital importance of red-ball cricket last year simply quit it this year. This exposes the existential war within the current cricket ecosystem: the traditional Championship, a six-month slog, is being cannibalized by fast-food formats like The Hundred and T20. Red-ball cricket has become a form of 'arcana,' while players, seeking the optimal expression for their own careers, pivot toward formats that offer faster returns and more attention. Against this structural collapse, Somerset's 'heartache' is merely a cheap cultural ornament.

This 'pain' persists for 136 years because it requires no responsibility of victory. It only requires a collective sigh at the end of every September to confirm a shared identity. This isn't sporting spirit; it is a game of pricing 'failure': as long as failure is defined as 'destiny,' incompetence is rebranded as romance.
