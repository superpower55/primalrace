十年志愿，一朝被诉Ten Years of Volunteering, One Lawsuit

文娱 结构层 · 元暴力 Polygon.com ↗ 2026-10-31 § 链接
志愿劳动没有合同，就只剩人情和代码仓库
Volunteer labor without contracts leaves only goodwill and a code repo

Skyblivion 这桩官司最值得看的不是版权，是十年志愿劳动里从来没有被写下来的那部分——谁拥有你走之后留下的东西。

一个志愿者离开项目，团队撤销了他对源码仓库的访问权限，然后为了维持已经建成的系统继续运转，反编译了他留下的那部分代码。他现在要起诉，要求交出其他成员的真实姓名和住址。团队把有争议的代码删了，他还在告。

这里没有 Bethesda，没有微软，没有大公司法务部。发起法律威胁的是另一个志愿者。这恰恰说明问题不在资本，在结构：一个由志愿者堆起来的项目，运行了十几年，人员进进出出，却没有任何机制规定离开的人和工作之间还剩什么关系。代码是谁的，贡献怎么算，走了以后能不能撤，全靠默契。默契在的时候叫社区，默契破了就叫诉讼。

要求交出真名和住址，是把博弈从代码层面直接推到人身层面。这一招不新鲜：当一个人在规则内赢不了，就去动对方存在的确证——你住在哪，你是谁，你怕不怕。表达在这里被武器化了，武器不是叙事，是地址。

Skyblivion 能走到今天，靠的是大量无偿劳动和一个默认前提：大家都是为了这个作品。这个前提让项目省掉了所有该有的东西——合同、贡献协议、退出条款。省下来的成本没有消失，它被推迟了，现在一次性到期。

团队在 X 和 YouTube 上向粉丝求助，问有没有人愿意提供法律援助。一个十几年、上百人参与的项目，走到法律威胁面前，能调动的资源是“有没有粉丝懂法”。这不是悲壮，这是结构裸奔。

Bethesda 送过免费游戏，表示过支持。支持是姿态，姿态不写进任何一份贡献协议里。等真出了事，姿态一分钱都兑不出来。

The interesting thing about the Skyblivion lawsuit isn't copyright. It's the part of a decade of volunteer labor that was never written down: who owns what you leave behind when you walk away.

A volunteer left the project. The team revoked his access to the source code repository. To keep systems already built into the project running, they decompiled parts of his work. Now he's suing, and demanding the real names and addresses of the other people on the project. The disputed code has been removed. He's still pursuing legal action.

No Bethesda here, no Microsoft, no corporate legal department. The legal threat comes from another volunteer. Which is exactly the point: the problem isn't capital, it's structure. A project built by volunteers over more than a decade, with people coming and going the whole time, and no mechanism at all for what a departing person's relationship to the work is. Whose code is it, how is contribution counted, can you revoke it after you leave — all of it rests on tacit understanding. When the understanding holds, it's called a community. When it breaks, it's called litigation.

Demanding real names and addresses moves the fight from code to persons. This move isn't new: when you can't win inside the rules, you go after the other side's proof of existence — where you live, who you are, whether you're afraid. Expression gets weaponized here, and the weapon isn't narrative. It's an address.

Skyblivion got this far on unpaid labor and one shared assumption: everyone is here for the work. That assumption let the project skip everything it should have had — contracts, contribution agreements, exit terms. The saved cost didn't disappear. It was deferred, and now it's due all at once.

The team went on X and YouTube to ask fans whether anyone with legal skills would volunteer to help. A project of over a decade and a large team of contributors, facing a legal threat, and what it can mobilize is "does any fan know law." That isn't tragic. That's structural nakedness.

Bethesda sent free copies of the remaster and expressed support. Support is a gesture, and a gesture isn't written into any contribution agreement. When it actually matters, the gesture cashes out at zero.
