环保皮革：一场关于“替代”的叙事 scamAlternative Leather: A Narrative Scam of Sustainability

哲学 文化层 · 结构层 The Guardian ↗ 2026-09-12 § 链接
用一种塑料伪装成另一种自然，是资本在认知入口制造的环保幻象。
Masking plastic as nature is a capitalistic illusion manufactured at the cognitive entry point.

所谓的“纯素皮革” (vegan leather) 是一场典型的武器化叙事。资本通过掌控“环保”这个认知入口，将 PU 和 PVC 等化石燃料产物重新包装成“动物友好的替代品”，成功地让消费者在购买塑料制品时产生一种道德上的优越感。这本质上是一种认知 scam：它用一个更时髦的标签（vegan）掩盖了更深层的结构性暴力——对化石燃料的依赖和微塑料对生态的永久性破坏。

在这场博弈中，所谓的“创新材料”如苹果皮、菠萝皮，大多只是在植物纤维和塑料之间做了一次“三明治”式的拼接。它们在商业上被定义为“替代方案”，但在实际的 Potential − Actual 差额中，它们并没有缩小环境破坏的数值，反而通过增加产品线（portfolio）来驱动更多的消费。正如文中提到的，这些材料没有成为真正的 alternative，而只是成了卖出更多货物的 story。

真正的暴力在于，这种叙事将讨论从“减少消费”转移到了“更换材料”。它诱导人们相信，只要更换了材质，就可以在不改变生活方式的前提下实现环保。这是一种极具欺骗性的“最优解表达”：它给了消费者一个无需反思主体性的快捷路径，而真正的环境修复——比如再生农业 (regenerative farming)——因为不符合快时尚的商业逻辑而被边缘化。

当一个行业习惯于通过“重新定义现实”来掩盖能力不足时，它就在制造一种元暴力。它定义了什么是“环保”，从而剥夺了公众讨论“耐用性”和“循环利用”的权利。一个能用几十年且可修复的真皮制品，在当前的文化叙事中被标记为“残酷”，而一个用两年就裂开、无法回收的塑料包则被标记为“文明”。这种解释权的垄断，让人们在崇拜枷锁的同时，心安理得地参与到地球的自毁进程中。

The concept of "vegan leather" is a textbook example of weaponized expression. By seizing the cognitive entry point of "sustainability," capital has rebranded PU and PVC—fossil fuel derivatives—as "animal-friendly alternatives," allowing consumers to feel a sense of moral superiority while buying plastic. This is a cognitive scam: using a trendy label (vegan) to mask structural violence—specifically, the reliance on fossil fuels and the permanent ecological destruction caused by microplastics.

In this game, so-called "innovative materials" like apple or pineapple leather are mostly just "sandwiches" of plant fibers and plastics. Commercially, they are framed as "alternatives," but in the Galtung formula of Potential − Actual, they fail to reduce environmental harm. Instead, they expand the portfolio to drive more consumption. These materials aren't true alternatives; they are just stories used to sell more stuff.

The real violence lies in shifting the conversation from "reducing consumption" to "changing materials." It tricks people into believing they can achieve sustainability without altering their lifestyle. This is a fake optimal expression: it provides a shortcut that bypasses the need for subjective reflection, while genuine environmental restoration—such as regenerative farming—is marginalized because it doesn't fit the fast-fashion business model.

When an industry habitually redefines reality to cover its lack of capability, it exercises meta-violence. It defines what "eco-friendly" means, thereby stripping the public of the right to discuss "durability" and "circularity." A real leather product that lasts decades and can be repaired is branded as "cruel," while a plastic bag that cracks in two years and cannot be recycled is branded as "civilized." This monopoly over the power of interpretation leads people to worship their own chains while comfortably participating in the planet's self-destruction.
